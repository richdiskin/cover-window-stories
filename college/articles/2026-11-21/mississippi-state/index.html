Mississippi State vs Tennessee Tech: Evans, Puckett and a 90-Yard Gayten Run Snap the Skid, 34-16

Final: Mississippi State Bulldogs (5-6) 34, Tennessee Tech Golden Eagles (9-3) 16, Sat., Nov. 21, Davis Wade Stadium, Starkville. Next: at Ole Miss, Fri., Nov. 27, 12:00 PM ET, Vaught-Hemingway Stadium, Oxford

By Wade Pettus, The Mississippi State Fanatic

XFacebook

The skid is dead and buried. Mississippi State 34, Tennessee Tech 16 at Davis Wade Stadium on Saturday, and the six-game losing streak is finally gone. We're 5-6, and the cowbells sounded like they'd been saving it up since September.

Thirty-four points is the most we've scored since the 41-34 win against South Carolina on Sept. 19. That's how long it had been, folks. Puckett had his best day in maroon, 17 of 25 for a career-high 232 yards and two touchdown throws. He threw one interception, and I'll forgive it, because everything else was crisp.

Anthony Evans III was the headliner. Eight catches, a season-best 129 yards and a 53-yard touchdown in the second quarter that put us ahead for good, 14-10. He's at 929 yards for the season and needs 125 more to own the school record. Get that man the ball on Friday, please and thank you.

Kyle Ferrie added field goals from 48 and 36, the second one right before halftime, and we went to the locker room up 20-10. Marquis Johnson caught a slant and took it 41 yards for a touchdown in the third quarter, and he finished with four catches for 70. Then, on the very first play of the fourth quarter, Xavier Gayten ran 90 yards to the end zone. Ninety! He had three carries for 100 yards on the day. Somebody check that kid's math.

Credit Tennessee Tech. The Golden Eagles came to Starkville at 9-2, held the ball for 40:59 and made us earn it. But our defense lived in their backfield with 10 tackles for loss and four sacks. Jardin Gilbert made 10 tackles and intercepted a throw, and LaKendrick James had 1 1/2 tackles for loss and a share of a sack.

This is what we've been waiting on all fall: receivers winning, a quarterback standing tall and a defense that hits first. It looked like the team we thought we had.

Now comes the one that matters most. We head to Vaught-Hemingway Stadium for the Egg Bowl on Friday, Nov. 27, at 12:00 PM ET, and a win puts us at six and sends us bowling. Ring 'em loud from Starkville to Oxford. Hail State!
