Indiana vs Washington: Charlie Becker's 167 Yards Not Enough in OT Loss

Final: Washington Huskies (7-4) 27, Indiana Hoosiers (9-2) 24, Sat., Nov. 21, Husky Stadium, Seattle. Next: vs Purdue, Sat., Nov. 28, time TBA, Memorial Stadium (Bloomington, IN), Bloomington

By Mallory Penhallow, The Indiana Fanatic

XFacebook

Three overtime games in four weeks, Hoosier Nation. We won the first two. Saturday in Seattle, the third one bit us. Washington 27, Indiana 24, and we're 9-2 and 6-2 in the Big Ten with a lot of nervous math ahead of us.

Good news first, because Charlie Becker earned it. On our first snap of the day, Grant Wilson put one on him in stride, and Becker went 74 yards for a touchdown. That catch pushed him past 1,000 yards for the season. He's the eighth Hoosier ever to do it and the first since Whop Philyor in 2019. He finished with six catches for 167 yards and now sits at 1,117. Ernie Jones has the school record at 1,265. Somebody tell Ernie to keep an eye on the mailbox.

Wilson also ran one in from 5 yards out, and we were up 14-7 in the first quarter. Then the offense went quiet. We went 2 for 15 on third down, and that's the story right there.

Coach Cignetti went to Josh Hoover in the third quarter, and Hoover gave us a jolt. With 9:06 left, he hit Nick Marsh for a 35-yard touchdown to make it 24-17. Hoover went 7 of 13 for 121 yards in about a half. Lee Beebe Jr. ran 15 times for 94 yards, including a 42-yarder.

And zero turnovers, none. Preston Zachman made eight tackles and grabbed an interception. Byron Baldwin Jr. had two sacks. Our defense gave us every chance.

The kicking game is what hurts. Their big offensive lineman blocked a 46-yarder in the third quarter, and then he blocked our 37-yarder in overtime after they'd made theirs. Paddy McAteer did hit a 34-yarder, but in a three-point loss, those two kicks are the ballgame.

I'm not giving up on this team. They won a national title last year, and they've won close games all fall. Losing one doesn't change who they are.

Next up: Purdue at Memorial Stadium, Saturday, Nov. 28, time to be announced. The Old Oaken Bucket stays in Bloomington. Go Hoosiers!
