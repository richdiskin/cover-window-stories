Miami vs Notre Dame: Canes Rally Falls Short in South Bend 24-31

Final: Notre Dame Fighting Irish (8-1) 31, Miami Hurricanes (8-1) 24, Sat., Nov. 7, Notre Dame Stadium, Notre Dame. Next: vs. Duke, Sat., Nov. 14, 12:00 PM ET, Hard Rock Stadium, Miami Gardens

XFacebook

Notre Dame 31, Miami 24, and the unbeaten ride ended in South Bend on a 40-degree night. Back home in Coral Gables, the Frost Musical Theatre Ensemble raised the curtain on Page to Stage at Gusman Concert Hall at 7:30, the same minute our Canes kicked off. The campus show got its happy ending. Ours in Indiana did not, and my sunglasses went on indoors before halftime.

The first half felt like somebody else's team. We had first-and-goal at the Irish 1 in the second quarter, ran it three times, lost three yards on the last one and settled for a 19-yard Jake Weinberg field goal. CJ Carr hit Jordan Faison for 27 yards and Micah Gilbert for 18 with 29 seconds left, and we walked into the locker room down 17-3. Gilbert scored again from 20 early in the third. 24-3. Silence in my living room.

Then the U remembered who it was. Darian Mensah found Elija Lofton for a 12-yard score, then Cooper Barkate for 12 more, and it was 24-17 with 5:29 left in the third. Mensah to Barkate, it's never too late! Luke Talich jumped a deep out to stop the surge, the Irish converted a fourth down for 36 yards, and Nolan James Jr. plunged in. We answered anyway: Mensah to Barkate from a yard out, 31-24 with 9:53 to go.

You know the rest, and I wish I didn't. Fourth down, 3:36 left, Mensah threw for the corner, and Lofton came down with both feet in and the ball in his hands. Talich tore it loose, the officials ruled no catch, and Notre Dame ran the clock down to about a minute. A sack ended the last drive, and a hook-and-lateral on the final snap bounced away on the turf.

The honest film: Mensah threw for 276 yards and three touchdowns with one interception. Barkate caught 10 balls for 92 yards and two scores, Lofton grabbed seven for 113, and Mohamed Toure made 12 tackles. We ran for just 101 yards, went 6 of 14 on third down and gave it away twice while Notre Dame never did. We also played the whole night without injured receiver Malachi Toney, and the red zone felt every inch of that.

We're 8-1 and still 6-0 in the ACC, and that is the race that decides where December goes. Duke comes to Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 14, at noon. The sunglasses come off Monday. It's all about the U, baby, and the U still has November.
