North Carolina vs UConn: Takeaways Fuel a 38-21 Road Win

Final: North Carolina Tar Heels (5-4) 38, UConn Huskies (4-5) 21, Sat., Nov. 7, Pratt & Whitney Stadium, East Hartford. Next: vs Louisville, Sat., Nov. 14, Kenan Stadium, Chapel Hill

XFacebook

North Carolina Tar Heels 38, UConn Huskies 21. Final score 38-21 in East Hartford, and the two-game losing streak is gone. Five and four. Three and one on the road. And yes, Bill Belichick coached his first game back in New England since the Patriots, and the Tar Heels sent him home with a win.

Back in Chapel Hill on Saturday, Festifall turned 53 with its arts market at 100 East Rosemary Street downtown, pottery and paintings and woodwork from all around the Triangle. Very nice. But the finest handiwork of the day was done in Connecticut, by our secondary.

Timir Hickman-Collins opened the scoring, reading Kalieb Osborne and taking an interception 62 yards to the house for a 7-0 lead. Later he fell on a Husky fumble, and Billy Edwards Jr. cashed it with a 6-yard touchdown throw to Jordan Shipp. Jalon Thompson grabbed the third takeaway in the fourth quarter, and Edwards turned that one into a 3-yard score to Jelani Thurman with 3:38 left. Three turnovers, 21 points. As a kinesiologist, I can tell you that is what force production looks like in a defensive back.

Edwards was sharp all afternoon, 29 of 38 for 255 yards and three touchdowns. Thurman caught seven balls for 64 and both of his scores came from 3 yards out, the first with 24 seconds left in the half for a 28-14 lead. That gives him eight touchdown catches this season, tying the school record for a tight end, per the AP. Benjamin Hall ripped off a 39-yard touchdown run and finished with 81 yards on nine carries. We went 9 of 16 on third down and 2 for 2 on fourth.

Peyton Seelmann was everywhere: nine tackles, two sacks, three throws broken up. Rece Verhoff hit from 27 and was perfect on extras. Not a perfect day, mind you. Edwards threw one interception, and UConn cut it to 28-21 in the third before we closed the door.

Louisville comes to Kenan Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 14, kickoff time still to be set. Somewhere eight miles down the road, a certain school is not having this much fun. Go Heels.
