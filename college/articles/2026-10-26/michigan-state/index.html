Wrong Pitch Calls: Undermanned Michigan State Grabs an Early Lead, Then Falls at UCLA 28-10

UCLA 28, Michigan State 10 (final), Sat., Oct. 24, Rose Bowl

By Shay Kowalczyk, The Michigan State Fanatic

Alright, gear on. Mask down. Squat. Shay Kowalczyk here, knees crackling like a bowl of cereal, and I'm giving it to you straight from behind the plate. UCLA 28, Michigan State 10, at the Rose Bowl. Six straight losses now. My knees hurt. My heart hurts more.

But first, the inning we won. UCLA fumbled in the first quarter, and Devin Vaught scooped it and took off 56 yards. A few plays later, Kayd Coffman, a third-string quarterback making his first career start, threw a 14-yard touchdown to Kenneth Williams. Seven to nothing, Spartans, in the Rose Bowl. I stood up so fast my shin guards rattled.

Then the pitch calls went wrong. UCLA tied it, went ahead 14-7, and then the big one: on the first drive of the third quarter, Ty Dieffenbach threw a 63-yard touchdown to Brian Rowe Jr. Coach Pat Fitzgerald pointed to that big play to open the half, and he was right. That's a hanging changeup. UCLA scored again for 28-7. In the second and third quarters, UCLA outgained us 280 to 13, per the AP. That's no-hitter territory, from the wrong dugout.

Hero: Vaught, with a fumble recovery, the long return and eight tackles. Nikai Martinez also made eight tackles and recovered a fumble. Coffman got his first career touchdown pass in his first career start, which is a memory nobody can take away. Liam Boyd hit a 46-yard field goal. Leo Hannan came in for the fourth quarter and kept battling.

The goat, with a hand on the shoulder, catcher to pitcher: the offense, 156 yards of offense and 47 on the ground. But honestly, look at the injury list. The Spartans were already without their top two running backs, per the AP. Then Marvis Parrish got hurt on the third play of the game and had to be helped off. Prayers to Marvis. You don't throw a gem with half your staff in the trainer's room.

What it means: Michigan State is 2-6 and 0-5 in the Big Ten, and 0-3 against ranked teams. The open week couldn't come at a better time. Ice the knees. Heal up.

Next up: an open date on Oct. 31, then at Michigan on Saturday, Nov. 7, time and TV to be announced. Rivalry week. Nothing heals like that.

Mask up. Go Green! Go White!
