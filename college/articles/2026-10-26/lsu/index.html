Gumbo on the Plains: Sam Leavitt Cooks Up Four Touchdowns as LSU Rolls Auburn 38-17

LSU 38, Auburn 17 (final), Sat., Oct. 24, Jordan-Hare Stadium

By Celestine 'Cece' Thibodeaux, The LSU Fanatic

Bonjour, Tiger fans! Ça va? Cece Thibodeaux here, and cher, I'm still licking the spoon. I drove home from Auburn with a pot of étouffée buckled into the passenger seat like a beloved child. LSU 38, Auburn 17, on the Plains, ending an eight-game road losing streak in SEC play. Mamère would have looked at that roux and said, now that's a color.

You build a gumbo in layers, and the Tigers built a 31-10 halftime lead the same way, scoring on five of six first-half possessions. Sam Leavitt hit Jackson Harris for a 10-yard touchdown, then capped a 95-yard drive with a 15-yarder to tight end Trey'Dez Green, back after missing four games with a sprained knee. Welcome home, baby. We saved you a bowl.

Auburn cut it to 17-10, and I put the lid on the pot for about a minute. Then Harris caught a 12-yard crossing route and turned it into a 70-yard touchdown sprint. He outran the defense, the cameras and my cousin Remy's truck. Leavitt finished the half with a 4-yard scoring run with 17 seconds left. Let it simmer.

Head chef: Leavitt, 32 of 45 for a career-high 387 yards and three touchdowns, plus 54 rushing yards and a score, with zero turnovers for a second straight week. Harris caught nine balls for 178 yards. And the defense? Mon Dieu. Byrum Brown ran for 115 yards against Georgia the week before. Against LSU, 21 yards on 19 carries and six sacks, with Davhon Keys, TJ Dottery and Princewill Umanmielen each getting one. LSU outgained Auburn 552 to 271. That's not a game, cher. That's a cooking class.

The burnt rice, with love: a scoreless third quarter, when we napped like Uncle Gaston after Thanksgiving. We'll scrape the pot.

What it means: LSU is 6-2 and 4-2 in the SEC and climbed from No. 18 to No. 15 in the AP poll released Oct. 25. The AP called it the best half the Tigers have played under Lane Kiffin.

Next up: an open date, then Alabama comes to Tiger Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 7, time and TV to be announced. I'll be there at dawn with a pot big enough for the whole lot, and I'll feed the Alabama folks too. Hospitality is a Louisiana sacrament. They just won't enjoy dessert.

Laissez les bons temps rouler, and Geaux Tigers!
