Cowbell's Quiet, Not Silent: Mississippi State Rallies Late but Falls to Oklahoma, 23-15

Oklahoma 23, Mississippi State 15 (final), Sat., Oct. 24, Davis Wade Stadium

By Wade Pettus, The Mississippi State Fanatic

Well. Evenin', folks. Wade Pettus here, on the tailgate of my truck outside the feed store, with a stack of receipts I drew plays on all week and a cowbell in my lap. Oklahoma 23, Mississippi State 15. Third straight loss. The bell's been quiet since Saturday. Not silent, mind you. Quiet.

Here's how it slipped. Right before halftime, Puckett drove us to Oklahoma's 6, and we fumbled on the next play. The Sooners went 96 yards the other way, and it was 10-0 at the half. Then they scored three plays into the third quarter. Seventeen to nothing. Coach Jeff Lebby said the Sooners' chunk plays were the difference, and the receipts agree. Per the AP, five passing plays of 15 yards or more made up 191 of Oklahoma's passing yards.

But listen. Then we got fun. Two trick plays back to back, the second one tight end Sam West throwing 18 yards to Camden Capehart for a touchdown. Per the AP, our first touchdown in eight quarters. I rang that bell till the feed store manager came outside. Then, with 2:10 left, Puckett found Sanfrisco Magee for 42 yards and a touchdown, then hit him again for the two. Twenty-three to fifteen. One possession. Oklahoma converted two third downs and ran it out, but we made 'em sweat.

Hero: Fluff Bothwell, 17 carries for 111 yards, and the name alone deserves a bronze statue. Puckett, 17 of 24 for 158 and a touchdown, stepping in. Tyler Lockhart and Fatt Forrest made 10 tackles each, Jaray Bledsoe had a sack, and Kendel Dolby recovered a fumble. The goat, said with love: third down, where we went 1 for 11. That's a receipt I'm tearing up.

And we're thinking of Kamario Taylor, who left in the first quarter after a hit. Lebby said they'd know more early in the week.

What it means: Mississippi State is 4-3 and 2-3 in the SEC. The season isn't over, and the bell is still in my lap.

Next up: at No. 11 Texas on Saturday, Oct. 31, at 7:30 p.m. ET on ESPN. Halloween night in Austin. Bring the bells. All of 'em.

Hail State, and ring 'em loud.
