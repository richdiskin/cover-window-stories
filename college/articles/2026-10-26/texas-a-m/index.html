Howdy, Heartache: Texas A&M Leads Into the Fourth but Falls at Alabama, 31-20

Alabama 31, Texas A&M 20 (final), Sat., Oct. 24, Bryant-Denny Stadium

By Colt Wendeborn, The Texas A&M Fanatic

XFacebook

HOWDY! That's not me yelling, friend. That's the official greeting, and I'll say it even this week. Colt Wendeborn here, petroleum engineering grad and lifelong member of the 12th Man. Alabama 31, Texas A&M 20, in Tuscaloosa. It hurts. But an Aggie stands for the whole game, so let's stand up and look at it.

The start was rough. Less than four minutes in, Marcel Reed was intercepted and Dijon Lee Jr. returned it 39 yards for a touchdown. Coach Mike Elko said afterward that he thought about a quarterback change. And then, friend, Reed went out and played what Elko called probably his best football for the rest of the half. He hit Mario Craver for a 9-yard touchdown, then ran in from 4 yards out for a 14-10 lead. David Olano kicked a 32-yarder before halftime and a 25-yarder in the third. Aggies 20, Tide 17, heading into the fourth. Bryant-Denny got awfully quiet. I may have hummed the War Hymn under my breath.

Then the turn. Fourth-and-1, near midfield, and Reed lost the ball on a sneak. Three plays later, Alabama hit a 38-yard pass to our 5 and scored to take the lead. We had a chance to answer, but Alabama got a sack. Then the Tide scored again. That's the tradition behind the tradition, friend: the fourth quarter is where SEC games get decided.

Hero: the defense held Alabama to 68 rushing yards. Daymion Sanford made eight tackles, Dalton Brooks had seven, and Dezz Ricks intercepted Keelon Russell. Olano was perfect. Craver caught six passes and a touchdown. KJ Edwards ran for 43 yards. The goat, said kindly: ball security. Two interceptions and a fumble against Alabama is a tall order to overcome.

What it means: Texas A&M is 4-4 and 1-4 in the SEC. Per the AP, that's four losses in five conference games after making the Playoff last season. It's tough. But the 12th Man doesn't sit down because the scoreboard says sit. We stand.

Next up: an open date, then at South Carolina on Saturday, Nov. 7, at Williams-Brice Stadium, time and TV to be announced. Two weeks to heal up and get right.

Gig 'em, Aggies. And Howdy, always.
