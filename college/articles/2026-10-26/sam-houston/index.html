A Ballad With a Sad Third Verse: Railyn Adams Shines, Tim Burns Goes 81, but the Aggies Ride Out of Huntsville With a 30-24 Win

New Mexico State 30, Sam Houston 24 (final), Wed. Oct. 21, Elliott T. Bowers Stadium

By Lem Coxwell, The Sam Houston Fanatic

Evenin', folks. Pull up a stump. Lem Coxwell here, Walker County born, history teacher by trade and keeper of Texas folklore by calling. Wednesday night's tale at Bowers Stadium had a hero, a villain and a sad third verse. New Mexico State won 30-24.

Verse one is a good one. Early in the first quarter, our special teams blocked a punt and Railyn Adams scooped it into the end zone. Seven to nothing before anybody found their seat. Later, Sam Carpenter hit a 38-yard field goal, and we led 10-7.

Verse two had a lightning bolt. To open the second half, Landyn Locke threw it to Tim Burns Jr., and Tim ran 81 yards for a touchdown. We led 17-14. If General Houston had a man that fast at San Jacinto, the battle would've been over in nine minutes instead of 18.

Verse three is where the ballad turns sad. New Mexico State scored 16 straight points in the third quarter, helped by a lateral that came loose on our end. Then, early in the fourth, we drove deep into Aggie territory and came away with nothing after a fourth-down throw into the end zone fell incomplete. MJ Dowdy finally scored from the 1 with 4:00 left after Railyn Adams intercepted Adam Damante, but the Aggies ran out the clock.

My hero is Railyn Adams: a blocked-punt touchdown and an interception. Tim Burns Jr. had 117 receiving yards. Our defense held the Aggies to 2-of-12 on third down.

My affectionate goat is the penalty flag. Thirteen of them for 118 yards. In Sam Houston's day, a man with 13 infractions got sent to the territories. We also ran for 11 yards on 20 carries. Our run game needs a revival tent.

What it means: the Bearkats are 1-6 and 0-3 in Conference USA. That's a hard chapter. But General Houston lost a few skirmishes before San Jacinto, and Huntsville still built him a 67-foot statue.

Next up, Sam Houston visits Missouri State at Plaster Stadium on Tuesday, Oct. 27, at 8 p.m. ET on ESPN2. Both teams are looking for a better verse. Bring a lawn chair and a story, folks. I'll bring the history.
