Failed Experiment: Boston College Runs Over Georgia Tech on Homecoming, 49-27

Boston College 49, Georgia Tech 27 (final), Sat., Oct. 24, Bobby Dodd Stadium

By Wendell Quist, The Georgia Tech Fanatic

Greetings, Jackets. Wendell Quist reporting, mechanical pencil behind my ear, graph paper on the desk and a lab notebook I'd rather not show anybody this week. Boston College 49, Georgia Tech 27, at Bobby Dodd on homecoming. Hypothesis: we contain the run. Result: rejected. Emphatically.

The early data was promising. Georgia Tech scored on its first two possessions. Alberto Mendoza completed a 43-yard pass, ran for 27 more and threw a 4-yard touchdown to Kevin Roche Jr. Aidan Birr added a 32-yard field goal. Ten to nothing. I drew a beautiful upward curve on my graph.

Then the variables changed. Boston College ran the same couple of plays over and over, and we never solved the equation. The Eagles scored on their first two possessions of the second half, including a 40-yard Mason McKenzie run off a fake up the middle. Then a deflected pass turned into an interception, and BC scored again. The decisive vector came in the fourth quarter. Down 35-20, Mendoza's pass was intercepted by Charlie Comella and returned 47 yards for a touchdown. That's a trajectory I didn't want to plot.

The full dataset: Boston College ran for 399 yards. McKenzie had 170 and Bo MacCormack III had 155. Physics, my friends, is about force and mass. Their mass kept generating force. Our tackling did not.

Positive results, recorded honestly: Birr made both his field goals and all three extra points. Dalen Penson caught five passes for 88 yards, including a 30-yard touchdown from sophomore Grady Adamson, who went 7 of 9 for 73 yards. Jordan Allen had four catches for 85. Chris Hewitt Jr. made 10 tackles. Justice Haynes scored from 15 yards out. The goat, with empathy: the run defense. That's where the experiment failed.

What it means: Georgia Tech is 2-5 and 1-3 in the ACC. Per the AP, after an 8-0 start in 2025, the Jackets have lost nine of their last 12. The homecoming crowd let its feelings be known. Coach Brent Key said what we're doing isn't working, across the board. Good scientists accept the data and redesign the experiment.

Next up: at Pitt on Saturday, Oct. 31, at 7:30 p.m. ET on ESPN2. Halloween night at Acrisure Stadium. New trial, new variables, same pencil.

Adjust, retest and rerun. I'm a Ramblin' Wreck from Georgia Tech, and a hopeful engineer. Go Jackets.
