The Comeback Blues: Marcus Stokes Scores Five Times After Halftime, and Memphis Rallies Past East Carolina in Overtime, 41-34

Memphis 41, East Carolina 34 (final, OT), Thu. Oct. 22, Simmons Bank Liberty Stadium

By Otis Delaney-Crum, The Memphis Fanatic

Well, good evening, Bluff City. Otis Delaney-Crum here, guitar on my knee, rib sauce on my sleeve, and a voice that cracked on the high notes Thursday night. Memphis beat East Carolina 41-34 in overtime at Simmons Bank Liberty Stadium. That wasn't a football game, friends. That was a blues song with a happy ending.

Verse one: the sad part. At halftime, we trailed 16-3. Early in the third, East Carolina made it 24-10. The crowd went quiet like a juke joint at closing time.

Verse two: the comeback. Marcus Stokes picked up his guitar. First, a 59-yard touchdown pass to Jermane Hayes. Then a 20-yarder to Jemyri Davis. Philippe Laforge kicked a field goal. Then Stokes hit Davis again from 4 yards out to tie it at 27, and with 3:31 left, he ran it in from 2 himself. Tigers 34, Pirates 27. The whole stadium was keeping time.

Verse three: the encore. East Carolina tied it with 1:30 left, which hurt like a broken string. But in overtime, Stokes found Davis for 20 yards, then ran in from the 5. East Carolina drove to our 4, and Ellis Ellis Jr. intercepted Mitch Griffis in the end zone. Curtain down, house lights up.

My hero is Marcus Stokes: 29 of 43 for 320 yards, three touchdown passes and two rushing touchdowns, all after halftime. Jemyri Davis caught seven passes for 91 yards and two scores. Ellis Ellis Jr. had the game-ending interception, and Chauncey Logan Jr. had another.

My affectionate goat: the first half. Three points, a punt-return fumble and some sour notes. Even B.B. King tuned up before the show, fellas.

What it means: Memphis is 6-2 and 3-1 in the American, and the bad taste from the Tulane loss is gone. We're right behind UTSA, North Texas and Army in the race. Beale Street is a national historic landmark, and on Thursday night it heard a real Memphis comeback.

Next up, Army comes to the Liberty Bowl on Saturday, Oct. 31, at 8 p.m. ET on ESPNU. Halloween night against the Black Knights. Get there early, fire up the smoker and bring your loudest Go Tigers Go.
