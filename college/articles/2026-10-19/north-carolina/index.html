Carolina Blue, Through and Through: The Victory Bell Comes Home, and Syracuse Visits Kenan From a City That Got 143.1 Inches of Snow and Still Plays Indoors

Syracuse (2-4) at North Carolina (4-2), Sat., Oct. 24, 7:30 p.m. ET, Kenan Stadium, ACC Network

By Ainsley Pemberton, The North Carolina Fanatic

Well, hello, darlings. Ainsley Pemberton here, Carolina-blue debutante, holder of a PhD in kinesiology and owner of more pearls than any one woman needs. I watch every Tar Heels game from the stands, and I analyze it the way I was trained: biomechanically, lovingly and with a gentle word or two about Duke.

And oh, this week I have more than a word or two about Duke. Because, sugar, the Victory Bell is coming home to Chapel Hill.

Part 1: Last Week, We Rang the Bell

North Carolina beat Duke 24-20 on Saturday in Durham. We're 4-2 and 2-1 in the ACC, and we've won two straight road games.

It started like a debutante ball. Rece Verhoff kicked a 28-yard field goal. Billy Edwards Jr. hit Jordan Shipp for a 5-yard touchdown. On the third play of the second quarter, Demon June ran in from 4 yards out, and we led 17-0. I was so composed. I took one sip of sweet tea and smoothed my skirt.

Then the punch bowl tipped over. Duke's safety returned a fumble 9 yards for a touchdown in the second quarter. Duke kicked two field goals in the second half. We went more than 41 minutes without scoring, and with 4:39 left, Duke took its first lead, 20-17, on a 7-yard touchdown catch. My kinesiology training tells me a sustained elevated heart rate is good cardio. That was a full workout.

But Carolina girls don't faint. Our final drive went 78 yards. With 3:48 left and facing fourth-and-4 from our own 28, we went for it, and Edwards found Jelani Thurman over the middle for 12 yards. Coach Bill Belichick made it clear afterward that he was done punting and wanted to take his best shot. Darling, I love a man with conviction.

Then, with 1:53 left, Jaylen McGill burst through for a 24-yard touchdown run. 24-20, Tar Heels. Kenton Dopson III had an interception in the game, and at the end the players rushed across the field to reclaim the Victory Bell. The bell, by the way, is an old railway bell from the Southern Railway, and the trophy dates to 1948. Duke will have to visit us to see it now, and we'll paint it blue for them. We're good hosts.

Edwards completed 15 of 25 passes for 191 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions. McGill ran for 75 yards on 12 carries. Shipp caught seven passes for 75 yards. Linebacker Peyton Seelmann made 16 tackles. And Duke's Nate Sheppard, the nation's leading rusher in yards per game, was held to 77 yards on 20 carries. Our defense played like it had studied the anatomy chart.

And Duke? Bless its heart. It had won eight straight games against in-state opponents. That streak is now in the history books, right next to Duke's hopes of keeping the bell.

Part 2: Syracuse Comes to Kenan

Saturday, 7:30 p.m. Eastern on ACC Network. Syracuse (2-4, 0-4 ACC) at North Carolina (4-2) at Kenan Stadium, in the pines.

Respect Where It's Due

Every lady compliments her guests first. Syracuse receiver Umari Hatcher is the real thing. He has 50 catches for 784 yards and seven touchdowns, and last week against Louisville he set career highs with 13 catches for 224 yards. Syracuse's passing offense ranks 12th in FBS at 291.0 yards a game, and its time of possession ranks fifth at 33:59. Ahmad Miller ran for 123 yards last week. Linebacker Chris D'Appolonia made 12 tackles, and Davien Kerr intercepted two passes. That's real talent.

Now, the Diagnosis

Syracuse lost 34-26 to Louisville last week, and it trailed 28-23 at halftime. According to the AP, Syracuse has now lost 11 straight ACC games and 12 straight against Power 4 opponents, and it has been outscored 78-36 in the second half this season. Darling, from a kinesiology standpoint, that's a stamina issue.

Syracuse also leads the country in penalties. It averages 98.0 penalty yards a game, 138th in FBS. Last week, it had three unsportsmanlike penalties in the first half alone. North Carolina, meanwhile, averages 33.7 penalty yards a game, fifth-fewest in FBS. Manners matter, sugar. My mother taught me that, and apparently our football team listened.

Syracuse's red zone offense scores on 78.1 of every 100 trips, 111th in FBS. Our red zone defense ranks 22nd, allowing scores on 73.7 of every 100 trips. And our scoring defense allows 19.2 points a game, 34th.

My Honest Self-Exam

A good doctor checks herself first. Our offense averages 329.7 yards a game, 117th in FBS, and our run game averages 116.2 yards, 120th. Our time of possession ranks 130th at 27:00. Our third-down defense allows conversions on 43.3 of every 100 tries, 113th, and against Duke we fumbled twice. We went 4-8 last season. So I'm not handing out bouquets yet.

But last year, on Oct. 31, 2025, we beat Syracuse 27-10 in its own dome. And Edwards has nine touchdown passes this season, with Shipp leading us in receiving at 441 yards.

My Three Prescriptions

1. Stay disciplined. Syracuse gives away yards with penalties. Let them. We keep our composure and take every free yard they offer. 2. Finish strong. Syracuse has been outscored 78-36 in second halves. Our conditioning should show late. 3. Shadow Hatcher. He's elite. Bracket him, rotate safeties and make the Orange beat us another way.

A Tale of Two Towns: Chapel Hill vs. Syracuse

Chapel Hill. The University of North Carolina was chartered on Dec. 11, 1789, and began enrolling students in 1795. It's one of three universities that claim to be the oldest public university in America, and it's the only one that conferred degrees as a public institution in the 18th century. Darling, we were handing out diplomas while George Washington was president. Our town is named for New Hope Chapel, a small chapel of ease built on the crest of the hill in 1752, and our main street, Franklin Street, was named in memory of Benjamin Franklin. Kenan Stadium opened in 1927, and its first game was a 27-0 win over Davidson. And our live mascot, Rameses, is a Dorset Horn sheep who attends games with his horns painted Carolina blue. Even the livestock has a signature color.

Syracuse. Syracuse is a charming city, and it's honest about its weather. A normal season brings about 127.8 inches of snow, and it won the Golden Snowball Award for 2025-26 with 143.1 inches, its second straight title. Its record season was 192.1 inches in 1992-93. So what does a city that gets 143 inches of snow do with its football team? It puts the team indoors. The JMA Wireless Dome is the largest domed stadium on any college campus. Sugar, they built a giant snow globe and climbed inside. Saturday night in Chapel Hill, they'll get to see football under an actual sky.

And Syracuse is named after an ancient Greek city in Sicily. It borrowed its name from a sunny Mediterranean island and then got 143 inches of snow. That's like naming your goldfish "Shark." Its mascot is Otto the Orange, a fruit, and the team name comes from a color. Our mascot is a ram. Fruit doesn't fare well against horns, darling.

The Benediction

Here's my final diagnosis. North Carolina has a top-35 scoring defense, one of the most disciplined teams in America and the Victory Bell. Syracuse has a terrific receiver, a passing offense to respect and a penalty problem that ranks dead last in FBS. If we stay disciplined, finish strong and keep Hatcher in front of us, Kenan will be singing on Saturday night.

Carolina blue, through and through.

My call: North Carolina Tar Heels 29, Syracuse Orange 20.
