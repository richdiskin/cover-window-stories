It's All About the U, Baby: No. 1 Miami Survives Double Overtime and Welcomes Pitt From the City of 446 Bridges

Pittsburgh (5-2) at No. 1 Miami (6-0), Sat., Oct. 24, 12 p.m. ET, Hard Rock Stadium, ESPN

By Javi 'The Breeze' Castillo, The Miami Fanatic

¡Wepa, familia! Javi "The Breeze" Castillo here, linen shirt pressed, sunglasses on, café cubano steaming on the balcony. I used to play defensive back, so I still scout every game like I'm backpedaling against a slot receiver: eyes up, hips loose, never panic. And I talk in rhymes, because life's too short for one-liners that don't land.

Saturday night, my hips were loose and my heart was not. Two overtimes, baby. Two!

It's all about the U, baby.

Part 1: Double Overtime, Double the Drama

No. 3 Miami beat Florida State 43-35 in double overtime at Hard Rock Stadium. We're 6-0 and 4-0 in the ACC, and this week the AP poll moved us up to No. 1. Uno. Número uno, ¿y qué? Rhyme number one: "Down in Miami Gardens, the 'Noles came to play; we sent 'em home in overtime, that's the 305 way."

Darian Mensah threw for 326 yards and three touchdowns, going 23 of 34. Malachi Toney caught 11 passes for 154 yards and two touchdowns, and then he threw the two-point conversion to Cooper Barkate after his overtime score. A receiver throwing the clincher? That's flavor, familia. Javian Mallory ran for 108 yards and two touchdowns, including a 25-yarder in the first overtime. Mark Fletcher Jr. added a 7-yard touchdown run.

Now, I'll be honest, because The Breeze doesn't blow smoke. This one got scary. Elija Lofton caught a 3-yard touchdown pass with 2:49 left to put us up 28-21, and Florida State tied it on a 75-yard touchdown pass 10 seconds later. In the first overtime, they scored first. Mallory answered. In the second overtime, Mensah hit Toney from 3 yards out, and Toney threw the two-pointer.

Then Florida State completed a 36-yard pass on fourth-and-21 that would've made it a two-point game, but it was called back for holding. On the next play, a final heave into the end zone, Bryce Fitzgerald came down with the interception. After a review, the call stood, and the Hurricanes stormed the field. ¡Qué noche!

Miami gained 583 yards, ran for 257 and held the ball for 37:20. Florida State went 0 for 3 on fourth down, including two stops inside our 5. AP says Miami has now won seven of its last 10 against the Seminoles. And here's a stat for the defensive backs out there: Mensah had thrown 153 passes as a Hurricane before his first interception. One-fifty-three. That's not a quarterback. That's a vault.

Part 2: Pitt Comes to the 305

Saturday, noon Eastern on ESPN. Pittsburgh (5-2) at No. 1 Miami (6-0) at Hard Rock Stadium. Rhyme number two: "Pitt's flying south, sun's shining bright; we'll be done by lunch, you'll be home by night."

Scouting the Panthers

Respect first, always. Pitt's run defense allows 57.3 yards a game. That's first in FBS. Its scoring defense is 23rd. Cataurus Hicks has 500 receiving yards on just 26 catches, and he caught a 75-yard touchdown on the very first play last week. Damon Ferguson Jr. ran for 119 yards. That's a real team.

Now, the scouting report.

Pitt has lost two straight, 21-17 at home to North Carolina and 27-21 at Boston College. AP noted the Panthers had a brief stay in the AP Top 25 before those losses. Against BC, Pitt was flagged 13 times for 133 yards, including five penalties for 63 yards on a single drive. Pitt averages 81.7 penalty yards a game, 132nd in FBS. Its pass defense allows 249 yards a game, 115th. AP also pointed to numerous breakdowns in the secondary against BC.

Familia, you know what Miami does? Miami throws for 333 yards a game, third in FBS. Mensah completes 80.8 of every 100 passes, with 18 touchdowns and one interception. Toney has 50 catches for 758 yards and eight touchdowns. Rhyme number three: "Pitt's secondary, I don't mean to be rude, but Toney's coming through, and he's bringing the U."

And the last time we met, on Nov. 29, 2025, Miami won 38-7 in Pittsburgh.

The Honest Breeze

The Breeze keeps it real. Our pass defense allows 223.3 yards a game, 91st in FBS, and Florida State threw for 305 on us last week. We gave up 35 points. The secondary needs to tighten up, and as a former DB, it hurts my soul to say it. But our run defense allows only 58.5 yards a game, second in FBS. We score 48.5 points a game, first in the country. Marquise Lightfoot has five sacks. And last season, we went 13-3 and played for the national championship, falling 27-21 to Indiana. That still stings. This team is playing like it remembers.

Postcards: The 305 vs. the Steel City

Miami. Miami is the only major city in the United States founded by a woman. Julia Tuttle owned the land, convinced Henry Flagler to bring his railroad south and became known as "the mother of Miami." The city was incorporated on July 28, 1896, with just over 300 people. Today it's 442,241 strong, and they call it the Magic City, The 305 and the Gateway to the Americas. The University of Miami sits in Coral Gables, a planned city incorporated in 1925 and built on the City Beautiful Movement.

And Hard Rock Stadium? Six Super Bowls, two World Series, four BCS title games and two College Football Playoff championships. Our program has five national championships, in 1983, 1987, 1989, 1991 and 2001. Our mascot, Sebastian the Ibis, has represented the U since 1926. Rhyme number four: "Sunshine and titles, palm trees and swagger; Miami's the city, the rest are the laggers." Okay, that one needs work.

Pittsburgh. Pittsburgh sits where the Allegheny and Monongahela Rivers meet to form the Ohio. Beautiful spot. But they call it the City of Bridges because it has 446 of them. Four hundred forty-six! Familia, in Miami we have one bridge we care about, and it takes us to the beach. In Pittsburgh, you need a bridge to get to the bridge.

And the hills? Pittsburgh's Monongahela Incline, a cable car up the side of a hill, opened in 1870 and is the oldest continuously operating funicular in the country. A whole city that needs a 156-year-old cable car to go up the street. In Miami, the land averages about 6 feet above sea level. You know what we call that? Walking.

Pitt also claims nine national championships, and only two of them, 1937 and 1976, came from the major wire-service polls. Nine claimed. We'll stick with our five. I'll let the rhyme speak: "Nine on the banner, but who's counting, bro? We counted ours twice, and it's still five, though." Okay, that's another one that needs work. But give me credit: I'm honest even when I rhyme.

Final Whistle

Here's the scouting report. Pitt has the best run defense in the country and a dangerous deep threat. Miami has the No. 1 ranking, the top scoring offense in FBS, a quarterback who almost never throws it to the other team and a home crowd that's ready to party at noon. Throw it early, throw it often and let the 305 do the rest.

Rhyme number five: "Panthers in the sunshine, here's my last clue: when it's all said and done, it's all about the U."

My call: Miami Hurricanes 37, Pittsburgh Panthers 19.
