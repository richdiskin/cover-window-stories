That's a Hit Single, Y'all: Vanderbilt Takes Its Guitar to Lexington, Where the Horses Are Famous and Third Down Goes to Die

Vanderbilt (3-4) at No. 12 Kentucky (6-1), Sat., Oct. 24, 7:30 p.m. ET, Kroger Field, SEC Network

By Penelope 'Pippa' Ashcroft, The Vanderbilt Fanatic

Hey, y'all, it's Pippa Ashcroft, tuning my guitar on a bench in Centennial Park, right in front of the Parthenon, because when your heart's a little bruised, you go sit next to the most dramatic building in Tennessee. I'm an economics student by day and a songwriter by night, and every week I write a country song about the Commodores. Some weeks it's a stadium anthem. This week, darlin', it's a slow one in a minor key.

But here's the thing about country music: the sad songs are always the setup for the comeback single. So let's get through verse one, and then let's talk about Lexington, where the chorus is going to be a whole lot louder.

Part 1: The Ballad of a Saturday Night

Arkansas 31, Vanderbilt 21, at FirstBank Stadium, in front of a full house of 35,000.

And it started like a hit. With 13 seconds left in the first quarter, Sedrick Alexander punched in a 4-yard touchdown run, and we led 7-0. In the second quarter, after Arkansas tied it, Jared Curtis threw a gorgeous 24-yard touchdown to Junior Sherrill, and we led 14-7 with under two minutes to go in the half. I was already humming the chorus.

Then the record skipped.

The Razorbacks' first touchdown came on a 25-yard interception return by Joker Johnson. Then Cam Settles scored with 38 seconds left before halftime to tie it at 14. In the third quarter, KJ Jackson ran in from eight yards, Makhilyn Young answered with a 3-yard touchdown run to tie it at 21, and then Settles scored again with nine seconds left in the quarter. In the fourth, Curtis drove us to the Arkansas 29 with 3:46 left, and Miguel Mitchell intercepted him on second-and-12. Max Gilbert added a 39-yard field goal, and that was the song.

Curtis threw for 274 yards and a touchdown, with two interceptions. Sherrill caught eight passes for 99 yards. Alexander had 57 rushing yards and a score, plus 40 receiving yards. On defense, Bryan Longwell made 13 tackles with 1.5 for loss. But the AP pointed out the hard part: Arkansas ran for 187 yards, Ole Miss ran for 196 against us the week before, and the run defense has to get fixed.

And yes, I know. That loss snapped Arkansas's 14-game SEC losing streak. We're very generous people in Nashville. We're a hospitality town.

Game-Day Song: "Fourth-Quarter Blues (Second-and-Twelve)"

Well, we had it at fourteen, I was singin' in the stands, Had a Sherrill touchdown ringin' and a hot dog in my hands, Then a Razorback came flyin' like a freight train off the track, And second-and-twelve took my Saturday back.

That's a hit single, y'all. A sad one. But a hit.

The Honest Verse

We're 3-4 and 0-4 in the SEC. We beat Austin Peay, Delaware and NC State, 35-31. Then we lost at Auburn 21-15, at Georgia 38-14, at home to Ole Miss 52-17 and at home to Arkansas. The AP preview says our turnover margin is -6, 126th in FBS, and we score 23.6 points a game. Those aren't great numbers. But I'm an economist, and I know a down cycle isn't the whole story.

Part 2: Off to Horse Country

Saturday night at 7:30 Eastern, on SEC Network, we go to Kroger Field to play No. 12 Kentucky.

Now, I'll be gracious first, because my mama taught me manners and a G chord on the same afternoon. Kentucky is 6-1 and tied for first in the SEC, and it beat Oklahoma 21-13 in Norman last Saturday by intercepting four passes. Kenny Minchey has thrown 13 touchdowns and one interception all year. That's real, y'all. I tip my cowboy hat.

And let's remember who has won this game the last two years. Vanderbilt beat Kentucky 20-13 at Kroger Field in 2024 and 45-17 in Nashville in 2025. That's two straight, darlin'. We know the way to Lexington. We've got the directions saved on the phone.

The Kentucky Stat Sheet, Set to Music

Here's where I start strumming a little faster.

The AP preview says Kentucky is dead last in FBS in third-down conversions, at 29.1%. Last. Out of everybody. On third down, Kentucky's offense is a song with no bridge.

The Kentucky pass defense allows 249.7 yards a game, 116th in FBS. Its defense overall allows 377 yards a game, 95th, and 25.9 points a game, 92nd. Last Saturday in Norman, the Wildcats won with 230 yards. Four interceptions bailed them out, and I respect a defense that takes the ball away. But 230 yards won't win every week, and Curtis and Sherrill have been waiting for a secondary that ranks 116th.

Who to Watch

Junior Sherrill, our receiver: 41 catches for 628 yards and four touchdowns, and he's coming off eight catches for 99 yards. Kentucky's pass defense is 116th. You do the math, sugar. I'm an economist, so I already did.

Jared Curtis, quarterback: 1,242 yards and six touchdowns. He throws a lovely deep ball, and he needs to keep it out of the other team's hands on Saturday, because Kentucky is third in FBS in turnover margin at +11.

Sedrick Alexander, running back: 270 yards on 61 carries and five touchdowns.

Kentucky's C.J. Baxter: 484 rushing yards and eight touchdowns. Our run defense needs to tackle like it's the last song of the night and everybody wants to go home happy.

A Brief History Seminar

Kentucky leads the series 49-42-4, according to CollegeFootballData.com. Ninety-five games, friends, and it's that close. That's not a rivalry. That's a duet.

Music Row Report: A Tale of Two Towns

Nashville. We're Music City, and we've been Music City since 1950, when a WSM radio announcer named David Cobb said on air that the sounds listeners were hearing were coming from "Music City, U.S.A." The Grand Ole Opry started here in 1925. The Ryman was home to the Opry until 1974. Lower Broadway, Second Avenue and Printer's Alley are packed with music clubs and honky-tonks. The 2020 census counted 689,447 people, the 20th-most populous city in America. And we're also called the Athens of the South, because by the 1850s Nashville was the first Southern city to set up a public school system. That's why there's a full-size replica of the Parthenon in Centennial Park. We didn't just say we were smart. We built the receipt.

Lexington. Now, Lexington is a beautiful town. Rolling hills, white fences, horse farms everywhere. It's known as the Horse Capital of the World. And honestly? That's the problem. The most famous athletes in Lexington have four legs. Keeneland has live races every April and October. The football team plays in October too. Guess which one the town dresses up for.

And then there's basketball. The Kentucky men's basketball program has won eight national championships and was the first program to reach 2,000 wins. Rupp Arena is downtown. Football in Lexington is what you do in the fall while you wait for basketball season. It's the opening act, darlin'. And I love an opening act. I've been one.

Then there's their stadium name. Kroger Field. Y'all, they named their football stadium after a grocery store. I don't hate it. I just think it's funny to drive all the way to Lexington to play football in the produce aisle. I'll grab a gallon of milk on the way out.

The Last Song

Here's the setlist. Kentucky's offense is last in the country on third down. Its pass defense is 116th. Vanderbilt has a receiver with 628 yards and two straight wins in this series. Yes, we need to stop the run, and yes, we need to hold on to the football. But this is Nashville, and Nashville writes comebacks for a living.

Every great album has a track nine that nobody expects to be the single. Saturday night in Lexington, that's us.

Anchor down, y'all.

My call: Kentucky Wildcats 33, Vanderbilt Commodores 22.
