Pedigree Tells: Kentucky Is 6-1, Tied for First in the SEC, and the Commodores Are Sailing a Landlocked Boat Into Lexington

Vanderbilt (3-4) at No. 12 Kentucky (6-1), Sat., Oct. 24, 7:30 p.m. ET, Kroger Field, SEC Network

By Rhett Abernathy-Coyle, The Kentucky Fanatic

Good evening, friends, and welcome to the paddock. Rhett Abernathy-Coyle here, writing from a fence rail on a horse farm off Versailles Road, where the October grass is still somehow blue if you squint, the Keeneland fall meet is in full swing, and I am wearing my lucky seersucker for the seventh straight week, because I do not mess with a streak.

And friends, what a streak it is. Your Kentucky Wildcats are 6-1. We're 4-1 in the SEC. We're ranked 12th in the country. And after Saturday night, the AP says we're tied for first place in the Southeastern Conference with Georgia. I had to read that sentence out loud to my mare, Lady Bluegrass, just to hear how it sounded. She nodded. She knows pedigree.

Part 1: Four Interceptions in Norman

Kentucky 21, Oklahoma 13, at Memorial Stadium in Norman, in front of 83,218 Sooner fans who came in feeling awfully good about themselves after beating then-No. 1 Texas the week before.

I won't sugarcoat the first three quarters, because a good horseman tells the truth about a slow start. We scored on our opening drive, a 67-yard march capped by Kenny Minchey's 2-yard touchdown pass to Willie Rodriguez. Then Oklahoma's defense held us to 76 yards and four punts on our next five drives. Tate Sandell kicked two field goals, John Mateer hit Rocky Beers for a 20-yard touchdown right before half, and we trailed 13-7 late in the third quarter. The horse was in the back of the pack, friends. Mud on the silks.

And then the closer kicked in.

Ty Bryant intercepted Mateer, the first of his two interceptions on the night. Minchey took us 44 yards in seven plays and found Rodriguez again, this time from 12 yards out, and Adam Zouagui's extra point made it 14-13 early in the fourth. Then, with 1:17 left, Jordan Castell stepped in front of a pass and took it 41 yards to the house. Ballgame. Castell had two interceptions, Bryant had two, and the Wildcats picked off Oklahoma four times. Four. That's not a defense, friends. That's a pickpocket convention.

Minchey finished 21 of 28 for 158 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions. Rodriguez caught five passes for 38 yards and both scores. Nic Anderson led us with 48 receiving yards. On defense, Grant Godfrey made 15 tackles, Elijah Barnes made 10, and Tavion Gadson had 1.5 sacks. We gained only 230 yards, and I'll own that. But we won the turnover battle 4-0, and in this family, that's what we call breeding.

The Bloodline Report

A good breeder looks at the whole record, not just one race. So here it is. That was our fifth straight win. Before Norman, we won 35-34 at South Carolina and then beat LSU 35-34 in overtime at Kroger Field. Earlier, we won at Texas A&M 31-21. Our only loss came to Alabama, 45-17, back on Sept. 12. Will Stein, our first-year head coach, came over from Oregon in December, and this team plays like it has never once heard that Kentucky is supposed to be a basketball school.

Now, the honest part, because pedigree also means knowing your flaws. The AP preview says we're dead last in FBS in third-down conversions, at 29.1 of every 100 tries. Last, friends. Our pass defense allows 249.7 yards a game, 116th. But we're third in FBS in turnover margin at +11, and our quarterback has thrown 13 touchdowns and one interception. We make our living stealing the ball and never giving it back. It is not pretty. It is a mudder. It wins.

Part 2: The Commodores Come to Lexington

Saturday night, 7:30 Eastern, SEC Network, Kroger Field. No. 12 Kentucky hosts Vanderbilt.

Now, I'll be gentlemanly first. Vanderbilt has beaten us two years running: 20-13 here in Lexington in 2024 and 45-17 in Nashville in 2025. I remember both. My mare remembers both. We do not forget a race we lost. Respect where it's due, Commodores.

But let's look at this year's form.

Vanderbilt is 3-4 and 0-4 in the SEC. It lost to Arkansas 31-21 at home last Saturday, and that snapped the Razorbacks' 14-game SEC losing streak. Friends, Arkansas hadn't won an SEC game since 2024, and the Commodores ended that drought for them, in Nashville, on a Saturday night. That's not a loss. That's a charitable donation. Before that, Ole Miss beat Vanderbilt 52-17 in Nashville, and Georgia won 38-14 in Athens.

To be fair, Jared Curtis threw for 274 yards against Arkansas, and Junior Sherrill caught eight passes for 99 yards and a touchdown. Sherrill leads Vanderbilt with 41 catches for 628 yards on the season. He is the real thing, and our secondary needs to know where he is.

Who to Watch

Kenny Minchey, our quarterback: 1,552 yards, 13 touchdowns, one interception and a 68.4 completion rate. One interception in seven games. That's a horse that doesn't throw a shoe.

C.J. Baxter, running back: 484 yards on 87 carries and eight touchdowns, our leader on the ground.

Kenny Darby, receiver: 34 catches for 436 yards and three touchdowns.

Our ball hawks: Castell and Bryant, two interceptions each on Saturday. Vanderbilt has a turnover margin of -6, 126th in FBS, and Curtis has thrown seven interceptions this season with two more on Saturday. I like this matchup the way I like a filly on a fast track.

The Vanderbilt run defense: The AP said the Commodores allowed 196 rushing yards to Ole Miss and 170 to Arkansas the last two weeks. Their run defense ranks 79th. Our backs should be licking their chops.

A Brief History Seminar

Kentucky leads the all-time series 49-42-4, according to CollegeFootballData.com. So we're ahead, but these two have been running neck and neck for a long, long time. We'd like to stop their two-race winning streak, please and thank you.

Bluegrass Paddock: A Tale of Two Towns

Lexington. They call us the Horse Capital of the World, and we earned it, thanks to hundreds of horse farms, the Kentucky Horse Park, The Red Mile and Keeneland, which runs live races in April and October and hasn't changed much since it opened in 1936. Keeneland hosted the 2015 Breeders' Cup, when American Pharoah won the Classic. The 2020 census counted 322,570 people in Lexington, the second-most populous city in Kentucky, and every one of them can tell a thoroughbred from a quarter horse at 50 paces. Rupp Arena is downtown, Kroger Field holds about 61,000, and on a crisp October Saturday night, the whole Bluegrass is blue.

Nashville. Now, I adore Nashville. Music City. The Grand Ole Opry. The Ryman. It's a wonderful place to visit. But friends, the Nashville Scene once counted 33 bachelorette parties on Lower Broadway in less than two hours on a Friday night, and said the real number was probably higher. Thirty-three in two hours. That's not a downtown. That's a bridal conveyor belt.

They also call Nashville the "Athens of the South," and they built a full-size replica of the Parthenon in Centennial Park to prove it. Friends, when your city needs a copy of an ancient Greek temple to feel smart, I gently suggest you just read a book.

And then there's their stadium. FirstBank Stadium seats 35,000 after a renovation that took it down from 40,350. It is officially the smallest stadium in the SEC. They renovated it and made it smaller. I've seen horse barns with more ambition. Last Saturday's crowd was 35,000. Our crowd will be bigger, louder and much more blue.

And finally, the name. Commodore is a naval rank. Nashville is a landlocked city on the Cumberland River, a long drive from any ocean. Their mascot is a sea captain who has never seen the sea, which, friends, is exactly how I feel about our third-down offense. We both have room to grow.

The Last Word

Here's how this race sets up. Kentucky has won five straight, takes the ball away like it's in the rulebook, and has a quarterback who doesn't give it back. Vanderbilt has lost four straight SEC games, hands the ball over more than almost anybody in the country, and just watched Arkansas end a 14-game skid on its home field. We'll play at night, at home, with a share of first place at stake and two years of Vandy wins in the back of our minds.

Pedigree tells, friends. Saddle up. Go Big Blue.

My call: Kentucky Wildcats 33, Vanderbilt Commodores 23.
