That's a Foot of Snow of a Take: Syracuse Brings Umari Hatcher to Chapel Hill, Where the Offense Ranks 117th and Once Went 41 Minutes Without Scoring

Syracuse (2-4) at North Carolina (4-2), Sat., Oct. 24, 7:30 p.m. ET, Kenan Stadium, ACC Network

By Nico Calabrese, The Syracuse Fanatic

Hey there, Orange Nation. Nico Calabrese here, Central New York born, lake-effect tested and shovel certified. I follow the Orange to every game from the stands, and I rate every play in feet of snow. One foot is a nice play. Three feet is a highlight. Six feet means school's canceled, and we're all at the Dome.

This week, the forecast is mixed. Let me give you the honest weather report.

Part 1: Last Week, a Snow Squall That Wouldn't Quit

Syracuse lost 34-26 to Louisville on Saturday at the Dome. We're 2-4 and 0-4 in the ACC. I'll be straight with you. It stung like a January wind off Onondaga Lake.

But the start? Oh, the start was a five-foot blizzard. Shay Barker kicked a 26-yard field goal. Then Malachi Nelson threw a 41-yard touchdown pass to Umari Hatcher, and we led 10-0. Louisville answered twice, but early in the second quarter, Nelson found freshman Calvin Russell III for a 24-yard touchdown, and we led 16-14. Later, Ahmad Miller ran 20 yards for a touchdown to make it 23-21. Louisville hit a 46-yard touchdown pass with 28 seconds left in the half, and we went to the locker room down 28-23.

The two teams combined for 669 yards in the first half, the most in the ACC since 2022, according to the AP. That's a whiteout. Nobody could see the defense.

Then our defense showed up like a city plow at 5 a.m. Louisville gained just 58 yards after halftime. Davien Kerr intercepted two passes. Linebacker Chris D'Appolonia made 12 tackles, including a sack and 3.5 tackles for loss. And Isaac Brown, who ran for 235 yards the week before, gained just 22 yards on nine carries against us. That's a three-foot defensive performance. I'm shoveling it with pride.

Barker's 24-yard field goal early in the fourth cut it to 28-26. But Louisville intercepted a pass to set up a 5-yard touchdown run with 7:34 left, and that was the final margin.

Now, the highlights. Hatcher caught 13 passes for 224 yards, both career highs. It was his fourth 100-yard game this season, and his touchdown was his ACC-leading seventh. That's an eight-foot performance, folks. Pack your boots. Nelson, in his second start, completed 26 of 39 passes for 315 yards and two touchdowns. Miller ran for 123 yards on 18 carries. Russell caught six passes for 53 yards and his first touchdown.

Coach Fran Brown said afterward he wasn't happy with the loss and there's plenty to clean up with a young team, but he was proud of how hard the guys fought at the end. That's the Syracuse way. We dig out.

Part 2: Saturday Night in Chapel Hill

Saturday, 7:30 p.m. Eastern on ACC Network. Syracuse (2-4) at North Carolina (4-2, 2-1 ACC) at Kenan Stadium. Forecast for Chapel Hill: no snow. I'll pack a light jacket anyway, because I'm from Syracuse, and we never trust a forecast.

The Honest Forecast for Us

Let me shovel the walkway first. Syracuse has lost 11 straight ACC games and 12 straight against Power 4 teams, according to the AP. We've been outscored 78-36 in the second half this season. We lead the country in penalties, at 98.0 penalty yards a game, 138th in FBS, and we had three unsportsmanlike penalties in the first half last week. Our red zone offense scores on 78.1 of every 100 trips, 111th. We went 3-9 last season, and last year North Carolina beat us 27-10 at the Dome. Those are snow days I'd rather forget.

But here's the sunshine. Our passing offense averages 291.0 yards a game, 12th in FBS. Our offense averages 449.7 yards a game overall, 29th. We hold the ball for 33:59 a game, fifth in FBS. And our pass defense allows 191.8 yards a game, 43rd.

The Forecast for Carolina

Now let me tell you about the folks in the pines. North Carolina beat Duke 24-20 last week, and I respect that. They reclaimed the Victory Bell with a 24-yard touchdown run with 1:53 left. Their defense allows 19.2 points a game, 34th in FBS, and they commit the fifth-fewest penalty yards in FBS. Billy Edwards Jr. has nine touchdown passes, and Jordan Shipp has 441 receiving yards. Credit where it's due.

But after jumping out to a 17-0 lead over Duke, UNC managed only about 100 yards until its final drive, according to the AP, and went more than 41 minutes without scoring. That's a flurry that melted on the driveway.

North Carolina's offense averages 329.7 yards a game, 117th in FBS. Its rushing offense averages 116.2 yards, 120th. It holds the ball for 27:00 a game, 130th. And its third-down defense allows opponents to convert 43.3 of every 100 times, 113th. They also went 4-8 last season. That's a half-inch dusting, Carolina. In Syracuse, we wouldn't even close the schools for that.

Three Shovels for Saturday

1. Feed Hatcher. He has 50 catches for 784 yards and seven touchdowns. Carolina will try to bracket him, so move him around and let him work. 2. Clean it up. No more unsportsmanlike penalties. Every flag is a snowdrift we have to dig out of. 3. Own the clock. We rank fifth in time of possession, and they rank 130th. Long drives keep their offense in the cold.

Weather Report: Syracuse vs. Chapel Hill

Syracuse. Let me tell you about the Salt City. Syracuse grew up around brine springs that made it the nation's leading salt producer in the 19th century. The Erie Canal, completed in 1825, ran right through the center of town, and the city became a crossroads of commerce and a center of the abolitionist movement and the Underground Railroad. On Tipperary Hill, a traffic light has hung green above red since 1928. And the snow? A normal season brings about 127.8 inches. We won the Golden Snowball Award for 2025-26 with 143.1 inches, our second straight title. We don't complain about it. We win trophies for it.

And our home, the JMA Wireless Dome, is the largest domed stadium on any college campus. Our football program won the 1959 national championship, and its alumni include Jim Brown and Ernie Davis, the first Black winner of the Heisman Trophy. Respect the Orange.

Chapel Hill. Chapel Hill is a pretty town, folks. But it's named after a chapel. On a hill. That's not a name. That's a set of directions. The town was founded to serve the university, so even the town is a student. And Kenan Stadium's first game in 1927 drew 9,000 spectators. In Syracuse, we get more people than that shoveling out a single block after a storm.

Then there's the mascot. Rameses is a live sheep who attends games with his horns painted Carolina blue. A sheep, Carolina. We have an orange. At least ours doesn't need a haircut every spring.

The Final Forecast

Here's the honest outlook. North Carolina has a strong scoring defense, great discipline and a fresh rivalry win. Syracuse has an elite receiver, a top-15 passing offense and a defense that just held Louisville to 58 second-half yards. If we cut the penalties and keep feeding Hatcher, this game could get snowy late, even in North Carolina.

That's a foot of snow of a take.

My call: North Carolina Tar Heels 29, Syracuse Orange 20.
