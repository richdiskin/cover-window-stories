Dat's a Yinz Problem: Pitt Heads to No. 1 Miami, Whose Pass Defense Ranks 91st and Whose Stadium Has Worn Eight Different Names

Pittsburgh (5-2) at No. 1 Miami (6-0), Sat., Oct. 24, 12 p.m. ET, Hard Rock Stadium, ESPN

By Dominic 'Yinz' Bertolucci, The Pittsburgh Fanatic

How's it goin', yinz? Dominic "Yinz" Bertolucci here, coming at you from a stool down the Strip District with a homemade whistle around my neck. I played linebacker back in a mill town, and I still grade every tackle on my own personal scale. A great hit's a "yinz." A whiff's a "yikes." Somewhere in between is a "n'at."

This week, I blew that whistle a lot. Some yinz. Some yikes. A lot of yellow flags.

Dat's a yinz problem, not a yikes problem. Mostly.

Part 1: The Fastest Start in Chestnut Hill

Boston College beat Pitt 27-21 on Saturday. We're 5-2 and 2-2 in the ACC, and we've lost two straight. I'll level with yinz, because a linebacker who lies about a missed tackle gets caught on film.

But first, a moment for the start. On the very first play from scrimmage, true freshman Corey Dailey, making his first career start, threw a 75-yard touchdown pass to Cataurus Hicks. First play! Yinz! After BC went three-and-out, Kanye Thompson blocked a punt, and the ball got knocked out of the end zone for a safety. Then Antonio Chadha kicked a 28-yard field goal. Pitt led 12-0 in a little over seven minutes. My whistle was singing.

Then the flags came. Pitt was penalized 10 times for 103 yards in the first half alone, including five penalties for 63 yards on a single BC drive early in the second quarter. That's a yikes, yikes, yikes, yikes and a yikes. We finished with 13 penalties for 133 yards. BC scored the next 14 points and hit a 69-yard touchdown on the first drive of the second half.

Chadha kicked field goals of 29, 42 and 38 yards to keep it close. Damon Ferguson Jr. ran 17 times for 119 yards. Hicks caught five passes for 135 yards. And Raion Strader intercepted two passes, the second one giving us the ball at the BC 8 with just over two minutes left. We couldn't finish. BC intercepted a pass to end it.

Here's the honest truth. Dailey stepped in after Mason Heintschel was lost for the season with a knee injury two weeks ago, and our whole city is pulling for Mason's recovery. Dailey's a true freshman, and he made plays. The flags weren't on him. They were on all of us.

Part 2: Down to the Sunshine

Saturday, noon Eastern on ESPN. Pittsburgh (5-2) at No. 1 Miami (6-0) at Hard Rock Stadium.

Grading the Hurricanes

I'll give Miami its due. It's No. 1 in the country, it scores 48.5 points a game, first in FBS, and Darian Mensah completes 80.8% of his passes with 18 touchdowns and one interception. Malachi Toney has 758 receiving yards. Their run defense is second in the country. That's a "yinz" on my scale, with a little extra whistle.

Now, the film.

Miami needed two overtimes to beat Florida State last week, and Florida State is 3-4. The Seminoles threw for 305 yards on the Hurricanes and scored 35 points. With 2:49 left, Miami took a 28-21 lead, and Florida State tied it 10 seconds later with a 75-yard touchdown pass. In the second overtime, Florida State completed a 36-yard pass on fourth-and-21 that would have made it a two-point game, but a holding penalty wiped it out. Miami survived on an interception that went to review.

Miami's pass defense allows 223.3 yards a game. That ranks 91st in FBS. That's a "yikes" in a stadium full of sunshine. And Hicks has 500 receiving yards on just 26 catches. That's 19.2 yards every time he touches it.

Grading the Panthers

Our run defense allows 57.3 yards a game. First in FBS. First! Miami runs for 226.5 yards a game, and something's got to give. Our scoring defense allows 16.4 points a game, 23rd, and our red zone defense is 13th. Cruce Brookins leads us with 46 tackles, and Jaeden Moore has 2.5 sacks. Our passing game ranks 17th. We're plus-4 in turnover margin.

And the honest part: we average 81.7 penalty yards a game, 132nd in FBS. Our pass defense allows 249 yards a game, 115th, and AP noted breakdowns in our secondary at BC. Miami beat us 38-7 the last time we met, in Pittsburgh, on Nov. 29, 2025. That's the film. Yinz gotta watch it, learn from it and go play.

The Whistle Plan

Here's how I'd draw it up on a napkin at the diner. One: keep stuffing the run. Miami likes balance, and if our front takes away Javian Mallory, Mensah has to do it all. Two: get Dailey comfortable early with Ferguson, because a true freshman on the road at noon in the sunshine needs a friendly first few plays. Three: take a shot to Hicks. He's already done it once on a first play this season. Four, and I'm gonna blow this whistle till it squeaks: no more flags. Thirteen penalties won't beat Boston College, and it sure won't beat the No. 1 team in the country.

Postcards: The Steel City vs. Miami Gardens

Pittsburgh. Pittsburgh sits right where the Allegheny and Monongahela Rivers meet to form the Ohio River. They call us the Steel City, and they call us the City of Bridges, because we have 446 of them. Yinz can get anywhere in Pittsburgh, and yinz can do it over water. The Monongahela Incline opened in 1870 and is the oldest continuously operating funicular in the United States. We don't just climb hills. We built a ride for it.

And Pitt's campus has the Cathedral of Learning, a 42-story, 535-foot Gothic tower that's the tallest educational building in the Western Hemisphere. Our program claims nine national championships, including 1937 and 1976 from the major wire-service polls. And Acrisure Stadium, our home since 2001, rose up after the old Three Rivers Stadium came down.

Miami Gardens. Miami's games are at Hard Rock Stadium, which is in Miami Gardens. The University of Miami itself is in Coral Gables. So the Miami Hurricanes play in a stadium that's not in Miami, for a university that's not in Miami either. That's a "yikes" on my map scale.

And the stadium's name? Hard Rock Stadium opened in 1987 as Joe Robbie Stadium. Since then, it's also been Pro Player Park, Pro Player Stadium, Dolphins Stadium, Dolphin Stadium, Land Shark Stadium and Sun Life Stadium. That's eight names, n'at. Yinz have had more names for one stadium than I've had whistles, and I go through a lot of whistles.

Miami's elevation averages about 6 feet above sea level. Our Cathedral of Learning alone is 535 feet tall. Yinz could stack Miami on top of itself about 89 times before you reached our classroom ceiling.

Final Whistle

Here's my grade. Miami is No. 1, has a great quarterback and runs a top-two run defense. Pitt has the No. 1 run defense in the country, a deep threat who averages 19 yards a catch and a lot of fight. If we cut the penalties in half, slow down Toney and take our shots to Hicks against that 91st-ranked pass defense, this could be a whole lot closer than folks think.

Dat's a yinz problem, not a yikes problem. Hail to Pitt!

My call: Miami Hurricanes 37, Pittsburgh Panthers 18.
