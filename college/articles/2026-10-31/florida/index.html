Florida vs Georgia: Gators Give Away Four Turnovers, Fall 27-8

Final: Georgia (7-1) 27, Florida (5-3) 8, Sat., Oct. 31, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta. Next: vs. Oklahoma, Sat., Nov. 7, Ben Hill Griffin Stadium, Gainesville

ShareXFacebook

Georgia 27, Florida 8, on Halloween afternoon in Atlanta, and the Reptile Index has a new entry for the Bulldogs: apex predator, feeds on turnovers. Back home in Gainesville, Carson Springs Wildlife Foundation ran its Halloween Safari Drive-Thru from 1 to 3 that afternoon, rare animals and spooky displays before kickoff. That was the last thing all day that behaved the way it was supposed to.

This was the first time our rivalry game was ever played in Atlanta, and we spent the first half handing Georgia the keys. Aaron Philo, back under center, got chased on our opening drive and flipped the ball underhand right to Ellis Robinson IV. A roughing penalty on the punt kept Georgia alive, and Nate Frazier went in from the 1. In the second quarter a ball bounced off our receiver's hands to Demello Jones, and Peyton Woodring made it 10-0.

Tramell Jones Jr. came in and Georgia kept feeding. Bo Walker scored from the 1 to make it 17-0. Then, with 29 seconds left before the break, Quintavius Johnson sacked Jones, the ball came loose, and Chris Cole ran it back 66 yards. 24-0 at the half. In herpetology terms, that is a monitor lizard cleaning out the whole nest.

We had our shot in the third. Jones drove us to the Georgia 3, and Braylon Conley intercepted him in the end zone with 4:50 left. DJ Coleman punched the ball loose from Talyn Taylor early in the fourth, Myles Graham recovered, and Jones finally put us on the board with a 2-yard run and a two-point conversion with 6:59 to go. Woodring answered with a 21-yard field goal.

The honest numbers: Jones went 18 of 25 for 172 yards. Bailey Stockton caught eight balls for 76 yards. Alec Clark averaged 51.3 yards on four punts. Coleman made eight tackles, Graham seven, and Emmanuel Oyebadejo got a sack. But we turned it over four times, ran for 48 yards on 28 carries, and Jadan Baugh, an Atlanta native, got 38 on 17. Georgia ran for 217. Eight penalties for 78 yards finished the job.

The Bulldogs earned it, and I'm saying that through clenched teeth. The Reptile Index files them as a species that lives off other animals' mistakes, and we served them a buffet.

We're 5-3, 3-3 in the SEC. Oklahoma comes to Ben Hill Griffin Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 7, the first time the Sooners have ever played in the Swamp. Gainesville gets its team back, and the Swamp gets its noise back. Go Gators.
