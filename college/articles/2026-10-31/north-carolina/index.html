North Carolina vs Miami: Tar Heels Push No. 1 to the Wire 38-35

Final: Miami (8-0) 38, North Carolina (4-4) 35, Sat., Oct. 31, Kenan Stadium, Chapel Hill. Next: at UConn, Sat., Nov. 7, Pratt & Whitney Stadium, East Hartford, Conn.

ShareXFacebook

Miami 38, North Carolina 35, and I'm going to hold my head high in this costume. Chapel Hill threw a real Halloween for the No. 1 team in the country: our team handed out candy on the walk to Kenan Stadium, children trick-or-treated on the concourse before kickoff, and thousands filled Franklin Street with no street closures. Then the Tar Heels handed Miami its closest finish of the season.

As a kinesiologist, let me describe the force Billy Edwards Jr. generated. He went 28 of 34 for 341 yards and four touchdowns with no interceptions. Jelani Thurman caught two of them, both from 8 yards. Jordan Shipp took a 16-yarder in the third quarter to give us a 21-17 lead. Then Demon June went in from a yard out with 11:46 left, and Kenan was rocking at 28-24. We went 8 of 11 on third down, which is how you keep a No. 1 offense on the bench.

Two moments broke my heart. Early in the fourth, Benjamin Hall ran 12 yards into the end zone and a holding flag brought it back. We still scored on that drive, but it took 16 plays and 8:28. Later, trailing 31-28, Edwards was sacked by Marquise Lightfoot at our 37, the ball came out, and Miami turned it into a 38-28 lead in six plays.

Did we quit? Absolutely not. Edwards moved 75 yards in 55 seconds and hit Trech Kekahuna for 15 yards to make it 38-35 with 1:38 to go. We went for the onside kick three times. Two were erased by our own offside flags, and Miami grabbed the third. Painful.

Credit where it's due, and I'll say it graciously: Darian Mensah went 22 of 29 for 274 yards with three touchdowns and zero turnovers, and Mark Fletcher Jr. scored three times. Our run game was the problem, 66 yards on 30 carries. Derek McDonald made 11 tackles and Peyton Seelmann had a sack. Kenton Dopson III added eight tackles.

We are 4-4 and 2-3 in the ACC, and 1-3 at home. Next is UConn at Pratt & Whitney Stadium in East Hartford on Saturday, Nov. 7, at noon ET. Carolina blue skies will return, and from the look of that fourth quarter, soon. And no, I will not be taking questions from anyone eight miles down the road.
