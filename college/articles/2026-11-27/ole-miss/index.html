Ole Miss vs Mississippi State: Purchase Flips the Egg Bowl, 41-17

Final: Ole Miss Rebels (8-4) 41, Mississippi State Bulldogs (5-7) 17, Fri., Nov. 27, Vaught-Hemingway Stadium, Oxford. Regular season complete

By Langston Fairbanks, The Ole Miss Fanatic

XFacebook

Halftime in Oxford, and the scoreboard at Vaught-Hemingway said Mississippi State 10, Ole Miss 6. I heard a few boos from our own side when the two-minute drill fizzled and Lucas Carneiro's 62-yard try fell short. By the final gun it was 41-17, and the Egg Bowl belongs to us for the fourth year in a row.

The second half was 35-7, and Trinidad Chambliss ran the show. He hit Horatio Fields for a 6-yard touchdown with 9:53 left in the third for our first lead, 13-10. Joshua Dye bulled in from the 4 to make it 20-10. Chambliss finished 23 of 36 for 237 yards, two touchdown throws and a touchdown run.

Give State credit for one punch. Parker Puckett found Fluff Bothwell for a 12-yard score on the first snap of the fourth quarter, and it was 20-17 with the Bulldogs driving again. Then Blake Purchase stepped in front of a Puckett throw and ran 58 yards the other way, all the way to the State 1. Chambliss went around left end on the next play, and it was 27-17 with 10:30 to go. That interception was the whole night in one snap.

After that, the floodgates. Chambliss found Michael Smith for 21 yards and a touchdown, and Dye punched in his second score from a yard out with 1:57 left. Dye carried 20 times for 91 yards on a night our ground game needed somebody to grab it.

The defense got meaner as the night went on. Tah'j Butler made 10 tackles and a sack, and Suntarine Perkins had 1.5 sacks and three tackles for loss. We went 8 of 13 on third down and didn't lose the ball once.

Honest notes for the film room: Bothwell ran for 129 yards on 18 carries, and State had the ball for 33:22. We were sloppy for two quarters. We were also the better team for two quarters, and that was plenty.

That's four straight Egg Bowls and six of the last seven. We're 8-4, 5-4 in the SEC, waiting on a bowl invitation, and the trophy stays in Oxford for another year. Hotty Toddy.
