North Dakota State vs Hawai'i: The Bison Land in Honolulu

North Dakota State Bison (7-2) at Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors (3-6)

11:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Clarence T.C. Ching Athletics Complex · CW

XFacebook

North Dakota State Bison vs Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors has never happened before. These programs have never played, and their debut comes on Military Night in Honolulu at 6 p.m. local, with military bands, an aircraft flyover for Veterans Day weekend and a Timmy Chang bobblehead for the first 3,000 fans. Kainoa Akana-Baird, who teaches big-wave surfing on the North Shore and has been tossed by more waves than he can count, says the island is ready. Ingeborg "Inga" Halvorsen, a Fargo agricultural economist who can tell you the price of durum wheat and every Bison title by year, landed with a winter coat she won't need.

Inga: It's eighty degrees. I packed for Fargo.

Kainoa: You packed for the wrong planet.

Inga: It was nineteen degrees when I left.

Kainoa: Then you're welcome.

That's the matchup in a nutshell. A team from the northern plains meets a team from the middle of the Pacific, and both think the other one is crazy.

North Dakota State is 7-2, and the defense has been ridiculous. The Bison allow 11.9 points a game. They shut out Fordham and Wyoming, won 38-32 at Air Force and beat Nevada 31-17. Their two losses were close, 13-7 at UNLV and 21-16 at New Mexico, and both came on the road. Donovan Woolen has 66 tackles, and Kelton McCaslin has five sacks. Nathan Hayes has 13 touchdown throws, and Jackson Williams has 614 receiving yards.

Inga: Bison football is like wheat futures. Boring, steady, and over time it always goes up.

Kainoa: That's the least exciting thing I've ever heard.

Inga: It works.

Hawai'i is 3-6, but the Rainbow Warriors have won two straight. They put up 56 at Northern Illinois and then won 17-14 at UTEP last week. Micah Alejado has 2,489 throwing yards and threw for 303 at UTEP. Kainoa Carvalho leads the receivers, and Titan Lacaden caught 11 balls last week. Linebacker Jamih Otis has 64 tackles.

Kainoa: Alejado is like a good surfer on a big day. He doesn't panic. He waits for the right wave.

Inga: And if the wave doesn't come?

Kainoa: Then he waits some more. It always comes.

Inga: That's not how football works.

Kainoa: It's how Alejado works.

The challenge for Hawai'i is the Bison defense. Alejado throws a lot, and North Dakota State has been very good at stopping teams that throw. Hawai'i lost 21-6 to UNLV and 21-10 to New Mexico at home, and the Rainbow Warriors need their quarterback to be sharp against a defense that rarely gives anything away. The Bison also had last week off, so they've had time to adjust to the trip.

Kainoa: The bobblehead is Timmy Chang. You know who he is?

Inga: The quarterback with all the records.

Kainoa: The quarterback with all the records. Every kid in Honolulu wants one.

Inga: I want one too.

Kainoa: You have to get here early. The first 3,000.

Inga: I've been here since Thursday.

The Bison have won a lot of games in a lot of places, and Inga has a shelf at home with a trophy replica for every title. But they've never played here, and this is a long way from Fargo. On a night built around the military, with the bands playing and the flyover overhead, Honolulu should give them a full taste of the island before the first snap.

Kainoa: When Hawai'i wins, you're coming out to the North Shore and paddling out with me.

Inga: And if North Dakota State wins?

Kainoa: Then you still paddle out. I'll keep you on the small waves.

Inga: I'll bring my coat.

My call: North Dakota State Bison 25, Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors 18.
