Florida vs Kentucky: Kroger Field Black Out, Sold Out

Florida Gators (5-4) at Kentucky Wildcats (7-2)

6:15 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Kroger Field · ESPN

XFacebook

Florida Gators vs Kentucky Wildcats is the Week 11 sellout at Kroger Field, and Lexington has been told to show up dressed in black for a night game against a ranked SEC visitor. Kentucky horse-country kid Rhett Abernathy-Coyle has his black jacket pressed for the Black Out, and Florida's Dax Marchetti, the herpetology minor who measures every defense against some reptile, says he'll be the only splash of orange and blue in a stadium that looks like a moonless pasture.

Rhett: Sold out, Dax. Every seat. Kentucky told folks to come early, park where they're told and wear black.

Dax: Black is fine. Plenty of things hunt at night in Florida.

Rhett: Name one that wins in Lexington.

Dax: Last year? Not us. Two years ago? Us, 48-20.

That's the back-and-forth these two have lived for a decade. Over the last ten meetings it's five and five, and the swings are wild. Kentucky won 38-7 last season. Florida won 48-20 the year before. Kentucky won 33-14 the year before that. Nobody in either town gets comfortable about this game, and nobody should.

Kentucky comes in at 7-2, ranked sixth, second in the SEC, and freshly bruised. The Wildcats went to Knoxville last Saturday and lost 34-6, the worst the offense has looked all year.

Rhett: One bad night. Everybody gets one. Before that we won at South Carolina and beat LSU, both 35-34 in overtime, and won at Oklahoma 21-13. You don't do that without a horse that can run all day.

Kenny Minchey is the horse. He's thrown for 1,979 yards with 14 touchdowns and just two interceptions, and he went 26 of 34 at Tennessee even on the bad night. CJ Baxter has 541 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and Kenny Darby is the top target with 576 receiving yards. Linebacker Grant Godfrey has 78 tackles, and Mi'Quise Humphrey-Grace has six sacks off the defensive front.

Dax: You know what Godfrey reminds me of? A snapping turtle. Patient. Then it's over.

Rhett: That's the nicest thing a Gator's ever said about Kentucky.

Dax: Don't get used to it.

Florida's year has two halves. In September the Gators scored 66 on Florida Atlantic, 52 on Campbell, 44 at Auburn and 52 on Ole Miss, with Aaron Philo running the show. Then things went sideways. They lost at Missouri and to South Carolina, went to Austin and stole a 27-24 win over Texas, and then lost to Georgia and Oklahoma while scoring 8 and 7. Tramell Jones Jr. has taken the most throws the last three games.

Dax: People forget the Texas game. We went into that stadium and walked out with it. That's a gator that knows how to wait in the water.

Rhett: People also remember 7 points against Oklahoma.

Dax: Jadan Baugh has 14 rushing touchdowns. Fourteen. He's the gator. Everything else is just water.

He has a point. Baugh's 836 yards and 14 scores make him the most dangerous back in this game, and Florida's defense has its own snapper in linebacker Myles Graham, who has 70 tackles. If Baugh gets loose on first down, Florida can keep Minchey on the sideline and make Kroger Field nervous.

The night itself belongs to Kentucky. The game is part of the program's Night Ops and Salute to Veterans weekend, and this is the fourth home sellout of the season. A sold-out black crowd under the lights is a different animal from the one Florida saw in September.

Rhett: My granddad used to say you can tell a good yearling by how it handles a crowd. Minchey handles a crowd.

Dax: My professor used to say you can tell a gator by how long it waits. We'll wait.

Rhett: Wait all night, then. The barn door closes at the final horn.

Dax: Leave it cracked. This one's going to the wire.

He might be right about that part. This has the look of a game that comes down to one drive, one kick or one stop, and in a stadium painted black, the home crowd gets the loudest vote.

My call: Kentucky Wildcats 28, Florida Gators 27.
