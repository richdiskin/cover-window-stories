Kansas vs West Virginia: Heroes Day Ends Mountaineer Week

Kansas Jayhawks (3-6) at West Virginia Mountaineers (5-4)

5:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Milan Puskar Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

Mountaineer Week wraps up in Morgantown with Heroes Day at Milan Puskar Stadium, as West Virginia honors active and former military members during Kansas Jayhawks vs West Virginia Mountaineers in Week 11. West Virginia insider Jolene Buckhannon showed up in a gold beanie with a bag of pepperoni rolls, and Kansas insider Fitz Calloway arrived with a laptop full of spreadsheets and a basketball conversion for everything.

Jolene: Fitz. Welcome to Morgantown. Short version. We shoot, we score, we sing.

Fitz: Jolene, I appreciate a short version. In basketball terms, you're a team that runs and guns and forgets to box out.

Jolene: Box out what?

Fitz: Exactly my point.

West Virginia is 5-4 and 2-4 in Big 12 play, and its games are not for the faint of heart. The Mountaineers score 37.2 points a game and allow 34.6. Last Saturday they lost 53-48 at Texas Tech, even with Michael Hawkins Jr. throwing for 303 yards on only 16 attempts. TaRon Francis caught three balls for 169 yards and a touchdown. Three catches. One hundred sixty-nine yards.

Jolene: Three shots. Three bullseyes. That's TaRon.

Fitz: In basketball, that's three half-court shots in a row. Nobody does that. Honestly, I'm a little scared.

Cam Cook is the steady part of the West Virginia offense. He has 1,072 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and he ran 23 times for 149 yards in Lubbock. Hawkins has 1,901 throwing yards with 12 touchdowns and just two interceptions, and DJ Epps has 564 receiving yards on only 23 catches.

Jolene: Cook doesn't miss. He just keeps firing.

Kansas comes in at 3-6 and 1-5 in the league, and Fitz will tell you the record lies a little. The Jayhawks lost 33-32 to UCF last Saturday, 22-16 at TCU and 24-17 to Arizona State. Isaiah Marshall ran 13 times for 125 yards and two touchdowns against UCF. He also has 1,716 throwing yards with 11 touchdowns and seven interceptions.

Fitz: We're a coin flip from 6-3, Jolene. Maybe two coin flips. I have it in a tab.

Jolene: Your tab can't tackle.

Fitz: My tab has feelings.

The Jayhawks' defense is better than its record. Kansas allows 24.8 points a game, about ten fewer than West Virginia. Leroy Harris III has six and a half sacks off the end, and Trey Lathan leads the team with 62 tackles. Dylan Edwards leads the offense on the ground with 595 rushing yards, and Nik McMillan has 497 receiving yards and five touchdowns.

Fitz: And don't forget last year, Jolene. Forty-one to ten. That's a 31-point win in a football game. In basketball, that's a 20-point win at halftime and the walk-ons get minutes.

Jolene: I remember. I'm still mad. Mad helps my aim.

The longer history belongs to the Mountaineers. Since 2016, West Virginia has won seven of nine against Kansas, including 48-21, 56-34 and 38-17 wins. But Kansas won 55-42 in 2022 and 41-10 last year, and Fitz treats those two like national titles.

Jolene: Two wins. Seven losses. Do the math, Fitz.

Fitz: I did the math. I made a chart. The chart is trending up, Jolene.

So here's the question as the last day of Mountaineer Week ends under the lights. Can Kansas' defense keep Cook in front of it and make Hawkins throw into traffic, or does West Virginia's offense pile up points the way it did against Cincinnati, when it won a 45-42 shootout?

Jolene: If we win, you sing Country Roads. Every verse. On the field.

Fitz: If we win, you wear a Kansas basketball jersey to the pepperoni roll stand.

Jolene: Deal. I don't miss.

Fitz: That's what scares me.

My call: West Virginia Mountaineers 29, Kansas Jayhawks 28.
