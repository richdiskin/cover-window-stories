Cincinnati vs Iowa State: Chuckwagon Breakfast, Then a Noon Fight in Ames

Cincinnati Bearcats (5-4) at Iowa State Cyclones (4-5)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Jack Trice Stadium · TNT

XFacebook

Cincinnati Bearcats vs Iowa State Cyclones is an 11 a.m. Central kickoff at Jack Trice Stadium on Saturday, and the sharpest people in Ames will have eaten long before the band warms up. The Iowa State Department of Animal Science throws open the Hansen Agriculture Learning Center on Mortensen Road for its annual Chuckwagon Breakfast from 8 to 10 that morning, free omelets, pastries, coffee and juice, and Cyclones storm-spotter Twyla Hemmingsen plans to be there at the bell, right before Cincinnati's Lionel "Skyline" Abernathy strolls in asking whether anybody has ever tried chili on eggs.

Twyla: Free eggs, free coffee, and a noon kickoff. This is civilization.

Skyline: In Cincinnati we respect them so much we stack them three high. That isn't breakfast. That's a game plan.

He's half right, because both of these teams show up hungry. Cincinnati is 5-4 after a 24-31 loss at Houston. Iowa State is 4-5 after a 31-42 loss at Baylor. Both sit at 2-4 in Big 12 play, and both know the difference between a winter of bowl prep and a winter of film-room regret is about three Saturdays long.

Twyla: Here's my read on the sky over Ames. Cardinal and gold blowing in early, then steady pressure from Aiden Flora all afternoon.

Flora is the reason she says it with a straight face. The Iowa State back has 1,119 yards and nine touchdowns on 157 carries, and he just went for 182 yards on 26 carries in Waco. When he gets downhill, the whole stadium rises with him, and the Cyclones get to play the way Ames likes it, heavy and patient and mean.

Skyline answers with JC French IV. French has carried Cincinnati with his arm all fall, 1,990 throwing yards with 15 touchdowns and only three interceptions. He threw for 389 yards and three scores in a 45-42 loss at West Virginia and put up four touchdown throws in the 37-24 win over Texas Tech. Behind him, Cole Tabb has 812 rushing yards and 10 touchdowns on just 91 carries, which is the kind of efficiency that makes a defensive coordinator stop eating his breakfast halfway through.

Skyline: I hear you. I just think the loudest day in this series was last October in Cincinnati, when we won 38-30 at Nippert. Thirteen months later, we get to see whether Ames can answer.

Twyla: Answer? Flora already answered Baylor. He'll answer you too.

The key matchup lives in the middle of the field. Iowa State linebacker Sullivan Schlimgen has 84 tackles and spends most Saturdays arriving at the ball carrier like he was mailed there overnight. Cincinnati's Jonathan Thompson has 66 tackles of his own, and Patrick Bauer has 3.5 sacks off the outside. Neither defense has been airtight. Cincinnati allows 29.4 points a game and Iowa State allows 28.9, so the team that gets the first two stops in a row probably gets to dictate the afternoon.

Skyline: Here's the Cincinnati recipe. French moves it around early, Tabb hits you late, and JV Gibson catches the third-down ball when your safeties get greedy.

Twyla: Here's the Ames recipe. Flora on first down, Flora on second down, and Raynor on third down when your linebackers are still out of breath. This is a farm town. We know how to wait for a harvest, and the fourth quarter is harvest.

Saturday's Ames calendar is crowded. The City of Ames runs a free yard-waste drop-off for leaves and grass out on 500th Avenue from 8 to 4, so plenty of locals will rake the front yard at dawn, haul the bags, grab an omelet at Hansen, and still make kickoff in Cyclone colors. That is a full Iowa Saturday, and it ends at Jack Trice with the leaves off the lawn and a Big 12 game that stays one possession deep into the fourth.

Skyline will talk chili. Twyla will talk Flora. The rest of Ames will talk about whether the omelets were better than last year.

My call: Iowa State Cyclones 30, Cincinnati Bearcats 28.
