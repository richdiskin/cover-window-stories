Colorado State vs Washington State: A Cold Night on the Palouse

Colorado State Rams (3-6) at Washington State Cougars (1-8)

10:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Martin Stadium · CBSSN

XFacebook

Colorado State Rams vs Washington State Cougars is a cold Saturday night on the Palouse, a 7:30 p.m. kickoff at Martin Stadium for the Native American and Military Appreciation Game, and the first time these teams have met in Pullman since 2022. Hank Palouse, whose family has grown wheat on the rolling hills outside town for three generations, says this has been the hardest season he can remember. Ansel Rimrock, a Fort Collins trail runner who logs his miles on the foothills above Horsetooth Reservoir, drove up with a thermos of coffee and a headlamp.

Hank: You brought a headlamp to a football game.

Ansel: I'm running the hills behind the stadium at halftime.

Hank: It's going to be dark and cold.

Ansel: That's the best time to run.

That's Ansel. Colorado State's season has been hard too, but he keeps moving.

Washington State is 1-8, and Hank isn't hiding from it. The Cougars have lost six straight, and it's their toughest year since 2009. The only win came against Duquesne. They lost 17-16 at Utah State, 26-20 at San Diego State and 34-24 to Arizona, so they've been close a few times. They had last week off, and Owen Eshelman has taken over at quarterback, throwing for 299 yards at San Diego State.

Hank: You know what a bad wheat year teaches you? You still plant in the fall. You don't stop.

Ansel: That's beautiful, man.

Hank: It's not beautiful. It's just farming.

Colorado State is 3-6 under Jim Mora, and its problem is obvious. The Rams give up 44.8 points a game, the worst in the country. They gave up 64 to Boise State last week, 66 at Texas State and 59 at UTSA. But they can score. Hauss Hejny has 16 touchdown throws and only two interceptions, and the Rams put up 56 against San Diego State and 45 at UTSA. RJ Vick leads the receivers, and linebacker Oumar Diomande has 85 tackles.

Ansel: We score like we're running downhill. We defend like we're running uphill.

Hank: Into the wind.

Ansel: Into the wind, with a backpack.

Washington State won 20-3 in Fort Collins last year, and the Cougars have won all three meetings since 2022. Hank remembers every one.

Hank: Twenty to three. We held you to a field goal.

Ansel: That was last year. We have Hejny now.

Hank: And we have Eshelman.

Ansel: Eshelman has no touchdown throws in his last two games.

Hank: He has two rushing touchdowns at San Diego State.

Ansel: That's a fair point.

The matchup is simple. Washington State has to score, and it hasn't done much of that, putting up 19.0 points a game. Colorado State's defense should give the Cougars a chance to have their best offensive night of the year. On the other side, Hejny needs to keep the Rams in it, because the Cougar defense, led by Keith Brown and his 71 tackles, is the better group.

Hank: The crowd's going to be small but loud. People who show up for a 1-8 team are the real ones.

Ansel: I respect that. I run ultras. Nobody shows up for those either.

Hank: Then you understand.

Pullman in November is no place for a team from out of town, and the Cougars should finally get one. But Colorado State will score, and this probably won't be comfortable for Hank until late.

Ansel: If Colorado State wins, you're running the hills with me at halftime.

Hank: And if Washington State wins?

Ansel: Then I still run them. You just hold the headlamp.

Hank: Deal. But I'm wearing my coat.

My call: Washington State Cougars 35, Colorado State Rams 27.
