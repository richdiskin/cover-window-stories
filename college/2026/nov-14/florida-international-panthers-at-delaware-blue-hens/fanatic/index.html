FIU vs Delaware: Homecoming and Military Appreciation in Newark

Florida International Panthers (4-5) at Delaware Blue Hens (4-5)

3:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Delaware Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

FIU Panthers vs Delaware Blue Hens is Homecoming in Newark, a 3 p.m. kickoff at Delaware Stadium that also doubles as Military Appreciation and a Celebration of Champions, with a thank-you to the university's faculty and staff on top of it. Henrietta "Hen" Lofland, a librarian by day, has checked out every book on Homecoming traditions just to be thorough, and she has returned them all on time, because of course she has.

Hen: Small state, big Saturdays, Lourdes. Welcome to Homecoming.

Lourdes Ibarguen-Tate has flown up from Sweetwater with a thermos of cafecito and a warning from her abuela, who told her to wear a real coat this time.

Lourdes: My abuela says November up here is not weather, it's a punishment. She also says we win today. She said it first, so it counts.

Hen: Your abuela is welcome at my tailgate. I'll even let her choose the music.

These two have met once, last September in Miami, and Delaware won it 38-16. Lourdes remembers it. Lourdes would prefer to remember it differently.

Lourdes: That was a long night. I don't talk about it. My abuela doesn't talk about it either, and she talks about everything.

Delaware is 4-5 in this new chapter of Blue Hens football, and Hen loves every page of it. The offense can really go. Delaware scores 30.3 points a game, and Nick Minicucci has 1,885 throwing yards, 16 touchdowns and only four interceptions. He threw for 414 yards at Western Kentucky and 408 at Kennesaw State last week, in a 42-40 loss that still has Hen muttering. Sean Wilson has 639 receiving yards, Bryson Graves caught seven passes for 125 against the Owls, and Blake Matthews has five sacks.

Hen: Nick Minicucci reads a defense like I read a card catalog. Quietly, carefully and then all at once.

Lourdes: That's very nice, Hen. Our defense eats card catalogs for breakfast.

FIU is also 4-5, and the Panthers are built differently. The defense allows just 20.3 points a game, and DeeJay Holmes Jr. has seven sacks. JJ Kohl has thrown for 2,035 yards, and Maguire Anderson has 610 receiving yards. The big news is Devonte Lyons, who ran for 216 yards and two touchdowns in last week's 23-20 win over Missouri State.

Lourdes: Two hundred sixteen yards, Hen. Devonte Lyons ran like he smelled croquetas at the other end of the field.

Hen: I'd run that fast for croquetas too. I'm a librarian, not a saint.

The Panthers have been in plenty of tight ones. They lost 27-24 at Jacksonville State, 26-23 to Middle Tennessee and 16-10 at Florida Atlantic, so a close game in the fourth quarter won't rattle them. Delaware has been in its share of shootouts, including a 61-38 win over Missouri State on Senior Day back in October.

The key for FIU is the ground game. If Lyons can keep running the way he did last week, the Panthers can shorten the game, keep Minicucci on the sideline and give their defense a chance to rest. For Delaware, it's protecting Minicucci from Holmes. If he has time, he will find Wilson and Graves, and Delaware Stadium will get very loud very fast.

Hen: You know what Homecoming means here, Lourdes? Alumni coming back to see what this program is becoming. It's a new chapter. Nobody skips to the end.

Lourdes: My abuela always skips to the end. She says life is short and dominoes are long.

Military Appreciation gives the afternoon some extra meaning, too, and both of them agree on one thing: thanking the people who serve matters more than any score.

Hen: If we win, I'm doing a bird pun so bad you'll have to report it.

Lourdes: If we win, I'm calling my abuela from the parking lot, and she's going to say she told me so.

The Blue Hens win it, so brace yourself for Hen's bird pun. My call: Delaware Blue Hens 28, Florida International Panthers 22.
