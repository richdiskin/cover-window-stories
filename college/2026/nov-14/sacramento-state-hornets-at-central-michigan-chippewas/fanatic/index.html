Sacramento State vs Central Michigan: A First Meeting on Veterans Day

Sacramento State Hornets (2-8) at Central Michigan Chippewas (4-5)

7:30 PM ET Wed Nov 11 · Kelly/Shorts Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

For one night, Mount Pleasant gets to meet somebody brand new. Sacramento State Hornets vs Central Michigan Chippewas is the first meeting ever between these two programs, and it lands on Veterans Day, Community Night and the last home game of the year at Kramer/Deromedi Field, so Brynn Haverkamp has packed her heaviest coat and her loudest voice.

Mateo Villaseñor flew in from Sacramento with a Hornets scarf and a chef's knife roll he swears is only for show. He's never been to Mount Pleasant. Neither has his team. Sacramento State is a first-year member of the MAC, and this is its first trip here.

"Welcome to the neighborhood," Brynn says. "Ope, sorry, I mean welcome to the tundra. The heaters are by the concourse. The noise is by the student section."

"In Sacramento, November is a light sweater," Mateo says. "Here it's a recipe for soup. I respect it."

Brynn takes this game personally, and not just because it's the last home date. Central Michigan is 4-5 and 2-3 in the MAC, and the school's own game notes lay it out plainly: the Chippewas need two more wins in their last three games to get bowl eligible for a second straight year. That clock begins here.

"Two wins," she says. "Three games. One of them is in my house, on Veterans Day, with the whole town invited. I'm not leaving Mount Pleasant without that one."

She also has to talk about who won't be out there. Central Michigan's notes say the Chippewas play their final three games without several key players, including quarterback Angel Flores and receiver Langston Lewis. That's a lot to lose in November, and Brynn doesn't pretend it isn't. But she likes who stepped up. Jadyn Glasser has been the guy under center the last two games, and he set career highs against Miami with 18 completions on 31 throws for 221 yards. Then at Ford Field against Eastern Michigan, he went 14 of 19 for 175 yards and a score.

"And Woodford," Brynn says. "Did you see Woodford?"

Mateo saw Woodford. Nick Woodford ran 21 times for 123 yards and three touchdowns against Eastern Michigan, all career highs, and the Chippewas held the ball for 40 minutes and 56 seconds. Central Michigan has also converted 18 of its 26 fourth downs this year, which ties the school record. One more sets a new one.

"So you go for it on fourth," Mateo says. "Every time?"

"Every time I'm watching," Brynn says.

Here's the thing about Sacramento State: the record says 2-8, and the Hornets don't play like it lately. They beat Kent State 31-10 on October 30. Then Toledo, a 7-2 team, came to town and barely got out, 20-17. Matt Coleman leads the team with 46 catches for 538 yards and 6 touchdowns, and Raiden Brown had 108 receiving yards against Toledo. Isaiah Ene has 411 yards on the ground. Jackson Sharman has thrown for 1,476 yards and 9 touchdowns, and Brian Salazar Jr. took the most throws against Toledo.

On defense, Mateo cooks from the back. Boogsie Silvera, the safety, has 78 tackles. "A safety with 78 tackles is a sauce that never stops simmering," Mateo says. "It's in every bite."

"That's a lot of tackling for a safety," Brynn says.

"It's a lot of running by your offense into my safety," Mateo says. "That's on you."

Central Michigan's defense is where Brynn hangs her hat. Mayson Hitchens has 68 tackles, Jaxon Cherry has 3 sacks, and the Chippewas give up 23.8 points a game. Coach Matt Drinkall's teams have won all 11 games in which they held the other side under 20 points, according to the school's notes. Sacramento State scores 17.0.

"So all we have to do is be us," Brynn says.

"All we have to do is make you be somebody else," Mateo says.

Sketch it out. The stadium is loud early, louder than you'd expect for a Wednesday in November, because it's the last home game and the town shows up for the veterans. Woodford grinds out the first two drives. Glasser hits a big throw in the second quarter. Sacramento State answers with Coleman, and Silvera makes about a dozen tackles. But the Chippewas keep converting on fourth down, they keep the ball and they wear the Hornets out in the second half. Sacramento State scores late and makes it respectable.

Brynn revs an imaginary snowmobile all the way to the parking lot. Mateo pulls his scarf tighter, takes a long look at the cold, and announces that next year's menu in Sacramento will feature something called revenge.

Brynn's imaginary snowmobile gets a victory lap in Mount Pleasant.

My call: Central Michigan Chippewas 30, Sacramento State Hornets 16.
