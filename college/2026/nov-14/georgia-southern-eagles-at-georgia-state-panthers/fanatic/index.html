Georgia Southern vs Georgia State: Georgia Grown Bowl, Round 13

Georgia Southern Eagles (5-4) at Georgia State Panthers (5-4)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Center Parc Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

Georgia's farm brand has its name on a football game again, because Georgia Southern Eagles vs Georgia State Panthers in Week 11 is the third-annual Georgia Grown Bowl. Georgia Southern insider Royce Ogeechee drove up from Statesboro with a cooler of boiled peanuts, and Georgia State insider Malik Overstreet met him at a MARTA station with a map of every shortcut to Center Parc Stadium.

Malik: Welcome to downtown Atlanta, Royce. This is the 13th meeting. Six wins for you, six wins for us. Georgia State Athletics says so. And today it's Heroes Day too, so be on your best behavior.

Royce: Malik, I'm always on my best behavior. I just eat boiled peanuts loudly. That's a Georgia Grown tradition.

The series is dead even, and it has been a seesaw for a decade. Over the last ten meetings, each side won five. Georgia State won 41-33 in 2022, then Georgia Southern answered with 44-27, 38-21 and 41-24 wins. Royce has those three framed above his granddad's tractor.

Royce: Three in a row. Granddad says it's like the old days at Paulson, when the Eagles ran the option and everybody else ran for the exits.

Malik: Your granddad also says the interstate used to be a dirt road. Things change, Royce.

Georgia Southern brings a hot hand. The Eagles have won three straight and lead the Sun Belt East at 4-1 in conference play. Last Saturday, Max Johnson went 19 of 26 for 271 yards and four touchdowns in a 49-13 rout of Marshall, and Jeremiah Ware caught five balls for 94 yards and two scores. Johnson has thrown for 2,195 yards with 15 touchdowns and only two interceptions all season.

Royce: Two interceptions. That boy protects the football like it's the last jar of peach preserves at the church supper.

Malik: Protecting it from Xavier Esqillion is different. Nine sacks, Royce. He finds quarterbacks the way I find parking on game day: fast and with attitude.

Georgia State's offense has a split personality at quarterback, and it is working. Cameran Brown leads the team with 1,356 throwing yards and 14 touchdowns, including a six-touchdown day against Old Dominion. Ayden Pereira has handled most of the throwing the last three games, and he threw three touchdowns in a 39-23 win at App State last Saturday. Lanear McCrary Jr. carried 21 times for 123 yards in that game and leads the Panthers with 526 rushing yards.

Malik: Two quarterbacks, one offense. It's like having the Red and Gold trains both running on time. Wait. Scratch that. Nobody in Atlanta would buy that.

Royce: I was with you till the trains.

The Panthers have one more weapon that keeps Royce awake. Rodshaun Dorsey has 29 catches for 476 yards and nine touchdowns. When Georgia State gets close, Dorsey is usually the guy waving his arms in the end zone.

Royce: Nine touchdowns on 29 catches. That's a lot of fruit off one tree.

Malik: And he's still blooming.

Georgia Southern's defense has been the quiet engine of its run. The Eagles allow 19.7 points a game, and they held App State to three points in a 19-3 win two weeks ago. Anthony Bynum has five sacks, and Brandon Tyson leads the team with 49 tackles. Georgia State gives up 26.2 points a game, but it scores 34.4, and it is 3-1 at home this fall.

Malik: Three and one in the city. The only home loss was Coastal. We don't talk about Coastal.

Royce: We beat Coastal on the road. Just saying.

Here is the matchup in one breath: Johnson's patience against Esqillion's speed, and Georgia Southern's defense against Dorsey near the goal. Whoever wins those fights probably carries the Georgia Grown trophy out of the stadium.

Royce: If we win, I'm handing out boiled peanuts to every Panthers fan on the train home.

Malik: If we win, I'm giving you a MARTA card loaded for one trip. Back to Statesboro.

Royce: That's the nicest thing a city boy ever did for me.

Malik: Don't get used to it.

My call: Georgia Southern Eagles 28, Georgia State Panthers 25.
