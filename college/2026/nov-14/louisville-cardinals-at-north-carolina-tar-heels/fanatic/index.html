Louisville vs North Carolina: Kenan's Third Straight Night Game

Louisville Cardinals (6-3) at North Carolina Tar Heels (5-4)

7:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Kenan Stadium · ESPN2

XFacebook

Louisville Cardinals vs North Carolina Tar Heels is Kenan Stadium's third straight night game, and Chapel Hill has officially retired its old nickname as the noon crew. Tar Heels kinesiology PhD Ainsley Pemberton has already rearranged her whole Saturday around a 7 p.m. ET kickoff, while Derby-party hostess Bianca Thornberry is bringing Louisville's four-game winning run to Kenan for the first time since 2017.

Ainsley: Five noon kickoffs to open the season, Bianca. Five. I ate so many hot dogs in the stands I started keeping score with the wrappers.

Bianca: And now?

Ainsley: Now we play under the lights. Syracuse, then Miami, now you.

Bianca: How did the first two go?

Ainsley: We don't talk about the first two.

That's fair, because they hurt. North Carolina lost 31-30 to Syracuse in overtime on Oct. 24 and 38-35 to Miami on Halloween, both at night at Kenan, both games the Tar Heels could have won. Billy Edwards Jr. threw for 341 yards and four touchdowns against Miami and still walked off a loser. Then the Heels went to UConn and won 38-21, with Jelani Thurman catching two touchdowns, and the mood in Chapel Hill came back up off the floor.

Ainsley: The body remembers what it's trained to do. We trained all fall to play close games. Now we finish one.

Bianca: Ainsley, I mean this kindly. We finish all of ours by halftime lately.

She's not wrong. Louisville is 6-3, third in the ACC at 5-2, and the last four weeks have been a parade. The Cardinals beat Florida State 44-20, won 34-26 at Syracuse, ran Stanford out of the building 52-24, and then went to Atlanta last Saturday and won 44-17 at Georgia Tech. They score 40.8 points a game.

Bianca: Lincoln Kienholz has 14 touchdowns and three interceptions. Isaac Brown has 745 rushing yards. Tre Richardson has 685 receiving yards. And Clev Lubin has seven sacks, which is about how many hats I own for the first Saturday in May.

Ainsley: Only seven?

Bianca: Seven I wear in public.

The Heels have their own case, and it's built on the defense. North Carolina allows 22.8 points a game. Linebacker Derek McDonald has 89 tackles, and Xavier Lewis matches Lubin with seven sacks. Edwards has 18 touchdown throws, and Jordan Shipp has 50 catches for 642 yards.

Ainsley: Here's the science. Kienholz runs. He ran for 81 yards at Georgia Tech. If McDonald keeps him in the pocket and Lewis gets home on third down, your parade turns into a slow walk.

Bianca: And if Brown gets to the second level?

Ainsley: Then I'm buying the first round of iced tea after the game.

The history here is short and loud. The last time Louisville came to Chapel Hill, in 2017, the Cardinals won 47-35. Ainsley was in the student section. Bianca was in a hat with a full-size rose on it.

Ainsley: That hat blocked three rows.

Bianca: Those rows thanked me.

The matchup that decides the night is Louisville's run game against a North Carolina defense that has been better than its losses. Brown has eight rushing touchdowns, and Louisville has turned short fields into points all month. If the Heels can make the Cardinals throw on third down, Edwards gets the ball back with a crowd behind him. If they can't, it's going to be a long walk to Franklin Street.

Ainsley: Saturday night, home crowd, three straight under the lights. We learned something from the first two.

Bianca: You learned how much the last minute hurts. I'm here to teach the rest of the lesson.

Ainsley: You'll be back in Kentucky by breakfast.

Bianca: With a win on the bus home and my hat still on.

Kenan gets its lights, and Louisville steals the show under them. My call: Louisville Cardinals 30, North Carolina Tar Heels 25.
