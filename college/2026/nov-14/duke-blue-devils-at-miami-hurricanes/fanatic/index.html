Duke vs Miami: Homecoming Brings the Canes Back to Hard Rock

Duke Blue Devils (7-2) at Miami Hurricanes (8-1)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Hard Rock Stadium · ABC

XFacebook

Duke Blue Devils vs Miami Hurricanes lands on Homecoming Saturday at Hard Rock Stadium, presented by The Islands of the Bahamas, and the Law Alumni Association and Student Bar Association are already planning a 10 a.m. tailgate in the parking lots before the noon kickoff. Javi "The Breeze" Castillo shows up early in orange, talking like the alumni bus never left. Harrison Wexley-Moore IV arrives with a notebook and a warning about Nate Sheppard.

Javi: Homecoming means everybody comes home. Mensah, Toney, Fletcher. The whole catalog.

Harrison: Sheppard just ran for 217 yards and three scores at NC State. Your catalog has a new chapter if you ignore him.

Miami is 8-1 and ranked No. 1 after a 24-31 loss at Notre Dame last Saturday. Duke is 7-2 after that 41-3 road win in Raleigh. The Canes still score 45.0 points a game. The Blue Devils allow 13.0. That is the collision at the heart of this afternoon: the highest-scoring offense in this building against the stingiest defense on the visiting sideline.

Darian Mensah has thrown for 2,694 yards with 28 touchdowns and two interceptions. Malachi Toney has 73 catches for 1,027 yards and 13 scores. Mark Fletcher Jr. has nine rushing touchdowns. Miami put 52 on Pittsburgh and 43 on Florida State in overtime. Even the Notre Dame loss featured a Canes offense that kept answering until the clock ran out. When Mensah gets into a rhythm, Hard Rock starts sounding like a place that forgot what a three-and-out feels like.

Duke's answer is the ground game. Nate Sheppard has 1,189 yards and 17 touchdowns on 191 carries. Walker Eget has 1,849 passing yards with 10 touchdowns and three interceptions. Jonah Burton leads the receivers with 360 yards. The Blue Devils scored 47 against Boston College and 41 at NC State. When Sheppard finds the corner, the whole Duke offense starts looking like a team that belongs in this conversation, and the last two Saturdays say he has found it.

Harrison: We don't need 45 points. We need stops, and we've been getting them all year. Thirteen points allowed a game is not an accident.

Javi: Stops against Toney? Good luck. He already has more touchdowns than some teams have wins. Homecoming is not a day for polite defense.

Marquise Lightfoot has 7 sacks for Miami. Mohamed Toure has 56 tackles. Duke's Kevin O'Connor has 4.5 sacks, and Nick Morris Jr. has 51 tackles. Both fronts can wreck a drive. The difference is what happens when the drive doesn't get wrecked. Miami has spent all fall turning those into 35-point afternoons. Duke has spent all fall turning those into field-position wars. One of those habits is about to meet the other in front of a Homecoming crowd that paid for noise.

Coral Gables residents even get a ticket offer for this game through the city's development agreement with the university, which is the kind of Homecoming detail that tells you the whole metro is treating Saturday like a reunion. Hard Rock will be loud. The alumni will be louder. The Canes will try to make the scoreboard the loudest thing of all.

Javi will talk touchdowns. Harrison will talk Sheppard. Homecoming will talk about who came back, and whether the Canes remembered how to finish after South Bend.

The last time these programs shared a November stage with this much at stake, the conversation was about whether Duke could travel and whether Miami could finish. This week the conversation is simpler. Miami wants Homecoming to feel like a reset after South Bend. Duke wants Hard Rock to feel like Carter-Finley did last Saturday, when Sheppard ran through arm tackles and the Blue Devils left town with a statement win. One of those wishes will survive the fourth quarter. The other will end up as a story the alumni tell on the walk back to the cars.

Javi keeps pointing at the 28 touchdown passes. Harrison keeps pointing at the 13 points allowed. Hard Rock will decide which number mattered more before the sun gets low over Miami Gardens.

My call: Miami Hurricanes 35, Duke Blue Devils 21.
