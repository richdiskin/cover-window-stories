Stanford vs Virginia Tech: A Lunch Pail Night in Blacksburg

Stanford Cardinal (4-5) at Virginia Tech Hokies (6-3)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Lane Stadium · ACC Network

XFacebook

Stanford Cardinal vs Virginia Tech Hokies brings a Saturday night in November to Lane Stadium, 7:30 kickoff, Blacksburg cold, and a game theme built around Ag Day and Veterans Day. It's also Stanford's last regular-season trip to the East Coast this year, which is a relief to Ignatius "Iggy" Yamamoto-Ross, the Palo Alto founder whose last four apps all failed and who still describes each one as "a learning." Ruthanne Dellinger, a former drum major in the Corps of Cadets who carries a real lunch pail to every home game, met him in the parking lot with a thermos.

Ruthanne: You look cold.

Iggy: I'm wearing three layers. One of them is a fleece vest with a company logo on it.

Ruthanne: Which company?

Iggy: It doesn't exist anymore.

That's Iggy. He's an optimist who has learned to live with crashes. This Stanford season has tested both halves of that.

The Cardinal are 4-5, and the wins have been real. They beat Hawai'i 37-27, beat Georgia Tech 34-27 with Davis Warren throwing for 396 yards, and beat NC State 31-28 with four touchdown throws from Warren. But the road has been brutal. Stanford lost 35-7 at Duke, 57-3 at Wake Forest, 58-7 at Notre Dame and 52-24 at Louisville. The Cardinal give up 37.3 points a game, and most of the damage has come away from home.

Iggy: We call those beta tests.

Ruthanne: Losing by fifty-one is a beta test?

Iggy: A rough one.

Stanford did get an extra week to prepare. Its last game was Oct. 31, and Iggy insists that matters. Virginia Tech, meanwhile, played last Friday night at SMU and came home with a 51-28 loss that Ruthanne doesn't want to discuss.

Ruthanne: We don't talk about Dallas. We talk about Clemson.

She means the 20-16 win at Clemson in October, the best day of the Hokies' season. Virginia Tech is 6-3, with wins at Maryland, at Boston College and over Georgia Tech, plus that Clemson game. The losses were 35-33 to Pitt, 25-20 at Cal and the SMU game. Ethan Grunkemeyer has been efficient all year, with 18 touchdown throws and only three interceptions, and Jeffrey Overton Jr. ran for 123 yards and two scores at SMU. Aycen Stevens has 8.5 sacks up front.

Ruthanne: That's Lunch Pail football. Show up, do the job, go home. Stevens is the whole philosophy in one guy.

Iggy: We have Micah Ford. He has 751 rushing yards. That's not nothing.

Ruthanne: It's not nothing. It's just not Overton.

The matchup is about whether Stanford can make this a game early. Warren has only three interceptions, so the Cardinal don't beat themselves through the air. The trouble is what happens on the other side. If Virginia Tech can run Overton and let Grunkemeyer choose his spots to Que'Sean Brown, who has 722 receiving yards, the Stanford defense is going to spend a long night on the field. Warren will need to keep pace, and in road games this season, the Stanford offense hasn't scored more than 24.

Iggy: We're due. Every founder is due eventually. That's the whole pitch.

Ruthanne: How many have hit?

Iggy: Define hit.

The last meeting, in 2024, was a 31-7 Virginia Tech win, and the Hokies have been 8-2 in their military appreciation games, a number Ruthanne recited from memory. Lane Stadium on a cold night, with the crowd jumping to "Enter Sandman" when the team runs out, is not the place a team with Stanford's road résumé wants to be.

Ruthanne: When we win, I'm opening my lunch pail on the sideline and eating a ham biscuit in your honor.

Iggy: And if Stanford wins?

Ruthanne: Then I'll eat it slower and think about my life.

Iggy: That's what I do after every product launch.

The Hokies should handle this one. Stanford will throw it around and get some points, but Virginia Tech has the better back, the better front and the home crowd on a night built for it.

Cold, loud and exactly what Blacksburg ordered. My call: Virginia Tech Hokies 37, Stanford Cardinal 20.
