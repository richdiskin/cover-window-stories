Memphis vs South Florida: Barbecue Smoke Over Tampa on Thursday

Memphis Tigers (7-2) at South Florida Bulls (6-3)

8:00 PM ET Thu Nov 12 · Raymond James Stadium · ESPN

XFacebook

"Sonny's," Otis Delaney-Crum says, reading the flyer twice. "In parking lot 6D. With a DJ." He folds it up and puts it in his jacket like evidence. "Reina, you invited a Memphis man to a barbecue in Florida. That's either friendship or a dare."

"It's both," Reina Valdés-Kowalski says. "Welcome to Tampa."

Memphis Tigers vs South Florida Bulls on Thursday night is USF's Salute to Service game at Raymond James Stadium, and the Office of Veteran Success teams up with USF Athletics every year to honor past and present military members. This year that means a Sonny's BBQ tailgate and a DJ out in lot 6D before the 8 p.m. kickoff, and then a stadium full of people saying thank you.

Otis is a blues guitarist who sings bass in his church choir back home, and he already has a slow song chosen for the veterans. Reina used to work the Tampa boat shows and now knows the Bulls roster more closely than her own phone number. She has a pirate flag in her bag, and she'd like to wave it on Thursday.

"Seven and two," Otis says, warming up. "Four and one in the American. Second in the conference. We score 37.6 points a game, Reina. That's not an offense. That's a horn section."

He has the names to back it up. Marcus Stokes has completed 179 of 259 throws for 2,532 yards, 25 touchdowns and 6 interceptions. Against Army on Halloween night, he went 29 of 35 for 371 yards and 4 scores. Jaylin Carter has 929 rushing yards and 10 touchdowns. Hunter Tipton, the tight end, leads the receivers with 495 yards, and Jermane Hayes caught 9 balls for 86 yards and 2 scores against Army. On defense, Elijah Kinsler has 5.5 sacks and Mike Montgomery has 67 tackles.

"Stokes hums," Otis says. "Carter hums along. And Tipton keeps time."

Reina lets him finish the verse. Then she pulls out her own sheet music, and it's mostly defense. South Florida is 6-3 and gives up 15.1 points a game. The Bulls held Kent State to 2 points and Bowling Green to 6. They held UAB to 10 on Halloween afternoon in this same stadium. Nobody has scored more than 31 on them all year.

"You're a horn section," she says. "We're the harbor patrol."

The Bulls do have a question at quarterback, and Reina doesn't hide from it. Michael Van Buren Jr. has 1,273 yards on the season, but Luke Kromenhoek took the most throws in each of the last two games, and he went 17 of 21 for 179 yards and a touchdown at East Carolina. D.J. Crowther ran for 111 yards in that one and has 689 yards and 7 touchdowns this year. Bryson Rodgers leads the receivers with 395 yards. C.J. Hicks has 5 sacks and Ayden Jackson has 50 tackles.

That East Carolina game still bothers her. South Florida lost 20-17 in Greenville last Friday. "Rough waters," she says, then catches herself. "No. That was last week. This week I steer."

Otis can't resist. "East Carolina came to Memphis, too," he says. "We beat them 41-34 in overtime."

"Overtime," Reina says. "You needed overtime against the team we lost to by three. Write that in your songbook."

Then there's the history, and Otis has been waiting all week to sing that part. In the last six regular-season meetings, Memphis won five. The Tigers won 34-33 in 2020, 59-50 in 2023, 21-3 in 2024 and 34-31 last season. South Florida's one win in that stretch was 49-42 back in 2016.

"Five out of six," Otis says, low and slow, like the end of a long blues number. "That's not a rivalry, Reina. That's a residency."

"Last year was by three," Reina says. "And the year before that was in a neutral stadium. Tonight you're in mine."

Here's how it goes. The tailgate in 6D runs long and smells great, and Otis admits, quietly, that the sauce is fine. Stokes comes out hot, Carter gets his yards, and Memphis scores early. But the Bulls don't panic. Crowther keeps carrying, Kromenhoek keeps it simple, and the South Florida defense makes Stokes earn every yard after the first quarter. It's close all night, with the lead flipping late, and the Bulls get one more stop than the Tigers do. That's enough.

Reina finally pulls the pirate flag out of her bag and yells "Gasparilla!" loud enough to shake the stands. Otis plays the veterans their slow song anyway, because some things matter more than a scoreboard, then takes one last plate of brisket for the road.

My call: South Florida Bulls 27, Memphis Tigers 26.
