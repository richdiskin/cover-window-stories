Auburn vs Mississippi State: Maroon for Military Appreciation Day in Starkville

Auburn Tigers (5-4) at Mississippi State Bulldogs (4-5)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Davis Wade Stadium · ESPN2

XFacebook

Mississippi State is calling Saturday Military Appreciation Day at Davis Wade Stadium at Scott Field, and the request from the home side is simple: wear maroon. Kickoff is 11 a.m. local, noon Eastern, and Wade Pettus already has the cowbell unholstered like it has somewhere to be. Lacey Bellamy arrived from the Plains in navy and orange, lemonade in hand, talking like the Iron Bowl is always one series away.

Wade: Wear maroon. That's not a suggestion. That's the day's official dress code.

Lacey: I brought orange. It photographs well against maroon.

Wade: It also photographs well against a cowbell, if you stand in the right place.

That's the game in miniature. Mississippi State wants the building loud at lunchtime. Auburn wants to leave town with another tight win on the road.

Auburn is 5-4 after a 26-23 win over Arkansas last Saturday. Byrum Brown threw for 180 yards and two scores in that game and ran for 121 yards and another. He has 1,740 passing yards and 11 touchdown throws this season, and he is also the Tigers' leading rusher, with 605 yards and 8 scores on the ground. Keshaun Singleton has 45 catches for 491 yards. Three weeks before the Arkansas game, Auburn walked into Athens and beat Georgia 27-24. Lacey has not stopped telling that story.

Lacey: We took down Georgia in their own yard. War Eagle does not get quieter after that.

Wade: And then LSU beat you 38-17, and Ole Miss beat you 45-15. The road does not always travel the same way twice.

Mississippi State is 4-5 and just lost 23-25 to Vanderbilt at home. AJ Swann threw for 254 yards and two scores in that game, and Sanfrisco Magee caught two balls for 95 yards and both of those scores. Anthony Evans III has 61 catches for 711 yards on the season. Fluff Bothwell has 613 rushing yards. Kamario Taylor still leads the team with 1,460 passing yards and 17 touchdown throws, and the last two home tapes have also featured Swann taking the most attempts. In the three games before Vanderbilt, the Bulldogs scored 3, 15 and 0 points, so last week's near-miss felt like a breath.

Wade: We were a kick away from walking out happy. Ring it if you mean it this time.

Lacey: I'll ring it if we win. Until then, I'm saving my wrists.

The Famous Maroon Band will be in the building, and the theme on the home schedule is military appreciation, not a farewell. Senior Day is next week against Tennessee Tech. This Saturday is for the people who served, for a noon-hour crowd in Starkville, and for a pair of teams trying to get to six wins. Davis Wade Stadium at Scott Field does not need nightfall to make a fuss. Eleven in the morning is late enough if the cowbells start on time.

In the regular-season meetings from 2016 through 2025, Auburn won five and Mississippi State won three. The Tigers have the recent memory of that 27-24 night in Athens. The Bulldogs have home turf, a maroon dress code, and a section that does not need a reminder to start early.

Wade: My cowbell has seen more football than most people on television. It likes the home team by a field goal and a little pride.

Lacey: Your cowbell can have its opinions. Auburn already won one of these tight ones this month, and we'll win another.

Wade: Then I'll put the cowbell in a sock out of respect. Until then, it's staying in the holster, ready for every point.

Lacey: If we win, I'll even let you ring it once for me. Once.

Wade: I'll hold you to that. Maroon first, though. The schedule said so.

Does maroon look better at 11 a.m. than it does at night? Ask Wade. My call: Mississippi State Bulldogs 28, Auburn Tigers 24.
