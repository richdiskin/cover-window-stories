Cal vs Virginia: Heroes Appreciation Day on the Grounds

California Golden Bears (3-6) at Virginia Cavaliers (4-5)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Scott Stadium · ACC Network

XFacebook

Charlottesville sets aside Saturday for its annual Heroes Appreciation Game at Scott Stadium, and the California Golden Bears vs Virginia Cavaliers matchup in Week 11 gets an extra jolt of purpose from it. Virginia insider Prescott Ashby-Lowell plans to thank every veteran in his section before kickoff, while Cal insider Quentin Oyelaran-Fitch has flown cross-country with a hand-lettered sign that reads "Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele for Mayor."

Prescott: Welcome to the Grounds, Quentin. Through the Seats for Service program, fans can buy $25 tickets that Virginia Athletics donates to veterans and active-duty service members. Mr. Jefferson would approve.

Quentin: That's the most Berkeley thing a Virginia fan has ever said to me. I respect it. I'm still here to organize a peaceful takeover of your end zone.

This is only the second meeting ever between these programs. Last year in Berkeley, Kam Robinson's fifth career interception with under a minute left sealed a 31-21 Virginia road win, and Quentin has been drafting a strongly worded essay about it ever since.

Quentin: Under a minute, Prescott. Under a minute. I have a whole dissertation chapter on that throw.

Prescott: Chapter one should be titled "Kam Robinson Was There."

Both sides arrive a little bruised. Each is trying to snap a three-game skid. Virginia lost to SMU, Duke and then Wake Forest, 51-7, in its last game on Oct. 31. Cal dropped games to Wake Forest, SMU and NC State. Both teams had last weekend off, so neither fan base can blame fatigue.

Prescott: An extra week of rest is how a gentleman prepares. We spent ours studying tape and sending a formal letter of complaint to the Wake Forest scoreboard.

Quentin: We spent ours in a teach-in. The topic was tackling.

Home has been kind to the Hoos. Virginia is 4-1 at Scott Stadium this season and has won 10 of its last 12 games there since 2025. The Cavaliers beat NC State 34-8, Delaware 42-3 and Syracuse 28-25 on the Grounds, and their lone home loss was 19-16 to Duke. On the road, Cal's only win this season came at Syracuse, 21-18, back on Sept. 12.

Quentin: One road win is not a flaw. It's a rare edition. Collectors pay extra.

Prescott: Collectors also check the condition, my friend.

The Virginia offense runs through Beau Pribula, who has thrown for 1,581 yards with 14 touchdowns and only three interceptions. Jekail Middlebrook leads the ground game with 513 yards, and Da'Shawn Martin has turned 26 catches into 366 yards and six touchdowns. In the Wake Forest game, Martin still caught nine balls for 119 yards.

Prescott: Pribula protects the football the way Mr. Jefferson guarded his books. Carefully.

Quentin: Sagapolutele has thrown for 1,751 yards and 11 touchdowns. He once threw for 413 yards against Wagner. That's not a quarterback, that's a movement.

Cal's top target is Chase Hendricks, who has 61 catches for 565 yards and four touchdowns, and he caught seven for 67 and a score at NC State. Adam Mohammed leads the backfield with 363 yards. On defense, AJ Tuitele has 52 tackles and JD McKinley has three sacks.

Virginia's front is the scariest thing Quentin has to protest. Matthew Fobbs-White has five sacks, and Maddox Marcellus leads the Cavaliers with 44 tackles.

Quentin: Fobbs-White is a five-sack argument. I need a bigger sign.

Prescott: Bring two. The man reads quarterbacks like a newspaper.

The hard truth for the Bears is the scoreboard against them. Cal allows 31.6 points a game, and SMU put up 59 on them in October. Virginia scores 25.9 a game and gives up 23.1, and it looked much more like itself at home than on the road.

Quentin: Here's my manifesto. Sagapolutele to Hendricks early. Tuitele everywhere. Nobody mentions the Wagner game, because it'll make the Hoos feel bad.

Prescott: Here's my reply, written in fountain pen. Pribula keeps it clean, Martin finds the end zone, Fobbs-White visits your quarterback, and every veteran in the house gets a standing ovation at the first media timeout.

Quentin: I'll stand for that part. Only that part.

Prescott: That's all the Grounds ask. Well, that and a win.

Quentin's sign stays up. So does Virginia. My call: Virginia Cavaliers 31, California Golden Bears 17.
