Kansas State vs TCU: Freedom Frogs Day in Cowtown

Kansas State Wildcats (4-5) at TCU Horned Frogs (4-5)

2:00 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Amon G. Carter Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

Kansas State Wildcats vs TCU Horned Frogs is the Freedom Frogs game at Amon G. Carter Stadium, a 1 p.m. kickoff in Fort Worth just a few days after Veterans Day, and it's the next-to-last home game of the year for the Frogs. TCU's Ryder Galloway has his hat on, his boots shined and his evening already planned: Ryan Bingham plays the main stage at Billy Bob's Texas in the Stockyards at 9 p.m., and Ryder intends to be there either celebrating or blaming Dallas.

Ryder: Hattie, this is Cowtown. We drive cattle, we play football, and we don't apologize for either one.

Hattie Brandvold came down from Manhattan with a notebook full of crop names, because she names every K-State drive after something you can grow.

Hattie: Big talk, Ryder. Our first drive is called Winter Wheat. It's slow, it's patient, and it ends with a bumper harvest.

Ryder: You name your drives after plants?

Hattie: You blame your losses on a city down the road. We all have our ways.

The recent history belongs to Kansas State, and Hattie knows every row of it. The Wildcats beat TCU 41-28 last October in Manhattan. They won 41-3 there in 2023, and they won the 2022 Big 12 championship game over the Frogs, 31-28. TCU's last win in the series came here in Fort Worth in October 2022, 38-28. Over the last ten meetings, K-State has won seven.

Ryder: Don't bring up December 2022. I still get upset thinking about it. I blame Dallas for that one too, on principle.

Both teams are 4-5, but they got there in very different ways. TCU's defense has been terrific. The Frogs allow just 15.8 points a game, and their five losses came by five, eight, seven, four and one point. Last week they lost 29-28 at Arizona. That's a season of almosts, and it has Ryder pacing.

Jaden Craig has carried the offense with 2,439 throwing yards and 12 touchdowns. The best target is tight end Kari Ashley, who has 52 catches for 655 yards and caught 14 passes at Arizona. Koron Hayward has six sacks and Hudson Hooper has 62 tackles.

Ryder: Kari Ashley runs routes like a cutting horse. He stops on a dime and leaves a nickel of change.

Kansas State has had a rougher month. The Wildcats have lost four in a row, including a 66-28 loss to Oklahoma State last week, and the passing job has moved around. Avery Johnson leads the team with 1,301 throwing yards and 10 touchdowns, but Jacob Knuth threw the most at Arizona State, and Blake Barnett did it the last two weeks. Rodney Fields Jr. has 574 rushing yards and six scores, and Derrick Salley Jr. caught two touchdowns against the Cowboys.

Hattie: You can't rush a harvest, Ryder. Some seasons you plant, and some seasons you wait. But Rodney Fields is a bumper harvest every week.

Hattie: And we scored 71 in the opener. Just saying.

Ryder: Against Nicholls, Hattie. My cousin's ranch could score on Nicholls.

The key for K-State is simple. The Wildcats have to run the football with Fields and keep the Frogs' defense on the field, because Hayward and that rush up front can make any quarterback uncomfortable. For TCU, the job is turning all that defense into points. Craig and Ashley have to finish drives, because the Frogs have lost too many games by a single score to leave field goals on the board.

Ryder: We're due. We've been knocking on the door all year. Saturday we kick it in.

Hattie: Respectfully, the door has a Powercat on it.

The Freedom Frogs crowd should be loud, and the Stockyards will be full by dark either way.

Ryder: If we win, I'm throwing this hat so high it lands at Billy Bob's.

Hattie: If we win, I'm doing the Wabash Cannonball in my seat, and I'm doing it tidy.

Ryder makes Billy Bob's on time, hat on and the Frogs on top. My call: TCU Horned Frogs 27, Kansas State Wildcats 21.
