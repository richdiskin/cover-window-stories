Georgia Tech vs Clemson: Senior Day Run Down The Hill

Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets (2-7) at Clemson Tigers (6-3)

12:30 PM ET Sat Nov 14 · Memorial Stadium (Clemson, SC) · CW

XFacebook

Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets vs Clemson Tigers is Senior Day at Memorial Stadium, and Clemson is sending its seniors down The Hill one at a time before the 12:30 p.m. kickoff, a solo run for every one of them. Dixie-Lynn Pruitt, the old Tiger Band flag captain, plans to cry through all of it and blame the wind, while Georgia Tech's Wendell Quist stands at the top of the slope calculating the exact speed of a big offensive tackle on a downhill grade.

Wendell: It's a fascinating slope, Dixie-Lynn. Gravity is doing a lot of the work.

Dixie-Lynn: Gravity has nothing to do with it, Wendell. That's tradition, and it runs on orange.

She has reason to want a good send-off, because the last time these two met, Georgia Tech wrecked her September. Clemson went to Atlanta in 2025 and lost 24-21, the only Yellow Jacket win in the nine meetings since 2016. Wendell has the final score saved as the home screen on his calculator.

Wendell: One win in nine. I'm aware it's not a large sample. I'm also aware it was the most recent one.

Dixie-Lynn: Enjoy it. We had a whole decade before that.

The 2026 Tigers are 6-3 and fifth in the ACC, and they have been winning games the hard way. They went to Syracuse last Saturday and won 18-13, and before that they beat Florida State 36-24 in Tallahassee. Tait Reynolds has thrown for 1,446 yards and does more managing than gunslinging, and Bryant Wesco Jr. caught six balls for 100 yards at Syracuse. Gideon Davidson leads the team with 528 rushing yards, and Jarvis Green ran for 85 at the Dome. The real Clemson story is defense. Will Heldt has seven sacks, safety Corey Myrick has 69 tackles, and the Tigers allow 20.7 points a game.

Dixie-Lynn: When Heldt gets a running start, Wendell, even your calculations get nervous.

Wendell: I don't get nervous. I get recalculated.

Georgia Tech is 2-7, and Wendell won't pretend otherwise. The Jackets have lost four straight, most recently 44-17 at home to Louisville. But they have been in games, too. They lost 14-13 to Colorado in the opener, beat Duke 28-25, and lost by seven at Stanford. Alberto Mendoza has 1,943 throwing yards and 11 touchdowns, and he threw for 335 yards at Virginia Tech. Justice Haynes has 635 rushing yards and ran for 87 against Louisville. Jordan Allen leads the receivers with 49 catches, and safety Tae Harris has 58 tackles.

Wendell: Mendoza is accurate. Our problem has been the other side of the ball, where we give up 32.2 points a game. That isn't a trajectory I enjoy.

Dixie-Lynn: Then don't look at the third quarter.

The football question is whether Clemson can turn its defense into points. The Tigers score 22.7 a game, which is modest for a team with this many wins, and Georgia Tech's defense has given up 34 or more six times. If Reynolds and Wesco hit a couple of early throws, the Tigers can make the afternoon comfortable in time for the seniors to enjoy the second half. If Mendoza keeps converting third downs, Wendell gets to stay in his seat a little longer than anybody in orange would like.

Saturday in Clemson starts early. Fan Fest at The Stacks, the new tailgate green space on the old motor pool site east of the stadium, has free live music before the game, and the BioSci graduate students are running an ice cream scooping fundraiser at the '55 Exchange, which means Dixie-Lynn will be eating a cone in November and calling it a breakfast tradition.

Wendell: Ice cream before noon is not a balanced diet.

Dixie-Lynn: It is on Senior Day. It's practically in the student handbook.

When the last senior hits the bottom of The Hill and the band kicks in, there won't be a quiet seat in the house, and Wendell knows it.

Wendell: I'll give you the run down The Hill, Dixie-Lynn. That's the best tradition in the conference.

Dixie-Lynn: Thank you, Wendell. Now go sit down before Heldt finds you.

My call: Clemson Tigers 31, Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets 18.
