"Ship It to Prod": Tessa Nakagawa-Ruiz Launches Spartan Week in Reno

By Our Silicon Valley product lead introduces herself and previews San José State at Nevada.

Tessa Nakagawa-Ruiz · San José State Insider · College Debut · Week 8

Meet Your Insider
Ship it to prod! Hi, Spartan Nation, I'm Tessa Nakagawa-Ruiz. By day I'm a product lead at a tech company down the road, and by Saturday I run Spartan game tape like an A/B test: version A, version B, and which one moved the chains. I travel with this team and I live with this team, and I treat every game week like a product launch, complete with a roadmap, a standup and a retro. Every week you'll get the players, the matchups, the history and the storylines, explained in plain football language (I promise to keep the jargon in the sprint backlog). When we win, I yell "Ship it to prod!" and do a celebratory keyboard smash. When we lose, I sigh "rollback," open a bug ticket and get to work on the fix. Let's launch.

The School in 60 Seconds
San José State first played a regular season in 1898. Since then the Spartans have produced more than 90 All-Americans, won 18 conference championships and sent 139 players to the NFL, including Pro Football Hall of Famers Bill Walsh and Dick Vermeil. Home is CEFCU Stadium, built in 1933 as Spartan Stadium, listed at 30,456 seats. Regional rivals include Fresno State, Stanford, Hawai'i and San Diego State. We play in the Mountain West. The last five years went 5-7, 7-5, 7-6, 7-6 and 3-9. Release notes: version 2026 already has more wins than all of 2025.

The Coach and the Scheme
Ken Niumatalolo is the head coach, hired Jan. 21, 2024. The 2026 build: an offense that throws for 246 yards a game (53rd) and runs for 138.7 (98th), averaging 21.4 points (111th). The defense is the stable release. It allows 21.7 points a game, 50th in FBS, and its pass rush has 27 sacks in seven games. That's a feature I'd demo to any investor. The defense does allow 240.9 passing yards a game (111th). Two known bugs to patch: third downs, where we convert 34.4% (111th), and time of possession, 26:41 a game (135th). We are clean, though, 47.3 penalty yards a game (31st). Our turnover margin is exactly even, 0. Balanced code.

The Conference Picture
San José State is 4-3 overall and 2-1 in the Mountain West, and 3-1 away from home. The wins: at Eastern Michigan 27-21, Cal Poly 30-20, at Hawai'i 20-16 and at UTEP 24-11. The losses: at USC 42-26, Fresno State 26-10 and Wyoming 16-13. Nobody in this game is ranked. Next up after Reno is New Mexico at home on Oct. 31, and that's a big sprint in the league race.
The retro so far: the three losses tell a story. USC is a Big Ten program and scored 42 on us in Los Angeles, but we put up 26. Fresno State held us to 10. And Wyoming beat us 16-13 in a game decided by a field goal. Meanwhile, three of our four wins came on the road, which is exactly the kind of resilience I want in a product that has to ship on a deadline. The defense has carried the release: we've allowed 16 points or fewer in four of seven games. If the offense can hold up its end in Reno, this team starts November in the thick of things.

This Week: San José State (4-3) at Nevada (2-4)
Saturday, Oct. 24 · 5 p.m. ET (2 p.m. Pacific) · Mackay Stadium, Reno · MW+.
Last week: we beat UTEP 24-11 on the road; Teu ran for 156 yards and two touchdowns. Nevada lost 31-17 at North Dakota State, where the Bison returned a missed field goal 100 yards for a touchdown and returned a botched two-point try for two more points.
Our offense vs. their defense: Nevada's pass defense is a tough firewall, 155.3 yards allowed a game, 7th in FBS. But its run defense allows 184.3 a game (116th), and opponents score on 94.4% of red zone trips (127th). My A/B test says: run the ball, then finish in the red zone.
Their offense vs. our defense: Nevada runs for 192.8 yards a game (37th) and throws for 171.8 (122nd). Dominic Kelley has 500 rushing yards. The Wolf Pack are minus-8 in turnovers, 134th, so our 27 sacks have a chance to force mistakes.
Series: in the 2016-2025 window, Nevada won five of nine meetings and we won four.
Availability: no injury or availability report found for either team as of Oct. 18.
Elevation: Mackay Stadium's field sits at 4,610 feet. Hydrate, team.
What decides it: (1) Our run game against a 116th-ranked run defense. (2) Red zone finishing. (3) Whether our pass rush can make Nevada's turnover problem worse.
