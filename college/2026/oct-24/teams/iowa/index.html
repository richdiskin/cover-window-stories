"A Good Punt Is a Love Letter to Your Defense": Orrin Kalvelage Heads North for the Pig

By Orrin Kalvelage · Iowa Insider

Meet Your Insider

Hello, neighbors. I'm Orrin Kalvelage, your Iowa insider at The Cover Window. I grew up on a farm near Iowa City and haven't missed a home opener in my life. I own one black-and-gold sweater, and I wear it with pride every single week. My wife says it needs retiring. I say it's undefeated in October.

I'm gentle and I'm stubborn, and I'm wildly proud of defense and special teams. I believe a good punt is a love letter to your defense. Here's my pledge: every number in this report has a source. If I don't know something, I'll tell you plainly. I celebrate our players as young men first, I respect every opponent, and I give thanks to God for honest work and honest football. Go Hawks.

The School in 60 Seconds

Iowa joined the conference that became the Big Ten in 1899 and played its first league season in 1900. The Hawkeyes play at Kinnick Stadium in Iowa City, capacity 69,250. They've won 13 conference championships, including 11 in the Big Ten. Iowa has been ranked No. 1 in the AP and Coaches polls 15 times.

The last five seasons are steady as a fence post: 10-4, 8-5, 10-4, 8-5 and 9-4. That's Iowa football. You know what you're getting, and what you're getting is tough.

The Coach and the Scheme

Kirk Ferentz is in his 28th season as head coach, the longest current tenure in FBS. He's led Iowa since 1999. He's the Big Ten's all-time leader in wins. Before Iowa he was head coach at Maine from 1990 to 1992 and an assistant with the Cleveland Browns and Baltimore Ravens. He played linebacker at UConn.

This year's defense is pure Iowa. It allows 293.3 yards per game (22nd in FBS) and just 14.5 points (8th). The run defense allows 84.7 yards per game, 7th in FBS. At Washington it held the Huskies to minus-3 rushing yards, with six sacks and nine tackles for loss. That's a defense built in a barn.

The offense runs for 194.0 yards per game (36th) and scores 31.0 points (61st). The passing game sits at 209.2 yards per game (93rd). Third downs convert at 39.4% (80th), and the red-zone offense ranks 36th at 89.5%.

Players to Know

QB Hank Brown: the season passing leader with 832 yards, five touchdowns and one interception. AP reported he was injured and didn't start at Washington. His status for Saturday is unknown.
QB Jeremy Hecklinski: started at Washington and went 14 of 19 for 202 yards and three touchdowns. Steady as you like.
RB Kamari Moulton: 519 yards on 76 carries and four touchdowns. At Washington he ran 26 times for a career-high 208 yards, including a 56-yard touchdown. That came a week after Ohio State held him to 27 yards. He said he had "a chip on my shoulder".
TE DJ Vonnahme: two touchdown catches at Washington, from 20 and 27 yards.
WR Tony Diaz: team-best 28 catches for 329 yards.
K Caden Buhr: field goals of 51 and 27 yards at Washington. A man after my own heart.
Cam Buffington: team-high 52 tackles.
Joseph Anderson: team-high three sacks.

The Conference Picture

Iowa is 5-1 overall and 2-1 in the Big Ten. The Hawkeyes are No. 15 in the AP poll and No. 14 in the Coaches poll. They're 3-1 at home and 2-0 on the road. The wins: 40-0 over Northern Illinois, 16-13 over Iowa State, 55-0 over Northern Iowa, 20-19 at Michigan and 41-24 at Washington. The only loss was 31-14 to Ohio State.

The win at Washington was Iowa's first in the Pacific Time Zone since the 2019 Holiday Bowl. Next come Wisconsin at home, Northwestern on the road, Purdue at home, Illinois on the road and Nebraska at home.

This Week: Game Preview from Both Sides

No. 15 Iowa (5-1) at Minnesota (4-2) · Saturday, Oct. 24, 3:30 p.m. ET · Huntington Bank Stadium, Minneapolis · FS1

Last time out. Iowa beat Washington 41-24 on Friday, Oct. 9, then had an open week. Minnesota lost 41-13 at Purdue on Oct. 10.

Iowa's offense vs. Minnesota's defense. Minnesota's defense is stout. It allows 288.8 yards per game (18th) and 159.7 passing yards (9th). Opponents convert 30.9% of third downs (17th). But the Gophers' red-zone defense ranks 102nd, allowing scores on 88.9% of trips. Run with Moulton, get inside the 20 and finish.

Minnesota's offense vs. Iowa's defense. Minnesota runs for 104.8 yards per game, 129th. Against Purdue it managed 25 rushing yards on 12 carries. Against our 7th-ranked run defense, that's a tough row to hoe. Drake Lindsey has 1,335 passing yards and nine touchdowns, and Javon Tracy has 32 catches. Our pass defense ranks 68th, so that's where Minnesota will try us. Minnesota holds the ball 32:47 per game (22nd), and Iowa 28:36 (104th).

Series. The winner takes home Floyd of Rosedale, a bronze pig trophy introduced in 1935. CFBD shows Iowa 9-1 against Minnesota from 2016 to 2025. Last year Iowa won 41-3. A TV station's summary lists Minnesota ahead 63-54-2 all-time and Iowa ahead 46-43-2 in trophy games. I'll call those figures thin.

Availability. No official conference availability report has been published. Hank Brown's status is unknown after his injury.

Three questions that decide it:
1. Who plays quarterback, and can he keep the ball safe?
2. Can Iowa's run defense keep Minnesota one-dimensional?
3. Can the Hawkeyes cash in against Minnesota's 102nd-ranked red-zone defense?

Local Flavor

It's a road trip north to Minneapolis for the pig. Late-October weather up there can turn, so bring the warm sweater. I'm bringing my one sweater. Be kind to the Gopher fans; they're good Midwestern folks like us. Not an ad. Go Hawks.

My call: Iowa Hawkeyes 28, Minnesota Golden Gophers 18.
