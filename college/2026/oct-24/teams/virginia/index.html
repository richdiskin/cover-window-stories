"As Mr. Jefferson Would've Charted It": Prescott Ashby-Lowell Welcomes Duke to the Grounds

By Prescott Ashby-Lowell · Virginia Insider

XFacebook

Meet Your Insider

Good evening, and welcome. I am Prescott Ashby-Lowell, your Virginia insider at The Cover Window. I'm a fourth-generation Wahoo, I wear a bow tie on game days, and I travel and live with this team. I refer to our university's home only as "the Grounds." Anyone who uses the other word owes me a nickel, payable in bagels.

A word on method. As Mr. Jefferson would've charted it, every claim deserves evidence, so every number here carries a source. If I don't know a thing, I'll say so plainly. I celebrate our players as fine young people first, I respect every opponent, and I'm grateful to God for autumn in Charlottesville. Hoos fans, the architecture is coming along nicely.

The School in 60 Seconds

Virginia football was established in 1887. Home is Scott Stadium, capacity 61,500, which sits next to the World Heritage Site portion of the university, including the historic Academical Village. CFBD lists the stadium as built in 1931.

History, you say? UVA helped shape the modern game's ethics, eligibility and safety rules. The program put together 28 straight winning seasons after its first in 1887 and won 12 Southern championships in 20 seasons. In 1915 Virginia became the first Southern program to beat Yale, 10-0, at the Yale Bowl.

The recent record is a renovation in progress: 6-6, 3-7, 3-9, 5-7, then a splendid 11-3 in 2025. Eleven wins. I had it framed.

The Coach and the Scheme

Tony Elliott is head coach. CFBD records his hire date as Dec. 10, 2021. Before Virginia he coached at Clemson from 2011 to 2021, finishing as associate head coach, offensive coordinator and tight ends coach. He won the 2017 Broyles Award as the top assistant in college football. He also played wide receiver at Clemson from 2000 to 2003.

This season the defense is the cornerstone. Virginia allows 326.6 yards per game (39th) and 19.7 points (38th). The pass defense is excellent at 173.9 yards per game (25th). The run defense is a wing under repair, at 152.7 yards per game (88th). UVA is +5 in turnover margin, 15th in FBS, and we're disciplined, at 49.3 penalty yards per game (38th).

The offense gains 362.9 yards per game (90th) and scores 30.0 points (65th). Two numbers need attention: a 33.0% third-down rate (120th) and a 74.1% red-zone scoring rate (124th). Even Monticello had a few rooms under construction.

Players to Know

QB Beau Pribula: 13 touchdown passes, one interception, 65.4% completions. AP lists 1,217 passing yards and ESPN lists 1,211, so that's conflicting. At SMU he went 13 of 22 for 137 yards and ran for a touchdown.
RB Jekail Middlebrook: 396 yards on 81 carries, three touchdowns. He ran 14 times for 59 yards at SMU.
WR Da'Shawn Martin: five touchdown catches on 15 receptions. AP lists 231 yards and ESPN 225, another small conflict. AP also spells his first name "Dashawn".
DL Matthew Fobbs-White: team-high four sacks.
Jacobie Henderson: team-high 38 tackles.

The Conference Picture

Virginia is 4-3 overall and 2-2 in the ACC. On the Grounds, the Cavaliers are a perfect 4-0. They beat NC State 34-8, Norfolk State 59-3, Delaware 42-3 and Syracuse 28-25. The losses came 38-27 to West Virginia at a neutral site, 38-7 at Florida State and 23-13 at SMU. Not one loss on the Grounds, and I intend to keep it that way.

Next come Wake Forest on the road, then California and North Carolina at home, and the finale at Virginia Tech.

This Week: Game Preview from Both Sides

Duke (4-2) at Virginia (4-3) · Friday, Oct. 23, 7:00 p.m. ET · Scott Stadium, Charlottesville, Va. · ESPN

Last time out. Virginia fell 23-13 at SMU on Oct. 17. Duke lost 24-20 to North Carolina the same day.

Virginia's offense vs. Duke's defense. Duke's defense is formidable. It allows 292.7 yards per game (21st) and 92.5 rushing yards (18th). Opponents convert only 29.2% of third downs, 9th in FBS. So Pribula must win early downs, and our red-zone trips must end in touchdowns. Duke's red-zone defense ranks 42nd, a bit more forgiving.

Duke's offense vs. Virginia's defense. Nate Sheppard has 820 rushing yards and also leads Duke in receiving. Duke runs for 209.5 yards per game (22nd) and holds the ball 33:17 per game (13th). That tests our 88th-ranked run defense. Walker Eget has 1,229 passing yards, but our pass defense ranks 25th. Our +5 turnover margin against Duke's 123rd-ranked penalty yardage could tilt a close game.

Series. CFBD shows Virginia 8-2 against Duke from 2016 to 2025. We respect Duke's recent rise all the same.

Availability. No official conference availability report has been published. I found no confirmed Virginia injury news.

Three questions that decide it:
1. Can our run defense slow Sheppard?
2. Can Virginia convert third downs against Duke's 9th-ranked defense?
3. Can the Hoos turn red-zone trips into touchdowns?

Harrison, the Duke insider, will mention his degrees. I'll mention our 1915 win at Yale.
