"Bow Down to Washington": Evangeline 'Vangie' Tollefsrud Takes the Open Week

By Our insider (archetype: lake Washington sailgate captain) introduces the Washington Huskies during an open date on the schedule.

Meet Your Insider
Bow Down to Washington! I'm Evangeline 'Vangie' Tollefsrud, and I'm your Washington insider here at The Cover Window. The short version: I'm a seattle native, rows crew, boats to every home game. I follow this team from the stands, every week, and I love it out loud. On the podcast, my segment is called Sailgate Report. The show card describes it like this: the Fanatic's weekly signature column and podcast segment. My rules are simple. I keep it clean and kind, I celebrate players as people, I respect every opponent, and I never invent a stat, a quote or a fact. If I can't source it, you won't hear it from me. This week the Huskies have an open date, so instead of a game preview you get the full tour: the school, the coach, the players, the league and what's next.

The School in 60 Seconds
Home is Husky Stadium in Seattle, WA, built in 1920 with a listed capacity of 70,138. The playing surface is Alaska Airlines Field, per Wikipedia. The fight song is "Bow Down to Washington", per Wikipedia. The band is the University of Washington Husky Marching Band, per Wikipedia. The program claims national titles from 1960, 1991, per Wikipedia. The last five seasons went 4-8, 11-2, 14-1, 6-7 and 9-4, from 2021 through 2025. The most frequent opponents from 2016 through 2025 tell you who matters around here: Oregon (5-5 in 10 meetings), Washington State (7-2 in 9 meetings), Stanford (5-3 in 8 meetings), Oregon State (7-1 in 8 meetings). That's a list of games, not an official rivalry list. The 2026 recruiting class ranked No. 13 nationally in CFBD's team rankings. And if you visit, ask somebody about sailgating on Lake Washington; it's the tradition I'd show you first.

The Coach and the Scheme
Jedd Fisch leads the Huskies; CollegeFootballData dates the hire to Jan. 14, 2024. Through 7 games, Washington scores 27.9 points a game and allows 22.7. By our math from CFBD figures, the offense runs on about 46% of its plays, gaining 104.0 rushing yards and 282.7 passing yards a game. That's a balanced menu. On third down, Washington converts 33.3% of the time and opponents convert 31.5%. The defense has 13 sacks and 43 tackles for loss, and the turnover ledger reads 9 giveaways against 11 takeaways. Penalties cost 67.4 yards a game. The high-water mark so far was 48 points against Eastern Washington on Sept. 19, 2026, and 3 of the 7 games were decided by eight points or fewer. Over the season, Washington has outscored opponents by a combined 36 points. Every one of those numbers is a little homework for the open week.

The Conference Picture
Washington is 4-3 overall, 3-2 at home and 1-1 away. In Big Ten play the Huskies are 1-3, which sorts them No. 15 of 18 by record. Ohio State sits on top at 4-0 in league games. Right around us: Rutgers (1-2) and Penn State (0-4). The top of the table: Ohio State (4-0), UCLA (4-0), Indiana (3-1). That order is a sort by record, not the official tiebreaker order. Every league game from here is a chance to move up, and the open week is when the plan for that climb gets built. An open date in late October is a chance to heal up and study the stretch run.

My Open-Week Take
Here's how I see it, friends. An open date isn't a day off; it's a deep breath. The coaches get to fix the little things, the players get their legs back, and we fans get a Saturday to call our families, clean the grill and argue happily about the depth chart. When the Huskies come back, I'll be right there in the stands, same seat, same voice, and I'll bring you every sourced detail. Thanks for spending part of your open week with me.
