Irish Eyes on Foxborough: No. 5 Notre Dame Meets Navy at Gillette Stadium

Meet Your Insider
I would posit! I'm Declan Moriarty-Chen, and I'm your Notre Dame insider. I'm a sweater-vested grad-student type who can recite the Irish schedule back to the leather-helmet era and the CFP selection protocol by heart. On the podcast, my segment is called The Thesis Defense, where I argue one Notre Dame proposition and defend it. My rules are simple. I keep it clean and kind, I celebrate players as people, I respect every opponent, and I never invent a stat, a quote or a fact. If I can't source it, you won't hear it from me.

The School in 60 Seconds
Home is Notre Dame Stadium in Notre Dame, Ind., built in 1930 with a listed capacity of 80,795. The mascot is the Notre Dame Leprechaun, the fight song is the "Notre Dame Victory March," and the band is the Band of the Fighting Irish. The program claims national titles from 1924, 1929, 1930, 1943, 1946, 1947, 1949, 1966, 1973, 1977 and 1988. The last five seasons went 11-2, 9-4, 10-3, 14-2 and 10-2, from 2021 through 2025. And if you visit, ask somebody about "Touchdown Jesus." It's the tradition I'd show you first.

The Coach and the Scheme
Marcus Freeman leads the Fighting Irish, and CollegeFootballData dates the hire to Dec. 3, 2021.

Through 7 games this season, we're scoring 41.1 points per game and allowing 13.4.

This Week: Game Preview from Both Sides
No. 5 Notre Dame (6-1, independent) at Navy (2-5, 1-4 American). Saturday, Oct. 31, 12 p.m. ET, Gillette Stadium, Foxborough, Mass. (neutral site), ABC.
Last time out. Notre Dame had the week off. Its last game was a 28-24 loss at BYU on Oct. 17, when Carr threw for 261 yards, Nolan James Jr. ran for a touchdown, and Jaden Greathouse caught eight balls for 125 yards.
Navy lost 49-39 to North Texas on Oct. 24. Gutierrez ran 26 times for 228 yards and three touchdowns, and he threw a touchdown, too.
Players to watch. Notre Dame quarterback CJ Carr has thrown the most passes in all seven Irish games. He's 129 of 193 for 1,639 yards, 16 touchdowns and just one interception. Jordan Faison leads the receivers with 34 catches for 451 yards and four scores, and Jonaz Walton leads the rushers with 426 yards. On defense, linebacker Drayk Bowen has 38 tackles and defensive lineman Boubacar Traore has 3.5 sacks.
Navy quarterback Jackson Gutierrez is the engine of the option. He has 869 rushing yards and 12 rushing touchdowns on 150 carries, and he's thrown for 692 yards and seven touchdowns on just 64 attempts. Tight end Jordan Collins has turned six catches into 212 yards and three scores. On defense, linebacker Coleman Cauley has 58 tackles and linebacker MarcAnthony Parker has four sacks.
Availability. No official availability report has been posted for this game yet, and box scores don't mark starters. Carr has thrown the most passes in every Notre Dame game, and Gutierrez has done it in every Navy game since the opener, so check the teams' pregame notes.
The coaches. Marcus Freeman was hired at Notre Dame on Dec. 3, 2021. His teams went 9-4, 10-3, 14-2 and 10-2 over the last four seasons, and this year's Irish score 41.1 points a game while allowing 13.4. They've forced 16 turnovers and lost just two.
Brian Newberry was hired at Navy on Dec. 19, 2022. Last season his Midshipmen went 11-2, won the Liberty Bowl 35-13 and finished No. 23 in the final AP poll. They also won the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy for the second straight season, beating Air Force 34-31 and Army 17-16.
Storylines. This game is a chess match between the Navy option and a Notre Dame defense that allows only 2.4 yards a carry. Navy gains 5.7 yards a carry, so something has to give. The Irish big guys up front will try to keep Gutierrez from turning the corner. On the other side, Carr has been nearly perfect with the ball, and Navy's defense allows 29.1 points a game.
Fun and true. Notre Dame claims 11 national championships, from 1924 through 1988. The Irish have won eight of the last nine regular-season meetings in this series, including 49-10 last year. Navy's lone win in that stretch was a thriller, 28-27 in 2016. And the Band of the Fighting Irish and the Navy midshipmen in the stands make this one of the most respectful scenes in the sport.
From the preview booth. I shared the mic this week with Cmdr. (Ret.) Annabel Merriweather, Navy's insider. Here's what I said:
"I would posit, Commander, that this is one of the great traditions in college football."
"A rare stumble. My thesis defense that week was a long one."
"Eleven wins and a trophy named for the Commander-in-Chief. I concede the point."
"Commander, whatever happens, I'd be honored to say grace with you after the game."
And the last word from Commander: "The honor is mine, young man. Anchors aweigh."

Local Flavor (unsponsored, not an ad)
This one's played away from both campuses, at Gillette Stadium outside Boston, and a Halloween noon kickoff in New England should bring plenty of sailors and leprechauns to the stands. Back in Annapolis, Navy usually plays at Navy-Marine Corps Memorial Stadium, which seats 34,000. Notre Dame Stadium, built in 1930, seats 80,795, and visitors never miss "Touchdown Jesus."

My call: Notre Dame Fighting Irish 43, Navy Midshipmen 15.
