Balloons Over San José: New Mexico Floats Into the Bay Area on Halloween

Meet Your Insider
Red or green? Both. ¡Hola, Albuquerque! I'm Paloma Begaye-Ortiz, and when I'm not at a Lobos game, I'm floating a hot-air balloon over the Rio Grande at sunrise. Every October I fly in the Balloon Fiesta, and every Saturday I read Lobo football like wind currents: where it's drifting, where it's rising, and when it's about to soar. I travel with this team and I live with this team, and every week I'll bring you the players, the matchups, the history and the storylines, with some green-chile spice and a balloon metaphor for every drive. That offense floated like a balloon at dawn, up and up. When we win, I yell, "Red or green? BOTH!" and pump my fist. When we lose, I sigh, "Too much heat today."

The School in 60 Seconds
The New Mexico Lobos compete in the Mountain West. Our colors are cherry and silver. Home is University Stadium in Albuquerque, opened in 1960 and listed at 39,224 seats. The last five years started low: 3-9, 2-10, 4-8, then 5-7 and a big 9-4 in 2025. That's a balloon catching a thermal, y'all. Up and up.

The Coach and the Scheme
Jason Eck is the head coach; CFBD dates his hire to Dec. 16, 2024.

Through 7 games this season, we're scoring 37.0 points per game and allowing 11.3.

Players to Know
QB Toa Fa'avae: 49 of 87, 790 yards, 5 touchdowns, 4 interceptions.
RB Scottre Humphrey: 453 yards on 95 carries, 9 touchdowns.
TE Cade Keith: 20 catches, 265 yards, 2 touchdowns.
DE Brett Karhu: 5 sacks, the team lead.
LB Mercury Swaim: a team-high 43 tackles.

The Conference Picture
New Mexico is 6-1 overall and 3-0 in league play. ESPN's standings have us 1st in Mountain West.
The season so far: W 38-7 vs Central Michigan Chippewas (Sept. 5), W 70-7 vs Mercyhurst Lakers (Sept. 12), L 6-14 at Oklahoma Sooners (Sept. 19), W 42-18 at New Mexico State Aggies (Sept. 26), W 61-7 vs UTEP Miners (Oct. 3), W 21-10 at Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors (Oct. 17), W 21-16 vs North Dakota State Bison (Oct. 24).

This Week: Game Preview from Both Sides
New Mexico (6-1, 3-0 Mountain West) at San José State (4-4, 2-2 Mountain West). Saturday, Oct. 31, 4 p.m. ET, CEFCU Stadium, San Jose, Calif., MW+.
Last time out. New Mexico beat North Dakota State 21-16 on Homecoming in Albuquerque on Oct. 24, during a Pack Week that honored the Route 66 centennial. Kiefer Sibley ran 13 times for 91 yards and a touchdown, and Keith caught five balls.
San José State lost 28-13 at Nevada on Oct. 24. Viliami Teu ran for a touchdown, and Dominique McKenzie caught two balls for 60 yards.
Players to watch. New Mexico quarterback Toa Fa'avae has thrown the most passes in the last six games. He's 49 of 87 for 790 yards, five touchdowns and four interceptions. Scottre Humphrey leads the rushers with 453 yards and nine scores, and tight end Cade Keith leads the receivers with 20 catches for 265 yards. Brett Karhu has five sacks, and linebacker Mercury Swaim leads the defense with 43 tackles.
San José State's Luke Weaver threw the most passes in each of the first six games and leads the team with 1,620 yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions. Jabari Bates leads the rushers with 586 yards, and freshman Carson Clark leads the receivers with 47 catches for 551 yards and five scores. Jayland McGlothen has six sacks, and linebacker Dylan Lee leads the team with 71 tackles.
Availability. No official Mountain West availability report has been posted for this game yet. San José State's quarterback spot is the big question. Freshman Daniel Rolovich started the last two games, but he was hurt in the first quarter at Nevada, and Weaver came back in the fourth quarter to lead a late scoring drive. Who starts Saturday hasn't been announced, so check the teams' pregame notes.
The coaches. Jason Eck leads New Mexico, with a hire date of Dec. 16, 2024. His defense allows 11.3 points a game and had taken the ball away 21 times through six games. The Lobos' only loss was 14-6 at Oklahoma.
Ken Niumatalolo was hired at San José State on Jan. 21, 2024. Before the UTEP game he went with Rolovich because, as he put it, the staff "just felt like we needed a spark." He also said of the freshman, "He's got a big arm. He's big and strong and can run. He's not afraid."
Storylines. The matchup to watch is Humphrey and the New Mexico big guys up front against a Spartans defense that allows 22.5 points a game. On the other side, whoever plays quarterback for San José State has to find Clark against a Lobos defense that loves taking the ball away. San José State has won the last four meetings, including 35-28 last season, and the Spartans are looking for an eighth straight home win over the Lobos.
Fun and true. San José State first played a regular season in 1898, and the Spartans count Pro Football Hall of Famers Bill Walsh and Dick Vermeil among their alumni. New Mexico's University Stadium opened in 1960, and last season the Lobos went 9-4.
