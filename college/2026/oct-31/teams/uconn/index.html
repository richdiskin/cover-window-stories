Huskies in High Gear: UConn Flies West to Meet the Falcons on Halloween

Meet Your Insider
What's up, Husky Nation. I'm Jo Ferrante, your UConn insider. I'm from the Hartford area. I played point guard in college and coached high school quarterbacks after that, so I treat football like a fast break and I know every Husky by number. I count possessions like a basketball coach, because every one matters.

Here's the deal. I'm brash and competitive, and I take "nobody watches UConn football" very personally. I also keep it honest. Every stat I give you has a source, and if I don't know something, I'll say so. Next play, next possession. I celebrate these players as people first, I respect every opponent, and I'm grateful to God for a Friday night under the lights. Pack the Rent.

The School in 60 Seconds
UConn first fielded a team in 1896 and played in Division I-AA until 1999. The Huskies began moving to Division I-A in 2000 and became a full member in 2002. They played in the Big East and then the American from 2004 through 2019. In 2019 the program went independent, since the current Big East doesn't sponsor football. CFBD lists UConn as an FBS independent this season.

Home is Pratt & Whitney Stadium in East Hartford. CFBD lists it as built in 2003 with 40,000 seats. The recent climb is real: 1-11 in 2021, 6-7 in 2022, 3-9 in 2023, then 9-4 in both 2024 and 2025. Back-to-back nine-win seasons. Say it louder for the people in the back.

The Coach and the Scheme
Jason Candle is the new head coach. CFBD records his hire date as Dec. 6, 2025. He was head coach at Toledo from 2016 to 2025 and an assistant there from 2009. Before that he coached at Mount Union from 2003 to 2008, and he played wide receiver at Mount Union and Geneva.

Through 7 games this season, we're scoring 35.6 points per game and allowing 23.9.

This Week: Game Preview from Both Sides
UConn (4-3, FBS independent) at Air Force (5-2, 3-2 Mountain West). Saturday, Oct. 31, 3 p.m. ET, Falcon Stadium, USAF Academy, Colo., MW+.
Last time out. UConn beat UMass 35-16 at home on Oct. 23. Osborne was 14 of 19 for 173 yards and two touchdowns, Bowers ran 15 times for 131 yards and a score, and JD Willoughby caught a touchdown.
Air Force lost 28-14 at Wyoming on Oct. 23. Medders ran 21 times for 115 yards, and Howard Fisher IV threw the most passes.
Players to watch. UConn quarterback Kalieb Osborne has thrown the most passes in every game. He's 112 of 182 for 1,594 yards, 12 touchdowns and only two interceptions. Running back Cyncir Bowers has 564 yards and seven touchdowns, and Shamar Porter leads the receivers with 265 yards and three scores on 14 catches. On defense, linebacker Hayden Pegg has 47 tackles and K'Von Sherman has 3.5 sacks.
Air Force runs the option, so the quarterback who carries it matters most. Evan Medders leads the team with 490 rushing yards and six touchdowns. Liam Szarka leads in throwing at 19 of 38 for 296 yards, and four different quarterbacks have thrown the most passes in a game this year. Tight end Simon Kibbee leads the receivers with eight catches for 135 yards. Linebacker Blake Fletcher has 72 tackles, and defensive back Max Mustell has three sacks.
Availability. No official availability report has been posted for this game yet, and box scores don't mark starters. Osborne has thrown the most passes in every UConn game, while four different Air Force quarterbacks have done it, including Fisher last week, so check the teams' pregame notes.
The coaches. Jason Candle is UConn's new head coach, hired on Dec. 6, 2025. He led Toledo from 2016 to 2025 after starting there as an assistant in 2009, and his UConn offense plays with pace, running for 195.3 yards a game.
Troy Calhoun has led Air Force since 2007, and CFBD dates his hire to Dec. 22, 2006. His Falcons run for 343.0 yards a game, second in FBS, and hold the ball 36:22 a game.
Storylines. The big matchup is the Air Force option against a UConn defense that allows only 3.3 yards a carry. Pegg and the Husky big guys up front will need to stay disciplined on every snap. On the other side, Bowers and Osborne will try to keep up with an Air Force defense that allows 20.9 points a game. Possessions will be precious, and Jo will be counting every one.
