Washington vs Nebraska: Skull and Crossbones for a Halloween Blackout

Washington Huskies (4-3) at Nebraska Cornhuskers (5-2)

8:00 PM ET Sat Oct 31 · Memorial Stadium (Lincoln, NE) · BTN

Washington Huskies vs Nebraska Cornhuskers is the Week 9 Halloween blackout in Lincoln, with a skull and crossbones painted at midfield and black balloons ready at Memorial Stadium. Vangie Tollefsrud, a Seattle native who rows crew and sails to every Husky home game, says Elijah Brown, Demond Williams Jr. and Jedd Fisch's Huskies spoil the party, and Darla Rasmussen, a former Husker volleyball player and farm girl, says Anthony Colandrea, Owen Chambliss and Matt Rhule's Blackshirts own the night.

Darla: Go Big Red! Welcome to Lincoln, Vangie. Wear black. Everybody wears black.

Vangie: Bow Down to Washington! I packed purple, Darla. I also packed a life jacket. Where's the lake?

Darla: There's a pond behind my uncle's barn. It's mostly ducks.

Nebraska athletic director Troy Dannen confirmed the look on his monthly radio show, and KETV reported it this way: "He also confirmed Memorial Stadium, on Saturday, will feature the skull and crossbones at midfield. Plus the black balloons will be out for the blackout game against Washington."

The day runs on a schedule. Nebraska's game page lists parking lots opening at noon ET, the Cornhusker Kickoff Tailgate at 4 p.m. ET, the Husker Legacy Walk at 5:30 p.m. ET, gates at 6:30 p.m. ET and kickoff at 8 p.m. ET on BTN.

Darla: Eight hours of tailgate, Vangie. On a farm we call that a short workday.

Vangie: In Seattle we call it a regatta with no water.

The history is older than either of them. Nebraska's notes say Saturday is "the 11th all-time meeting," and the Huskers lead the series 5-4-1, including 2-1-1 in Lincoln. The first one, per Washington's notes, was played in Lincoln on Oct. 17, 1925, and "The game ended in a 6-6 tie." In 1991 in Lincoln, the Huskies trailed 21-9 in the third quarter and won 36-21. In 1998, "Nebraska buried the Dawgs, 55-7." The last one was 2011, when "Nebraska rolled up 309 rushing yards in a 51-38 win." This is the first meeting as Big Ten opponents, and Nebraska goes to Seattle in 2027.

Vangie: Thirty-six to twenty-one, Darla. In your house. I'd like that one framed.

Darla: Frame 1997 too. We won at No. 2 Washington that year, and Nebraska's notes say it's still the last time the Huskers beat a top-10 team on the road.

Then there's the holiday itself. Nebraska's game notes say, "Nebraska owns a 17-2 record all-time on Halloween." The last Halloween game was 2015 at Purdue, and the last one at home was 1998 against Texas.

Darla: Seventeen and two on Halloween. We don't hand out candy, Vangie. We hand out losses.

Vangie: Big talk. I'll take full-size candy instead.

Both teams come in off an open week. Washington is 4-3 and 1-3 in the Big Ten after a 38-26 win at Purdue, where Brown went 21 of 27 for 302 yards and three touchdowns, with three interceptions. Williams, who has 1,396 yards and eight touchdown throws this year, is working back from a fractured rib, and Fisch isn't saying who plays. "Obviously, there's a competitive advantage that we will not give up," he said. He also had kind words for both: "Rising tides lift all ships."

Vangie: Our guy Williams has passed 1,000 career rushing yards. Washington's notes say he's at 1,076, the third Husky quarterback to do it, after Jake Locker and Marques Tuiasosopo.

Darla: Those are big names. Tell him to save his legs for the Penn State game.

That's next for Washington, on Nov. 7 at Husky Stadium, and the notes say it will be the Nittany Lions' "first visit to Seattle in 105 years." The Huskies also have punter Hunter Green, who sits No. 1 in the Big Ten at 47.9 yards a kick. Sports Illustrated reported that center Landen Hatchett is out for the season with a neck injury, a real loss up front.

Vangie: Hunter Green kicks it farther than I can row in a minute. I've checked.

Nebraska is 5-2 and 2-2 in the league after two three-point losses, 20-17 to Indiana and 17-14 at Oregon. Colandrea has 1,570 yards and 14 touchdown throws, Kwazi Gilmer has 38 catches for 492 yards and seven scores, Jamal Rule has 391 rushing yards and Chambliss leads the team with 46 tackles. The Blackshirts allow 15.4 points a game, and Nebraska's release says Big Ten opponents "have converted just 8-of-49 third downs."

Darla: Eight of forty-nine, Vangie. That's my free-throw shooting at the county fair, and I'm a volleyball player.

Rhule knows what's coming. "They're a vertical passing offense," he said. "They push the ball down the field." He also put the losses on the staff: "I think we've got to do a better job as coaches."

On the Nebraska side, the Huskers have held all seven opponents to 23 points or fewer, which the release says Nebraska last did in 2001. Washington scores 27.9 a game, so something gives.

The Big Ten's availability report, posted at 8 p.m. ET Wednesday, Oct. 28, lists Demond Williams Jr. questionable and Landen Hatchett out.

Vangie: Fine, Darla. I'll wear black. But I'm wearing my rowing jacket over it.

Darla: Deal. And I'll find you a lake by 2027. Go Big Red.

Vangie: Bow Down to Washington. See you at the Legacy Walk.

My call: Nebraska Cornhuskers 33, Washington Huskies 19.
