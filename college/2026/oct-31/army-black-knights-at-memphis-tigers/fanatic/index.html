Army vs Memphis: Homecoming, Hero Day and Halloween in the 901

Army Black Knights (5-2) at Memphis Tigers (6-2)

8:00 PM ET Sat Oct 31 · Simmons Bank Liberty Stadium · ESPNU

Army Black Knights vs Memphis Tigers is the Week 9 Homecoming and Hero Day game on Halloween night at Simmons Bank Liberty Stadium, and Memphis promises fireworks around the bowl of a stadium it just spent $226.5 million to rebuild. Capt. (Ret.) Thaddeus Rourke, a retired Army logistics officer who reads every option play like a supply chain, says Cale Hellums, Dawson Jones and Jeff Monken's Black Knights march out of Memphis happy, and Otis Delaney-Crum, a Memphis blues guitarist who sings bass in his gospel choir, says Marcus Stokes, Hunter Tipton and Charles Huff's Tigers keep the party going.

Otis: Well, hello there, Captain. Welcome to the 901. I tuned the guitar just for you.

Thaddeus: Attention on deck, Otis. I've got a mission brief, a pressed uniform and a costume for later.

Otis: What's the costume?

Thaddeus: A slightly more relaxed Army officer.

The whole week has been a party in Memphis. The university's homecoming page lists Friday's ROARFest Block Party and Pep Rally on the Alumni Mall, with "food trucks, giveaways, the Mighty Sound of the South, the Memphis Spirit Squads, a live DJ and dance party, appearances by the 2026 Homecoming Court, games, a kid zone and Tent-or-Treat fun for children." On Saturday, the Blue Crew tailgate opens at noon ET in the Student Tan Lot, the alumni tailgate opens at 5:30 p.m. ET on Tiger Lane, and the band alumni play during the game.

Otis: Tent-or-Treat, Captain. Kids go tent to tent for candy. It's the most organized thing in Memphis.

Thaddeus: Otis, that's a supply chain. I approve.

The stadium is new, too. Memphis says the renovation added a four-level West Tower, "360 degree sound, fireworks around the bowl of the stadium and one of the largest ribbon boards in all of college football," and capacity now sits at 45,632.

Otis: Fireworks every time we score. Bring earplugs, Captain.

Thaddeus: I've heard louder, Otis. But never in a stadium.

These two don't see much of each other. Memphis notes say this is "just the sixth time" they've met, and the first since Nov. 23, 2002, when DeAngelo Williams led Memphis to a 38-10 win at home. The release adds that only three current Tigers were alive for that one, and Anthony Richard Jr. was "just 8 months old."

Otis: Eight months old, Captain. He was a baby. Now he's a cornerback.

Thaddeus: Time marches, Otis. So do we.

Both teams come in hot. Army is 5-2 and 4-1 in the American after Jones booted a 50-yard field goal as time expired to win 38-35 at Tulsa. He also hit from 51 in the first quarter, and Army says he's the first Black Knights kicker to make two from 50 or longer in a game. Hellums ran 30 times for 121 yards and three touchdowns that night, and Godspower Nwawuihe added 135 yards on 12 carries, with a 66-yard score.

Thaddeus: Two from fifty, Otis. Our kicker resupplied the whole operation from long range.

Otis: That's a long note to hold, Captain. Even for a bass.

For the season, Hellums has 840 rushing yards and 16 rushing touchdowns on 159 carries. Army scores 35.3 points a game, and Elijah Walton leads with 52 tackles.

Memphis is 6-2 and 3-1 in the league, and the Tigers are fresh off a 41-34 overtime win over East Carolina. They trailed by two scores before Stokes won it with a scramble and Ellis Ellis Jr. sealed it with an interception. That win made Memphis bowl eligible for the 13th season in a row, which the school calls the longest such run by a non-Power 4 program. Stokes has 2,161 yards and 21 touchdown throws, Jaylin Carter has 833 rushing yards and eight scores, Tipton has 30 catches for 409 yards and Elijah Kinsler has 5.5 sacks. Memphis scores 36.1 a game.

Otis: Thirteen in a row, Captain. That's a long gig with no night off.

There's a coaching wrinkle, too. The Brigade Review notes that the last time Huff saw a triple-option team was 2021, when his Marshall team beat Navy 49-7. His defensive coordinator that day, Lance Guidry, is the same one Army sees on Saturday.

Thaddeus: Then Coach Guidry knows what's coming, Otis. Knowing and stopping are two different missions.

Otis: Captain, that's the most Army thing anybody's ever said to me.

The matchup is simple to say and hard to do: Army's option against a Memphis defense that has never seen it this year, and Stokes against an Army defense that allows 22.1 a game.

Otis: After the game I'm playing "Hound Dog" on Beale. Come sing with me, Captain.

Thaddeus: I'll hold the low notes, Otis. Copy that, and beat Navy.

Otis: Navy's not here, Captain.

Thaddeus: Navy's always here, Otis. Go Army.

My call: Memphis Tigers 31, Army Black Knights 28.
