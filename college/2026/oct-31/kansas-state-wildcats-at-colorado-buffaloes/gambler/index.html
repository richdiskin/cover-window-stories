Kansas State Wildcats at Colorado Buffaloes

Kansas State Wildcats (4-3) at Colorado Buffaloes (2-5)

2:00 PM ET Sat Oct 31 · Folsom Field · TNT

Moss Feldstein is swaying in a hammock strung between two pines outside Folsom Field, kombucha in hand. Hattie Brandvold walks up in her purple denim jacket, field notebook open, and says, "Moss, that hammock is not regulation tailgate equipment." Moss grins. "Hattie, gravity disagrees. Pull up a pine."

Kansas State (4-3) at Colorado (2-5), Saturday at 2 p.m. ET on TNT at Folsom Field in Boulder. It's a Big 12 game: K-State is 1-3 in league play, Colorado 0-4. DraftKings has Kansas State Wildcats −1.5 with a total of 47.5. My card: Kansas State Wildcats −1.5, Over 47.5, and a final of Kansas State Wildcats 28, Colorado Buffaloes 23.

Hattie speaks for Kansas State, brooch shining. "Sugar, Avery Johnson has run for 462 yards and thrown for 1,301. We score 32.9 points a game. We beat Kansas 35-24. And we've beaten Colorado the last two years, 31-28 and 24-14. You can't rush a harvest, but we're due for one."

Moss speaks for Colorado, from the hammock. "Joseph Williams has 46 catches for 643 yards. Lamont Lester Jr. has five sacks. Gideon Lampron has 65 tackles. We lost to Utah 27-24, so we can hang with anybody. Ralphie's running."

Why Kansas State? Because the Wildcats score and Colorado has trouble stopping people. K-State puts up 32.9 points a game, and Colorado allows 30.1. Colorado lost 57-24 at Oklahoma State last week and has dropped all four Big 12 games. Isaac Wilson has thrown multiple interceptions in each of his last three games. In The Trenches likes the K-State run game against this front. A team that scores like K-State only has to win by a field goal. My final has the Wildcats by more than that.

The risk is that K-State has slipped. It lost three of its last four games, including a 28-17 loss at Arizona State in which Jacob Knuth, not Johnson, threw the most passes. And Folsom Field is a tough place for a visitor, at altitude and on a day the home crowd is desperate for a win.

Why the Over? Five of K-State's seven games went past 47.5. Colorado's last two games finished 27-24 and 57-24, and its defense gives up 30.1 a game. Wilson can throw it, too: he had 280 yards in Stillwater, and Williams caught nine balls for 135. Both offenses should find room. My 28-23 adds up to 51, over 47.5.

Now the room, and it mostly agrees with me. The Formula has K-State 28-24 and takes the Wildcats and the Over. Next Man Up has K-State 28-22, same side, same Over. In The Trenches backs K-State too. Follow The Money goes the other way, Colorado and the Under, because the market moved toward the Buffs.

Next Man Up started it: "Wildcats by nearly a touchdown. Hattie, start planting."

Follow The Money pushed back: "The market's been coming to Colorado all week. I'm riding that."

The Formula answered: "Colorado allows 30.1 a game. The market can ride. I'll take the points on the board."

Bottom of the card: a K-State offense that scores, a Colorado team that hasn't beaten a Big 12 opponent, and two teams that can light up a scoreboard. Hattie gets her bumper harvest. Give me the Wildcats and the Over.

Kansas State Wildcats 28, Colorado Buffaloes 23. Kansas State Wildcats cover the 1.5. Over 47.5.
