Balloons and Product Launches: New Mexico Visits San José State on Halloween

New Mexico Lobos (6-1) at San José State Spartans (4-4)

4:00 PM ET Sat Oct 31 · CEFCU Stadium · MW+

New Mexico (6-1, 3-0 Mountain West) at San José State (4-4, 2-2 Mountain West). Saturday, Oct. 31, 4 p.m. ET, CEFCU Stadium, San Jose, Calif., MW+.

Your hosts are Paloma Begaye-Ortiz, a New Mexico fan who flies a hot-air balloon in the Balloon Fiesta every October, and Tessa Nakagawa-Ruiz, a San José State fan and tech product lead who treats game week like a launch.

Tessa: Welcome to San Jose, Paloma! I've got the roadmap, the standup and the retro all scheduled.

Paloma: Thanks, Tessa. I brought green chile. Red, too. Both!

Tessa: Can I A/B test them?

Paloma: You can, but the answer is always both.

Players to watch

New Mexico quarterback Toa Fa'avae has thrown the most passes in the last six games. He's 49 of 87 for 790 yards, five touchdowns and four interceptions. Scottre Humphrey leads the rushers with 453 yards and nine scores, and tight end Cade Keith leads the receivers with 20 catches for 265 yards. Brett Karhu has five sacks, and linebacker Mercury Swaim leads the defense with 43 tackles.

San José State's Luke Weaver threw the most passes in each of the first six games and leads the team with 1,620 yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions. Jabari Bates leads the rushers with 586 yards, and freshman Carson Clark leads the receivers with 47 catches for 551 yards and five scores. Jayland McGlothen has six sacks, and linebacker Dylan Lee leads the team with 71 tackles.

Paloma: Humphrey runs like a balloon catching a thermal. Up and up.

Tessa: Carson Clark has 47 catches as a freshman. That's a feature I'd demo to anybody.

What happened last week

New Mexico beat North Dakota State 21-16 on Homecoming in Albuquerque on Oct. 24, during a Pack Week that honored the Route 66 centennial. Kiefer Sibley ran 13 times for 91 yards and a touchdown, and Keith caught five balls.

Paloma: Too much heat for a while, then we soared. Red or green? BOTH!

San José State lost 28-13 at Nevada on Oct. 24. Viliami Teu ran for a touchdown, and Dominique McKenzie caught two balls for 60 yards.

Tessa: Rollback. I opened a bug ticket on the bus ride home.

Injuries and availability

No official Mountain West availability report has been posted for this game yet. San José State's quarterback spot is the big question. Freshman Daniel Rolovich started the last two games, but he was hurt in the first quarter at Nevada, and Weaver came back in the fourth quarter to lead a late scoring drive. Who starts Saturday hasn't been announced, so check the teams' pregame notes.

What the coaches are up to

Jason Eck leads New Mexico, with a hire date of Dec. 16, 2024. His defense allows 11.3 points a game and had taken the ball away 21 times through six games. The Lobos' only loss was 14-6 at Oklahoma.

Ken Niumatalolo was hired at San José State on Jan. 21, 2024. Before the UTEP game he went with Rolovich because, as he put it, the staff "just felt like we needed a spark." He also said of the freshman, "He's got a big arm. He's big and strong and can run. He's not afraid."

Paloma: Coach Eck builds a defense the way I pack a balloon basket. Nothing loose.

Tessa: Coach Niumatalolo ships fixes fast. I respect that.

Player storylines

The matchup to watch is Humphrey and the New Mexico big guys up front against a Spartans defense that allows 22.5 points a game. On the other side, whoever plays quarterback for San José State has to find Clark against a Lobos defense that loves taking the ball away. San José State has won the last four meetings, including 35-28 last season, and the Spartans are looking for an eighth straight home win over the Lobos.

What's happening on campus

It's Halloween in San Jose, and San José State is hosting Trick or Treat with the Spartans and Youth Spirit Day. Families can trick-or-treat before kickoff, and local youth dance and cheer groups will perform on the field at halftime. CEFCU Stadium opened in 1933 as Spartan Stadium and is listed at 30,456 seats.

Paloma: I'm dressing as a hot-air balloon. Cherry and silver, of course.

Tessa: I'm dressing as a software update. Nobody knows when I'll arrive.

Anything else fun and true

San José State first played a regular season in 1898, and the Spartans count Pro Football Hall of Famers Bill Walsh and Dick Vermeil among their alumni. New Mexico's University Stadium opened in 1960, and last season the Lobos went 9-4.

Tessa: Whatever happens, Paloma, supper's on me. We'll say grace first.

Paloma: Deal, Tessa. I'll bring the green chile.

My call: New Mexico Lobos 31, San José State Spartans 15.
