Louisville vs Kentucky: Governor's Cup Blue Out in Lexington

Louisville Cardinals (8-3) at Kentucky Wildcats (7-4)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Kroger Field · ESPN2

XFacebook

Louisville Cardinals vs Kentucky Wildcats for the Governor's Cup is Senior Day and a Blue Out at Kroger Field, and it falls smack in the middle of Thanksgiving break, when the University of Kentucky campus empties out and the students who left their cars behind had to move them to the Comers and South Broadway lots by Tuesday. Rhett Abernathy-Coyle says the place will be full and loud by noon Saturday anyway, and Bianca Thornberry says that's fine, because it will be very quiet by about three.

"You're going to be lonely in that Blue Out," Bianca says. "The whole state's going to be watching you look at a scoreboard."

"It's Senior Day," Rhett says. "Seniors don't lose the Governor's Cup on Senior Day."

"Your seniors lost it 41-0 last year."

Rhett lets that one sit for a while. The last two years of this rivalry haven't been kind to Lexington. Louisville won 41-14 in 2024 and 41-0 last year, and Bianca has both final scores saved as the wallpaper on her phone, just to be safe.

"Before that, we won five straight," Rhett says. "Remember 2018? Fifty-six to ten. I wore that shirt until it fell apart."

What he doesn't want to talk about is November. Kentucky started this season like a team on a mission. The Wildcats won at Texas A&M, beat South Carolina and LSU in overtime, won at Oklahoma, and were 7-1 at the end of October. Then the road got hard. They lost at Tennessee, at home to Florida and at Missouri, scoring 14 points or fewer in all three.

"Every team hits a wall," Rhett says. "We hit ours early so we can climb over it on Saturday."

Kenny Minchey has had a good year overall, with 15 touchdown throws and only four interceptions, but Missouri made it a long afternoon for him. CJ Baxter has 610 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and Kenny Darby is the receiver Minchey trusts most, with 711 yards. On defense, Grant Godfrey has 96 tackles and is the heartbeat of that group, and Mi'Quise Humphrey-Grace leads the Wildcats with six sacks.

Bianca's team has no such wall. Louisville is 8-3, has won five straight and just put 42 points on Pittsburgh. Lincoln Kienholz threw five touchdowns in that game, and he has 20 on the season against four interceptions. Isaac Brown has 834 rushing yards and runs like he's late for supper. Tre Richardson caught eight balls for 114 yards and two scores against Pitt.

"Nothing bothers Lincoln," Bianca says. "You could play this game in a snowstorm and he'd ask if it was supposed to get cold."

"He's never played in front of a Blue Out," Rhett says.

"He played at North Carolina two weeks ago and won 16-14. He's played in front of worse."

The Louisville defense has its own name to watch. Clev Lubin has nine sacks, and Bianca says he's the first guy she watches on every snap. Stanquan Clark leads the team in tackles.

Rhett's real hope is the noise and the moment. Kroger Field on Senior Day, with blue in every seat, has been a hard place for anybody to win this year. Kentucky lost only twice there, to Alabama in September and to Florida.

"You've got to beat us here, in this," Rhett says, waving at the stands. "On Senior Day. I don't care what happened the last two years."

Here's how Saturday goes. Kentucky comes out flying, the way rivalry teams do, and Baxter scores early to make the Blue Out roar. Louisville settles down and gets Brown going on the ground, and Kienholz finds Richardson behind the defense for the go-ahead score before halftime. The second half turns into a shootout. Minchey answers twice, once to Darby, and with five minutes left the Wildcats are within a score. But Lubin gets to Minchey on a big third down, Louisville runs the clock with Brown, and the Cardinals hang on.

When it's over, Bianca walks past Rhett on her way to the exit and pats him on the shoulder, gently, the way you'd pat a cousin at Thanksgiving who just lost the backyard football game for the third year running, and tells him the cup will be well taken care of until he comes to visit it.

My call: Louisville Cardinals 31, Kentucky Wildcats 26.
