Air Force vs New Mexico: Senior Day Salute, Title in Reach

Air Force Falcons (9-2) at New Mexico Lobos (9-2)

4:30 PM ET Fri Nov 27 · University Stadium (NM) · CW

XFacebook

Air Force Falcons vs New Mexico Lobos on the Friday after Thanksgiving is Military Appreciation and Senior Day at University Stadium in Albuquerque, with a joint color guard presenting the colors before kickoff and military and law enforcement vehicles lined up along Louie Lane. Lt. Col. (Ret.) Marissa Okonkwo-Hale has walked the whole row of vehicle displays twice already, and Paloma Begaye-Ortiz has followed her the entire way, explaining why none of it will help the Falcons once the Lobos get the ball.

"That's a beautiful truck," Marissa says, stopping in front of one.

"It's going to be a beautiful afternoon," Paloma says. "For us. In a very respectful way."

This isn't just a send-off for the Lobo seniors. It's for everything. New Mexico is 6-1 in the Mountain West and sitting in first place, and if the Lobos win, they're in the Mountain West Championship Game on Dec. 4. Air Force is 5-2 in the league and needs a win in Albuquerque plus help from somebody else. That's a big ask for a holiday weekend.

"We've been asking for help our whole lives," Marissa says. "We call it teamwork. And we usually end up doing it ourselves."

She has reason to be proud. Air Force is 9-2 and has won four straight since a loss at Wyoming, and last week the Falcons beat UTEP 52-3. The offense is the triple option, the same as always, and it is wearing people out. Evan Medders leads the team with 741 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and when defenses crowd the box to stop him, he flips it over the top. He has thrown for five touchdowns on just 29 throws all year. Against UTEP, he hit Jonah Dawson for a 77-yard score.

"Twenty-nine throws," Marissa says, "five touchdowns. That's not a stat. That's an ambush."

Paloma has heard it before. She has a stat of her own, and it's the one Lobo fans have been quoting since September. New Mexico gives up 12.4 points a game. The Lobos held Nevada to three and Mercyhurst and UTEP and Central Michigan to seven apiece, and opponents run for 2.5 yards a carry against them.

"Two and a half," Paloma says, holding up two fingers and then half of a third one, which looks painful. "You can't run the option on two and a half yards. You'll run out of field by Christmas."

The man Paloma would like Marissa to meet is Brett Karhu. The defensive end has 8.5 sacks, and he plays like a guy who's mad about all of them. Behind him, Mercury Swaim leads the team in tackles. And on offense, the Lobos have their own bulldozer in Scottre Humphrey, who has 762 rushing yards and 14 touchdowns on the ground.

"Fourteen," Paloma says. "I counted every one out loud."

The quarterback spot is the one Lobo question. Toa Fa'avae took most of the throws all fall, but Luke Moga handled most of them in the 24-17 win at Wyoming last week, going 22 for 40 for 234 yards. Paloma doesn't care who it is, as long as the ball eventually ends up in Cade Keith's hands. The big tight end has six touchdown catches.

Air Force won five straight meetings from 2018 through 2022, and Marissa remembers every one of them. Paloma remembers the last two. New Mexico won 52-37 in 2024 and 20-3 a year ago, and she was loud at both.

"Twenty to three," she says. "We kept you to a field goal, Marissa. Bring the option. We'll bring the eleven guys who can read it."

Here's how Friday goes. Air Force takes the opening kickoff and grinds out a ten-minute drive that ends in a field goal, and Marissa nods like she planned it. New Mexico answers with Humphrey, again and again, and the Lobos punch it in to take the lead. Medders breaks one long run in the third quarter to keep the Falcons close, and he hits a deep throw that makes the stadium groan. But Karhu gets to him twice in the fourth quarter, and Humphrey closes it with his fifteenth rushing touchdown of the season. The Lobos go to the conference title game.

When it's over, the seniors come to the rail, and Marissa does something Paloma didn't expect. She snaps a sharp salute toward the New Mexico sideline, then turns around and salutes the Air Force bench too, because a holiday weekend like this one belongs to everybody who has ever worn the uniform.

My call: New Mexico Lobos 28, Air Force Falcons 19.
