Nebraska vs Iowa: Black Friday Fight for the Heroes Trophy

Nebraska Cornhuskers (7-4) at Iowa Hawkeyes (9-2)

12:00 PM ET Fri Nov 27 · Kinnick Stadium · CBS

XFacebook

Nebraska Cornhuskers vs Iowa Hawkeyes on Black Friday is the Pioneer Heroes Game, and before kickoff at Kinnick Stadium one everyday hero from Iowa and one from Nebraska will be honored on the field and have their names inscribed on the Heroes Trophy. Darla Rasmussen drove in from Lincoln still floating from the Ohio State win, and Orrin Kalvelage met her in the parking lot at sunrise to remind her that floating and winning in Iowa City are two different things.

"Six in the morning," Orrin says. "That's when the lots open. I was here at five-fifty. That's how you respect Senior Day."

"I didn't sleep," Darla says. "I'm not tired. I don't think I'll ever be tired again. We beat Ohio State, Orrin. Thirty-one to twenty-eight. Anthony Colandrea threw three touchdowns. Do you know what that feels like?"

"I know what it feels like to hold that trophy," Orrin says, and he points at the stadium like it's a family farm.

That's where the needling starts, because the recent history between these two programs is not much of a fight. Iowa has won nine of the last ten meetings. Last year it was 40-16. The two years before that were 13-10 and 13-10, which is about as Iowa as football gets.

"Thirteen to ten twice in a row," Darla says. "That's not a rivalry. That's a hostage situation."

"It's a love language," Orrin says.

What makes this one different is that Darla's team arrives with real momentum. Nebraska went through a hard stretch in October, four straight losses, three of them by a touchdown or less, and it looked like the season was getting away. Then the Huskers went to Rutgers and won 31-0, came home and knocked off Ohio State, and suddenly Colandrea looks like the guy Darla promised everyone in August. He has 21 touchdown throws and only seven interceptions. Kwazi Gilmer has eight touchdown catches, and Jamal Rule has 705 rushing yards.

"We figured something out," Darla says. "And you know what we figured out? We can score on anybody."

"You can score on Rutgers," Orrin says. "Come score on us."

He has reason to talk. Iowa's defense has given up 12.2 points a game this season. It shut out Northern Illinois, Northern Iowa and Wisconsin. Linebacker Cam Buffington has 97 tackles, and Orrin can tell you where every one of them happened. The Hawkeyes have lost twice, to Ohio State and by three points at Minnesota, and they are No. 15 in the country coming into the last home game of the year.

Then there's the part of Kinnick that gets Darla every time, even when she's rooting against it. The Swarm, with the players arm in arm and coming out together, the way Hayden Fry started it. The I-O-W-A chant moving corner to corner after every score. And somewhere in the middle of it, a fan trying to win the Pancheros Burrito Lift on the big screen by hoisting his buddy over his head.

"I'm not doing the burrito," Darla says.

"Nobody asked you to be the burrito," Orrin says. "You'd have to earn that."

Iowa's offense is never going to make a highlight show, and Orrin is fine with that. Hank Brown runs it carefully, with 10 touchdown throws and four interceptions, and the Hawkeyes trust the ground game when it matters. Last week L.J. Phillips Jr. ran for 154 yards at Illinois in a 34-17 win.

"We run the ball, we punt it deep, we hit you," Orrin says. "If you want fireworks, there's a Fourth of July."

The Nebraska worry is the week itself. The Huskers poured everything into last Saturday, and now they have to turn around and play a Friday road game in a stadium that will be loud from the Hawk Walk on. Darla knows it. She just won't say it.

Here's how Friday goes. Nebraska comes out loose and confident, and Colandrea finds Gilmer for an early touchdown that quiets the student section for about four seconds. Iowa answers on the ground, Phillips grinds out a long drive, and the I-O-W-A chant goes around twice before halftime. In the second half the Hawkeyes' defense does what it does, squeezing the field until Colandrea is throwing into coverage on third down. Nebraska keeps it close until late, then Iowa runs out the last five minutes with the ball on the ground.

Darla gets one good look at the Heroes Trophy as it goes past her, close enough to read both names on it, and she tells Orrin the honest truth: the real heroes on it were the only part of the day she didn't argue with.

My call: Iowa Hawkeyes 24, Nebraska Cornhuskers 18.
