Louisiana Tech vs Georgia Southern: Blue Out for the Title Host

Louisiana Tech Bulldogs (9-2) at Georgia Southern Eagles (7-4)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Allen E. Paulson Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

Louisiana Tech Bulldogs vs Georgia Southern Eagles at Paulson Stadium is Senior Day, a Blue Out and First Responders Appreciation all at once, and the winner gets to host the Sun Belt Championship. Royce Ogeechee has been waiting his whole life to see Georgia Southern play for a league title, and Booker "Champ" Tullos drove all the way from Ruston to make sure he waits a little longer.

"Blue Out," Champ says, looking around at the stands. "Y'all picked our color. That's hospitable."

"Ours is a different blue," Royce says. "Older. Wiser. It's been to more championships."

That's the heart of the thing for Royce. The Eagles have won national titles at the lower level and built a proud program in Statesboro, but they have never played in the Sun Belt Championship. One win on Saturday changes that, and it gets the game at home, too.

"First-ever," Royce says. "You know what that word means in Bulloch County? It means my uncle cries in the truck on the way home."

Champ understands. Louisiana Tech has been waiting a while, too. This is the Bulldogs' first year in the Sun Belt, and they've gone 7-0 in it, which is not how most newcomers introduce themselves. Tech is 9-2 overall, with the only losses coming at LSU and at Baylor in September, and the Bulldogs haven't lost a home game or a conference game all year.

"We came into this league like we'd been here forever," Champ says. "Like we were just back from a long hunting trip."

The man Champ wants you to meet is Blake Baker. The quarterback has 21 touchdown throws this year and exactly one interception, and last week he accounted for five touchdowns in a 48-3 win over Arkansas State, four through the air and one on his feet. Jalen Mickens is his go-to receiver, with 805 yards on the year and a 110-yard day last week.

"One interception," Champ says, holding up a single finger. "I keep it up so I don't forget it. Because he won't throw another."

On defense, Tech has a guy Royce has been watching on film all week. Alonzo Jackson Jr. leads the Bulldogs in both sacks and tackles, with 11 sacks and 81 stops. The Bulldogs shut out UL Monroe and Old Dominion back to back in October.

Royce has a quarterback story of his own, and it might be the best one in the league. Max Johnson is a seventh-year quarterback, which in Royce's mind makes him basically a member of the family. He has 20 touchdown throws and only three interceptions, and last week at Troy he went 22 for 26 for 227 yards and three scores. Andre Greene Jr. caught two of those touchdowns.

"Seven years," Royce says. "He's been in college longer than some of our professors. He's earned a home title game."

But the thing Royce is proudest of is the Georgia Southern defense. Against Troy, the Eagles allowed 127 total yards and negative rushing yards. Linebacker Brandon Tyson returned an interception on the very first play of that game. Against App State in October, the Eagles allowed three points.

"Minus seven yards rushing," Royce says. "Troy ran backward all afternoon. We should've charged them for the tour."

These two have only met once before, back in December 2020, and Georgia Southern won that one 38-3. Royce brings it up. Champ points out that none of these players were on the field for it, and also that it was a long time ago, and also that it doesn't count.

Here's how Saturday goes. Both defenses come out swinging, and the first quarter is all punts and loud stands. Johnson finds Taylor for the first touchdown, and Paulson Stadium shakes. Baker answers before halftime with a strike to Mickens. In the second half, it turns into a field-goal game, the kind where every third down feels like the whole season. Georgia Southern takes a two-point lead late. Then Baker, calm as a Sunday morning, drives Tech down the field, and the Bulldogs kick the go-ahead field goal with under a minute to play. Jackson gets home on the last Eagles drive to finish it.

Royce takes it hard, but he takes it like a Georgia Southern man. He shakes Champ's hand, tips his cap to the first responders being honored near the end zone, and tells Champ that the Eagles will be the ones hosting next year.

My call: Louisiana Tech Bulldogs 26, Georgia Southern Eagles 25.
