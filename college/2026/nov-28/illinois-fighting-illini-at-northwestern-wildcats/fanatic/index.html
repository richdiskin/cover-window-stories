Illinois Fighting Illini vs Northwestern Wildcats: New Ryan Field

Illinois Fighting Illini (6-5) at Northwestern Wildcats (6-5)

12:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Ryan Field · BTN

XFacebook

Margery Vandermolen has never sat in Northwestern's new Ryan Field, and neither has any Illinois team, so she plans to get to Evanston early Saturday and take notes on the place like she's grading it. Illinois Fighting Illini vs Northwestern Wildcats is the Land of Lincoln Trophy game, it's Senior Day for the Wildcats, and Ezra Lindqvist has already written a welcome speech with three footnotes for the visitors.

"Footnote one," Ezra says. "Welcome. Footnote two: the trophy is staying. Footnote three: see footnote two."

"We've had that trophy for two years," Margery says, tapping a slide rule against her orange notebook. "It likes Champaign. It's used to the corn."

She's not wrong about the run. Illinois has won the last two meetings and wants to keep the trophy for a third straight year, and the Illini have done it with a guy Ezra can't stop talking about. Collin Dixon has 80 catches for 1,088 yards and 13 touchdowns. He caught eight balls for 142 yards against Iowa last week, which is the kind of afternoon that makes a secondary go home and stare at the ceiling.

"Dixon is a problem," Ezra admits. "I have a whole chapter on him. It has its own appendix."

The problem for Illinois is that the rest of the Iowa game went badly. The Illini lost 34-17 at home, Katin Houser threw two interceptions after throwing three at UCLA the week before, and the ground game got nowhere. Houser can still sling it, with 2,449 yards and 20 touchdowns, and he threw for 318 against the Hawkeyes, but he's been giving the ball away at the worst times. Matthew Bailey leads the defense with 85 tackles from the secondary, and that group is going to see a lot of running backs on Saturday.

Margery has a bigger memory to hold onto, one from this very season. Illinois beat Oregon 21-10 in October. Nobody in Champaign has stopped talking about it.

"We beat the Ducks," she says. "I graphed it. The graph is one arrow pointing up."

Northwestern is coming off a rough month and isn't hiding from it. The Wildcats have dropped four in a row, the last one a 20-17 heartbreaker at Minnesota. Nicco Marchiol led a 10-play, 70-yard drive late in the fourth quarter and ran in the tying touchdown from seven yards out, and then the Gophers kicked a 44-yard field goal on the last play. Before that, Griffin Wilde had caught a 49-yard touchdown to cap a 98-yard drive right before halftime.

"Ninety-eight yards," Ezra says. "I footnoted that one twice."

Marchiol has taken the most throws in each of the last three games, and he's been steady, with a touchdown in each. The real engine is Caleb Komolafe, the redshirt junior running back who went for 109 yards on 19 carries in Minneapolis and now has seven career games over 100 yards, the most by a Wildcat since Evan Hull. Wilde has 920 receiving yards. Braydon Brus leads the defense in tackles and sacks.

"Komolafe against your run defense is my whole thesis," Ezra says. "The rest is citations."

The Wildcats are 5-1 at home this year, and the seniors are walking out of the tunnel for the last time in a building most of them only got to call home this season. That counts for something on a cold November morning by the lake.

Here's how Saturday goes. Illinois goes right to Dixon early, and he gets his catches, because he always does. Northwestern answers the way it wants to, feeding Komolafe until the Illinois front gets tired. Marchiol hits Wilde for a big gain in the second quarter, the Wildcats take a lead into the half, and Houser has to throw his way back into it. He moves the ball, but the Northwestern defense gets one big stop in the fourth quarter, and Komolafe grinds out the last few minutes behind the senior linemen.

Ezra marches down to the field and publishes a correction to everybody who wrote off the Wildcats in November. Margery tucks her slide rule into her coat, writes "next year, Champaign" in orange ink, and underlines it twice.

My call: Northwestern Wildcats 28, Illinois Fighting Illini 21.
