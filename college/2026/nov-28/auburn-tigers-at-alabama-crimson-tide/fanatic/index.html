Auburn vs Alabama: Iron Bowl Night, and Nobody Knows Who's Throwing

Auburn Tigers (7-4) at Alabama Crimson Tide (9-2)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Bryant-Denny Stadium · ABC

XFacebook

Auburn Tigers vs Alabama Crimson Tide is a Saturday night Iron Bowl in Tuscaloosa, and Mary-Kathryn "MK" Strickland has been counting. Alabama has won the last seven Iron Bowls played at Bryant-Denny Stadium. She wants eight. She's already planning what she'll text her cousin in Opelika when it's over.

"Seven straight here," MK says. "I don't like to brag. I just like to say it out loud, slowly, to people from Auburn."

Lacey Bellamy came over from Auburn with an orange-and-blue scarf and an attitude. Her Tigers are 7-4, and she thinks that's a lot better than it sounds, because one of those wins came in Athens.

"We went to Georgia and won," Lacey says. "Twenty-seven to twenty-four. Y'all played them in Tuscaloosa and lost. In your own house."

MK pretends she didn't hear that.

Alabama is 9-2 and ranked third. The Tide lost to Georgia 20-17 and at Tennessee 26-10, but they've won four straight since, including 31-13 at LSU and 21-6 at Vanderbilt. Keelon Russell sat out last week against Chattanooga, and the coaches said he could have played. They just didn't want to risk him before this one.

"Keelon's fine," MK says. "He was resting. Like a racehorse. You don't run your racehorse in the practice race."

Russell has 2,480 yards, 21 touchdowns and six interceptions. Lotzeir Brooks is his go-to receiver, with 597 yards. The Tide score 36.8 points a game and give up 17.2.

And then there's the Alabama defense. It allows just 2.7 yards a carry. Yhonzae Pierre has 6.5 sacks. The Tide have 31 sacks as a team, and they've taken five interceptions back for touchdowns.

"That's the scary part," Lacey admits. "Our blockers have given up 39 sacks. If your guys get loose, I'm hiding my eyes."

The other question is who Lacey's watching at quarterback. Byrum Brown has started all year. He's thrown for 2,035 yards and leads Auburn in rushing with 644. But in the Samford game, Alex Golesh pulled him after two plays, and Tristan Ti'a came in and went 17 of 18 for 341 yards and four touchdowns in one half.

"I don't know who's starting," Lacey says. "Golesh doesn't either, or he isn't telling. I'd play Ti'a. I'd play both. I'd play anybody who can throw it to Keshaun."

Keshaun Singleton has 744 receiving yards and eight touchdowns, and Chas Nimrod had 157 yards against Samford. Auburn scores 29.0 points a game, and Lacey thinks the Tigers can get to 20 against anybody.

"We put 41 on Mississippi State," she says. "We put 39 on Florida. We can score."

"You lost to Florida," MK says.

"By five," Lacey says. "Close ones count for something."

"Not in this state they don't," MK says. "In this state, you win the Iron Bowl or you hear about it until next Thanksgiving."

Here's how Saturday goes. Bryant-Denny is loud from the start. Auburn takes a deep shot early and hits it, and Lacey jumps out of her seat and stays up there a while. Alabama answers with a long drive, and Russell finds Brooks for a touchdown. The Tide's rush starts getting home in the second quarter, and Pierre gets a big sack that leads to an Alabama field goal. Alabama pulls away in the third. Auburn gets a couple of late scores to keep it respectable, and Lacey is still standing at the end.

When it's over, MK sends the text to her cousin in Opelika. It just says: Eight. Lacey tells her she'll see her next year, and that she's going to be insufferable when it's her turn.

My call: Alabama Crimson Tide 33, Auburn Tigers 20.
