Pittsburgh Panthers vs California Golden Bears: Last Stand in Berkeley

Pittsburgh Panthers (6-5) at California Golden Bears (3-8)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · California Memorial Stadium · ACC Network

XFacebook

Cal and Pitt played five times between 1955 and 1966, then went almost six decades without seeing each other until a 2024 game in Pittsburgh. Cal won two of the three games ever played at its place. Now they're ACC opponents, Cal is closing out its season at Databricks Field on Saturday, and Quentin Oyelaran-Fitch is standing outside California Memorial Stadium with a cardboard sign that reads HISTORY IS ON OUR SIDE.

Dominic "Yinz" Bertolucci reads it twice and blows his homemade whistle.

"History? Quentin, buddy, the guys in that history are your grandpa's age. I'm talking about this week."

"History is a living document," Quentin says. "Also, I made a second sign."

The second sign says RESPECT THE BEAR. He made a third that says DEFEND THE TURF, and he's got a backup roll of tape in his jacket in case the wind kicks up.

The truth is both of these fan bases could use a good Saturday.

Cal is 3-8 and has lost five in a row. The skid started with a 41-7 loss to Wake Forest, ran through a 59-20 loss at SMU, and ended last week with a 44-28 loss at home to Stanford, which is the one that keeps Quentin up at night. A loss to Pitt would leave the Bears at 3-9, their worst finish since a 1-11 season back in 2013. Cal is tied with Georgia Tech for last place in the ACC.

"I'm not saying it's been a good year," Quentin says. "I'm saying the year isn't over. There's a difference, and I'm prepared to march about it."

Pitt's year has been strange in a different way. The Panthers started 5-0, and Mason Heintschel looked like one of the best young quarterbacks in the league. He threw seven touchdowns against Miami (OH) and six against Bucknell. Then he tore his ACL at Virginia Tech on Oct. 2, and Pitt has gone 1-5 since. Holden Geriner and freshman Corey Dailey have traded the job back and forth, and Geriner got pulled in the third quarter of last week's 42-10 loss at Louisville. Nobody outside the Pitt building knows yet who's taking the first snap in Berkeley.

"Yikes," Yinz says. "That whole stretch is a yikes. I've had the whistle in my mouth since October and I haven't blown it for anything good."

He's not wrong. Pitt is 0-3 on the road since Heintschel went down. The Panthers lost 16-10 at home to Florida State with Dailey under center, and the offense has scored 10 points in each of the last two games.

But here's what Yinz keeps coming back to. Pitt is 6-5 and already going to a bowl. Damon Ferguson Jr. has 740 yards on the ground. Cataurus Hicks has 750 receiving yards on just 38 catches, so he turns a lot of them into big gains. Cruce Brookins has 80 tackles, Jaeden Moore has 4.5 sacks, and the Pitt defense gives up just 21.5 points a game. When the Panthers beat Georgia Tech 34-11 on Halloween, Geriner was the quarterback.

"So it can be done," Yinz says. "I've seen it. I got it on my phone."

Cal's defense hasn't been able to slow much of anybody down lately. The Bears give up 33.1 a game and have allowed 31 or more in five straight. Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele has 2,019 yards and 12 touchdowns, and Chase Hendricks has 69 catches, but the offense hasn't topped 28 in any of the last five games. Adam Mohammed ran for 90 yards and a touchdown against Stanford, and he's the guy Quentin wants to see carry it 25 times.

"Give it to Adam," Quentin says. "Power to the play-call. Run the ball, keep their quarterback, whoever it is, standing on the sideline."

"That's a good plan," Yinz says. "That's a real yinz plan. You just don't got the guys to stop us after."

Quentin writes that down on the back of the RESPECT THE BEAR sign so he can protest it later.

The one recent meeting was close. Pitt won 17-15 in Pittsburgh in 2024. Pitt leads the all-time series 4-2, and the Panthers have never lost to Cal at home. In Berkeley, it's a different story, and that's the whole point of Quentin's first sign.

Kickoff is 4:30 local, so the sun will be going down over the bay by the second half. It's the last game of the year for Cal, and for a lot of those guys in blue and gold, it's the last time they'll play in that stadium.

Here's how I see it. Pitt figures out who's playing quarterback by Thursday and keeps it simple. Ferguson carries it, Hicks gets behind the Cal secondary once or twice, and the Panthers defense does what it does to a Cal offense that's been stuck. Mohammed gets his yards, and Cal scores enough to make Quentin wave his signs in the fourth quarter. But Pitt pulls away.

Yinz will yell "Yinz SEE that?!" so loud the board op flinches. Quentin will fold up his signs, look at the sunset, and announce a peaceful protest against the punter.

My call: Pittsburgh Panthers 31, California Golden Bears 19.
