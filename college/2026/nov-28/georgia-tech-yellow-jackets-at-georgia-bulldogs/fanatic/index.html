Georgia Tech vs Georgia: The 120th Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate

Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets (3-8) at Georgia Bulldogs (10-1)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Sanford Stadium · ABC

XFacebook

Wendell Quist still has the ticket stub from the eight-overtime game, and he says he keeps it in a drawer next to his birth certificate, because both of them prove he was there. Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets vs Georgia Bulldogs is back in Sanford Stadium for the 120th Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate, and Thad Okafor-Brandt says that 44-42 night in 2024 was the most fun he's ever had being terrified.

"Eight overtimes," Thad says. "I aged a year. I had to sit down in the fifth. I've never sat down at a Georgia game."

"And you still won," Wendell says. "That's the worst part. You always still win."

That part is true lately. Georgia has won eight straight in this series. One more makes it nine, and that would be the longest run either school has had against the other in a rivalry that's 120 games old. Wendell has heard about the streak so many times he can tell you the score of every one of them without being asked, which nobody has ever asked him to do.

But Wendell also has one thing he likes to point out. The last three games were all one-score games. The eight-overtime game in Athens. A 31-23 Georgia win in 2023. And last year's 16-9 game in Atlanta, where neither offense could find the end zone.

"Close," Wendell says. "Every year it's close. You're No. 1 and it's still close."

Thad would like to talk about this year's team, which might be the best one he's seen. Georgia is 10-1, ranked No. 1, and already headed to the SEC Championship Game on Dec. 5. The only loss was 27-24 to Auburn in October. Since then, the Bulldogs have beaten Florida, Ole Miss, Missouri and South Carolina by double digits every time. Gunner Stockton has 22 touchdown throws and only 4 interceptions. He went 19 of 23 for 281 yards at South Carolina last week. Talyn Taylor is the top receiver. Nate Frazier ran for two scores in Columbia. And the defense, led by Chris Cole with 71 tackles and 5.5 sacks, has given up 13.9 points a game.

"This team doesn't make mistakes," Thad says. "It just shows up, does its job and goes home. I love them. I'd like them to show some emotion once in a while, but I love them."

Wendell's team has shown plenty of emotion. Some of it the wrong kind. Georgia Tech is 3-8 and has lost five of its last six, including a 50-29 loss to Wake Forest last Saturday at Bobby Dodd. But that one win in the middle of it was a 20-6 stunner at Clemson, and Wendell has decided that's the version of the team that's coming to Athens.

"Brent Key said it's the biggest game of the year," Wendell says. "He's right. It's the only game that matters now."

The quarterback picture is the question. Alberto Mendoza threw it most of the year, with 2,123 yards and 11 touchdowns. Grady Adamson took over against Wake and ran 16 times for 143 yards and a score. Justice Haynes has 806 rushing yards, Jordan Allen has 664 receiving yards, and Tae Harris leads the defense with 68 tackles from safety.

"Run Adamson," Wendell says. "Run Haynes. Run the clock. Make them get bored."

"Georgia doesn't get bored," Thad says. "Georgia gets efficient."

Here's how I see it. Sanford Stadium is rocking for the rivalry, and Georgia scores on its first three drives. Stockton finds Taylor for one, Frazier runs for two, and the hedges are humming by the end of the first quarter. Tech gets a field goal and a long Adamson run to keep the visiting section on its feet. The Bulldogs pile on before halftime, and the starters take a seat early in the fourth. The Jackets score one more time in the final minutes.

Wendell walks out of Sanford Stadium with his ticket stub in his pocket, the new one, and says he's putting it in the drawer too. Thad asks why. Wendell says it's proof he was there for the night Georgia Tech scored a touchdown on the No. 1 team in the country.

My call: Georgia Bulldogs 43, Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets 11.
