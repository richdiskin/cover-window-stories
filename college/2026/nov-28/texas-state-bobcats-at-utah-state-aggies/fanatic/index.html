Texas State vs Utah State: The Rematch in Logan

Texas State Bobcats (7-4) at Utah State Aggies (4-7)

9:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Maverik Stadium · USA Net

XFacebook

Hyrum Tolleson went out to his pasture in Cache Valley the week after the San Marcos game with a tape measure and a football. He marked off forty-eight yards from a fence post and tried the kick himself, over and over, until the cows lost interest. Texas State Bobcats vs Utah State Aggies is the rematch at Maverik Stadium, and Hyrum says he never made the kick either, but he wasn't wearing a uniform, so he has an excuse.

"Wide left," Hyrum says. "Every time. It's like the posts move."

Yesenia Calderwood bought her first real winter coat for this trip. She lives in San Marcos, where the river is the main event in summer and a hoodie is plenty in November. She tried it on in the store and said she looked like a sleeping bag with arms.

"I'm not complaining about Logan," Yesenia says. "I'm just telling you I'm wearing every shirt I own under this."

The first game is still a sore spot in Logan. Texas State won 20-17 on Oct. 24 after Utah State missed a 48-yard field goal wide left in the final seconds. The Aggies' defense was terrific that day. They held Brad Jackson to 167 throwing yards and sacked him four times. But Jaylen Jenkins took a kickoff back 100 yards for a Texas State touchdown in the first quarter, and that was the difference.

"One kick returned and one kick missed," Hyrum says. "That's the whole game."

"And the score," Yesenia says. "The score was also part of the game."

Yesenia's Bobcats have been one of the stories of the year in their first season in the Pac-12. Texas State is 7-4 and has won three straight, beating Oregon State 42-21, Fresno State 37-35 and Washington State 42-14. The only losses since September were by two at San Diego State and by one at Boise State, a 46-45 shootout.

Jackson has thrown for 2,624 yards and 23 touchdowns with only 6 interceptions. Torrance Burgess Jr. has 1,371 rushing yards and ran for 146 against Washington State. Chris Dawn Jr. leads the receivers with 649 yards. Jayden Bradley has 69 tackles from the secondary.

Hyrum's Aggies have had a harder fall. Utah State is 4-7 and out of the bowl picture after a 31-24 loss at Oregon State last week. McCae Hillstead has taken over at quarterback the last four games, and he threw for 277 yards in Corvallis. Grady Brosterhous started most of the year before that. Eli Wood has 479 receiving yards and 5 touchdowns. Brevin Hamblin leads the defense with 84 tackles.

"We don't have a bowl," Hyrum says. "We have this game. That's enough. Beat the team that beat us. Then go home and milk the cows."

"The cows can wait," Yesenia says.

"The cows cannot wait," Hyrum says. "You have never met a cow."

Here's how I see it. Maverik Stadium is cold and loud for the last home game. Utah State's defense starts the way it did in San Marcos and holds Texas State to a field goal on the first drive. Hillstead hits Wood for a touchdown, and the Aggies lead early. But Burgess starts running through tackles in the second quarter, and Jackson finds Dawn for a score before halftime. Texas State pulls ahead in the third quarter and adds a late touchdown. Utah State scores again, but it doesn't get close enough to need a kick.

Hyrum says that's the best part, because nobody had to try a forty-eight-yarder. Yesenia zips her coat all the way up and says she's flying home to San Marcos before her toes stop speaking to her.

My call: Texas State Bobcats 31, Utah State Aggies 22.
