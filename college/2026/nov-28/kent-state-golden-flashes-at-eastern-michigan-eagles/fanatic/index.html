Kent State vs Eastern Michigan: 734 Night Senior Sendoff

Kent State Golden Flashes (3-8) at Eastern Michigan Eagles (4-7)

7:00 PM ET Tue Nov 24 · Rynearson Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

Kent State Golden Flashes vs Eastern Michigan Eagles is a Tuesday-night goodbye in Ypsilanti, where Eastern Michigan is calling it 734 Night: seven-dollar seats inside "The Factory" at Rynearson Stadium and 30 members of the program walking onto Crosby Field for Senior Night before the 7 p.m. kickoff. Imogen Tremblay plans to clap for every single name, then spend four quarters making sure Ozzie Brannigan drives home to Kent with nothing but a ticket stub and a long face.

Neither of these teams is headed to a bowl game, and nobody in either section is pretending otherwise. This one is about the last walk off the field, and which group of seniors gets to make it smiling.

"Seven bucks," Imogen says, waving her ticket like it's a winning raffle stub. "Seven bucks to watch the Eagles beat Kent State for the fourth straight time. They should charge twenty. I'd still come."

"Fourth? You've won three," Ozzie says.

"I'm counting Tuesday early. I'm efficient."

She has a point about the recent history, and Ozzie knows it. Eastern beat Kent State 31-24 in 2022, 28-14 in 2023 and 52-33 in 2024. Kent State still holds the overall series, 19-17, per Eastern's own game notes, and Ozzie brings that up the way some people bring up a grandfather who fought in a war.

"Nineteen to seventeen," he says. "Look it up. It's in your own handout."

There is a quiet part of the night, too, and Imogen doesn't joke about it. Noah Kim is on that senior list. He hasn't played since the Toledo game in October, and coach Chris Creighton told reporters this week he doesn't think it's likely Kim plays Tuesday. Kim has been a captain, a leader and a big reason Eastern started 3-0 in the MAC.

"Whatever he's wearing, pads or a parka, he gets the loudest noise of the night," Imogen says. "Then Brogan McCaughey goes out and plays like he's been here five years."

McCaughey has taken over, and he was sharp in Kalamazoo last week, 12 of 16 for 135 yards. But the guy Imogen really wants to talk about is Amareon Blue. Blue carried 27 times for 160 yards and three touchdowns against Western Michigan, and he looked like a guy who had decided he wasn't going down without company.

"He runs angry," Imogen says. "Angry and short. They can't find him behind the big guys until he's already past them."

Ozzie's answer is Wayne Harris. Harris has 863 receiving yards and seven touchdowns, and when Dru DeShields has time, Harris is the first place the ball goes. DeShields threw for 332 yards at Bowling Green and four touchdowns in the 35-20 win over Akron, a game Ozzie has watched more times than he will admit to his family.

"When Dru and Wayne get going, we're a different team," Ozzie says. "The trick is getting them going before halftime. We've been working on that trick since Labor Day."

The trouble is up front. Kent State has had a hard time moving the ball on the ground all year, and when the Flashes can't run, DeShields ends up throwing into crowds. Last week on their own Senior Night, Kent managed three points against Miami.

"Don't remind me," Ozzie says. "My seniors deserved better. My seniors deserve your seniors losing on their night, just to even it out."

"That's not how fairness works, Ozzie."

"It's how it works in Kent."

Eastern's defense has a couple of names Ozzie keeps an eye on. Quincy Byas leads the Eagles with five sacks, and Bryce Llewellyn, another senior being honored, leads the team in tackles. Linebacker Zach Mowchan, a captain, has 67 tackles of his own. On the other side, Jaihien Roy has six sacks for Kent State, and Hayden Dawahare leads the Flashes in stops.

Here's how Tuesday goes. Eastern hands the ball to Blue early and often, and the cheap seats get loud in a hurry. Kent State hangs around in the first half because Harris gets loose down the sideline once and DeShields finds him for a touchdown. But Byas gets home twice in the second half, the Eagles grind out the clock with Blue, and Llewellyn makes one last big tackle in front of the home crowd to end Kent's last real chance. The Flashes add a late score to give Ozzie something for the drive back.

When it's over, the seniors make one more lap toward the student section, and Imogen holds her seven-dollar stub up next to her face for a picture, the cheapest souvenir she's ever kept and the one she'll never throw out.

My call: Eastern Michigan Eagles 28, Kent State Golden Flashes 18.
