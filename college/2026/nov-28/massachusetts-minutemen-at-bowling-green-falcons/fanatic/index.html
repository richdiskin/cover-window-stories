UMass vs Bowling Green: Six Wins or Bust at Doyt Perry

Massachusetts Minutemen (5-6) at Bowling Green Falcons (3-8)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Doyt L. Perry Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

Darcy Fenwick tapes her old goalie stick before every home game, the same way she did before every game she ever played in, and she props it against the wall of the Slater Family Ice Arena on her way to Doyt Perry Stadium. Massachusetts Minutemen vs Bowling Green Falcons is the last game of the year for the Falcons and Senior Day in Bowling Green, and Darcy says the seniors deserve a ritual too, so she's making the walk with her stick taped up blue and orange.

Abner Kettleworth drove out from the Pioneer Valley, and he talks the way he drives: slow, careful and with plenty of pauses. "Darcy," he says. Then he waits. "We were up nineteen on Akron."

"I heard."

"Nineteen," Abner says again. Then he waits again. "Then we weren't."

That's the story of the UMass season in one game. The Minutemen opened 4-0, beating Rutgers on the road to start the year, and Abner says he enjoyed every minute of that September. Then October hit. Losses to Eastern Michigan, Miami (Ohio), Buffalo in overtime and UConn. A 31-30 overtime win over Ball State. A shutout at Toledo. And then the Akron game last Wednesday night in Amherst, where UMass led by nineteen in the second quarter and lost 34-33.

Joe Harasymiak didn't sugarcoat it. He called it disgusting and said it was his fault. Abner says he appreciated that. "Man stood up there and said it. I like a man who says it."

Now the math is simple. UMass is 5-6. A win at Bowling Green gets the Minutemen to six. Six wins would be the first shot at a bowl game for UMass since it moved up to the FBS, and even then the Minutemen would need an opening.

"We're not the ones who decide," Abner says. "We're the ones who show up. So we show up."

Logan Inagawa took the throws the last two games and threw 3 touchdowns against Akron. William Watson III has 1,349 yards and 9 touchdown throws this year. Elijah Faulkner ran for 105 yards against Akron and leads the team with 508. Timmy Hinspeter has 98 tackles, and William DePaepe has 4 sacks.

Darcy's Falcons are 3-8, but she'll tell you they're 3-4 in the conference, and she'll also tell you what happened last year. Bowling Green 45, UMass 14. Abner doesn't remember it. Darcy remembers all of it.

"And we've got Myles Bradley," Darcy says. "Sixteen sacks. Sixteen. He lives in the other team's backfield. He's got a mailbox back there."

Bradley is the reason anyone in Bowling Green feels good. Dorian Pringle has 105 tackles behind him. The offense is the problem. The Falcons got shut out 19-0 at Toledo last week. Austin Novosad has taken over at quarterback the last two games, and he threw 3 touchdowns in a 33-28 win over Kent State. Jeremiah Scoby, the tight end, leads the team with 37 catches.

"Novosad gives us a chance," Darcy says. "Bradley gives us a better one."

Here's how I see it. Bowling Green honors its seniors, the crowd is loud, and Bradley gets home early on Inagawa. The Falcons go up by ten in the second quarter. But UMass doesn't fold the way it did against Akron. Faulkner runs it in, Inagawa hits a big throw in the fourth, and the Minutemen tie it late. Then Bowling Green drives into range, and the Falcons win it with a field goal.

Abner takes a long time to say anything after the kick. Darcy waits him out. Finally he says he'll be checking the bowl lineup in the truck all the way home. Darcy grabs her stick at the ice arena and says the seniors went out the right way.

My call: Bowling Green Falcons 24, Massachusetts Minutemen 22.
