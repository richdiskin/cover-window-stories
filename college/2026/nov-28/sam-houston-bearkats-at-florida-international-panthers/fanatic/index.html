Sam Houston Bearkats vs FIU Panthers: Wear White for Senior Day

Sam Houston Bearkats (1-10) at Florida International Panthers (4-7)

2:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Pitbull Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

FIU has asked everybody at Pitbull Stadium to wear white on Saturday, and Lourdes Ibarguen-Tate has already ironed two shirts, one for her and one for her abuela, who has been to more Panther games than most of the roster. Sam Houston Bearkats vs FIU Panthers is Senior Day and Fan Appreciation Day at the last home game of FIU's 25th season of football, and Lem Coxwell is flying in from Huntsville in an orange cowboy hat that will stand out in a sea of white like a campfire in the dark.

"Twenty-five years," Lourdes says. "The first FIU team took the field in 2002. My abuela was there. She says she knew we'd make it."

"Your abuela says a lot of things," Lem says.

"She's been right about most of them."

There's a real sense of history around this game in Miami. FIU has played in six bowl games in its 25 seasons, won six Shula Bowls, and sent guys like T.Y. Hilton and Jonnu Smith to the NFL. Last year, in Willie Simmons' first season, the Panthers went to their first bowl since 2019. This year has been harder. FIU is 4-7, and Saturday is the last game for a senior class that helped make last season happen.

"That's why we wear white," Lourdes says. "Clean slate. One more for the seniors, then we start building again."

Lem settles in, the way he always does, with a campfire story. His is about a season that never stopped being close. Sam Houston is 1-10, but the Bearkats lost to Troy by three to open the year, to Tulsa by six, to New Mexico State by six, and to Middle Tennessee by three last Saturday. Their only win was a 59-0 blowout of Nicholls back in September.

"We've been on the doorstep all year," Lem says. "We've knocked. We've rung the bell. We've sat on the porch. Nobody's let us in."

Last week's loss had a new face in it. Mason Holtzclaw took most of the throws against Middle Tennessee and threw three touchdowns, after another quarterback handled the job for most of the season and piled up 1,869 yards and 14 touchdown throws. Chris Reed leads the receivers with 511 yards, and Grady O'Neill caught seven balls last week. On defense, Dean Ford has 63 tackles and Josh Alexander-Felton has 5 sacks.

"Whoever's throwing it, he's got Chris Reed," Lem says. "That boy catches the ball like it owes him money. Bigger than the Huntsville statue."

"In Miami, we'd call him a very nice receiver for a team that's 1-10," Lourdes says, and hands him a pastelito so he can't argue.

The Panthers have their own guys to brag about. JJ Kohl has 2,579 yards and 14 touchdowns, and he threw for 367 against Buffalo back in September. Maguire Anderson is his go-to target with 803 receiving yards. Devonte Lyons has 760 yards on the ground. The defense has carried FIU for long stretches this year: DeeJay Holmes Jr. has 7 sacks, and DeAndre Boykins leads the team in tackles. Last year, FIU beat Sam Houston 56-16.

Lourdes hands out a dominoes nickname for the visitors' offense. "The double-blank," she says. "It's in the box. Nobody wants to play it."

"Now that's just mean," Lem says, grinning.

"It's dominoes. Dominoes is mean."

Here's how Saturday goes. The seniors walk out to a white-shirted crowd, and the FIU defense sets the tone on the first series with a sack from Holmes. Sam Houston moves the ball in spurts and gets a touchdown in the first half to keep Lem upright. Then Lyons starts finding room, Kohl hits Anderson twice on third down, and the Panthers pull away in the third quarter. The Bearkats score once more late, Lem stands up and waves his hat, and the seniors get the sendoff they wanted.

Lourdes heads for the tunnel to find her abuela, who will tell everybody she called it in 2002. Lem pulls his hat low and starts working on next year's campfire story on the walk to the parking lot.

My call: Florida International Panthers 27, Sam Houston Bearkats 13.
