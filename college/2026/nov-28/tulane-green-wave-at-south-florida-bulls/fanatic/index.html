Tulane vs South Florida: Last Dance at Ray-Jay

Tulane Green Wave (6-5) at South Florida Bulls (6-5)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Raymond James Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

South Florida Bulls vs Tulane Green Wave is goodbye to Raymond James Stadium, and Reina Valdés-Kowalski has her speech ready. The Bulls first took the field in 1997. This is season 30. And on Saturday, they play their final scheduled game at Ray-Jay before they move into the new on-campus stadium next fall. Reina wants this one to feel like a party, not a funeral.

"Thirty years," Reina says. "Thirty years of driving to that stadium and fighting for parking. Now we'll have our own place on campus. But I'm not sad. Not yet. Ask me at the end of the fourth quarter."

The school is making it a Senior Day and a celebration of the Bulls' history in that building. Reina plans to cry once, during the senior walk, and then she plans to yell for three hours straight.

Antoine "Twan" Ducros-Labat drove in from the Big Easy, and he's here to crash the party. His Green Wave are 6-5 and bowl eligible for the fifth straight season, and they're coming off two straight fourth-quarter comebacks.

"We don't do anything easy," Twan says. "We wait until you think you've got us, and then we take it. Two weeks in a row."

He's not wrong. Tulane beat Rice 20-17, and then last week the Green Wave trailed North Texas 24-14 with under eight minutes left. Trace Johnson threw two touchdowns late, including a 28-yarder to Cameron Roberts with 2:13 left, and E'Zaiah Shine picked one off on the last play. Johnson threw for 283 yards and four touchdowns.

"Trace was a different guy last week," Twan says. "He looked like he finally figured it out."

Reina has had a rough month. The Bulls started 4-0 in Brian Hartline's first year as head coach, and everyone in Tampa was happy. Now they've lost three straight. They lost 20-17 at East Carolina, then 52-41 to Memphis, and then last week came the FAU game. The Bulls lost 35-14 in Boca in a game that lasted six hours and got stopped three times for weather, including a tornado warning.

"A tornado," Reina says. "A real one. On video. I've been to a lot of bad games, but that's the first one where the sky was against us too."

She does like her quarterback. Luke Kromenhoek threw for 385 yards against FAU, and his interception that day was his first of the season. D.J. Crowther has 689 rushing yards and seven touchdowns, and against Memphis, the Bulls ran for 268 yards as a team.

"When we run the ball, we're good," Reina says. "When we forget to run the ball, we lose to FAU in a tornado. It's simple."

Tulane's defense has had some bad days, too. The Green Wave gave up 60 to Tulsa at home and 42 to UTSA, and North Texas ran all over them last week. Dickson Agu, though, is a tackling machine. He has 109 tackles, and he had 13 against North Texas.

"Agu's everywhere," Twan says. "If your running back goes anywhere, Agu's already there, waiting."

Tulane leads the series 4-1, and the Bulls' only win came in 2017. Twan brings that up three times before lunch.

"You've beaten us one time," he says. "One."

"And Saturday, it's two," Reina says. "Write it on the stadium. It's the last thing we'll ever write there."

Here's how it goes. The crowd is loud early, and the Bulls come out running. Crowther breaks one for a touchdown in the first quarter, and Kromenhoek hits a long one before halftime. Tulane hangs around with a couple of Johnson throws, and Twan starts getting that comeback look in his eye. But the Bulls' defense finally holds late, and South Florida adds a field goal to put it away.

When it's over, Reina takes a picture of the scoreboard and a picture of the field. Twan offers to take one of her with both. She says she'll frame it and hang it in the new place.

My call: South Florida Bulls 28, Tulane Green Wave 19.
