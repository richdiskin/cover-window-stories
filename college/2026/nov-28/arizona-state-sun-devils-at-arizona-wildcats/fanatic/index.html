Arizona State Sun Devils vs Arizona Wildcats: The 100th Territorial Cup

Arizona State Sun Devils (6-5) at Arizona Wildcats (8-3)

9:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Casino Del Sol Stadium · Fox

XFacebook

Arizona and Arizona State have played ninety-nine times, and on Saturday night at Casino Del Sol Stadium they play the 100th Territorial Cup, with Arizona leading the all-time series 52-46-1. Kickoff for Arizona State Sun Devils vs Arizona Wildcats is 7 p.m. in Tucson on Fox, and Inez Paloverde has staked out a spot with a cooler of prickly-pear lemonade and a hand-lettered sign that just says "52."

Skylar Vanterpool drove down from Tempe and read the sign out loud.

"Fifty-two. Wow. You know there's a forty-six on the other side of that."

"I know. That's why it's on my sign and not yours."

"I'm bringing a sign next year. It's going to say forty-seven."

"That's a sign about losing, Skylar."

"It's a sign about progress."

Inez has a good reason to be feeling it. Arizona is 8-3 and ranked No. 19, and last week the Wildcats won 35-28 in overtime at Kansas State, in the rain, with Noah Fifita throwing for 323 yards and running in a touchdown. Fifita has 3,473 passing yards and 26 touchdowns against six interceptions this year, and Isaiah Mizell has 990 receiving yards and 10 scores. This will be Arizona's 11th nationally televised game this season.

"Eleven times on national television," Inez says. "People in Ohio know who Fifita is now. My aunt in Ohio called me about it."

"Your aunt in Ohio doesn't watch football."

"She does now."

Skylar's Sun Devils are 6-5 after a 30-27 overtime loss to Oklahoma State, and he spent the drive down talking himself out of being upset about it. Jake Fette has taken over at quarterback, and Reed Harris caught 11 balls for 172 yards and two touchdowns in that game. Harris has 1,159 receiving yards and 11 touchdowns this season, and Skylar calls him "the best receiver in this state, and I'll say it in your parking lot."

"You just did," Inez says.

"And I'll say it again at halftime. Louder. Right next to your lemonade."

The Arizona State defense is the part Skylar feels best about. Owen Long has 110 tackles, Jalen Thompson has eight sacks, and the Sun Devils held Colorado to 10 points and UCF to 13 earlier this month. Arizona's defense has Chase Kennedy, with 6.5 sacks, and Taye Brown, with 73 tackles.

"Your defense is good," Inez admits. "Your offense has had three quarterbacks."

"Three quarterbacks means depth."

"Three quarterbacks means nobody knows who's starting."

The last two meetings went 49-7 to Arizona State and 23-7 to Arizona, and both fans have chosen to remember only one of them. Inez likes the most recent one. Skylar likes the one with more points.

Here's how Saturday goes. The stadium is packed for the hundredth meeting, the band is loud from warmups on, the student section is on its feet from the first snap, and Arizona gets the ball first. Fifita hits Mizell on a long one down the sideline, and the Wildcats are up early. Arizona State hangs in with Cardae Mack running hard and Harris making a big catch for a touchdown before the half. In the second half, Arizona's offense finds another gear, and Reescano punches in a short score. Fette keeps throwing, but Kennedy gets to him on a big third down, and Arizona pulls away late.

Inez will hold up her sign and cross out the 52, writing a 53 under it in marker. Skylar will ask if she has another marker, and she'll say no. He'll drive back up to Tempe with forty-seven already written on a napkin in his cup holder, ready for next year.

My call: Arizona Wildcats 34, Arizona State Sun Devils 20.
