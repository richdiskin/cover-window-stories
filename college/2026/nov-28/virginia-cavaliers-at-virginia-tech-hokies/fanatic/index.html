Virginia vs Virginia Tech: The Cup, Franklin's First, and a Long Drought

Virginia Cavaliers (6-5) at Virginia Tech Hokies (6-5)

3:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Lane Stadium · CW

XFacebook

Virginia Cavaliers vs Virginia Tech Hokies is the game Ruthanne Dellinger circles before the schedule even comes out, and this year she circled it twice. The last time Virginia won a football game at Lane Stadium was 1998. She was in grade school. She's now got kids of her own who've never seen it happen, and she would like to keep it that way.

"Twenty-eight years," Ruthanne says. "My son thinks it's a law. I haven't corrected him."

Prescott Ashby-Lowell drove in from Charlottesville with a thermos and the Commonwealth Cup on his mind. Virginia took it home last year, 27-7, and he says it looks good in Charlottesville. He's not ready to give it back.

"We had it last year," Prescott says. "We're keeping it. History is a nice thing to have when you don't have anything else."

That one stings, because this Hokies season has been hard. James Franklin came in with a lot of fanfare, and it started great. Virginia Tech beat VMI 73-3, won at Maryland and Boston College, and went into Clemson and won 20-16 to become bowl eligible in October. Then the wheels wobbled. The Hokies lost 51-28 at SMU, 40-22 at home to Stanford, and 28-20 at Miami last week. They've lost three straight.

"It's not about the record now," Ruthanne says. "It's about this one. You win this one, everybody forgets November."

Virginia's coming in the other way. The Cavaliers had a rough middle of the year, including a 51-7 loss at Wake Forest, but they've won two straight. They beat Cal 36-7 and then ran past North Carolina 41-24 last week to get to six wins. Beau Pribula threw for 297 yards and four touchdowns, and he's got 19 touchdowns and just three interceptions this season.

"Beau doesn't throw it to the other team," Prescott says. "Hasn't all year. Write that down."

"I'll write it on your car," Ruthanne says.

The guy Prescott can't stop talking about is Jekail Middlebrook. The running back caught five balls for 140 yards and a touchdown against North Carolina, and Prescott says that's what happens when you finally throw it to him.

Ruthanne has her own guys. Ethan Grunkemeyer has thrown for 2,869 yards and 21 touchdowns. Que'Sean Brown has 73 catches for 887 yards. Jeffrey Overton Jr. has 907 rushing yards, even if Miami held him to 38 last week. And Aycen Stevens has 8.5 sacks up front.

"Aycen's going to be in Pribula's ear all day," she says. "He'll hear him in his sleep."

Here's the thing about this game: neither team runs the ball well against the other. Virginia Tech's defense is tough to run on, and Virginia hasn't been great running it all year. Tech's ground game has been stuck for weeks. So this comes down to the quarterbacks, and two teams that are trying to get the bad taste out.

"Thanksgiving weekend in Blacksburg," Ruthanne says. "Cold and loud. I want them to feel every bit of it."

"We'll feel it," Prescott says. "We'll feel it on the bus home, with the Cup."

Here's how it goes. It's tight and physical early, with a lot of punts and long third downs. Pribula hits Middlebrook out of the backfield for a long touchdown, and Grunkemeyer finds Brown to answer. Neither team leads by more than a touchdown all day. Virginia gets the ball back late with a chance, but Stevens gets home on a big third down, and Tech holds on by one. Lane Stadium shakes for the first time in a month.

Ruthanne looks for Prescott in the parking lot afterward and finds him sitting on his tailgate with the thermos. She tells him the drought is safe for another year. He tells her the next one is in Charlottesville, and he'll be waiting.

My call: Virginia Tech Hokies 26, Virginia Cavaliers 25.
