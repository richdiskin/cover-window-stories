Purdue Boilermakers vs Indiana Hoosiers: The Bucket Stays Home

Purdue Boilermakers (3-8) at Indiana Hoosiers (9-2)

7:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Memorial Stadium (Bloomington, IN) · FS1

XFacebook

For the first 125 games of a series that goes back to 1891, Indiana and Purdue never played each other at night. Then came 2024, and now, for the third straight year, the Old Oaken Bucket game is under the lights, with Purdue Boilermakers vs Indiana Hoosiers kicking off at 7 p.m. Saturday at Memorial Stadium in Bloomington. Mallory Penhallow is in the parking lot with a garment bag, a folding table and a printed spreadsheet she calls "the Bucket plan."

"What's in the garment bag?" asks Barnaby Wolcott-Reese, who drove down from West Lafayette and parked next to her on purpose.

"The jersey I wear when we carry the Bucket off the field."

"You have a special jersey for carrying a bucket."

"I have a special jersey for winning. The Bucket is just where it ends up."

Barnaby has heard this before. Indiana has held the Bucket since 2024, and the last two games ended 66-0 and 56-3. That's 122 points to three, and Mallory has it on her spreadsheet in bold, right under a row labeled "Bucket polish schedule."

"You put that in bold?"

"I put everything in bold, Barnaby. It's a celebration."

Purdue is 3-8, and Barnaby isn't going to pretend otherwise. The Boilermakers have lost five in a row, including 42-10 to Wisconsin last week on their own field. Ryan Browne has 2,551 passing yards, Fame Ijeboi has 724 rushing yards and 11 touchdowns, and Asaad Waseem leads the team with 816 receiving yards. Charles Correa has 83 tackles. But there were good days early, like a 41-13 win over Minnesota and a 24-17 win at Illinois.

"We beat Illinois," Barnaby says. "On the road. Write that in bold."

"I'll write it in a footnote."

Mallory's Hoosiers are 9-2, and they lost 27-24 in overtime at Washington last week, which she describes as "a scheduling error." Indiana's coach, Curt Cignetti, said afterward that the team was down five or six starters. Josh Hoover came off the bench to lead a touchdown drive in the fourth quarter, and Charlie Becker caught six balls for 167 yards, including a 74-yard score.

"Becker's got 1,117 yards," Mallory says. "Eleven touchdowns. He's going to catch two more on Saturday and I'm going to cry both times."

"You cry at kickoffs."

"I cry at the national anthem, Barnaby. I'm an emotional person."

The Indiana defense is the real reason Mallory brought the garment bag. Tobi Osunsanmi has 10 sacks, Isaiah Jones has 77 tackles, and the Hoosiers give up 13.8 points a game. Western Kentucky and Howard didn't score in Bloomington. Purdue has scored three points in the last two meetings combined.

"Three," Barnaby says. "We kicked a field goal. That's a start."

"That's not a start. That's a souvenir."

Here's how Saturday goes. The lights come up, the student section is loud before the team even gets out of the tunnel, and Indiana gets the ball first. Lee Beebe Jr. runs it straight at the Purdue front, and Becker gets loose for the first touchdown. Purdue answers with a field goal, and Barnaby high-fives three strangers. Then Osunsanmi gets to Browne on third down, Indiana scores again before halftime, and the second half turns into a long walk for the visitors. Purdue gets a late touchdown on an Ijeboi run, which is more than the last two years combined.

Mallory will carry the Bucket out of the stadium in her special jersey. Barnaby will ask to take a picture with it, just once, so he can remember what it looks like up close. Then he'll drive back to West Lafayette and start counting the days until next November.

My call: Indiana Hoosiers 38, Purdue Boilermakers 13.
