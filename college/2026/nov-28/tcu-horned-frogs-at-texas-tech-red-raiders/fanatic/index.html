TCU Horned Frogs vs Texas Tech Red Raiders: Senior Night Under New Management

TCU Horned Frogs (5-6) at Texas Tech Red Raiders (10-1)

8:00 PM ET Thu Nov 26 · Galaxy Stadium · ESPN

XFacebook

Debbie McGuire will stand on the Galaxy Stadium grass Thursday night next to her husband and a long row of Red Raider families, and Bodie Ratliff plans to be in his seat early enough to clap for every senior by name. Ryder Galloway drove up from Fort Worth for TCU Horned Frogs vs Texas Tech Red Raiders with a team that changed head coaches on Sunday, and he says he'll clap for the seniors too, right up until the opening kickoff.

"Thanksgiving night in Lubbock," Bodie says. "Senior Day. Ten wins already. My mama's bringing two pies and a folding chair she does not need, because nobody sits down in this building."

"Nobody sits down because the wind would take the chair," Ryder says.

Bodie lets that one go. Anybody raised on the South Plains has chased a hat or two across a parking lot.

The Red Raiders have earned the swagger. Texas Tech is 10-1, unbeaten at home, and the only loss came at Cincinnati back in October. Since Thomas Castellanos took over the throwing, the offense has turned every Saturday into a track meet. He threw for 362 yards and five touchdowns in the 53-48 shootout with West Virginia, then 263 yards and four more at Oklahoma State. Behind him, J'Koby Williams has run for 1,195 yards and 9 touchdowns, and he went for 117 in the 20-10 win at Baylor last week.

"You see what Castellanos does on a third down?" Bodie asks. "He looks at the defense like it owes him money."

Ryder knows what Castellanos does. He watched it on a small screen in a Fort Worth kitchen and didn't enjoy one minute of it.

The week in Fort Worth has been something else. TCU fired Sonny Dykes on Sunday, four years after he took the Frogs to the national title game, and handed the team to defensive coordinator Andy Avalos for the finale. The Frogs are 5-6. One more win means a bowl trip. One more loss means the offseason starts Friday morning.

"So here's the part you're missing," Ryder says. "Avalos runs the defense. The defense is the reason we're still standing. We've given up 17.3 a game. We lost to North Carolina by five, UCF by eight, BYU by seven, West Virginia by four and Arizona by one. Every one of those came down to a drive."

"And Utah?"

"We don't talk about Utah."

That was a 38-17 loss at home Saturday, and it's the one game all season the TCU defense didn't hang around. The other question is who's throwing. Jaden Craig handled the job for most of the year and has 2,664 yards and 13 touchdowns, with Kari Ashley as his go-to tight end at 697 yards. Against Utah, Adam Schobel took the throws. Ryder figures whoever takes the snaps needs to hand it to Jeremy Payne, who ran for 108 yards and two scores even in the blowout, and get out of the way.

"Keep it on the ground," Ryder says. "Shorten the game. Make Castellanos sit on the bench and think about it."

On defense, the Frogs need Koron Hayward, who has 7 sacks, and Hudson Hooper, who leads the team with 79 tackles, to make Williams work for every yard. Bodie's counter is A.J. Holmes Jr., who has 10 sacks and has spent the fall living in other people's backfields, and Austin Romaine, who has 95 tackles and seems to show up on every snap.

"Ryder, I like you," Bodie says. "I want you to know that before the third quarter."

Here's how I see the night. TCU's defense comes out angry and plays for its interim coach like it means it, and the first half is closer than anybody in the south end zone wants. Payne breaks a long run, the Frogs kick a field goal, and the TCU section gets loud enough to hear from the press box. But Castellanos finds Kenny Johnson down the sideline before halftime, Williams starts leaning on a tired front in the third, and Holmes gets home on a third down that flips the field.

The Frogs make it a game into the fourth quarter. They just can't make it a win. The seniors walk off unbeaten at home, and Ryder drives back down to Fort Worth, waiting to hear who's coaching his team next.

Bodie walks him to the parking lot anyway, still holding one of his mama's pies, and hands it over without a word.

My call: Texas Tech Red Raiders 30, TCU Horned Frogs 18.
