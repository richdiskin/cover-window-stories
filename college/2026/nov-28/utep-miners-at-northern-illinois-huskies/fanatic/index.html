UTEP Miners vs Northern Illinois Huskies: One Win Left on the Table

UTEP Miners (1-10) at Northern Illinois Huskies (0-11)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Huskie Stadium · MW+

XFacebook

Somebody's leaving DeKalb on Saturday with a lot of losses and nothing to show for the season. Northern Illinois is 0-11 in its first year in the Mountain West, UTEP is 1-10, and both of them are 0-7 in league play, so when the Miners and the Huskies meet at Huskie Stadium in the season finale, one team finally gets a conference win and the other goes home with a goose egg next to its name.

Nola Brandvik has been looking at a cornfield photo on her phone for about a week.

"You plant in April," she says. "You do everything right. Then it doesn't rain. That's this season. But you still go out and harvest what's there. Saturday, there's something there."

Neto Galindo, who drove up from El Paso with a cooler full of green chile and no winter coat, has a different view of the forecast.

"It's going to be cold, I'm going to be miserable, and we're going to win," he says. "That's the most UTEP sentence I've ever said."

Let's be fair to both of them, because neither one of these seasons has been fun.

UTEP's only win came back in September, 51-10 over Texas Southern, when EJ Colson Jr. threw four touchdowns. Since then it's been hard. The Miners lost 61-7 at New Mexico and 52-3 at Air Force last Friday, and they've scored 17 or fewer points in all nine games since the Texas Southern win. Raymond Moore III took over at quarterback in October and has given them a pulse, mostly with his legs. He leads the team with 678 rushing yards, and on the season he has more yards running than most of the Miners' receivers have catching.

"Raymond Moore runs like a jalapeño," Neto says. "Not the hottest, but it gets you every time."

The closest UTEP came was a 17-14 loss to Hawai'i at home in early November. Neto rates that game a ghost pepper. He rates the Air Force game "a bell pepper. No heat. Just sad."

Northern Illinois has been closer more often, which is what makes 0-11 hurt so much. The Huskies lost 28-24 to Illinois State in the home opener. They lost 31-24 at UNLV. They went to Fargo last week and came storming back in the second half at North Dakota State before falling 31-25. Taron Dickens threw three touchdowns in that one, and DeAree Rogers caught six balls for 116 yards.

"That's the one that kept me up," Nola says. "We were right there. A bushel short."

Dickens has thrown for 2,330 yards and 13 touchdowns this year. Rogers has 86 catches for 899 yards and is one big afternoon away from four figures. Telly Johnson Jr. has 721 rushing yards, and Elijah Porter ran for 107 at North Dakota State. Jack Murray leads the defense with 84 tackles. On paper, this is the better offense on the field, and by a good bit.

The problem is that both defenses have been getting run over. Northern Illinois gives up 35.5 a game, and UTEP gives up 35.3. Hawai'i put 56 on the Huskies in October. If you're looking for a game where somebody gets stuffed on fourth-and-goal to save the day, this probably isn't it.

These two don't have any recent history, so this is a new rivalry built entirely on misery. Neto says that's the best kind. Nola says the best kind is the one you win.

Here's the thing about Senior Day for a team that hasn't won. Those seniors at Northern Illinois have been through a move to a new conference, and a season where they lost close and lost big. Saturday is their last chance to walk off that field in DeKalb with a win. That's a lot of motivation for a group of guys who've heard nothing good since Labor Day.

UTEP has motivation too. Nobody wants to fly home 1-11. But the Miners have been held to a field goal in their last game, and it's hard to see the offense suddenly catching fire in the cold in northern Illinois.

Here's how I see it. Dickens finds Rogers early, and the Huskies get an early lead for once. Moore makes some plays with his legs and keeps UTEP in it for a half. Then the Huskies' offense grinds them down, the seniors get their win, and the Huskie Stadium crowd gets to celebrate something.

Nola will put the cornfield photo away and say, "Knee-high by the Fourth of July," which is a very big deal for her. Neto will eat green chile in the parking lot and tell everybody it was cold.

My call: Northern Illinois Huskies 30, UTEP Miners 21.
