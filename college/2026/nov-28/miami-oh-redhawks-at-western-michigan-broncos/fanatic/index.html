Miami (OH) RedHawks vs Western Michigan Broncos: Free Seats at Waldo

Miami (OH) RedHawks (7-4) at Western Michigan Broncos (8-3)

7:00 PM ET Tue Nov 24 · Waldo Stadium · ESPN2

XFacebook

Nobody in Kalamazoo has an excuse to skip Tuesday night, because Western Michigan is giving away tickets to the last home game of the year at Waldo Stadium. Miami (OH) RedHawks vs Western Michigan Broncos is a rematch of last December's MAC title game, and Corwin Dahlquist has spent the week texting the free-ticket link to guys he hasn't talked to since high school while Cordelia Van Doren packs a red scarf and a grudge in Oxford.

"Free," Corwin says. "Greenleaf Trust, Greenleaf Hospitality, Ziegler Auto Group. I want to shake every one of their hands. I want Waldo so loud your freshman hears it through his helmet."

"Pack it," Cordelia says. "We like an audience."

Here's what the free seats get you. Jalen Buckley, a redshirt senior who just moved into third in program history with 40 rushing touchdowns, took the first snap against Eastern Michigan last week and went 72 yards to the end zone. It was the third game in a row a Bronco back broke a run of at least 72 yards, after Cole Cabana went 82 against Akron and again against Bowling Green. Buckley finished with 140 yards and two scores, and Western Michigan beat Eastern 34-27 to keep the Michigan MAC Trophy for a fourth straight season.

"Fourth straight," Corwin says. "Central, Eastern, sit down. That's a full stack, and I'm pouring the syrup."

"You won a trophy for beating two schools from your own state," Cordelia says. "Congratulations. We own the series. Thirty-nine wins to your twenty-five, with a tie in there to keep it polite."

She's right about the history, and Corwin knows it. He also knows what happened the last time these two met: Western Michigan 23, Miami 13, in Detroit, with a championship on the table. That's the one Cordelia doesn't bring up at Thanksgiving.

Miami shows up with plenty at stake. The RedHawks are 7-4 and 5-2 in the league, and they need a win in Kalamazoo plus some tiebreaker help elsewhere to get back to Detroit for a fourth trip in a row. They're doing it on their third quarterback of the season. Drew Bellisari is a true freshman who stepped in against Ohio and then ran the show at Kent State, a 20-3 win where Miami handed the ball to D'Shawntae Jones 29 times for 121 yards and two touchdowns.

"Miami is the Cradle of Coaches," Cordelia says. "We have been teaching young men how to win in November since before your school had a paper engineering program. Our freshman will be fine."

Here's the part that keeps Corwin up at night. Miami's defense stops the run better than anybody in the MAC, giving up 94.8 yards a game, 13th in the country. Malcolm McCain has 90 tackles. Grant Lyons has 7 sacks. Amariyun Knighten has three interceptions and 14 breakups, second in the nation in passes defended. And then there's Scoop Smith, a freshman receiver who has already taken a punt and a kickoff back for touchdowns.

"Scoop Smith scares me," Corwin admits. "I want every kick out of bounds. I don't care if it costs us twenty yards."

His answer is the linebackers. Western Michigan's linebackers have five of the team's eight interceptions, and senior Dillon Moore has three of them, including one he ran back for a touchdown against Eastern Michigan. Justyce Betts leads the team with 69 tackles, and Scoop Gardner Jr. has 6 sacks coming off the end. A freshman quarterback who has to throw in the fourth quarter is exactly what that group has been waiting for all month.

Broc Lowry doesn't have to be a hero, either. He has 1,652 yards with 12 touchdown throws and 3 interceptions, and he went 15 for 15 at Akron two weeks ago. Lance Taylor is already the second coach in Western Michigan history with eight wins in back-to-back seasons, next to PJ Fleck. One more and he stands alone.

"Row the boat," Corwin says. "Then row it again."

"Fleck," Cordelia says, and smiles. "Another coach who left. You do produce them."

Here's how Tuesday goes. Miami's front keeps Buckley quiet for a half, Jones grinds the clock, and it's a one-score game at the break with Cordelia doing tiebreaker math on a napkin. Then the Broncos wear the RedHawks down. Lowry finds Aveion Chenault on third down, Buckley breaks a long one in the third quarter, and Moore's group gets its shot at a freshman who has to throw. Miami scores late to make the bus ride quieter, not to change anything.

Corwin walks out holding a ticket stub that cost him nothing, and he's putting it in a frame anyway.

My call: Western Michigan Broncos 28, Miami (OH) RedHawks 19.
