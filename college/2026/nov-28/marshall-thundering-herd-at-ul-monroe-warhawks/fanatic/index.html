Marshall Thundering Herd vs UL Monroe Warhawks: Pocatello, Again

Marshall Thundering Herd (6-5) at UL Monroe Warhawks (2-9)

1:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Malone Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

The last time Marshall and the school in Monroe played for something big, it was 1987 in Pocatello, Idaho, and the Northeast Louisiana Indians beat the Herd 43-42 for the I-AA national championship. Marshall Thundering Herd vs UL Monroe Warhawks on Saturday is the Herd's first trip ever to Monroe, and Darnell Vidrine has been playing tapes of that title game on his overnight radio show all week while Mercy Holderby pretends she can't hear him.

"Forty-three to forty-two," Darnell says. "I was not born yet. I remember it anyway."

"You're welcome to remember it," Mercy says. "We remember 2024. Twenty-eight to twenty-three, in Huntington, on the way to a conference title."

That's the whole series, as far as the record books go: one wild title game when ULM still had another name, and one Marshall win at the FBS level two years ago. Saturday is the tiebreaker, and Darnell is treating it like a national holiday. His optimism counter, the one he resets to a hundred every week no matter what happened the week before, is at a hundred and ten.

"That's illegal," Mercy says.

"That's faith," Darnell says.

He needs it. UL Monroe is 2-9, and the two wins came on the road, 21-14 at Troy and 19-17 at Arkansas State. But last week the Warhawks went up to Boone and took App State to overtime before losing 17-16, and Austin Carlisle played the best game of his year. He threw for 167 yards and a touchdown and ran for another, and he leads the team with 524 rushing yards. Aidan Armenta has thrown for more yards this season, but Carlisle is the one who's been making things happen lately. Marcellus Jackson leads the receivers with 421 yards. Noah Flemmings has 95 tackles at linebacker, and Kevontay Wells has 4 sacks up front.

"Austin Carlisle runs like he's got somewhere to be," Darnell says. "Usually that place is the end zone. Sometimes it's the sideline. I love him either way."

Mercy is not here to make friends. Marshall is 6-5 and already has its six wins, and the Herd just beat Georgia State 28-27 in a game that came down to the wire. Carlos Del Rio-Wilson threw three touchdowns in that one, including a 94-yarder to Chris Culliver on Culliver's only catch of the day. That's the kind of thing that happens when the Herd lets it fly. Del Rio-Wilson threw for 413 yards against Gardner-Webb back in September, so he can do it.

"We are," Mercy says, before anybody even asks how she's doing.

Jo'Shon Barbie leads the backs with 482 yards, Xayvion Turner-Bradshaw leads the receivers, and Caleb Clark-Glover has 82 tackles from the secondary. Kenyatta Hardge has 3.5 sacks.

Neither defense is going to win any awards. Marshall gives up 30.4 points a game, ULM gives up 33.8, and both of them have had days they'd rather forget. The Herd lost 45-0 at Penn State in the opener and 49-13 at Georgia Southern. ULM lost 62-13 at Mississippi State and 52-35 at South Alabama.

"So it's going to be a shootout," Darnell says.

"It's going to be a Marshall shootout," Mercy says. "We'll let you shoot. We'll just shoot more."

Here's how Saturday goes. Monroe comes out loud for a 2-9 team, because it's the last home game and there's nothing else on the schedule. Carlisle runs for a long one on the opening drive and Darnell is out of his seat. Marshall answers through the air, Del Rio-Wilson finds Turner-Bradshaw twice, and the game turns into the kind of back-and-forth afternoon both defenses would rather not watch on film. The Herd gets a late stop, Barbie grinds out a few first downs, and Marshall leaves Louisiana with a seventh win and the series lead.

Mercy ties a fresh green ribbon on her microphone in the parking lot. Darnell signs on for his overnight show, announces the score, resets the counter to a hundred, and plays the 1987 tape one more time anyway.

My call: Marshall Thundering Herd 29, UL Monroe Warhawks 24.
