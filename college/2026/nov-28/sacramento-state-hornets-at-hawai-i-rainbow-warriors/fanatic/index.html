Sacramento State vs Hawai'i: White Out for the Seniors

Sacramento State Hornets (2-9) at Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors (3-8)

11:00 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Clarence T.C. Ching Athletics Complex · MW+

XFacebook

Sacramento State Hornets vs Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors is Senior Night at the Ching Complex, and Kainoa Akana-Baird pulled his white shirt out of the closet on Tuesday. The school wants everybody in white for the seniors, and he takes that seriously. He also wants the seniors to walk off with a win, because this season has given them very little.

"Three and eight," Kainoa says. "That's not how you send anybody off. Give them one good night. That's all I'm asking."

Mateo Villaseñor flew in from Sacramento, and he's been on the beach for two days. He says he's here for football, but his sunburn says otherwise. His Hornets are 2-9 in their first season in the MAC, and they haven't played since November 11.

"Seventeen days off," Mateo says. "That's not rust. That's a vacation. Same as me."

The Hornets have had a long first year at this level. They lost by 46 at Fresno State and 41 to Ohio. They gave up 319 rushing yards at Central Michigan in their last game. They've started three different quarterbacks, and Brian Salazar Jr. got the last start. They did beat Kent State 31-10, and they lost to Toledo by just three.

"We're new," Mateo says. "You'd struggle too if you showed up to the big party and everybody already knew each other."

Kainoa's had his own rough fall. Hawai'i lost at Stanford, lost to UNLV at home, and lost six of its last eight games. The Warriors scored eight points at Nevada last week, and Micah Alejado threw for just 95 yards. The week before that, North Dakota State came to the Ching Complex and won 21-13.

"It's been a lot of close nights and not enough fun ones," Kainoa says. "I'm due a fun one."

Alejado has had better nights than last week. He has 2,815 yards on the year, and he threw for over 300 four times. He put up 323 yards and three touchdowns at Northern Illinois in a 56-21 win. Kainoa thinks that's the Alejado who shows up on Saturday.

"Micah throws it like he's never heard of a punt," Kainoa says. "I love that about him. Sometimes it scares me. Mostly I love it."

DeVon Rice has seven rushing touchdowns, and Kainoa expects him to get a lot of work against a Sacramento State defense that just let Central Michigan run wild.

Mateo has a guy he loves, too. Boogsie Silvera is a senior safety with 88 tackles, and he's had four games with at least ten. Matt Coleman has 545 receiving yards and six touchdowns. And the Hornets have a punter, Hamish Adams, who boomed one 62 yards at Central Michigan.

"Our punter's the best player on the field," Mateo says. "That's not a complaint. That's a compliment to the punter."

"Bring him," Kainoa says. "We'll let him punt all night."

Mateo laughs. Then he admits he isn't sure who's playing quarterback.

Here's how it goes. The stadium fills up in white, and the Warriors come out sharp. Alejado hits a couple of long throws early, and Rice punches one in for a touchdown. Sacramento State gets a field goal after a nice drive, and Mateo cheers like it's a touchdown. Hawai'i adds two more scores before halftime and pulls away. The Hornets get a late touchdown, and Mateo says it was worth the flight.

After the game, the seniors walk out onto the field, and the whole place stays to cheer them. Kainoa takes his white shirt off and waves it over his head. Mateo says he'll come back next year, if only for the beach.

My call: Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors 30, Sacramento State Hornets 13.
