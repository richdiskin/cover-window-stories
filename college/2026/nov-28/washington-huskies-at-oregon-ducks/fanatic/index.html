Washington vs Oregon: Senior Night at Autzen With Indianapolis in Sight

Washington Huskies (7-4) at Oregon Ducks (8-3)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Autzen Stadium · NBC

XFacebook

Vangie Tollefsrud rows on Lake Washington at five in the morning, every morning, and she says it's the only time of day in Seattle when nobody is talking about Oregon. Washington Huskies vs Oregon Ducks is Senior Day at Autzen Stadium, and Vangie is making the drive down Interstate 5 with her rowing gloves still in the back seat.

Nix Wyatt-Aldana rides a yellow scooter to Autzen on game days, and she parks it in the same spot every time. "Same spot, same helmet, same result," Nix says. "We've won four in a row. I'm not changing anything."

Nix has reasons to be superstitious. The Ducks were 4-3 after a 21-10 loss at Illinois in late October, and people in Eugene were starting to grumble. Then Oregon rattled off four straight. A 34-26 win over Northwestern. A 41-28 win at Ohio State. A 31-7 win over Michigan. And a 45-7 win at Michigan State last Friday night. Now a win over Washington likely sends the Ducks to Indianapolis for the Big Ten championship game.

"Indianapolis," Nix says. "I've already looked up the parking."

The Ducks have a quarterback playing his best football. Dylan Raiola has 16 touchdown throws and just 2 interceptions. He threw for 341 yards and 4 scores against Michigan. Dakorien Moore has 9 touchdown catches, Dierre Hill Jr. leads the team in rushing, and Brandon Smith ran for 121 yards and two scores at Michigan State. On defense, Matayo Uiagalelei has 9.5 sacks and Devon Jackson has 66 tackles.

Vangie listens to all of it, and then she brings up last Saturday.

"Indiana," she says. "The defending national champions. In overtime. In Husky Stadium. I don't need to say anything else."

She says plenty else anyway. Washington is 7-4, and the Huskies have been close in all four losses but one. They lost 27-24 to Minnesota, 25-21 at USC and 28-25 to Penn State. Demond Williams Jr. is back at quarterback, with 2,182 yards and 14 touchdowns. He ran for 88 yards and a score against Indiana. Chris Lawson has 61 catches for 910 yards, and Dezmen Roebuck caught 10 throws against the Hoosiers. Alex McLaughlin leads the defense with 78 tackles.

"We don't get blown out," Vangie says. "We get close. Sometimes we win."

Nix has an answer. "You got blown out here last time. Forty-nine to twenty-one."

This rivalry has been wild lately. Washington won three straight in 2022 and 2023, including two in the same season, the second one in the conference title game. Then Oregon won 49-21 in 2024. Vangie remembers the first three. Nix remembers the last one.

Then there's the quarterback picture, which has been a story in both towns. Oregon opened the year with Dante Moore, who threw for 378 yards against Boise State and 4 touchdowns against Portland State, before Raiola took over at USC in late September. Washington went to Elijah Brown for three games in October, and he threw for 302 yards at Purdue, before Williams got the job back.

"We've both changed quarterbacks," Vangie says. "At least ours came back."

Here's how I see it. Autzen is loud from the start, and Raiola hits Moore for a touchdown on the first drive. Washington answers with a Williams run, and the Huskies hang around through halftime. Then Uiagalelei gets to Williams twice in the third quarter, and Oregon scores on back-to-back drives. Smith breaks a long one, and the Ducks pull away. Washington adds a late score.

Nix rides her scooter home with the Indianapolis parking map open on her phone. Vangie drives back up I-5 and says she'll be on the lake at five in the morning, and that nobody on the water will be talking about Oregon.

My call: Oregon Ducks 33, Washington Huskies 18.
