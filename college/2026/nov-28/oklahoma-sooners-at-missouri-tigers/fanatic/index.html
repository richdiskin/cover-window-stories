Oklahoma Sooners vs Missouri Tigers: Senior Day on Faurot Field

Oklahoma Sooners (7-4) at Missouri Tigers (7-4)

4:15 PM ET Sat Nov 28 · Memorial Stadium · SEC Network

XFacebook

For the first time since 2013, not counting the 2020 season, Missouri isn't playing Arkansas to finish its regular season. Instead it gets Oklahoma, a ranked team on a three-game win streak, on Senior Day in Columbia, and Delphine Arceneaux-Moss has been treating the schedule change like a scoop for a full week.

She hits the ticker sound on her phone. "Breaking news. The season finale is not against Arkansas. Developing."

"It's been on the schedule since last year," Savannah Redbird-Lowe says.

"Still developing."

Savannah came up from Norman in a crimson jacket and the boots she wore when she rode in rodeos, and she starts the way she always does. "Boomer!"

She waits. Delphine sips her coffee.

"You're supposed to say Sooner."

"I'm a Missouri fan in Missouri, Savannah. I'm going to let that one sit."

Here's why both of them showed up early. Missouri and Oklahoma are both 7-4. The Tigers are 4-4 in the SEC, and a win would give them a winning conference record and move them up into fifth in the league. Oklahoma is 5-3 in conference and has won three straight, including a 28-7 win over Texas A&M in Norman last week. This is the last regular-season game for both teams, and it's going to feel like a bowl game in November.

Delphine's case starts with the building. Missouri is 5-1 at home this season. It beat Florida 45-17 and Texas A&M 27-6 there in October, and it beat Kentucky 28-14 last week. Austin Simmons has been terrific all year, with 2,693 yards, 25 touchdown throws and only 4 interceptions, and he went 17 for 22 for 314 yards and four scores against Kentucky. Jamal Roberts has carried it 251 times for 1,200 yards. Cayden Lee has 940 receiving yards, and Donovan Olugbode caught two touchdowns against the Wildcats.

"Breaking," Delphine says. "Austin Simmons doesn't throw it to the other team. More at eleven."

"Four interceptions," Savannah says. "I'll give him that."

Savannah's case starts with the defense. Oklahoma allows only 17.9 points per game. The Sooners held Florida to 7, Ole Miss to 17 and Texas A&M to 7 in the last three weeks, and Owen Heinecke has 90 tackles in the middle of all of it. David Stone has 4.5 sacks up front. John Mateer has 18 touchdown throws and threw three of them against the Aggies, and he ran one in too.

"Mateer reads a defense like a barrel pattern," Savannah says. "Tight around the first one, open it up on the second, and fly home on the third."

"Delphine has no idea what that means," Delphine says, about herself, in her news voice.

Missouri's worries are real, too. It lost at home to Texas, 34-20, and got beat 48-21 at Georgia and 31-3 at Ole Miss. When the Tigers have played the best defenses on their schedule, they've had trouble finishing drives. Daeden Hopkins has 9 sacks and will need a couple more, because Mateer is dangerous if he gets out of the pocket.

Oklahoma has its own holes. The Sooners lost 21-13 at home to Kentucky, the same Kentucky team Missouri just beat by two touchdowns, and they gave up 42 to South Carolina. Mateer threw three interceptions against the Wildcats. Lloyd Avant leads the team with only 469 rushing yards, so it's mostly on the quarterback.

The recent history is as even as it gets. Missouri won 30-23 at home in 2024, and Oklahoma won 17-6 in Norman last year. One each. Saturday breaks the tie.

Kickoff is 3:15 local on Faurot Field, and the Mizzou seniors will get their names called one last time before the game.

Here's how I see it. Oklahoma's defense keeps it tight for most of the afternoon, and Mateer makes a couple of plays that make the visiting section loud. But Simmons doesn't give the ball away, Roberts wears down the Sooners in the second half, and Missouri gets one more stop late with Hopkins coming around the corner.

Delphine will hit the ticker one more time. "Breaking news: we won." Savannah will put her chin up and say they'll roll the Schooner again next year.

My call: Missouri Tigers 23, Oklahoma Sooners 21.
