Minnesota Golden Gophers vs Wisconsin Badgers: The Axe Comes Back to Madison

Minnesota Golden Gophers (6-5) at Wisconsin Badgers (7-4)

7:30 PM ET Fri Nov 27 · Camp Randall Stadium · NBC

XFacebook

Two years in a row, Tor Engebretsen has watched Minnesota players run across the field with Paul Bunyan's Axe, and two years in a row Lars "Brat" Ellefson has gone home and stared at the wall. Minnesota Golden Gophers vs Wisconsin Badgers is back at Camp Randall on Black Friday night, and Lars has spent the whole Thanksgiving weekend telling his family the Axe is coming back to Madison.

"I said it at the table," Lars says. "Between the green beans and the pie. I said it twice so everybody would remember."

"Did anybody remember?" Tor asks.

"My uncle. He's been saying it since 2024."

Tor smiles. He's earned the right to smile. Minnesota won 24-7 in 2024 and took the Axe again last fall, and that's two straight years of Gophers celebrating with the Axe while Lars looked at his shoes. This year was supposed to be a third, and then the season got rough. Minnesota lost three in a row, to Indiana in overtime, to UCLA and at Penn State, and on Nov. 15 P.J. Fleck fired his offensive coordinator.

"Rough river," Tor says. "But we came out the other side."

The Gophers came out the other side by an inch. Northwestern tied the game with 27 seconds left last Saturday, and Drake Lindsey hit Roman Voss and Noah Jennings to set up Luke Drzewiecki from 44 yards. He'd already missed two in the first half on a windy day. He made the one that mattered, as time expired, and Minnesota was bowl eligible for the eighth time in Fleck's ten seasons.

"Same kick he made to beat Iowa," Tor says. "Same distance. The guy likes 44. I'd like him to make another one Friday."

Lars would like to talk about something else. He'd like to talk about Purdue.

Wisconsin went to West Lafayette last Saturday and ran for 381 yards. Colton Joseph was back at quarterback after Ryan Hopkins took the snaps against Rutgers and Maryland, and he ran for 187 yards and two touchdowns. He also caught a touchdown on a trick play from receiver Eugene Hilton Jr. Darrion Dupree ran for 125 yards and two scores. The Badgers won 42-10, and it was the 19th straight time they beat Purdue.

"It looked like the old days," Lars says. "Big guys up front, a quarterback who runs like a fullback when he feels like it, and the other team looking at the clock in the third quarter."

The defense is the bigger reason the Badgers are 7-4. Wisconsin has given up 16.5 points a game. Cooper Catalano has 103 tackles and Sebastian Cheeks has 7 sacks, and this group held Michigan State to 3, USC to 7 and Rutgers to 10. There's the Iowa game, a 30-0 loss nobody in Madison will discuss, and a 24-21 loss at Maryland where the offense went missing. But on most Saturdays, the defense has kept Wisconsin in it.

"Here's what I keep telling my uncle," Lars says. "Minnesota scored 14 at Penn State and 20 against UCLA. It's going to be cold, it's going to be a Friday night, and it's going to be our guys in the trenches. Nobody's scoring 30 on us in our building."

Tor has heard that one before, and it hasn't scared him. Lindsey has 2,206 yards and 15 touchdowns. Darius Taylor ran for 108 yards against Northwestern, his first 100-yard game this year, and he drags people for yards when it's cold. Maverick Baranowski has 90 tackles. TJ Bush Jr. has 6 sacks. And Eric Koehler, the tight ends coach who took over calling plays, just had a full week to plan for this one.

"Nobody expected us to win the last two either," Tor says. "That's the whole point of a rivalry."

Here's how I see the night. The first quarter is exactly what everybody expects in November in Madison, short drives and long punts. Then Joseph breaks one on a keeper and Dupree adds a short score before halftime, and Camp Randall finally gets loud enough to shake the press box. Minnesota answers with a Drzewiecki field goal and a late touchdown from Lindsey, but the Badgers keep running and the Gophers keep chasing. Catalano makes a fourth-down stop late to put it away.

Lars runs down to the front row to watch his team carry the Axe around Camp Randall. Tor stays in his seat for a while, then texts Lars's uncle, of all people, to tell him he was right.

My call: Wisconsin Badgers 28, Minnesota Golden Gophers 15.
