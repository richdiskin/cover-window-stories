Alabama Rolls Into Tiger Stadium, and LSU Has the Jambalaya Ready

Alabama Crimson Tide (6-2) at LSU Tigers (6-2)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Tiger Stadium (LA) · ABC

ShareXFacebook

Alabama Crimson Tide vs LSU Tigers prediction for Saturday, November 7: the Fan Zone outside the PMAC is full of kids for the Mike's Kids Club Tailgate, with food, games and STEM activities, and Remy-Jean Fontenot has a pot of jambalaya going big enough to feed every one of them.

Hollis Dunaway walked into her tailgate in crimson from head to toe.

"Roll Tide," he said, like a man saying good morning.

"Geaux Tigers," Remy-Jean said, like a woman saying get out. Then she handed him a bowl, because that is how it works down here.

Hollis is a retired band director from Tuscaloosa, and he hears football like music: tempo, rhythm, a big finish. Remy-Jean is a Baton Rouge lawyer, and she argues football like a closing statement. They have been doing this for years.

"Eight of the last ten," Hollis said. "We won 20-9 last year. We won 42-13 the year before that."

"And we won 46-41 in 2019," Remy-Jean said. "And 32-31 in 2022. In overtime. I was there. You were there. You cried."

Hollis did not deny it.

This year, both teams are 6-2, and both of them can score. Alabama puts up 38.2 points a game. LSU puts up 40.1. The difference is defense. LSU gives up 16.0 a game, the fewest in this matchup by a lot. Alabama gives up 21.2.

"Keelon Russell," Hollis said, counting it out on his fingers like a downbeat. "Two thousand fifty-seven yards. Fifteen touchdowns. Five interceptions. He threw for 365 against South Carolina and 311 against Texas A&M."

The Tide have only stumbled twice. They lost 17-20 to Georgia and 10-26 at Tennessee. Lotzeir Brooks has 488 receiving yards. Rico Scott caught 6 balls for 156 yards against the Aggies. Caleb Woodson has 63 tackles, and Yhonzae Pierre has 3.5 sacks.

Remy-Jean let him finish. Then she began her closing.

"Sam Leavitt," she said. "Two thousand one hundred seventy-three yards. Seventeen touchdowns. He went 32 for 45 for 388 yards at Auburn. Jackson Harris caught 9 for 178 that day. Dilin Jones has 657 rushing yards. And Princewill Umanmielen has 8.5 sacks."

"Eight and a half," Hollis said quietly.

"Eight and a half," she repeated, like a verdict.

LSU's two losses came on the road: 24-32 at Ole Miss and 34-35 in overtime at Kentucky. At home, the Tigers have been a wall. They beat Clemson 51-10, Texas A&M 35-6 and Mississippi State 31-0. Remy-Jean says Tiger Stadium does that to people.

Hollis says Alabama has been to Tiger Stadium before.

Here is how it shakes out. Russell comes out sharp, Brooks gets behind the defense once, and the Tide quiet the crowd for a few minutes. But Leavitt answers, Jones runs hard, and Umanmielen gets home at the worst possible moment for Alabama. It is a shootout into the fourth quarter, the kind both of them will talk about for years. Alabama has the ball late with a chance, and Hollis is on his feet. LSU makes one more stop.

Hollis will say the band was better. Remy-Jean will say that is not how scoring works.

Before any of that, she is taking her nieces to the Mike's Kids Club Tailgate in the Fan Zone. There is a meal package for kids, and there are STEM tables where you can build things and break things. Hollis says that sounds like an Alabama practice. Remy-Jean says he can come along if he keeps the crimson zipped under a jacket.

He agrees, mostly because the jambalaya is that good. By the time they reach the PMAC, he has helped two kids build a paper bridge and lost an argument with a little girl about who has the better mascot. Remy-Jean says that is the only argument he will lose all day. Hollis says the day is young.

My call: LSU Tigers 31, Alabama Crimson Tide 27.
