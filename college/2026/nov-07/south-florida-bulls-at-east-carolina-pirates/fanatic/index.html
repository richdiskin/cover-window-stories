South Florida Bulls at East Carolina Pirates

South Florida Bulls (6-2) at East Carolina Pirates (4-4)

7:30 PM ET Fri Nov 6 · Dowdy-Ficklen Stadium · ESPN2

ShareXFacebook

South Florida Bulls vs East Carolina Pirates prediction for Friday, November 6: downtown Greenville throws its First Friday ArtWalk that night, from 5 to 8, with a free Emerald Express trolley rolling past the galleries about every 20 minutes. Bart Quidley plans to ride it straight from the art to the stadium. Reina Valdés-Kowalski plans to ride it straight from the stadium back to the art, because she says that will be the only pretty thing the Pirates fans see all night.

Bart has tailgated in the same patch of Pitt County grass for so long his uncles mark it with a lawn chair in August. He meets Reina at the ECU Proctor Yongue House, where the porch show is called Pirates on the Porch: Wild Friends.

"Wild Friends," he says. "That's what we call visiting teams. We're wild and you're our friend until kickoff."

Reina laughs. She drove up from Tampa with sunscreen she will not need and a Bulls sweatshirt she will. "Bart, we're 6-2. We give up 14.5 points a game. We held Kent State to two points. Two. We beat UAB 30-10 last week. My defense doesn't make friends."

Bart has the memory card ready. "Three straight, Reina. Last three meetings. 44-24. 29-14. 48-28. We eat the Bulls."

That stings, and Reina knows the rest of the history too. South Florida won the four before that, including a 61-31 blowout in 2017. Across those seven meetings, South Florida has four wins and East Carolina has three.

"Old news," she says. "You want old news, go to the holiday show."

There is one, as it happens. The 25th annual Down East Holiday Show runs in Greenville that week, with more than 180 small businesses selling everything from candles to Christmas ornaments. Bart says the Pirates will be gift-wrapping a win for the home crowd. Reina says she will buy a souvenir to remember the night ECU fans went quiet.

East Carolina comes in at 4-4, and Bart admits it has been a bumpy ride. The Pirates lost two straight in overtime in October, 17-30 at UAB and 34-41 at Memphis, before beating Temple 17-9. TJ Engleman Jr. carried 16 times for 107 yards in that win and has 689 yards on the year. DJ Johnson Jr. leads the defense with 56 tackles.

"Engleman runs like a farm truck with no brakes," Bart says. "And when he gets going, the whole stadium gets going."

Reina is worried about her own offense, and she says so. Michael Van Buren Jr. has thrown for 1,273 yards, but Luke Kromenhoek ran the show against UAB, carrying 15 times for 92 yards. D.J. Crowther has 578 rushing yards and seven touchdowns, and C.J. Hicks has five sacks on defense.

"Whoever's throwing," she says, "we run it and we hit you. That's the South Florida way this year."

"Your way scored 13 against Temple," Bart says.

"And you scored 17 against Temple and needed a lawn chair to celebrate."

The quarterback in purple is the question for Bart. Mitch Griffis has 753 passing yards, four touchdown throws and five interceptions. Ray Ray Joseph Jr. leads the Pirates with 303 receiving yards. If Griffis gives Reina's defense extra chances, the Bulls do not need much offense.

Here is how it shakes out. Engleman makes the home crowd loud, and East Carolina has the lead late in a game that never gets loose. But the South Florida defense makes one more stop than the Pirates do, Crowther punches one in, and the Bulls sneak out of Greenville by a single point.

Reina rides the trolley back downtown to see the art. Bart rides it too, quietly, and says the paintings look better when you lose.

My call: South Florida Bulls 23, East Carolina Pirates 22.
