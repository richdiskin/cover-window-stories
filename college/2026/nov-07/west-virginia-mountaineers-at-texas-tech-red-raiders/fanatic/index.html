Mountaineers Ride Into Lubbock to Face No. 13 Texas Tech

West Virginia Mountaineers (5-3) at Texas Tech Red Raiders (7-1)

9:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Galaxy Stadium · ESPN

ShareXFacebook

West Virginia vs Texas Tech prediction for Saturday night in Lubbock: the Mountaineers come in at 5-3 off a win at TCU, the Red Raiders are 7-1 and ranked No. 13, and it is the Celebrate America Game at Galaxy Stadium.

Jolene Buckhannon grew up in the hills outside Morgantown and still sings "Country Roads" every time she crosses into a new state. Bodie Ratliff is a Lubbock lifer who has a tortilla in his pocket on every home Saturday and says the wind on the South Plains is just weather to him.

"Nice of you to fly out," Bodie says.

"Nice of you to have a stadium we can find," Jolene says. "There's nothing else out here."

Texas Tech calls this one the Celebrate America Game. The school will recognize each branch of the military and its campus military and veterans programs throughout the night. Before the game, fans pack Raider Alley in the Engineering Key, which is free and open to the public, with live music, food, family activities and the Goin' Band from Raiderland. Raider Alley has been part of game day in Lubbock since 1994. Kickoff is 9:30 p.m. ET.

"Raider Alley," Jolene says. "Do they have pepperoni rolls?"

"They have better," Bodie says.

"Nothing is better," Jolene says.

Jolene goes first. West Virginia can score. The Mountaineers put up 35.9 points a game. Michael Hawkins Jr. has 1,598 yards with 11 touchdowns and only 2 interceptions. Against Cincinnati, he went 25 of 33 for 464 yards and 3 touchdowns in a 45-42 win. Cam Cook is a workhorse with 923 rushing yards and 7 touchdowns. DJ Epps is the big-play receiver with 556 yards on just 22 catches. On defense, Isaiah Patterson leads the team with 48 tackles.

"We beat TCU 24-20 on the road last week," Jolene says. "We're hot."

"You lost 31-52 to Arizona at home," Bodie says.

"Arizona was a bad day," Jolene says. "Everybody has a bad day."

Bodie takes his turn. Texas Tech is 7-1, and the only loss was 24-37 at Cincinnati. Will Hammond has led the team with 1,561 throwing yards, but Thomas Castellanos took the most snaps last week against Arizona and threw for 144 yards and 2 touchdowns in a 34-26 win. J'Koby Williams ran wild in that game with 175 yards. He has 794 on the season. Kenny Johnson leads the receivers with 548 yards. The defense is the real reason Texas Tech is ranked. A.J. Holmes Jr. has 7.5 sacks, and Austin Romaine has 66 tackles.

"Holmes is going to live in your backfield," Bodie says.

"Hawkins runs," Jolene says. "Good luck catching him."

The long history is close, but lately it has been all Texas Tech. In the ten meetings since 2016, Texas Tech won six and West Virginia won four. Last year Texas Tech won 49-0. The year before that it was 52-15.

"Forty-nine to nothing," Bodie says. "Remember that one?"

"I've moved on," Jolene says. "That was a different team."

"It was last year," Bodie says.

"Different team," Jolene says.

Jolene does have some old wins to point to. West Virginia won 48-17 in 2016, 46-35 in 2017 and 42-34 in 2018. She has those scores memorized and says them like a prayer.

Here's how it plays out. Galaxy Stadium is loud and the flags are out for Celebrate America. Texas Tech gets the ball first and Williams breaks a long run to set up the opening score. West Virginia answers right away. Hawkins finds Epps deep, and Cook finishes the drive. It is a shootout in the first half, and Jolene is on her feet in the visitors' section yelling about every flag.

Then Holmes takes over. He gets to Hawkins twice in the third quarter, and Texas Tech turns those stops into points. The Red Raiders pull away in the fourth. West Virginia keeps scoring, though, and Cook gets a late touchdown that makes Jolene feel a little better on the long drive home.

Bodie says the Red Raiders are headed somewhere special. Jolene says she will be singing "Country Roads" all the way home anyway.

My call: Texas Tech Red Raiders 41, West Virginia Mountaineers 24.
