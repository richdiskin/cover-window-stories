Motor City MAC Night: Central Michigan and Eastern Michigan Take Ford Field

Central Michigan Chippewas (3-5) at Eastern Michigan Eagles (4-5)

7:30 PM ET Wed Nov 4 · Ford Field · ESPN2

XFacebook

Central Michigan Chippewas vs Eastern Michigan Eagles prediction for Wednesday, November 4: Motor City MAC night moves the oldest rivalry in the MAC to Ford Field in Detroit, and Brynn Haverkamp has already claimed the downtown parking garage like it's her deer blind. Imogen Tremblay showed up with a spreadsheet titled "Fan Challenge" and a bag of canned soup, because this rivalry now keeps score off the field too.

Here's the deal. The two schools are competing in a Fan Challenge that awards points for the most food donated to the MAC's annual Canned Food Drive, for social media support, for tickets donated to Detroit-area youth and first responders, and for a chef cook-off between the two universities. On game day, fans score points for wearing their colors and cheering loud. General admission in the end zones costs $20. Eastern Michigan is the home team this year. Central Michigan gets that title in 2027.

"Ope, sorry, Imogen," Brynn says, clearly not sorry. "Did you say home team? Ford Field is our home away from home. We've played here 11 times. Six bowl games. Four MAC title games. I could find the Chippewas tunnel blindfolded."

Imogen pushes up her glasses. "Brynn, I'm from Ypsilanti. I have spent my entire life being the little sibling of Ann Arbor. I am not about to be the little sibling of Mount Pleasant. I donated eleven cans of chickpeas. The spreadsheet is very green."

This series has been going since November 1, 1902, and it's the longest-running rivalry in the MAC at 103 games. Central Michigan leads it 65-32-6. Over the last ten meetings, the Chippewas won six and the Eagles won four. Last year Central won 24-13. The year before, Eastern won a wild one 38-34.

"Thirty-eight to thirty-four," Imogen says, closing her eyes like she's at a concert. "I have that game framed."

"I have last year framed," Brynn says. "Bigger frame."

Then the arguing gets serious, because these two know their rosters like the back of their hands.

Brynn goes first. Angel Flores was the engine of the Central Michigan offense, and he did it with his legs, running for 618 yards and 4 touchdowns as a quarterback. Now he's done for the season after ankle surgery, so the keys go to Jadyn Glasser. When the Chippewas needed to throw against Miami (OH), Glasser went 18 of 31 for 221 yards, and Langston Lewis caught 10 balls for 163 yards in a one-point loss. That was Lewis's last game for now. He's been suspended from all team activities for a violation of team rules. Mayson Hitchens leads the defense with 62 tackles, and Jaxon Cherry has 3 sacks.

"Lewis against Miami was a snowmobile on fresh powder," Brynn says. "Nobody was catching him."

"Except the scoreboard," Imogen says. "Twenty-eight to twenty-seven."

Brynn retreats briefly to her imaginary deer camp.

Imogen takes the floor. Noah Kim has thrown for 1,678 yards with 11 touchdowns and only 4 interceptions, if he's healthy. His status is up in the air, and Brogan McCaughey started at Ohio. Joshua Long is the go-to tight end with 42 catches for 383 yards. Amareon Blue leads the ground game with 405 yards. And then there is Quincy Byas, who has 5 sacks and plays like he's personally offended by quarterbacks. Zach Mowchan backs him up with 58 tackles.

"Byas is the green cell," Imogen says. "Every spreadsheet needs one."

The two common opponents make this even tighter. Both beat Akron, Central by 41-17 and Eastern by 25-23. Both lost at Ohio, Central 31-14 and Eastern 24-20. Eastern has the better league mark at 3-2, while Central is 1-3. Central has dropped three straight, the last one that 28-27 heartbreaker. Eastern has dropped two straight too.

"So we're both hungry," Brynn says.

"We're both bringing canned food," Imogen says. "Literally hungry is the theme."

Here's how it plays out under the roof downtown. It is close all night. Kim, or McCaughey if Kim can't go, moves the Eagles with Long working the middle, and Byas gets home at least once to make the Eastern section shake. Glasser keeps the Chippewas alive without Flores or Lewis, somebody new wins a big third down, and the defense led by Hitchens gets one critical stop late. The whole building is loud enough to win a few Fan Challenge points for both sides. In the end, a single point separates them, and the food drive is the real winner.

Brynn revs her imaginary snowmobile all the way up the aisle. Imogen updates her spreadsheet, grimly, then donates one more can.

My call: Central Michigan Chippewas 23, Eastern Michigan Eagles 22.
