Hawai'i Flies to El Paso, and UTEP Wants This One Bad

Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors (2-6) at UTEP Miners (1-7)

9:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Sun Bowl · FS1

ShareXFacebook

Hawai'i vs UTEP prediction for Saturday night in El Paso: the Rainbow Warriors fly in at 2-6, the Miners are 1-7, and Kainoa Akana-Baird and Neto Galindo both say this is the most important game of the season.

Kainoa grew up on the windward side of O'ahu and has been following the Rainbow Warriors since he could say "Warriors." He packs a cooler of spam musubi for every road trip. Neto Galindo was born a few blocks from the Sun Bowl and has had the same seats under Franklin Mountain since he was in high school. He says nothing beats a night game in the desert.

This one has history. UTEP is hosting Hawai'i for the first time since Oct. 16, 2004, when the Miners won 51-20. Neto was there. He still has the ticket stub in his wallet.

"Twenty-two years," Kainoa says. "You've been waiting a while."

"I've been waiting for a rematch," Neto says. "Now I get one."

Kickoff is 9:30 p.m. ET, which is 7:30 p.m. in El Paso.

Kainoa goes first. Hawai'i has been through a rough year, but the Warriors just had their best game. Micah Alejado threw for 323 yards and 3 touchdowns in a 56-21 win at Northern Illinois. On the season, he has 2,186 yards, 9 touchdowns and 6 interceptions. He throws it a lot. He had 51 throws at Stanford and 49 at Arizona State. DeVon Rice ran for 3 touchdowns last week and has 385 yards on the season. Kainoa Carvalho leads the receivers with 399 yards.

"Carvalho," Kainoa says. "Same first name. Same greatness."

"Different player," Neto says.

On defense, Jamih Otis leads Hawai'i with 54 tackles, and Wynden Ho'ohuli has 2 sacks.

"We lost close games," Kainoa says. "San José State by four. Stanford by ten. We're better than 2-6."

"You lost to UNLV 6-21," Neto says.

"UNLV is good," Kainoa says.

Neto takes his turn, and he is honest about the Miners. UTEP is 1-7. The only win was 51-10 over Texas Southern. The Miners have given up 36.5 points a game. They lost 7-61 at New Mexico and 0-51 at Oklahoma. Last week they lost 7-31 at North Dakota State.

"It's been tough," Neto says. "But we're home."

UTEP has used two quarterbacks. EJ Colson Jr. has 687 throwing yards with 7 touchdowns and 5 interceptions. Raymond Moore III leads the team in rushing with 481 yards and has also played quarterback. Against Nevada he went 16 of 26 for 132 yards and 2 touchdowns. Last week he ran for 67 yards on just 3 carries. Kam Thomas leads the receivers with 283 yards. On defense, Sterling Miles has 3.5 sacks and Jayden Wilson has 47 tackles.

"Moore is the guy," Neto says. "Put the ball in his hands and let him go."

"Hawai'i will be ready," Kainoa says.

"Hawai'i hasn't been ready all year," Neto says.

Kainoa throws a spam musubi at him. Neto catches it and eats it.

Kick it off and watch. The Sun Bowl is not full, but the people who come are loud. Hawai'i gets the ball first, and Alejado goes to work. He hits Carvalho on a deep throw down the right side, and Rice punches it in. UTEP answers with Moore. He runs for a big gain on the first play, and the Miners get a field goal.

Alejado keeps throwing. Hawai'i scores twice more in the first half and takes a big lead into the break. The Miners don't quit, though. In the second half, Moore runs for a touchdown and Colson finds Thomas for another. UTEP gets within one score in the fourth quarter, and the Sun Bowl gets loud. But Rice puts it away late with a long run.

Kainoa says the Warriors will finish the season strong. Neto says he will be back in the same seats for the next one. They split the last spam musubi.

My call: Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors 29, UTEP Miners 20.
