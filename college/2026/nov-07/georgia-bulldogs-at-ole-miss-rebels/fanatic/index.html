Georgia Bulldogs at Ole Miss Rebels

Georgia Bulldogs (7-1) at Ole Miss Rebels (6-2)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Vaught-Hemingway Stadium · ABC

ShareXFacebook

Georgia vs Ole Miss prediction for Saturday night in Oxford: the Rebels and Bulldogs meet at Vaught-Hemingway Stadium at 7:30 PM ET, and Ole Miss has already turned the whole week into a contest. It is called Beat Week, and it runs from Monday, Nov. 2, through Saturday, Nov. 7.

Here is how it works. Ole Miss and Georgia are racing to see which school can collect the most charitable gifts before kickoff. Every gift of $5 or more counts as one point, no matter the size, so a $5 gift counts the same as a $5,000 one. Georgia has the bigger alumni base, so Ole Miss is asking every fan to make three gifts to three things they love about the place, maybe a school, a scholarship and the Pride of the South Marching Band. Three gifts, three points. The school even pointed out that the Rebels beat Georgia in the Sugar Bowl in January and would like a second win over the Bulldogs this year.

I talked it through with two people who see this game in very different light.

Thad Okafor-Brandt is a DJ on a college station who plays indie records nobody else has heard of and spends his off nights watching defensive film. Langston Fairbanks lives in Oxford, wears seersucker well past the season for it and will quote Faulkner at you whether or not you have asked.

"I already made my three gifts," Langston said. "School of Law, a scholarship and the band. That's three points."

"That's very Oxford of you," Thad said.

"Faulkner said the past is never dead. It's not even past. That is this series."

He is not wrong. The last four meetings have swung back and forth. Ole Miss won 45-14 in 2016 and 28-10 in 2024. Georgia won 52-17 in 2023 and 43-35 last year.

Georgia comes in at 7-1 and ranked second in the country. The Bulldogs score 41.0 points a game and allow just 14.9. They beat Oklahoma 41-13, won 20-17 at Alabama and beat Florida 27-8 last Saturday. The one loss was 24-27 to Auburn.

"Watch the Georgia defense on film," Thad said. "It's like a perfect record with one scratch. Auburn found the scratch. Alabama almost did."

Gunner Stockton has 1,671 yards, 16 touchdowns and 4 interceptions, though he threw for only 101 against Florida. Dwight Phillips Jr. leads the rushers with 415 yards, Craig Dandridge leads the receivers with 349, and Raylen Wilson leads the defense in both tackles, with 51, and sacks, with 4.

Ole Miss is 6-2 and ranked 12th, scoring 37.1 a game and allowing 24.0. The Rebels opened by beating Louisville 41-38, then beat LSU 32-24 at home. They lost 28-52 at Florida and 27-34 at Texas. But at home they have been very good, beating Missouri 31-3 and Auburn 45-15 last week.

"Trinidad Chambliss is the whole thing," Langston said. "He has 2,189 yards and 18 touchdowns. Deuce Alexander has 669 receiving yards. When the Grove is full and the sun goes down, this place is a different stadium."

"Chambliss also has 8 interceptions," Thad said. "Three at Texas. Georgia's defense eats that up."

That's the matchup. Ole Miss throws it all over the place, and Georgia's defense is the best it has seen. The Rebels also have a defense with some bite, with Blake Purchase at 4.5 sacks and Keaton Thomas at 52 tackles.

I asked them both how they want Saturday to feel. Thad said he wants a game he can play back like a great record. Langston said he wants the Hotty Toddy to ring out under the lights at the end of the fourth quarter, with the Beat Week scoreboard settled too.

Here is how I see it. Georgia is the better team on paper, with the better defense and more depth. But this is a night game in Oxford, and Ole Miss has been very good at home. Chambliss will make big plays and a couple of mistakes. Stockton will be careful and get it done late. It comes down to one drive, and Georgia has the last one.

Thad says he will play the deep cuts all the way home. Langston says the ending will be long and close, like a Faulkner sentence.

My call: Georgia Bulldogs 31, Ole Miss Rebels 30.
