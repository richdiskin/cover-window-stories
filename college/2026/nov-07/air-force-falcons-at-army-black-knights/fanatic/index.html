Air Force Invades West Point for the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy

Air Force Falcons (6-2) at Army Black Knights (5-3)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Michie Stadium · CBS

ShareXFacebook

Air Force vs Army prediction for Saturday night at West Point: the Falcons come east at 6-2, the Black Knights are 5-3 at home, and the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy is sitting right in the middle of Michie Stadium.

Marissa Okonkwo-Hale is a retired lieutenant colonel who flew for the Air Force and still salutes the Falcon on the helmet. Thaddeus Rourke is a retired Army captain who has not missed a home game at Michie in years and brings the same wool blanket every November.

This one is a night game, and that is a big deal in the Hudson Valley. It is Army's first night home game against Air Force since 2006. The first night game ever played at Michie Stadium was against Air Force in 1984, and Army won it 24-12. Thaddeus was there. He mentions it every time somebody stands still long enough.

"Forty years of telling the same story," Marissa says.

"It's a good story," Thaddeus says. "It ends 24-12."

The forecast calls for about 47 degrees with wind around 9 miles per hour. Thaddeus says that is football weather. Marissa says that is a reason to stay in Colorado.

Marissa goes first. Air Force is 6-2 and scores 29.2 points a game. The quarterback spot has moved around. Liam Szarka took most of the early snaps, then Evan Medders, then Howard Fisher IV. Medders leads the team in rushing with 490 yards and 6 touchdowns. Last week against UConn, the Falcons handed the ball to Kemper Hodges and let him go. He carried it 35 times for 200 yards and 4 touchdowns in a 35-26 win. On defense, Blake Fletcher has 79 tackles and Max Mustell has 3 sacks.

"We beat Navy 14-9," Marissa says. "We beat UNLV 41-31. We won in overtime at Nevada. This team finds a way."

"You lost 14-28 at Wyoming," Thaddeus says.

"Wyoming is at altitude," Marissa says. "Nobody likes it up there."

"You live at altitude," Thaddeus says.

Thaddeus takes his turn. Army runs through Cale Hellums. He has 874 rushing yards and 17 rushing touchdowns, which is the kind of number that ends arguments. He has also thrown for 462 yards. Army scores 33.6 a game. The Black Knights put up 56 on Florida Atlantic and 38 at Tulsa. Elijah Walton leads the defense with 55 tackles, and Victor Afolabi has 3 sacks.

Then there is last week. Army lost 22-49 at Memphis. Godspower Nwawuihe ran for 100 yards, but the defense had no answers.

"Memphis was one bad night," Thaddeus says.

"Forty-nine points is a lot of bad night," Marissa says.

History belongs to Army lately. In the ten meetings since 2016, Army won seven and Air Force won three. Army won 20-17 last year and 20-3 the year before. But Marissa reminds everyone that Air Force won 31-12 in 2016 and 13-7 in 2022, and that this rivalry tends to stay close.

"Seven of ten," Thaddeus says. "Write that on the trophy."

"The trophy has two academies on it already," Marissa says. "We beat the other one."

This game is also the middle step of the trophy chase. Air Force already beat Navy. Army plays Navy in the 127th Army-Navy Game on Dec. 12. If Army wins Saturday, everything rides on Philadelphia, and Thaddeus has already booked his hotel.

Here's how it plays out. Both teams run the ball, and the clock moves fast in the first half. Air Force gets the first score when Medders breaks a long run around the corner. Hellums answers with a touchdown of his own, and the cadets in the stands go wild. It stays close into the third quarter, with each offense putting together long drives that eat up time and patience.

In the fourth quarter, Army's defense finally gets a stop on a fourth down near midfield. Hellums takes over from there, running for one more score. Air Force answers late, but it is not enough.

Marissa says the Falcons will get their revenge next time. Thaddeus says he will bring the blanket.

My call: Army Black Knights 31, Air Force Falcons 25.
