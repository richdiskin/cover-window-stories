Virginia Tech Hokies at SMU Mustangs

Virginia Tech Hokies (6-2) at SMU Mustangs (6-2)

7:00 PM ET Fri Nov 6 · Gerald J. Ford Stadium · ESPN

ShareXFacebook

Virginia Tech Hokies vs SMU Mustangs prediction for Friday, November 6: it is Arbor Day in University Park, the leafy little city that wraps around the SMU campus, and the schoolkids are planting a tree in one of the parks. Tatum Delacroix-Price says that is the only thing in the Park Cities getting planted Friday that isn't a Hokie on his back.

Ruthanne Dellinger arrived from Blacksburg in her old Corps boots, and she heard that. "Tatum, sugar, we don't get planted. We grow. Six and two, just like you. And we went into Clemson and won 20-16. Ask the Tigers who got planted."

University Park takes its trees seriously. Since 1982, residents have bought more than 3,300 of them through the Trees for Town program for the parkways along their sidewalks. Tatum has a theory that the shade is why SMU fans are always so relaxed. Ruthanne has a theory that the shade is where they hide when the fourth quarter gets tough.

The Mustangs are asking the crowd to wear blue Friday night. Tatum is in blue from her earrings to her boots.

"Kevin Jennings," she says, like she is introducing royalty. "Two thousand four hundred fourteen yards. Twenty-one touchdown throws. He went 29 of 36 for 352 yards at Syracuse last week."

"And you lost," Ruthanne says.

"By three. 27-30. On the road. Up there in the cold." Tatum waves a hand. "Kevin comes home, Kevin gets warm, Kevin cooks."

SMU has cooked plenty in Dallas this fall. The Mustangs beat UC Davis 56-10, Missouri State 34-24, Boston College 25-16 and California 59-20 at home. Kendrick Raphael has 692 rushing yards and nine touchdowns, and Yamir Knight leads the receivers with 523 yards.

Ruthanne answers with the thing Virginia Tech always answers with: defense and patience. Aycen Stevens has eight sacks this year, and she says he gets off the ball like a drum major hitting the first beat.

"Eight," she says. "Kevin Jennings is going to meet Aycen Stevens enough times Friday that they'll exchange Christmas cards."

The Hokies' quarterback is the reason she sleeps at night. Ethan Grunkemeyer has 2,140 yards, 17 touchdown throws and just two interceptions all season. Que'Sean Brown has 51 catches for 685 yards. Virginia Tech scores 35.0 points a game and allows 20.5, and it handled Maryland 35-26 and Boston College 21-14 on the road.

"Two interceptions," Ruthanne says. "In eight games. Our quarterback treats the football like a family heirloom."

Tatum is not impressed. "Your running game went to sleep at Clemson. Jeffrey Overton Jr. had 10 carries for 26 yards. If you can't run on us, Ruthanne, you're throwing into Marques White all night, and he has five and a half sacks of his own."

That is the fight in the trenches. Virginia Tech wants to grind it out, keep Jennings on the bench and win the way it won at Clemson. SMU wants to make it a race, because the Mustangs figure nobody in the ACC can keep up with Jennings for four quarters at home.

The one game that bugs Ruthanne is Pitt. Virginia Tech lost 33-35 to the Panthers in October, a two-point heartbreaker that she still replays on the drive home.

"Two points," she says. "We'll get them back. Maybe Friday."

"Not in my yard," Tatum says.

Here is how it shakes out. Grunkemeyer keeps the ball safe and Virginia Tech hangs in it, with Stevens getting home for a big sack or two. But Jennings is too sharp at home, Raphael finds the end zone, and SMU pulls away just enough in the fourth quarter. The Hokies make it a game. The Mustangs make the last big throw.

Ruthanne marches out under the trees anyway. Tatum tells her University Park has 3,300 of them, and every one of them is wearing blue tonight.

My call: SMU Mustangs 32, Virginia Tech Hokies 25.
