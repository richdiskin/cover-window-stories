Iowa's First Trip to the New Ryan Field: Two 6-2 Teams Collide

Iowa Hawkeyes (6-2) at Northwestern Wildcats (6-2)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Ryan Field · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Iowa vs Northwestern prediction for Saturday night in Evanston: two 6-2 teams, a brand-new stadium, and two insiders who have not agreed on anything since the first week of September.

Orrin Kalvelage has had Iowa season tickets for longer than some of these players have been alive. He brings the same thermos to every road game. Ezra Lindqvist lives a short walk from the lake and spent the last two years watching cranes swing over the old site, waiting for the new Ryan Field to open.

It finally did. The new 35,000-seat Ryan Field opened last month after 2 2/3 years of construction, and Saturday is Iowa's first look at it. It is also the first time these two have met since 2024. While the new place was being built, Northwestern played its home games at Northwestern Medicine Field at Martin Stadium, right on the lakefront. Kickoff Saturday is 7:30 p.m. ET.

"Nice building," Orrin says. "Shame about the football."

"The football is 6-2," Ezra says. "Same as yours. And we haven't lost at home."

That's true. Northwestern is 5-0 at home this season. Iowa is ranked No. 21 and walks in off a 30-0 shutout of Wisconsin.

Orrin goes first. The Iowa defense is the whole story. The Hawkeyes allow 12.5 points a game. Cam Buffington leads them with 70 tackles, and Joseph Anderson has 4 sacks. On offense, Hank Brown has been steady with 1,190 yards, 8 touchdowns and 3 interceptions, and against Wisconsin he went 20 of 24 for 222 yards and 2 touchdowns. Kamari Moulton leads the backs with 541 yards. Tony Diaz leads the receivers with 461 yards.

"We beat Michigan in their own house 20-19," Orrin says. "We'll be fine in a stadium that still smells like paint."

Ezra has heard this speech before. His turn. Aidan Chiles is having the year of his life. He has 1,898 yards, 13 touchdowns and just 1 interception. He also runs. At Oregon last week he had 93 yards on the ground to go with 281 through the air. Griffin Wilde caught 5 balls for 146 yards in that game and has 761 on the season. Caleb Komolafe leads the backs with 618 yards. Braydon Brus does everything on defense, with 67 tackles and 4 sacks.

"We scored 26 at Oregon," Ezra says. "You'd be lucky to score 26 against a high school."

"We scored 30 against Wisconsin and they scored zero," Orrin says.

"Wisconsin scores zero against everybody," Ezra says.

History is close, too. In the nine meetings since 2016, Iowa won five and Northwestern won four. Iowa won the last one 40-14 in 2024. But Northwestern won 21-20 in 2020, 14-10 in 2018 and 38-31 in 2016. Ezra lists every one of them from memory. Orrin says he has blocked those out.

"You can't block out 2020," Ezra says. "It was one point."

"I can block out anything," Orrin says. "I'm from Iowa. We're patient people."

Now the football. The new stadium is loud from the opening kick, and it gets louder when Chiles scrambles for a first down on the opening drive. Iowa answers the way Iowa always does. Moulton runs it, Brown hits Diaz on third down, and the Hawkeyes keep the ball for what feels like a full quarter. The Iowa defense gets after Chiles and forces a punt or two. It is tight at the half.

In the second half, Chiles finds Wilde deep, and the crowd by the lake finally gets the moment it has been waiting two years for. Iowa comes back with a long drive and makes it a three-point game. The Hawkeyes get the ball back one more time, but Brus gets home on a big third down, and Northwestern holds on.

Orrin pours what is left in the thermos and says the new stadium has bad karma for visitors. Ezra says that is called a home team.

My call: Northwestern Wildcats 24, Iowa Hawkeyes 21.
