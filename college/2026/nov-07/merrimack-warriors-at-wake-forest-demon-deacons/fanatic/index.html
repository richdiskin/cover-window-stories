Gold Rush Homecoming: Wake Forest Welcomes Merrimack to Winston-Salem

Merrimack Warriors (4-5) at Wake Forest Demon Deacons (7-1)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Allegacy Federal Credit Union Stadium · ACC Network

ShareXFacebook

Merrimack Warriors vs Wake Forest Demon Deacons prediction for Saturday, November 7: it is Homecoming in Winston-Salem, the Quad is getting its annual paper decorating, and Eloise Brightman-Rowe has a gold blazer pressed and a roll of toilet paper on her keychain for the occasion.

"Gold Rush game," she says. "Everybody wears gold. If you show up in anything else, I will hand you a rebuttal and a scarf."

She means it. Wake Forest is asking every fan to wear gold, and the football program is again partnering with Brenner Children's Hospital to raise money and awareness for childhood cancer. There is a Golden Deacs lunch, Party So Dear, a Festival on the Quad, the Alumni Tailgate and a pile of class reunions, so half the people in the stands will be catching up with somebody they lived down the hall from twenty years ago. Over in Ardmore, the art walk puts 100 artists on front lawns from 11 a.m., which Eloise calls the warm-up act.

Kickoff is 3:30 p.m. ET. This is the first meeting ever between the Demon Deacons and the Warriors, and that is the part Eloise keeps circling back to.

"We don't know them," she says. "They don't know us. That is how upsets get written. I have read those essays. I have graded those essays."

Here is the honest picture. Wake Forest is 7-1, ranked No. 14, and 5-1 against the conference. The only loss came on September 18, 20-33 to Miami. Since then the Deacs have won five in a row, and they have not been close lately. They beat Stanford 57-3. They won 38-14 at NC State. They went to California and won 41-7. Last Saturday they beat Virginia 51-7. Across eight games they score 39.1 a game and allow 17.9.

"That last month reads like a closing argument," Eloise says. "And the closing argument is: sit down."

The offense runs through Gio Lopez. He has 2,176 yards, 18 touchdown throws and 1 interception, and that last figure is the one Eloise taps on the table. Carlos Hernandez has 57 catches for 856 yards and 5 touchdowns. Ty Clark III has 376 rushing yards and 7 touchdowns. On defense, Langston Hardy has 5.5 sacks and Vincent Firenze leads the team with 52 tackles.

"Firenze tackles like he read the syllabus," Eloise says. "Everybody else on that field is cramming."

Now Merrimack, because the Warriors deserve a fair hearing, and Eloise was on the debate team, so she knows how to give one. Merrimack comes down from Massachusetts at 4-5, but it is playing its best football right now. The Warriors have won two in a row, 24-17 over New Hampshire and 19-14 over Wagner, and three of their last five, with a 17-10 win over New Haven before that. They also won 23-14 at Maine in September. Matthias Latham is the workhorse, 121 carries for 623 yards. AJ Hairston Jr has thrown for 1,362 yards with 7 touchdowns and 6 interceptions. Austin Palmer has 25 catches for 417 yards, and Danny Simms has 57 tackles, more than anybody on Wake's roster.

"Fifty-seven tackles," Eloise says. "That's a man who has had to make a lot of tackles. I respect that. I also know why."

The tough part for Merrimack is the road. The Warriors opened with a 7-34 home loss to Rhode Island, and on the road they lost 7-42 at Delaware, 20-45 at Tarleton State, 24-41 at Yale and 21-27 at Dartmouth. They score 18.0 a game and give up 27.1. The Delaware game is the closest thing to a measuring stick, and that one got away early.

There is also history on Wake's side, just not with Merrimack. The Deacs have won their last 20 games against FCS opponents, going back to 2000.

"Twenty in a row," Eloise says. "I was in grade school for the first one. I have opinions about all of them."

So how does it play out? The building is gold and loud early, and Wake goes right at it. Lopez gets the ball to Hernandez on the outside, and Clark finishes a drive or two in close. Merrimack tries to keep it on the ground with Latham, shorten the afternoon and keep Lopez on the sideline, and for a quarter that works well enough to make the visitors' section happy. Hairston hits Palmer for a big gain at some point, because good teams give up one of those. But Hardy gets home, the Deacs' defense that held Stanford, California and Virginia to single digits does it again, and by the fourth quarter the reunion classes are taking pictures instead of watching.

Eloise rolls the Quad that night with the Class of 2016. Merrimack goes home with a first trip to Winston-Salem and a story to tell, and with Monmouth and Sacred Heart still ahead, the Warriors have plenty to play for.

"Roll the Quad!" she says. "Then be nice to the visitors. They drove a long way."

My call: Wake Forest Demon Deacons 41, Merrimack Warriors 10.
