Western Kentucky Hilltoppers at Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders

Western Kentucky Hilltoppers (3-5) at Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders (4-4)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Johnny "Red" Floyd Stadium · ESPN+

ShareXFacebook

Western Kentucky Hilltoppers vs Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders prediction for Saturday, November 7: it's Homecoming at Floyd Stadium, it's the Hall of Fame Game, and Opal Stonebriar brought two casseroles, one for her people and one for Thurman "Tops" Whitsitt, because she's going to feed you before she corrects you. Tops drove down from Bowling Green with a hot towel still slung over one shoulder from his last customer.

Here's the deal. This is Middle Tennessee's first November Homecoming since 2008, and the Blue Raiders are making a whole day of it. It's also Youth Sports Day and Blue Raider Kids Club Day, so kids walk in the Homecoming parade and form the high-five tunnel when the team runs out. Kickoff is 3:30 p.m. ET in Murfreesboro.

"A high-five tunnel," Tops says. "Very sweet. We'll wave on our way through."

"Thurman, the children will remember your manners," Opal says. "Mind them."

Floyd Stadium opened in 1933. It holds 27,303 people. The Blue Raiders are 55-29-2 all-time on Homecoming, and 21-9-2 in Homecoming games played in November. Opal has those figures written inside the lid of her casserole dish.

"I didn't need to know that," Tops says.

"Everybody needs to know that," Opal says.

But this is a rivalry, and Tops has his own figures. Western Kentucky leads the all-time series 39-35-1. The Hilltoppers have won 7 in a row and nine of the last ten. Last year was 42-26. In Murfreesboro, though, Middle Tennessee leads 21-17-1, which is the one fact Opal holds onto like a hymnal.

"Seven straight," Tops says, polishing an imaginary razor. "That's a lot of fresh haircuts, Opal."

"Aren't you sweet for counting," Opal says. "Now count to 21-17-1."

Now the football.

Tops goes first, unhurried, like he has all afternoon. Brock Glenn is the guy for Western Kentucky. He has 887 passing yards, and he leads the team in rushing with 451 yards and 5 touchdowns. Against Delaware he ran 17 times for 129 yards. Jonathan Stafford Jr. leads the receivers with 46 catches for 433 yards. On defense, Jaylen Lewis has 62 tackles and Westen Ard has 2.5 sacks.

"Glenn is a straight-razor quarterback," Tops says. "Smooth, quick, and you don't see him coming."

Opal takes the floor. Roman Gagliano has thrown for 1,587 yards with 12 touchdowns. Cam'ron Lacy is his go-to target, or as Opal calls him, "my boy," with 45 catches for 510 yards. Antonio Martin leads the ground game with 586 yards, and he ran for 106 against Kennesaw State. Korey Smith leads the defense with 73 tackles.

"Korey Smith is the deacon of that defense," Opal says. "He keeps everybody in their pew."

"You gave up 44 to Kennesaw State last week," Tops says.

"We did," Opal says, setting down the casserole spoon very carefully. "And you gave up 35 to Delaware. Hand me the rolls."

Both teams have had a tough fall. Western Kentucky is 3-5 and gives up 31.1 a game. Middle Tennessee is 4-4 and gives up 29.6. Both offenses can score. Both defenses have had rough weeks.

"So it's a shootout," Tops says.

"It's a homecoming," Opal says. "Different thing. More casseroles."

Here's how it plays out in Murfreesboro. The kids give the Blue Raiders a loud send-off through the high-five tunnel, and Middle Tennessee comes out swinging with Gagliano hitting Lacy early. Glenn answers with his legs, breaking a long run that makes Tops stand up in the visitors' section. It goes back and forth all afternoon. Martin grinds out tough yards in the fourth quarter, Korey Smith makes a stop on WKU's last real chance, and the Homecoming crowd gets to sing on the way out.

Opal hugs every Hall of Famer she can find. Tops sits quietly with his hot towel over his face for a full minute, then says, "Good game, Opal. Next year you're coming to my shop."

My call: Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders 28, Western Kentucky Hilltoppers 27.
