Bruins Bring Hollywood to Minneapolis, Gophers Bring the Cold

UCLA Bruins (8-0) at Minnesota Golden Gophers (5-3)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Huntington Bank Stadium · Fox

ShareXFacebook

UCLA vs Minnesota prediction for Saturday in Minneapolis, from two people who agree on nothing except that the other one dresses wrong for the weather. Kenzie Rhee produced short films at UCLA and still frames every argument like a trailer. Tor Engebretsen spends most of the winter on a frozen lake in a shack with a hole in the floor and considers that a vacation.

Tor: Saturday morning, The Museum of Russian Art opens a show called Painting Snow. Forty paintings of winter. Wear a winter hat and you get in free. I want every UCLA fan in town to go. Study the paintings. That is what the afternoon is going to look like at Huntington Bank Stadium.

Kenzie: Okay, cold open. A team from Los Angeles walks into Minneapolis. It is 8-0. It is ranked No. 5 in the country. It has given up 13.4 points a game. Cut to the home crowd, in hats, realizing they are about to watch the best defense they have seen all year.

Tor: Cut to the scoreboard in 2024. Minnesota 21, UCLA 17. I remember that one. I remember it the way I remember a walleye that was this big. Every year it gets a little bigger.

Kenzie: You are counting a two-year-old game against an unbeaten team. That is not a story, Tor. That is a flashback, and flashbacks slow down the movie. We beat Oregon on the road, 17-15. We beat Wisconsin 17-7. We beat Nevada 27-0 last week.

Tor: Seventeen points against Oregon. Seventeen against Wisconsin. Your offense makes its movies on a budget. And you have two quarterbacks. Nico Iamaleava has 982 yards, but Ty Dieffenbach has thrown the most in each of the last three games. When you have to tell people which quarterback is in your movie, the audience gets confused.

Kenzie: The audience is watching Wayne Knight. He has 680 rushing yards and 12 touchdowns. Twelve. He is the lead. He is in every scene. And Sammy Omosigho has 5.5 sacks. He is the villain, and the villain gets your quarterback.

Tor: Drake Lindsey can take a hit. He has 1,592 yards and 10 touchdowns. Javon Tracy has 460 receiving yards. We beat Michigan 20-14. We beat Iowa 13-10. We went to Indiana last week and lost in overtime, 23-16. We know how to play in a close game. You have not been in one since Oregon.

Kenzie: We have not been in one because nobody can score on us. That is not a weakness. That is the premise.

Tor: The premise changes when it gets cold. You know what the Gophers hockey team is doing this weekend? Playing Wisconsin in a Gold Out. Whole town in gold. Football Saturday, hockey Saturday night, and in between people go to the museum to look at paintings of snow because they cannot get enough of it.

Kenzie: That is the most Minnesota sentence anybody has ever said.

Tor: Thank you. I practice it on the ice.

Kenzie: Here is my pitch. UCLA scores 33.5 a game. Minnesota scores 25.2. The Bruins allow 13.4. Minnesota allows 20.5. On paper that is a comfortable win. On film, it is a road team in November that does not love the cold and a home team with nothing to lose.

Tor: That is the first honest thing you have said all day. Nothing to lose is the best way to play. We are 5-3, we have a home crowd, and we have Maverick Baranowski with 60 tackles waiting for Knight at the hole like a pike under the ice.

Kenzie: A pike. You are comparing your linebacker to a fish.

Tor: To a very patient fish with a lot of teeth. And TJ Bush Jr. has 5 sacks. He is a fish too. Everybody on our defense is a fish.

Kenzie: Final scene. Knight runs for a touchdown, the defense holds in the fourth quarter, and UCLA flies home 9-0 with snow on its shoes.

Tor: My final scene. Minnesota hangs around, Lindsey finds Tracy late, and the crowd in winter hats goes home hoping until the very last snap.

Kenzie: Hoping is not winning.

Tor: In Minnesota, it is a lifestyle.

My call: UCLA Bruins 25, Minnesota Golden Gophers 20.
