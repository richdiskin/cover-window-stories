Salute to Service in Amherst: UMass Hosts Ball State on Wednesday

Ball State Cardinals (3-5) at Massachusetts Minutemen (4-4)

7:00 PM ET Wed Nov 4 · Warren McGuirk Alumni Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

Ball State Cardinals vs UMass Minutemen prediction for Wednesday night, November 4, at McGuirk Alumni Stadium: it's Salute to Service and Faculty & Staff Appreciation night in Amherst, and Abner Kettleworth is already saluting anyone in a uniform or a tweed jacket. Gus Ponder rolled in from Muncie with a whiteboard under one arm and a Garfield tie in his glovebox, just in case.

Wednesday football in the Pioneer Valley is its own animal. University Drive closes to southbound traffic at 2:30 in the afternoon, kids 12 and under get in for $5, and the folks who teach organic chemistry all day are going to spend the night yelling at third-down calls. Abner calls it the best faculty meeting of the year.

"You know what I love about tonight, Gus?" Abner says, adjusting his specs. "The professors grade us, we grade them back. One if by run, two if by throw. And Ball State had better bring both lanterns."

Gus pats his whiteboard. "Abner, I brought a punt chart, a thermos and a joke. Why did the Cardinal sit on the scoreboard? He heard the Minutemen were short on fans up high." He waits. Nothing. "Tough crowd. Jim Davis went to Ball State, you know. Garfield hates Mondays. Lucky for me, it's a Wednesday."

The history is short and even. These two have met four times, and the series sits 2-2. UMass won the first one 26-10 in 1984. Ball State won the last one 20-10 back in 2015 in Muncie. Abner remembers the first. Gus remembers the last. Neither of them will admit to remembering the other.

"Here's my worry," Abner says, and the deadpan cracks just a hair. "We've dropped four in a row. Eastern Michigan rolled us 38-14 right here. Miami (OH) beat us 20-19. Buffalo got us 25-18 in overtime. UConn won 35-16. If I plant one more tree for a loss, the Valley is going to be a forest."

"And I've got the opposite problem," Gus says. "We finally remembered how to win. Bowling Green, 32-31 in overtime. Sacramento State, 34-17. Two in a row, Abner. In Muncie we call that a parade."

The Minutemen have more than hope, though, and Abner knows every name on the roster card. William Watson III has thrown for 1,025 yards with 8 touchdowns and only 2 interceptions. He went 19 of 22 for 197 yards and 3 touchdowns in the opener at Rutgers, a 37-21 win that Abner still replays at the kitchen table. T.Y. Harding leads the receivers with 28 catches for 327 yards. Elijah Faulkner carries it on the ground with 376 yards.

"Watson doesn't give the ball away," Abner says. "He treats it like heirloom seed."

On the other side of the field, the Massachusetts defense has a heartbeat named Timmy Hinspeter, who has 72 tackles, and a front with William DePaepe and his 3 sacks. That group held Sacred Heart to 10 and Stonehill to 14 and went to Sacramento State and allowed 6.

Gus is not backing down. Keldric Luster has thrown for 1,300 yards with 9 touchdowns, and when he gets hot he can make a defense look silly. He was 27 of 32 for 256 yards and 3 touchdowns against Stony Brook. TJ Horton is the battering ram in the backfield, 425 yards and 9 touchdowns, and Donovan Hamilton leads the receivers with 310 yards. Kaleb Stewart anchors the Cardinals defense with 37 tackles and 2.5 sacks.

"Horton runs like Garfield going after lasagna," Gus says. "Straight ahead, no apologies, and you are not getting it back."

Abner nods slowly. "Fair. But your defense has given up 56 to Ohio State, 51 to Liberty and 45 to Northwestern. Those are big schools. We are not a big school. We are a stubborn school."

"Stubborn," Gus says, "is my whole punt chart."

Here's the part that makes this one fun. Both teams played Sacramento State this year. The Minutemen won 35-6 out in California. The Cardinals won 34-17 at home. Abner has that written on an index card in his pocket and keeps patting it like it's a pocket watch.

So here is how it plays out under the lights in Amherst. Watson keeps the ball clean, Harding wins on third down, and Faulkner grinds the clock. Ball State hangs around because Horton is real and Luster can hurt you, and Gus gets at least two reasons to stand up and wave the whiteboard. But the Minutemen defense makes one more play when it counts, the veterans in the crowd get a proper Wednesday win to cheer, and the faculty go home with a good grade for the home side.

Abner rings his tiny colonial bell. Gus loosens the Garfield tie and says it sounds like a Monday after all.

My call: Massachusetts Minutemen 31, Ball State Cardinals 22.
