Funk in Reno, Football at Mackay: New Mexico Battles Nevada

New Mexico Lobos (7-1) at Nevada Wolf Pack (3-5)

9:00 PM ET Fri Nov 6 · Mackay Stadium · CBSSN

XFacebook

New Mexico Lobos vs Nevada Wolf Pack prediction for Friday, November 6: the Reno Public Market has Margret's Funk Band playing for free that night from 7 to 10, and Paloma Begaye-Ortiz has already decided that is where she will warm up. Ridge Corbell tells her funk is fine, but the only music in Reno that matters is the noise at Mackay Stadium.

Paloma pilots hot-air balloons over Albuquerque, so she talks about everything in terms of altitude. "We're 7-1, Ridge. Number 22 in the country. First in the Mountain West. We are flying so high you need binoculars."

Ridge patrols the slopes at Tahoe and has pulled a lot of overconfident people out of the snow. "Balloons come down, Paloma. Usually in somebody's field."

New Mexico has plenty to brag about. The Lobos give up 11.9 points a game. Darren Agu has six sacks. Mercury Swaim has 50 tackles. Scottre Humphrey has 11 rushing touchdowns, and Kiefer Sibley ran for 102 yards in a 21-16 win at San Jose State last week.

"Mercury Swaim," Paloma says. "Even his name is fast."

Ridge goes right at the quarterback. Toa Fa'avae threw three interceptions at San Jose State, and he has seven on the year against only six touchdown throws. The Lobos have scored 21 points in each of their last three wins.

"Your quarterback throws to my guys like he's handing out lift tickets," Ridge says. "EJ Smith has 85 tackles. He's going to find Toa."

Paloma waves it off. "Toa doesn't need to throw. We run. We hit. We win. Ask Hawai'i. Ask North Dakota State."

Nevada's year has been rough, and Ridge admits it. The Wolf Pack are 3-5 and were shut out 0-27 at UCLA last week. Carter Jones leads the team with 654 passing yards, and Gary Givens III leads the receivers with 305. Dylan LaBarbera has four and a half sacks.

"We beat San Jose State 28-13 the week before," Ridge says. "Same team you squeaked past. We can play with anybody at Mackay."

The history gives him something to hold on to. Since 2016, Nevada has won three of the five meetings, including 34-24 in 2023. New Mexico won the last one 24-22 last year.

"Two points," Paloma says. "Close, but we went home happy."

"Close is all we need Friday," Ridge says.

The cold is part of Ridge's case. He swears the Lobos have never felt a Reno November, and he expects them to be stiff by the second quarter. Paloma tells him balloon pilots get up before dawn in the desert cold all fall. Cold doesn't scare her. Wind does, and she says the Wolf Pack aren't strong enough to make any.

Paloma has a few more numbers for the drive home. New Mexico beat UTEP 61-7 and Mercyhurst 70-7, and the only loss came 6-14 at Oklahoma in September.

"Six points at Oklahoma," Ridge says. "Your offense went for a balloon ride and forgot to come down."

"And we still only gave up 14 to the Sooners," Paloma says. "Ask your guys how they did against Montana State."

Ridge winces. Nevada lost 7-20 at home to Montana State and 33-36 in overtime to Air Force. But the Wolf Pack opened the year with a 49-14 win over Western Kentucky, and Ridge swears that team still lives somewhere inside Mackay.

Here is how it shakes out. Nevada's defense hangs around early, and Fa'avae gives Ridge a scare or two. But the New Mexico run game wears the Wolf Pack down, the Lobos' defense keeps Nevada out of the end zone when it counts, and New Mexico pulls away in the second half to stay on top of the Mountain West.

Paloma heads back downtown to catch the last of the funk band. Ridge goes with her, because a ski patroller knows when to call it a night.

My call: New Mexico Lobos 28, Nevada Wolf Pack 18.
