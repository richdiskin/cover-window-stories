Dark Mode Vols Host No. 8 Kentucky, and Neyland Gets Loud

Kentucky Wildcats (7-1) at Tennessee Volunteers (6-2)

6:15 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Neyland Stadium · ESPN

ShareXFacebook

Kentucky vs Tennessee prediction for Homecoming in Knoxville: the theme this year is All Roads Lead to Tennessee, and on Saturday at 6:15 PM ET the road from Lexington ends at Neyland Stadium, where the Vols will be wearing their Dark Mode uniforms and the whole place will be loud.

Homecoming week has been busy. Monica Abbott is the grand marshal. The parade steps off Friday afternoon at 4 p.m. on Volunteer Boulevard and finishes at Neyland. The Europa and the Bull fountain on campus has been dyed orange, and there is Smokey's Howl on the schedule too. If you are a Kentucky fan, you are walking into an orange wall.

I got two old hands to talk it over.

Rhett Abernathy-Coyle grew up on a horse farm outside Lexington and still talks about football like a horseman sizing up a yearling. Earl Haskew, who everyone calls Rocky Top, was a long snapper for the Vols and brings a banjo to every tailgate he can find.

"Here's the thing nobody in Knoxville wants to hear," Rhett said. "We're 7-1. We're the better team on paper."

Earl plucked a string. "Paper doesn't play in Neyland."

Kentucky has been a story all fall. The Wildcats are 7-1, scoring 32.9 points a game and allowing 24.8. The only loss was 17-45 to Alabama back in September. Since then, it has been one escape after another. They won 35-34 in overtime at South Carolina, then 35-34 in overtime against LSU the next week. Then they went to Oklahoma and won 21-13, and beat Vanderbilt 34-17.

"Kenny Minchey has thrown one interception all year," Rhett said. "One. He has 1,755 yards and 14 touchdowns. He doesn't panic."

That is the heart of the Kentucky case. CJ Baxter leads the rushers with 487 yards and 8 touchdowns, Kenny Darby is the top receiver at 507 yards, Grant Godfrey has 72 tackles and Mi'Quise Humphrey-Grace has 5 sacks.

Earl put the banjo down for this part. "Tennessee is 6-2. We give up 16.2 a game. We held Alabama to 10 points at home. We lost to Texas by three and at South Carolina by three. That's it."

He's right about that. The two Tennessee losses were 17-20 to Texas and 24-27 at South Carolina. The Vols score 32.2 a game. DeSean Bishop is the engine, with 957 rushing yards and 11 touchdowns, and he carried 32 times for 120 yards at South Carolina. Mike Matthews leads the receivers with 528. On defense, Tyree Weathersby has 7 sacks and Amare Campbell has 52 tackles.

"What about the quarterback?" Rhett asked.

George MacIntyre has taken over the throwing, with 728 yards. Faizon Brandon took the most throws in the first four games and MacIntyre has done it since.

"George doesn't need to be a hero," Earl said. "Hand it to Bishop. Let the defense work. That's how we beat Alabama 26-10."

Rhett had one more card to play. "Two overtime wins. You know what that tells me? We don't lose close games."

"You know what it tells me?" Earl said. "You're lucky."

Then there is the history, which Earl was happy to bring up. Over the ten meetings from 2016 to 2025, Tennessee won eight and Kentucky won two. Last year it was 56-34 for the Vols. In 2021 it was 45-42.

"2020," Rhett said. "34-7. I remember that one."

"Everybody remembers the one they won," Earl said, and started playing Rocky Top on the banjo.

So here is how I see it. Kentucky has the better season, and Minchey is not going to give the game away. But Tennessee at home, on Homecoming, in Dark Mode, with Bishop running behind that front, is a different animal. The Vols defense has not given up more than 27 all year. The Wildcats will move the ball some and Minchey will make a few big throws. But late in the game, Bishop carries it, the crowd sings, and Tennessee pulls away by a touchdown.

Rhett says Kentucky has earned some respect. Earl says Neyland hands it out one Saturday at a time.

My call: Tennessee Volunteers 30, Kentucky Wildcats 23.
