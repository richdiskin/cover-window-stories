Navy Tigers, Military Appreciation, and Arkansas Comes Looking for a Fight

Arkansas Razorbacks (3-5) at Auburn Tigers (4-4)

12:45 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Jordan-Hare Stadium · SEC Network

ShareXFacebook

Arkansas Razorbacks vs Auburn Tigers prediction for Saturday, November 7: Auburn has made this its Military Appreciation game, the Tigers are wearing navy, and Bubba Fayette-Dorsey drove down from the Ozarks with a cooler, a red hat and a speech he has been practicing since Monday.

Lacey Bellamy met him on the Plains with a tray of biscuits and a look that said she had heard the speech before.

"Woo Pig Sooie," Bubba announced to the parking lot, to the trees, to anybody within a hundred yards. "We are here. We are hungry. We are 3-5 and we do not care."

"You should care a little," Lacey said. "Y'all scored 10 points against Missouri."

That landed, and Bubba knew it. Arkansas has had a long fall. The Hogs beat North Alabama, Tulsa and Vanderbilt, and then lost to Utah 10-43, to Georgia 17-45, to Texas A&M 7-34, to Tennessee 17-24 and to Missouri 10-34. They score 19.6 points a game and give up 27.6. Bubba teaches science to grade-schoolers in the Ozarks, and he will tell you that numbers do not lie. He will also tell you that he does not have to like them.

"KJ Jackson has thrown for 1,673 yards," he said. "Chris Marshall has 633 receiving yards. That is a real receiver. And we beat y'all 24-14 in 2024, right in front of everybody."

"And we beat y'all 33-24 last year," Lacey said, handing him a biscuit like a peace offering. "Eat. You're going to need your strength."

Auburn has its own bruises. The Tigers opened 4-2 and then ran into LSU and Ole Miss. LSU won 38-17 at Jordan-Hare. Ole Miss won 45-15 in Oxford. Auburn is 4-4, it scores 24.1 a game and it gives up 26.8. Lacey was a sorority president on this campus, and she runs a tailgate the way she ran chapter meetings: on time, with a plan, and with no patience for sad talk.

"We beat Georgia 27-24 in Athens," she said. "Nobody gets to tell me this team is done."

Bubba could not argue with that one either.

Her case starts with Byrum Brown. He has thrown for 1,560 yards and run for 484 more, with 7 rushing touchdowns. When the throws are not there, he puts his head down and goes. Behind him on defense, Elijah Melendez has 63 tackles and 4 sacks. Lacey calls him the hall monitor, because nobody gets down the hall without permission, and she is very proud of that joke.

Bubba's case is simpler. Arkansas has to protect Jackson, let him find Marshall and keep the game close enough that Auburn begins to feel the last two weeks. Ja'Quavion Smith has 53 tackles for the Hogs, and Quincy Rhodes Jr. has 3 sacks. If they can get Brown on the ground early, Bubba thinks the crowd gets nervous.

"Your crowd will be loud early," he said. "And quiet by halftime."

"Our crowd is going to be standing for the veterans," Lacey said, and she meant it. "Then it will be loud all day."

That part they agree on. Military Appreciation Day on the Plains is the kind of afternoon that makes even a visiting Hog take his hat off. Bubba already promised he would stand for the salute. He also promised to call the Hogs right after, which Lacey has decided to allow, once.

Here is how it shakes out. Brown makes enough plays with his legs to keep Auburn in front, and Melendez makes Jackson uncomfortable all afternoon. But Arkansas does not fold. Marshall gets loose a couple of times, the Hogs kick a field goal or two, and Auburn never quite gets the runaway Lacey wants. The Tigers snap the two-game skid. Bubba drives home with a full stomach, an empty cooler and a fresh reason to call the Hogs next fall.

Lacey says he can keep the biscuit recipe. Bubba says he would rather keep the score.

My call: Auburn Tigers 29, Arkansas Razorbacks 19.
