Cowboys Crash Kansas State's Holiday Weekend in Manhattan

Oklahoma State Cowboys (5-3) at Kansas State Wildcats (4-4)

1:00 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Bill Snyder Family Stadium · TNT, HBO Max

ShareXFacebook

Oklahoma State Cowboys vs Kansas State Wildcats prediction for Saturday, November 7: downtown Manhattan is throwing its Holiday Open House this weekend, the biggest shopping days of the year on Poyntz Avenue, and Dusty Lockridge has decided that a man from Stillwater can shop and gloat at the same time.

Hattie Brandvold has decided otherwise.

"You can buy a scarf," she told him outside a boutique. "You cannot buy a road win."

Dusty grinned under his hat. He is an ag-econ grad who explains offense with rodeo timing, and he thinks this whole thing comes down to eight seconds of nerve. "Drew Mestemaker has thrown for 2,649 yards," he said. "Twenty-five touchdowns. We score 41.1 points a game, Hattie. That is not a team. That is a stampede."

Hattie is an agronomy grad, and she explains defense with crop rotation. You plant one thing, you let the field rest, you plant another, and you never let the soil get lazy. "A stampede runs out of grass," she said. "We give up 21.9 points a game. You give up 27.1. And you lost 49-51 at Iowa State last week."

That was a wild one. Mestemaker threw 5 touchdowns in Ames. Caleb Hawkins ran for 194 yards. Justin Bowick caught 7 balls for 124 yards and 2 scores. And the Cowboys still lost by two. A week before that they put 57 on Colorado. Two weeks before that they lost 32-35 at Houston. Oklahoma State is 5-3, and almost every Saturday ends with somebody's scoreboard operator needing a nap.

"We beat Oregon," Dusty said. "Thirty-nine to thirty-one. Remember that?"

"I remember 14-6," Hattie said. "Last year. And 42-20 the year before."

Kansas State has won the last two meetings. Over the last ten, it is 5-5, which tells you how stubborn this series is. Neither side gets to keep the bragging rights for long.

The Wildcats have their own worries, and Hattie does not hide from them. They are 4-4. They lost 24-27 in overtime at Colorado last week, and 17-28 at Arizona State the week before. Avery Johnson leads the team with 1,301 yards through the air, but Jacob Knuth threw the most at Arizona State, and Blake Barnett threw the most in Boulder, with 3 touchdowns. Three different quarterbacks have led the way in three weeks.

"Crop rotation," Hattie said. "We are resting the field."

"You are guessing," Dusty said.

What she trusts is the defense. Jayden Bryant has 4 sacks. Logan Bartley has 54 tackles from the back of it. Rodney Fields Jr. has 487 rushing yards and 5 touchdowns, and when Fields gets going, Manhattan gets loud. Hattie wants the Wildcats to run the ball, keep Mestemaker on the sideline and make this an afternoon game in the old Bill Snyder way: patient, physical and a little bit mean.

Dusty wants the opposite. He wants tempo. He wants Hawkins, who has 906 rushing yards, to get the Wildcats back on their heels, and then he wants Bowick, with 731 receiving yards and 9 touchdowns, running free behind them. "Rodeo timing," he said. "You let the bull think he's winning, then you ride him for eight."

Hattie said that is not how bulls work. Dusty said that is exactly how bulls work. They let it go.

Here is how it shakes out. Mestemaker gets his yards, because he always does, and the Cowboys put up points in bunches. But Kansas State runs it, Fields finds room late, and the home crowd keeps this thing tight into the fourth quarter. It comes down to one more drive, and Oklahoma State gets it. Barely. Dusty celebrates like he roped a calf. Hattie says she has seen this movie, and that the ending changes in Stillwater next year.

Sunday, the Flint Hills Card Show sets up at Manhattan Town Center. Dusty wants to look for an old Cowboys card. Hattie says she will help him, as long as he pays for lunch.

My call: Oklahoma State Cowboys 32, Kansas State Wildcats 31.
