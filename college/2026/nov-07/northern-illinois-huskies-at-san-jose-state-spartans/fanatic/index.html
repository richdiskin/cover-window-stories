Farm Girl Versus Software: Northern Illinois Heads to San José State

Northern Illinois Huskies (0-8) at San José State Spartans (4-5)

4:00 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · CEFCU Stadium · MW+

ShareXFacebook

Northern Illinois Huskies vs San José State Spartans prediction for Saturday, November 7: Nola Brandvik has flown in from DeKalb wearing a seed-company cap, and Tessa Nakagawa-Ruiz, who writes software in the South Bay, meets her in the parking lot with a laptop under one arm and a sign that says "Ship it to prod."

"What does that mean?" Nola asks.

"It means we're ready," Tessa says. "Tested all week. No bugs."

"Out on the farm we'd call that ready for harvest," Nola says.

Saturday is Parent & Family Weekend at San José State, and the Spartans are holding a Military & First Responders Salute to Service with Veterans Day coming up. Kickoff is 4:00 p.m. ET, which is 1:00 in the afternoon in San José, and the gameday is presented by Webcor. Students get in free with their Tower ID.

"Free with an ID card," Nola says. "Back home we'd charge them in corn."

Tessa's parents are coming in for Family Weekend, and she has already promised them a win. Nola says her grandpa used to say not to count your bushels until the combine is in the shed.

Now the football.

Nola goes first, and she's honest about it. Northern Illinois is 0-8. The Huskies score 16.2 a game and give up 37.6. But they lost only 24-31 at UNLV last week, and they have real players. Taron Dickens has thrown for 1,617 yards. DeAree Rogers has 64 catches for 633 yards. Telly Johnson Jr. has 574 rushing yards. Jack Murray leads the defense with 58 tackles.

"Knee-high by the Fourth of July," Nola says. "We were a little late planting this year. But the crop is coming."

"That's a long sprint cycle," Tessa says, kindly. "But I've shipped worse code than an 0-8 roster. Every team gets a hotfix eventually."

"We're due," Nola says. "My grandpa always said the late corn is the best."

Tessa takes over, scrolling through her laptop. Luke Weaver has thrown for 1,810 yards and 11 touchdowns. Carson Clark leads the receivers with 55 catches for 642 yards. Jabari Bates has 649 rushing yards. On defense, Jayland McGlothen has 6 sacks, and Dylan Lee leads the team with 78 tackles. San José State gives up only 22.3 a game.

"McGlothen is our firewall," Tessa says. "Six sacks. Nothing gets through."

"And your offense?" Nola asks.

Tessa winces. San José State scores 19.9 a game, and the Spartans lost 16-21 to New Mexico last week. Weaver has 8 interceptions this season.

"Some days you push to production and it breaks," Tessa says. "Rollback, patch, try again."

The two teams haven't played each other since 2016, so neither side has fresh bragging rights. Nola says that's fine, because DeKalb people don't brag anyway, they just show up on time.

In San José, the game comes in waves. The Family Weekend crowd fills in late, and the first quarter is slow, two teams still warming up. McGlothen gets to Dickens early, and Weaver finds Clark for the first touchdown. Northern Illinois answers with Johnson running hard and Rogers making tough catches. But the Spartans defense takes over in the second half. Bates grinds out yards, Lee makes stop after stop, and San José State pulls away.

Nola shakes Tessa's hand and says there's always next season's harvest. Tessa closes her laptop and says the release went fine, then invites Nola to dinner with her parents, who want to hear all about the corn.

My call: San José State Spartans 30, Northern Illinois Huskies 18.
