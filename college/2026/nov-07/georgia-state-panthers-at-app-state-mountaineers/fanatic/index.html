Heroes Day in Boone: App State Welcomes Georgia State to the Mountains

Georgia State Panthers (4-4) at App State Mountaineers (5-3)

2:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Kidd Brewer Stadium · ESPN+

ShareXFacebook

Georgia State Panthers vs App State Mountaineers prediction for Saturday, November 7: this is the App State Heroes Day Game, a celebration of military members, veterans and their families, and Zeb Whitlock has been up since dawn getting the Heroes Day Tailgate ready on the McKinney Alumni Center patio.

Malik Odom drove up from Atlanta with a thermos and a long memory.

"Ten years," Malik said, stepping out of the car into the mountain air. "Ten meetings. Zero wins. I am here to end that today."

Zeb handed him a cup of coffee. Zeb makes his coffee strong enough to stand a spoon in, and he explains football like the weather on the Blue Ridge: you respect it, you dress for it, and you never assume it will stay nice. "You said that last year," he said. "It was 41-20."

It was. App State has won all 10 meetings since 2016. Some were close, like 17-13 in 2020 and 33-26 in 2024. Most were not. The Mountaineers won 56-27 in 2019 and 45-16 in 2021. Malik remembers every one.

But this fall feels a little different to him, and he has reasons.

Georgia State scores 33.9 points a game. Cameran Brown has 14 touchdown throws. He threw 6 against Old Dominion in a 42-10 win. Rodshaun Dorsey has 459 receiving yards and 8 touchdowns. Qwantavius Wiggins has 446 rushing yards. And Xavier Esqillion has 7 sacks, which is the kind of number that keeps a quarterback awake at night.

"We beat Kennesaw State 31-17 on the road," Malik said. "We beat Northern Illinois. We can play."

Zeb nodded. He does not argue with facts. He just adds more.

"You also lost three in a row," he said. "Twenty-two to forty-nine at James Madison. Thirty-two to thirty-eight at Arkansas State. Twenty to thirty-one to Coastal last week."

That is the other part. Georgia State is 4-4. Brown led the Panthers in throws for the first six games, and Ayden Pereira led them the last two. Malik says the Panthers can win with either. Zeb says that is what people say when they are not sure.

App State has its own fog to drive through. The Mountaineers are 5-3, but they managed 3 points at home against Old Dominion in October and 3 points at Georgia Southern last week. Malachi Singleton has 1,915 yards through the air and threw for 331 against Charlotte, but he went 5 for 23 against Old Dominion. Sam Pickett III has 560 receiving yards. Jaquari Lewis has 8 rushing touchdowns. On defense, Colton Phares has 63 tackles and Caleb Sandstrom has 5 sacks.

"When we are good, we are really good," Zeb said. "We beat James Madison 34-31 in overtime. We won 35-32 at Coastal."

"And when you are bad," Malik said, "you score 3."

Zeb poured himself more coffee. He did not answer that one.

Here is how it shakes out. Georgia State comes out loose and scores early, and for a while Malik looks like a man about to make history. Esqillion gets home a couple of times. But App State settles down, Lewis finds the end zone, Pickett makes a big grab on third down, and the home crowd carries the Mountaineers through a tight fourth quarter. It is close all afternoon, closer than most of the last ten. App State hangs on.

Malik will drive home with an eleventh story to tell. Zeb says he is welcome back next year, same patio, same coffee.

Before kickoff, they both stand with the veterans and their families at the tailgate. On Heroes Day, the series can wait for a few minutes. Then it is back to football, and back to ten years and counting.

My call: App State Mountaineers 30, Georgia State Panthers 27.
