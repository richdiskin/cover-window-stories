Washington Throws Penn State a Homecoming Party, 105 Years Later

Penn State Nittany Lions (4-4) at Washington Huskies (5-3)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 7 · Husky Stadium · FS1

ShareXFacebook

Penn State Nittany Lions vs Washington Huskies prediction for Saturday, November 7: Penn State is coming to Husky Stadium for the first time in 105 years, and Washington made sure the welcome party is Homecoming. Vangie Tollefsrud says she has been waiting for this since she was a freshman on the water at dawn, and Luann Kessler says she has been waiting since 1921, which is not true but she said it with a straight face.

The last time Penn State played on Montlake was the final game of the 1921 season. It was the seventh game ever played at Husky Stadium. This time the Huskies are calling it the Purple Reign Game, and it opens with the Blanket Parade, when former Washington letterwinners stand along the field to welcome the team back out. Kickoff is 3:30 p.m. ET.

"A blanket parade," Luann says. "Good for you. In Happy Valley we just call that November."

Vangie sets down her third coffee of the morning. "Luann, the blankets are for you. We're polite people. We figured you'd be cold."

The Nittany Lions brought company. Penn State's alumni group booked its first-ever Sailgate, a cruise on Lake Washington aboard the Spirit of Seattle with the Pep Band trumpets, the cheerleaders and the Nittany Lion himself, and it sold out. Washington parents are coming in too, since it doubles as UW Family Weekend, complete with a family section and a scarf.

"Penn State fans on a boat on my lake," Vangie says. "I rowed that lake in the rain for four years. You people get a buffet."

"We get a buffet and a trumpet section," Luann says. "Try to keep up."

Now the football, because these two came to argue.

Luann goes first. Rocco Becht has thrown for 1,839 yards with 13 touchdowns and 5 interceptions. Chase Sowell is the go-to guy with 34 catches for 510 yards. Carson Hansen leads the ground game with 374 yards, but the back everybody in State College is talking about this week is Cam Wallace, who ran 22 times for 135 yards in the 31-17 win over Purdue. And then there's Tony Rojas, the linebacker who leads the team in sacks with 5 and tackles with 57. Penn State scores 29.0 a game and gives up 18.6.

"Linebacker U," Luann says. "Rojas is the Peachy Paterno of linebackers. Friendly on the outside, and then he takes your quarterback home."

Vangie takes the floor. Demond Williams Jr. has thrown for 1,495 yards and 9 touchdowns, but Elijah Brown has been the guy taking most of the throws in the last three games, including 19 of 30 for 136 yards in the 17-10 win at Nebraska. Chris Lawson made that day happen with 12 catches for 155 yards, and he has 44 catches for 638 yards this season. Alex McLaughlin leads the defense with 56 tackles, and Jacob Manu has 2.5 sacks. Washington scores 26.5 a game and allows 21.1.

"Lawson against Nebraska was a coxswain calling the stroke," Vangie says. "Every catch right on the beat."

"Seventeen to ten," Luann says. "Very rhythmic. Very low."

"We won in Lincoln in November," Vangie says. "Nobody scores in Lincoln in November. You lost 18-21 at Michigan."

Luann does not respond to that. She looks out the window at the drizzle like it owes her money.

Both teams have been through it. Penn State dropped four straight in the middle of the season, including 20-24 to Wisconsin, 23-31 to USC and that 18-21 loss in Ann Arbor, before getting right against Purdue. Washington lost three in a row too, to Minnesota, at USC and to Iowa, then won at Purdue and at Nebraska.

"We have both been to the dark place," Luann says.

"Ours has better coffee," Vangie says.

There's one more thing Luann wants on the record. The last time these two met, in 2024, Penn State won 35-6.

"That was in State College," Vangie says. "Rain doesn't count when it's yours."

Picture Saturday on Montlake. The Blanket Parade gets the place loud early, and the Washington defense, led by McLaughlin, keeps the Penn State run game honest in the first half. Becht and Sowell find a rhythm before halftime, and Wallace grinds out the hard yards. Brown looks for Lawson every time Washington needs a first down, and Rojas makes the Husky backfield miserable at least twice. Nobody gets away. It comes down to one last Washington drive and a kick that splits the uprights while the rain gets heavier.

Vangie leaps out of her seat and nearly spills her coffee. Luann says Linebacker U doesn't cry, then cries a little, then buys a Washington scarf anyway because it's cold.

My call: Washington Huskies 24, Penn State Nittany Lions 23.
