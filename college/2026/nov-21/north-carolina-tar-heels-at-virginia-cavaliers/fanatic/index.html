GameDay Report

North Carolina at Virginia: Two 5-5 Teams, One Bowl Spot in Charlottesville

Both teams are 5-5, the winner becomes bowl-eligible, and Scott Stadium gets the 12:30 kickoff that decides it. North Carolina and Virginia have been playing each other longer than most of the country has been playing anybody, and Virginia's own notes have called this the South's Oldest Rivalry, tied for the second-most played series between FBS opponents. The history will take care of itself. The sixth win will not.

The week has been about who can actually walk out there. On3 and the Fayetteville Observer, tracking the ACC reports, have had North Carolina quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. listed as questionable, with freshman Travis Burgess the likely starter if Edwards cannot go. Pass rusher Melkart Abou-Jaoude was listed out with a broken hand, and safety Greg Smith was listed out for the first half. Virginia's list has been even longer, with reports of 13 Cavaliers out, including linebackers Maddox Marcellus and Landon Danley and safety Corey Costner. Those are midweek and Thursday updates, not a final Saturday card, but they tell you how fragile this one is for two teams that cannot afford a wasted possession.

A 5-5 home finale against a 5-5 rival is the kind of afternoon Charlottesville was built for, even when the rosters are patched. Somebody is going to leave Scott Stadium with a bowl. Somebody is going to leave with a winter that starts too soon. The oldest rivalry on the block is just the setting. The sixth win is the whole story.

North Carolina and Virginia meet for the 131st time on Saturday, and Virginia's own release says that ties this for the second-most played rivalry between FBS opponents. They call it the South's Oldest Rivalry. This year, it might as well be called the South's Most Even Rivalry, because both teams arrive at Scott Stadium at 5-5, and the winner gets the sixth win that makes a team bowl eligible.

Virginia insider Prescott Ashby-Lowell adjusts his bow tie and points out that Mr. Jefferson would appreciate the symmetry. "Two schools, ten games each, five wins each," he says. "The Grounds will settle it. They usually do."

He says "the Grounds" on purpose. Prescott has never said the other word in his life and will not start for a visiting team.

North Carolina insider Ainsley Pemberton has a different read on the symmetry. "We're 5-5 against a schedule with Clemson, Notre Dame and Miami on it," she says. "Virginia went 5-5 by beating Norfolk State and Delaware. And yes, we beat Duke in Durham. I mention that every chance I get. It's the best thing that happened in October."

That's the second season of Bill Belichick in Chapel Hill, and it's been a season of close calls. The Tar Heels lost to Syracuse 31-30 in overtime and to Miami 38-35. Last Saturday, they lost to Louisville 16-14 at home. They also won at Pitt 21-17 and at Duke 24-20. Seven of their ten games were decided by eight points or fewer.

Billy Edwards Jr. has been steady through all of it. He's thrown for 2,247 yards with 18 touchdowns and six interceptions. Jordan Shipp leads the team with 57 catches for 703 yards. On defense, linebacker Derek McDonald has 100 tackles, and Xavier Lewis has seven sacks.

"Derek McDonald hits like a Duke fan's feelings after we leave Durham," Ainsley says. "Hard and often."

Virginia's season has come in waves. The Cavaliers started 3-1, then lost four of five, including a 51-7 loss at Wake Forest on Oct. 31. Then came last Saturday, when Virginia beat California 36-7 at home to snap a three-game skid. Beau Pribula has been efficient all year, with 1,727 passing yards, 15 touchdowns and only three interceptions. Da'Shawn Martin has seven touchdown catches, and Jekail Middlebrook leads the team with 540 rushing yards. Matthew Fobbs-White has five and a half sacks.

"Pribula makes good decisions," Prescott says. "Jefferson believed an educated citizenry was the foundation of the republic. Beau believes in throwing it to the open man. Same principle."

The history is a coin flip. Virginia has won six of the last ten meetings, but those games swing wildly. North Carolina won 59-39 in 2021 and 41-14 here two years ago. Virginia won 44-41 in 2020 and 17-16 in Chapel Hill last October. Prescott remembers the one-point game the best.

"Seventeen to sixteen," he says. "A gentleman's margin."

"A lucky margin," Ainsley says. "We'll take this one by a field goal and drive home before the traffic."

Saturday is also Virginia's home finale and Senior Day, and Prescott wants the Cavaliers' upperclassmen to leave the Grounds with something to remember. A bowl trip would do it. So would beating North Carolina in the 131st meeting.

Here's the matchup. North Carolina allows 22.1 points a game. Virginia allows 21.5. North Carolina scores 25.8. Virginia scores 26.9. Those numbers are close enough to be a Jefferson riddle. The difference may be up front. Lewis and Fobbs-White could each decide a series, and Pribula and Edwards have both been careful with the ball, so a single turnover could be the whole game.

Ainsley has her worries. Edwards went 10 of 14 for 120 yards in the Louisville loss, and the Tar Heels managed only 14 points. "We need the Miami Billy, not the Louisville Billy," she says. "Miami Billy threw four touchdowns."

Prescott has his own worry, and it's Wake Forest. "When this team loses, it doesn't lose politely," he says. "We gave up 51 in Winston-Salem. We gave up 38 in Tallahassee. I'd prefer not to discuss either."

I think this is a one-score game into the fourth quarter, with both offenses doing just enough. Virginia has the home crowd, the seniors and the better rhythm after the Cal win. North Carolina has the tougher résumé and a quarterback who's played in a lot of tight finishes this fall.

Ainsley will probably spend the last five minutes roasting Duke for comfort. Prescott will straighten his bow tie and hum the Good Old Song. One of them will go home with a bowl game. The other will have to wait a year to even things out.

My call: North Carolina Tar Heels 24, Virginia Cavaliers 22.
