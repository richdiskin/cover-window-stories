LSU vs Tennessee: Lane Kiffin Returns to Neyland on Senior Day

LSU Tigers (6-4) at Tennessee Volunteers (8-2)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Neyland Stadium · ABC

XFacebook

Lane Kiffin coached exactly one season at Tennessee, 2009, and on Saturday the LSU Tigers vs Tennessee Volunteers game brings him back to Neyland Stadium as the man running LSU. It is also Senior Day in Knoxville, the Vol Navy will be tied up along the Tennessee River from noon, and Earl "Rocky Top" Haskew and Cece Thibodeaux have been arguing about it since the schedule came out.

Earl opens with a banjo roll, as always, then sets the instrument across his knees like a man about to tell a long story.

"He's been back before," Earl says. "Came back with Alabama as a coordinator. Came back with Ole Miss. Every time, this place lets him know it hasn't forgotten. But here's the thing, Cece. Saturday isn't about him. Saturday is about our seniors and a team that controls its own path to the playoff."

Cece, who grew up between crawfish boils and film sessions in Baton Rouge, does not let him finish the sentence. "Saturday is about LSU being hungry. We had Texas beat. We led 21-10 in the second half and lost 22-21 on a throw in the final seconds. Do you know what that does to a team, Earl? It makes it mean."

Tennessee is 8-2, and the way the Vols win is not complicated. They run DeSean Bishop until the other team gets tired of tackling him. Bishop has 230 carries for 1,163 yards and 13 touchdowns, and he carried 34 times last week at Texas A&M in a 22-7 win that felt like a long, slow squeeze. George MacIntyre has thrown the most in each of the last six games, and his job is simple: hit Mike Matthews when the safeties creep up, and never give the ball away. MacIntyre threw only 17 times in College Station and completed 13.

"Rocky Top football," Earl says. "Run it, hit it, run it again. Tyree Weathersby has seven sacks. Arion Carter has 66 tackles. Our defense gives up 14.3 a game. Fourteen point three, Cece. You'll need a map to find the end zone."

Cece has her own map. LSU scores 35.5 a game, and Sam Leavitt is the reason. He has thrown for 2,797 yards and 19 touchdowns, including 388 yards at Auburn and 380 against Alabama. Jackson Harris has 55 catches for 864 yards and nine touchdowns. Dilin Jones has 838 rushing yards and ran for 125 against Texas. And on defense, Princewill Umanmielen has nine and a half sacks off the end.

"Umanmielen is going to introduce himself to MacIntyre early," Cece says. "And often. He's very polite. He just doesn't leave."

The worry for Cece is the turnovers. Leavitt threw three interceptions against Texas and three more against Louisiana Tech, and he has 12 for the year. In a stadium this loud on third down, a quarterback who forces throws can turn a close game into a long ride home.

"He'll be fine," she says. "He threw for 380 against Alabama. Loud doesn't scare him."

Earl strums a minor chord and gives her the history. Tennessee has won 21 of the 34 games in the series, against 10 LSU wins and 3 ties, going all the way back to a 0-0 tie in Knoxville in 1925. The Vols crushed LSU 40-13 in Baton Rouge the last time these two met, in 2022. But here's the part Earl hums quietly: the Vols have not beaten LSU in Neyland Stadium since 2001.

"Twenty-five years," Cece says. "Tell me again how the stadium remembers."

"The stadium remembers," Earl says. "It's just been patient."

Senior Day at Neyland is one of the great scenes in the sport, and Earl gets a little choked up talking about it, a whole class of players who stuck around to put Tennessee back in the playoff talk. Cece respects it. She will clap for those seniors. Then she will try to ruin their afternoon.

Here's how it plays out. LSU strikes first, Leavitt finding Harris on a deep ball that quiets the checkerboard end zones for a minute. Tennessee answers the only way it knows, with Bishop, again and again, until the Vols are inside the red zone and the crowd is thundering. Umanmielen gets a big sack that forces a field goal. The game swings back and forth through the third quarter, both defenses bending, both offenses landing punches. Late in the fourth, Weathersby gets home on third down, and the Vols take over with a chance to put it away. Bishop does the rest, and a field goal makes it a two-point game that LSU cannot quite get back.

Earl plays the first bars of Rocky Top. Cece puts her hands over her ears and smiles anyway.

"He'll be back," she says.

"We'll be here," Earl says.

My call: Tennessee Volunteers 26, LSU Tigers 24.
