Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders (5-5) at Sam Houston Bearkats (1-9)

GameDay Report

Middle Tennessee at Sam Houston: A Bowl Bid on the Road, a Skid at Home

Middle Tennessee is 5-5 and one win from becoming bowl-eligible, and the Blue Raiders' official preview made Saturday's 1 p.m. Central kickoff at Bowers Stadium sound exactly that simple. The trip is to Huntsville, Texas, against a 1-9 Sam Houston team that has lost seven in a row and is spending the afternoon on Senior Day. The series is tied, this is only the fifth meeting, and it is the third as conference foes. That is a short history, and it is a heavy afternoon for both locker rooms.

The Houstonian, the campus paper, described a Bearkats team trying to snap that skid in front of its seniors, with co-interim talk on the home side about putting pressure on the quarterback. Middle Tennessee coach Derek Mason, in the same week-of coverage, called Sam Houston a roster with "weapons on both sides of the ball," which is a polite way of saying a 1-9 football team can still wreck a bowl trip if you let it. The Blue Raiders have been close enough to six wins to taste it. They have not put it away.

A 5-5 team on the road, a 1-9 team at home, and a Senior Day crowd that has not had much to hold onto: that is the entire setting. Middle Tennessee wants a sixth win and a December that still has football in it. Sam Houston wants to send its seniors out with the first good hour in two months. Somebody's winter changes at Bowers Stadium.

Lem Coxwell has a campfire story about Middle Tennessee, and it starts in 1956. "Pull up a stump," he says. "The first time these two teams met, it was the first of December, and Sam Houston won 27-13. Then nobody saw each other for 35 years."

That's true, and it's on Sam Houston's own game page. The series between the Bearkats and the Blue Raiders is tied 2-2. Sam Houston won in 1956 and won 23-20 in 2023. Middle Tennessee won 20-19 in 1991 and 31-17 last November. Saturday's game at Bowers Stadium breaks the tie.

It's also Fan Appreciation Day and Senior Day in Huntsville, and Lem, Sam Houston's insider, says the fans have earned the thank-you more than usual this year.

"We're 1-9," he says, poking the fire. "Our folks kept coming. That's appreciation in both directions."

Middle Tennessee insider Opal Stonebriar is bringing a casserole dish to the visitors' section, because that's what she does. She hums "Blessed Assurance" when she's nervous, and she's been humming it since last Saturday.

"We lost 28-23 at Liberty," Opal says. "Liberty is 9-1, and we lost by five. I'm proud of those boys, and I'm nervous about this week, because a team that plays that hard on the road sometimes forgets to show up the next Saturday."

Opal's Blue Raiders are 5-5, and a sixth win would make them bowl eligible. They've been up and down. They lost 55-0 at Kansas in October, and they lost 44-28 at home to Kennesaw State. They've also beaten Delaware 33-30, won at FIU 26-23 and put up 51 on Western Kentucky on Nov. 7.

Roman Gagliano is the steady one. He's thrown for 2,101 yards with 18 touchdowns and only four interceptions. Antonio Martin leads the team with 750 rushing yards, and Cam'ron Lacy has 64 catches for 676 yards. Korey Smith is the tackling machine, with 96 stops.

"Roman takes care of the ball like it's my grandmother's china," Opal says. "Nobody touches the good dishes."

Lem's Bearkats have had a hard fall. They opened with a 24-21 loss at Troy and a 23-17 loss to Tulsa, two games that could have gone either way. They beat Nicholls 59-0. Then they lost seven straight, and they scored three points in three of those games.

"The fire's been low," Lem says. "But it's not out. You blow on the coals and it comes back."

Sam Houston's quarterback is the one doing the blowing. He's thrown for 1,850 yards with 14 touchdowns and five interceptions, and he threw for more than 300 yards in each of the first three games. Chris Reed leads the receivers with 485 yards and five touchdowns. Josh Alexander-Felton has five sacks, and linebacker Dean Ford has 56 tackles.

"Our quarterback had it going in September," Lem says. "That's the one I'm hoping shows up for Senior Day."

The trouble for Sam Houston is points. The Bearkats score 16.5 a game. But Middle Tennessee gives up 30.7, and that's the opening Lem is counting on. Middle Tennessee has allowed at least 20 points in nine of its ten games, and 42 or more three times.

Opal won't argue the point about her defense. "We make it exciting," she says. "My pastor says excitement builds faith. Our defense has built a lot of faith this year."

The game, I think, comes down to whether Sam Houston can score enough to stay in it. The Bearkats played Tulsa and Troy within a touchdown early in the season, and Middle Tennessee's defense has given up points to almost everyone. Gagliano should move the ball, and Middle Tennessee should win. But a 1-9 home team on Senior Day, with the old series tied, has every reason to fight for four quarters.

Lem will tell the 1956 story one more time at halftime, to anyone who stops by his fire. Opal will offer him a plate of casserole, and he'll take it.

"Good game," Lem will say. "That's three for you, two for us. We'll even it up next time."

"Bless you, Lem," Opal will say. "Bring a bigger stump."

My call: Middle Tennessee Blue Raiders 27, Sam Houston Bearkats 24.
