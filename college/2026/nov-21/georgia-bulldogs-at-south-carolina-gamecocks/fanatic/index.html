GameDay Report

Georgia at South Carolina: Senior Night Against the No. 1 Team in the Country

South Carolina put Senior Night on the home-finale calendar at Williams-Brice Stadium, with No. 1 Georgia in town and ABC on the 7:30 Eastern kickoff. The Gamecocks billed themselves as No. 21. The live card on this site lists South Carolina at No. 17 and Georgia at No. 1. ESPN's game header also showed the Gamecocks at No. 21. This story stays with the card and flags the school's number.

Georgia is 9-1 after a 48-21 win against Missouri, a night Gunner Stockton went 18-of-24 for 252 yards and Nate Frazier ran for three scores. Stockton has 2,109 passing yards, 21 touchdowns and four interceptions. South Carolina is 6-4 after a 6-34 loss at Arkansas, when Cutter Woods went 24-of-43 for 271 yards with three interceptions. LaNorris Sellers still leads the Gamecocks in season passing (2,100 yards, 17 touchdowns) and rushing (637 yards, seven scores). That last box was Woods. This story will not turn a season leader into Saturday's starter without a designation.

A Senior Night against the No. 1 team in the country, a home finale at Williams-Brice, and a ranking the school and the card do not share: that is Saturday night in Columbia, South Carolina. If Georgia's running game looks like three scores from Frazier again, the farewell will be a long one. If South Carolina protects the football better than it did in Fayetteville, Williams-Brice will have a chance to make the last drive loud. Officially, this is a Senior Night. On the field, it is still No. 1 against a 6-4 home team that scored six last week.

Somewhere in the southwest corner of Williams-Brice Stadium on Saturday night, the lights will drop, the opening notes of "2001" will roll out of the speakers, and South Carolina will run onto the field for the last time this season. Beau Cheraw has been humming it since Monday. He hummed it at breakfast, he hummed it in the elevator, and he hummed it while Thad Okafor-Brandt tried to tell him about a vinyl reissue he found in Five Points.

Thad: You know where that entrance came from, right?

Beau: Tommy Suggs. Saw Elvis walk out to it, decided the Gamecocks deserved the same thing.

Thad: The band played it for two games in 1981 and nobody could hear it.

Beau: So they fixed the sound system, and in 1983 we ran out to it for real. Now his name is on the tunnel. The Tommy Suggs 2001 Gamecock Football Entrance.

Thad: I respect a man who takes his walk-up music that seriously.

That's the kind of night it is in Columbia: last home game, 7:30 kickoff, Georgia ranked No. 1 in the country and coming to town. The Gamecocks are hurting, too. A 34-6 loss at Arkansas last Saturday ended their College Football Playoff hopes, and it also snapped a four-game run that had included wins over Florida, Tennessee, Oklahoma and Texas A&M.

Beau: That Arkansas game was a bad dream. Cutter Woods threw 43 times, three interceptions, six points. I don't talk about it.

Thad: You just talked about it.

Beau: That was the last time.

Georgia, meanwhile, arrives at 9-1 and rolling. The Bulldogs beat Ole Miss 42-7 on the road and Missouri 48-21 at home in their last two, and the only blemish is a 27-24 loss to Auburn back in October. Gunner Stockton has thrown for 2,109 yards with 21 touchdowns and four interceptions. Nate Frazier ran for three touchdowns against Missouri, and Talyn Taylor leads the receivers with six touchdown catches. Chris Cole, the linebacker, leads the team in tackles with 64 and in sacks with five.

Thad: Georgia's defense is like a great album. No skips. Every track hits.

Beau: Every album has a skip.

Thad: Not this one. They give up 14.7 points a game. Play it front to back.

The history is lopsided. Georgia leads the series 55-19-2 and has won the last four meetings, including 24-14 in 2023. South Carolina's last win over the Bulldogs came in 2019, a 20-17 game, and the Gamecocks haven't beaten Georgia at home since 2014.

Beau: 2019. I still have the ticket stub in my wallet.

Thad: That's a lot of wallets ago.

Beau: I move it every time.

So what's the South Carolina case? It starts with LaNorris Sellers, who has thrown for 2,100 yards and 17 touchdowns and run for 637 yards and seven more scores. When he's had the ball this season, the Gamecocks have been hard to put away, losing at home to Mississippi State by seven and to Kentucky by one in overtime. Mazeo Bennett Jr. leads the receivers with 558 yards. Fred Johnson has 83 tackles, and Tomiwa Durojaiye has four sacks. At home, under the lights, with a crowd that knows it's the last time this year, Williams-Brice can make a good team flinch.

Thad: Georgia doesn't flinch. Georgia went into Tuscaloosa and won.

Beau: By three.

Thad: A win is a win.

Beau: And a three-point game in Tuscaloosa means a close one is possible here.

The way it goes: Georgia's defense makes life hard early, Stockton finds Taylor once or twice, and the Bulldogs build a lead by halftime. South Carolina gets a touchdown in the fourth to wake everybody back up, and the crowd stays long after the clock runs out to sing one more time.

Thad: Encore. That's what this is.

Beau: Encore is when people want more.

Thad: Exactly. They'll want another shot at us next year.

Beau hums the first four notes one more time, and even Thad stops talking to listen.

My call: Georgia Bulldogs 34, South Carolina Gamecocks 21.
