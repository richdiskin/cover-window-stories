GameDay Report

East Carolina at Army: Bowl Number Three Travels to West Point

East Carolina already has its bowl, and the Pirates' Friday game preview made sure nobody missed it. A 17-16 win at Charlotte last week made them bowl-eligible for a third straight season, the first time that has happened since 2012-14, and it sent them to West Point with a 6-4 record and a 4-2 mark in the American. Ashton Gray ran for two touchdowns in that game, and the defense held Charlotte to 200 yards of offense, including a scoreless second half in which the 49ers managed two first downs.

Army is 5-4 and 4-2 in the league, coming off a bye after a 24-21 loss to Air Force. Saturday is the 11th meeting in the series, which East Carolina leads 9-1, though the two have split the two conference games since Army joined the American in 2024. Kickoff is noon at Michie Stadium, on CBS Sports Network, in a place that always feels like it has more at stake than a November record. The Black Knights had the week to get their legs back. The Pirates had the week to prove the Charlotte win was a beginning, not a sigh of relief.

Blake Harrell, in his second full run as East Carolina's head coach, has the Pirates in a three-year bowl stretch and a 20-9 mark in 29 games as the school's official preview laid out. Army, under Jeff Monken, has to answer for a close loss to the Air Force and then for a visitor that just learned how to close a game on the road. West Point at noon does not need extra decoration. Two bowl-minded teams, a short list of first downs for the last opponent, and a stadium that will be loud for the home side if the Pirates let it get that way.

Scouts are coming to West Point on Saturday, and the ones who show up for East Carolina vs Army get to spend the day with cadets from the academy's Scoutmaster's Council and leave with an Army Football Scout Day patch. Army insider Capt. (Ret.) Thaddeus Rourke says Michie Stadium's final home game of the year, with Youth Football Day, Military Appreciation Day and the Class of 2027 Senior Day all on one ticket, is the best classroom on the Hudson, while East Carolina insider Bart Quidley says the Pirates are sailing up from Greenville to teach a lesson of their own.

Rourke: Quidley, I want you to picture it. Kids in their scout uniforms, cadets showing them how this place works, youth football players in the stands, and the seniors who'll be commissioned in the spring walking out for their last home game. That's not a promotion. That's a mission statement.

Bart: Captain, I'm moved. I really am. I'll be wearing my tricorn hat, so the scouts will have something to talk about at the next meeting, too.

There's a football game in the middle of all that, and it's a good one. East Carolina comes in at 6-4 and riding three straight wins. None of them was pretty. The Pirates beat Temple 17-9, South Florida 20-17 and Charlotte 17-16 last Saturday, when Ashton Gray ran 24 times for 88 yards and two scores.

Bart: Ugly wins are still wins, Captain. You don't hang the scoreboard in a museum. You hang the flag. Three in a row, hoist the colors.

Rourke: And Army is coming off a week of rest. We lost to Air Force 24-21 on Nov. 7. That one stung. We had a bye last Saturday, and a rested Army team is an organized Army team.

Army's offense starts and ends with Cale Hellums, and anybody who's watched this team knows it. Hellums has 908 rushing yards and 18 rushing touchdowns. He's thrown just 86 times all year. The Black Knights run, run again, then run somewhere else.

Bart: Eighteen rushing scores is a lot, Captain. I respect a quarterback who runs like he's late for formation. But Crews Law has 64 tackles for us, and our defense gives up 22.6 a game.

Rourke: Elijah Walton has 60 for us. Victor Afolabi has three sacks, and we don't even throw it enough to know what a sack feels like from the other side.

East Carolina's side of the ball is a little harder to pin down. The Pirates have gone back and forth between two quarterbacks, Emory Williams and Mitch Griffis, for most of the season. Williams has thrown for 1,034 yards and nine touchdowns. TJ Engleman Jr. leads the Pirates with 695 rushing yards, and Ray Ray Joseph Jr. is the top receiver.

Bart: Two quarterbacks means two plans, Captain. You'll be ready for one of them.

Rourke: We'll be ready for whichever one is holding the ball when our linebackers arrive.

The history here is short and split. Army won 45-28 at Michie in 2024. East Carolina won 28-6 in Greenville last September. Each side won at home, and the scouts in the stands won't care about either one.

Bart: That 28-6 game was a fine night in Greenville. I'll tell the scouts about it. It has a moral.

Rourke: The moral is to win at home. We intend to.

The weather will be November on the Hudson, and Army will run it until somebody makes it stop. For East Carolina, the plan is to make the game long enough to matter in the fourth quarter. The Pirates have won three close games in a row. They know how to stay in one.

Rourke: Our seniors walk out on Saturday knowing they've still got a December game left on the calendar against the team that matters most. But first, they finish at Michie. Copy that, and beat Navy.

Bart: Even when you're playing us, Captain?

Rourke: Especially then. It's a habit.

The scouts go home with their patches, the young football players go home with stories, and the seniors go out the way cadets do: on schedule and in order.

My call: Army Black Knights 28, East Carolina Pirates 21.
