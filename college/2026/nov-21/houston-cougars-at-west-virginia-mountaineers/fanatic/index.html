Houston Cougars (7-3) at West Virginia Mountaineers (5-5)

GameDay Report

Houston at West Virginia: Houston Finally Gets to Morgantown

Houston had never played in Morgantown until today. The Cougars' own preview made that the first thing to know: two previous Big 12 meetings with West Virginia were at TDECU Stadium, including a 2023 Hail Mary that was Houston's first conference win, and Saturday is the program's first trip to Milan Puskar Stadium. The Cougars are 7-3 and 4-3, No. 14 in the live rankings we publish, and they arrive after a 42-32 loss at Colorado that knocked them out of the loudest playoff talk. West Virginia is 5-5 and 2-5, still hunting the sixth win that would make the Mountaineers bowl-eligible, with a trip to Utah waiting after this.

Houston's preview hung a lot of the pride on quarterback Conner Weigman, who has 68 touchdowns since 2025, 47 through the air and 18 on the ground, and a 17-6 record in a Cougar uniform. The defense has forced a turnover in nine straight games, the longest streak of the Willie Fritz era, after Javion White's interception at Colorado. The offensive front is a Joe Moore Award semifinalist for the first time in school history. West Virginia, in the same notes, was listed 13th nationally in offense at 458.9 yards a game and sixth in rushing at 240.7, which is exactly the kind of visitor a Senior Day crowd in Morgantown wants to believe in.

The Mountaineers' Saturday game-day story called it Senior Day and a chance to rebound from a 37-14 loss to Kansas. Houston called it a road finale. Somebody's season tilts on the first trip this series has ever made to this building. The home crowd will be in gold and blue, and the Cougars have to find out if a first visit to this stadium feels new, or like the start of a long afternoon.

West Virginia is calling Saturday "Gold and Blue Gratitude," a thank-you to Mountaineer fans for sticking with the team all season, and the school says more than 1,000 ticket holders will get a fan experience or a giveaway at Milan Puskar Stadium. It's also Senior Day and the last home game of the year. Jolene Buckhannon has her own way of saying thanks. She's bringing a cooler of pepperoni rolls to the tailgate, and she's keeping count.

"One roll for every Mountaineer touchdown," she says. "I've eaten a lot of pepperoni rolls this year. Load up, I don't miss."

She isn't exaggerating. West Virginia scores 34.9 points a game. It also allows 34.8, which is how a team with that much offense ends up 5-5. The Mountaineers scored 48 at Texas Tech and lost. They scored 42 at Iowa State and lost. They scored 45 against Cincinnati and won by three, on a day Michael Hawkins Jr. threw for 464 yards.

Houston insider Yo Pacheco has heard the stories. He's flying in with a No. 14 ranking and, as he puts it, "a fully fueled launch vehicle."

"Houston, we have a quarterback," Yo says. "Conner Weigman has 25 touchdown passes and three interceptions. That's not a passer. That's mission control."

The numbers back Yo up. Houston is 7-3. Weigman has thrown for 2,552 yards, and Amare Thomas has 53 catches for 764 yards and nine touchdowns. Makhi Hughes leads the backfield with 712 rushing yards. The Cougars score 35.1 and allow 23.4, and they've beaten Oklahoma State, Kansas State, UCF and Cincinnati.

But Yo's rocket has had some trouble on reentry. Houston lost 42-32 at Colorado last Friday night. It lost 21-13 at Utah in October and 28-26 at Texas Tech in September. All three losses came on the road.

"We had a small anomaly in Boulder," Yo says. "A very small one. Ten points. The crew has reviewed the data and corrected course."

Jolene laughed at "anomaly" for a full minute.

West Virginia's own trip to the launch pad didn't go well last week. Kansas came into Morgantown and won 37-14. Hawkins was 19 of 34 for 227 yards, and the Mountaineers never found their usual rhythm. Rich Rodriguez's team now needs one more win for a bowl trip, with Houston at home and a Friday night trip to Utah left.

"Kansas was a bad day," Jolene says. "I ate two rolls. Two. I haven't eaten that few since September."

Her case for Saturday is Cam Cook. The running back has 1,084 yards and eight touchdowns, and he's the reason West Virginia can stay on the field long enough to keep Weigman on the sideline. DJ Epps is the big-play threat, with 615 yards on only 28 catches. Isaiah Patterson leads the defense with 65 tackles and three sacks.

"Cam Cook runs like he's late for a shift at the mine," Jolene says. "He gets where he's going."

Yo has a rebuttal for that, too. "Kentrell Webb has 59 tackles for us, and Ashton Porter's got three sacks. And remember, we've played Texas Tech too. We lost by two in Lubbock. You lost by five. In orbital mechanics, that's called a rounding error."

The teams have met twice in recent years, and they've split. The Cougars won 41-39 in 2023, and West Virginia won 45-35 last November. Both games were the kind of shootouts Jolene likes.

"Two shootouts," she says. "If you're keeping score at home, that's a lot of pepperoni rolls."

Here's where I land. Houston is the better team, with the better quarterback and the better defense. Weigman has three interceptions all year, and a West Virginia defense allowing 34.8 a game isn't likely to force many more. But the Mountaineers will have a lot of emotion on Senior Day, and Cook can make this a long afternoon of handoffs. I think West Virginia keeps it close into the fourth quarter before Houston pulls away.

Yo will probably call it a successful splashdown. Jolene will count her rolls, find four of them gone, and say it could have been worse.

"Gold and Blue Gratitude," she says. "I'm grateful we're still in it in November."

My call: Houston Cougars 34, West Virginia Mountaineers 28.
