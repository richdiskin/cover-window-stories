Arkansas State at Louisiana Tech: Commencement, Then Kickoff

Arkansas State Red Wolves (4-6) at Louisiana Tech Bulldogs (8-2)

7:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Joe Aillet Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

The morning in Ruston belongs to the graduates. Fall commencement fills the Thomas Assembly Center at 9 a.m. Saturday, and Louisiana Tech is letting every new alum walk into Joe Aillet Stadium one last time with their graduate credentials for Senior Day and a 6 p.m. kickoff against Arkansas State, so Booker "Champ" Tullos expects a lot of gowns folded into the backs of trucks by halftime. Ty Doucet-Harmon drove down from Jonesboro before daylight, the way he leaves for the duck blind, and he says the Red Wolves came to spoil a party.

Champ: You picked the wrong Saturday. Graduation, seniors, the whole town's here.

Ty: Big crowds are just more people to hush.

Champ: That's a duck-blind thing to say.

Ty: Everything I say is a duck-blind thing.

These two programs used to see each other all the time. They haven't met since 2015, when Louisiana Tech won a bowl game 47-28, and the Bulldogs lead the all-time series 26-12, including 10 of the last 11. Champ knows every number by heart.

Champ: Ten of eleven. That's a good harvest.

Ty: That's an old harvest. Nobody on either team was alive for half of it.

Louisiana Tech is having the kind of season Champ dreams about between peach seasons. The Bulldogs are 8-2, 6-0 in the Sun Belt West, and they've won seven straight since losing at LSU and Baylor in September. They pitched shutouts against UL Monroe and Old Dominion, won 41-17 at Troy and put up 63 on Southern Miss last week.

Blake Baker is the reason the offense hums. He has 17 touchdown throws and one interception all season. One. He has completed 143 of 196, and Jalen Mickens has caught 55 balls for 695 yards. Andrew Burnette leads the backs, and Rayshun Hurt broke off 88 yards on six carries last week.

Champ: Baker is a peach in August. Perfectly ripe, no bruises.

Ty: Every peach gets a bruise eventually.

Champ: Not this one. We grade them carefully.

The defense might be better. Alonzo Jackson Jr. leads the team with 10 sacks and 75 tackles, which is the kind of season people talk about for years.

Ty: We know about Jackson. Trey Owens has seen pressure before.

Arkansas State is 4-6, and Ty won't pretend otherwise. The Red Wolves have lost two straight, 19-17 to UL Monroe and 28-21 at Coastal Carolina, both one-score games they could have won. Owens has thrown for 2,350 yards and 12 touchdowns, and he can go off. He threw for 418 yards and four touchdowns in a 56-49 loss to South Alabama. Jaylen Bonelli has 561 receiving yards, and safety Joe Drew is everywhere with 97 tackles.

Ty: Joe Drew could guide a hunt. He's always where the birds are going to land.

Champ: Baker doesn't throw to where Drew's going to be. That's the trick.

What Ty is hoping for is a close game into the fourth quarter. What Champ expects is a Louisiana Tech team that hasn't let anyone breathe for five straight weeks. The Bulldogs have won their last five games by 15 points or more.

The graduates are part of the plan, too. Champ figures there's nothing louder than a crowd that just finished college that morning.

Champ: Those kids just survived finals. They'll yell for three hours, no problem.

Ty: My guys survived a duck season in a flooded rice field. Loud doesn't scare us.

Champ: What does?

Ty: A quarterback with one interception. That's a little scary.

The honest read is that Arkansas State has enough offense to make it interesting, and Owens will make a few big throws. But Louisiana Tech is better on both sides, it's at home on a day built for a celebration, and Baker doesn't give the ball away. Tech's defense gets to Owens a few times, the Bulldogs pull away in the second half, and Ruston gets one more reason to stay out late.

My call: Louisiana Tech Bulldogs 34, Arkansas State Red Wolves 20.
