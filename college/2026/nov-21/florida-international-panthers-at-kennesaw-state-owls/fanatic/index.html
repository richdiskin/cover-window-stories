Florida International at Kennesaw State: Two 4-6 Teams, One Last Home Saturday in Kennesaw

Kennesaw and FIU arrive at Walens Family Field at Fifth Third Stadium in the same boat: 4-6, with a 3 p.m. ET ESPN+ kickoff, and a league date that has to be decided on the field, not on the record. The Owls are 3-3 in league play. The Panthers are 2-4. That is the whole argument for why a late-November home Saturday in Kennesaw still matters.

FIU is coming off a 16-24 loss at Delaware, where JJ Kohl threw for 313 yards and a score but also had two interceptions, and Maguire Anderson caught nine balls for 122 yards. Kohl has 2,348 passing yards, 12 touchdowns and 12 interceptions on the year. Devonte Lyons has 706 rushing yards. Kennesaw, by contrast, just won 26-7 at Sam Houston. Rickie Collins has 1,687 passing yards, seven scores and seven interceptions; Boogie Woods III leads the Owls' ground game in the facts we can source.

The AccuWeather forecast on ESPN's game page had a 49 percent chance of rain and an 18-mph gust, which is enough to make a messy afternoon possible in Cobb County. Official sites for both programs were thin this week on a fresh game preview, so this story stays with what is dated and sourced: the records, the last boxes, and the stadium. No player is being declared out or available without a report.

Two 4-6 teams, one of them at home for a conference date, and a passing attack that just threw for 313 on the road: that is the 3 o'clock game in Kennesaw. If the Owls defend the way they did in a 26-7 win last week, the home stands will have a night. If Kohl keeps finding Anderson, the walk to the cars will be a long one.

Kennesaw State closes its home schedule Saturday with a Salute to Service game, and the Florida International Panthers vs Kennesaw State Owls matchup at Walens Family Field will stop at one point so every veteran and active-duty service member in the stands can stand and be recognized. Ximena Holcombe-Reyes wants the Owls to send the seniors out on a four-game run; Lourdes Ibarguen-Tate drove up from Miami with a cooler, a flag and a plan to spoil it.

"Senior Tribute, Salute to Service, final home game," Ximena says, scrolling through the game-day notes on her phone. "Operation Hat Trick is giving recognition boxes to nominated veterans. Vet Tix donors are in a drawing for two field passes. This is a big day for our community, Lourdes. I'm just letting you know before it happens."

"Miami honors its veterans too," Lourdes says. "We will stand up with everybody Saturday, and then we will sit down and yell at your quarterback. Both things are allowed."

Ximena loves this program because it is still being built in front of her. Kennesaw State is new to this level, and the early part of 2026 was a lesson. The Owls lost six straight after the opener, including a 27-26 game against Jacksonville State, a 24-21 game at Missouri State and a 17-14 game at Arkansas State. Close, close, close. Then something clicked. They went to Murfreesboro and put 44 on Middle Tennessee. They beat Delaware 42-40 at home on Homecoming. Last Saturday they went to Huntsville, Texas, and beat Sam Houston 26-7.

"Three straight," Ximena says. "Landon Varnes has thrown the most in both of the last two, and he looks like he owns the place. Boogie Woods ran for 101 at Sam Houston. And Ugonna Nwoha has five and a half sacks, which means your quarterback is going to have company."

That quarterback is JJ Kohl, and Lourdes will defend him until the sun goes down. Kohl has thrown for 2,348 yards this year. He threw for 367 against Buffalo and 313 last week at Delaware. The receiver he looks for first is Maguire Anderson, who has 53 catches for 732 yards and caught nine balls for 122 in Newark.

"Kohl throws it like he is angry at the air," Lourdes says. "Anderson catches it like it's a family heirloom. And Devonte Lyons runs for 706 yards and you never see him coming."

The thing Lourdes does not love talking about is the road. FIU is 4-6, and all four wins came at home. Away from Miami, the Panthers lost at South Florida, at Florida Atlantic, at Jacksonville State, at Liberty and at Delaware. Some of those were close, like 27-24 in Jacksonville, Alabama. One was not, the 49-10 night at Liberty that she refers to only as "the Lynchburg thing."

"This time is different," she says. "DeeJay Holmes Jr. has seven sacks. Seven! Your quarterback has never seen him. DeAndre Boykins is going to be everywhere in that secondary."

Ximena has a defender to brag about too. Tywon Christopher leads the Owls with 74 tackles, and he has been the steady voice of a defense that held Sam Houston to a single touchdown. And she has history on her side. Kennesaw State has beaten FIU both times these programs have met, 27-26 in 2024 and 45-26 last year.

"One point and then nineteen," Ximena says. "We're trending."

"You're counting," Lourdes says. "Counting isn't trending."

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a stadium during a military recognition, and both of them will take their hats off for it. Then the noise comes back, louder, the way it always does, because the people being honored would want it that way.

Here is how the afternoon goes. Holmes gets to Varnes early and the FIU sideline erupts, and Kohl hits Anderson on a long throw to make it a game. But the Owls answer with Woods running downhill behind a front that has been getting better every week. Nwoha gets a sack on a third down in the second half that sends the student section into a frenzy. FIU keeps pushing into the fourth quarter, but Christopher makes the stop that matters, and the Kennesaw State seniors get to wave goodbye with a fourth straight win.

Lourdes packs up her cooler. "Your fans are polite," she says. "I will give you that."

"Come back next year," Ximena says. "We'll be bigger."

My call: Kennesaw State Owls 24, Florida International Panthers 19.
