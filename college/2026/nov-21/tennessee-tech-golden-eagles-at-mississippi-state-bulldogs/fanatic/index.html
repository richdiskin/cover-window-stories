Tennessee Tech vs Mississippi State: Senior Day in Starkville

Tennessee Tech Golden Eagles (9-2) at Mississippi State Bulldogs (4-6)

1:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Davis Wade Stadium · SECN+

XFacebook

Tennessee Tech vs Mississippi State is the final 2026 home date at Davis Wade Stadium this Saturday, and the published gameday schedule puts a Senior Day ceremony at 11:38 a.m. before a noon Central kickoff. Mississippi State insider Wade Pettus calls it a cowbell goodbye, while Tennessee Tech visitor Harlan Pike says Jax Leatherwood and a 9-2 Golden Eagle team did not drive down from Cookeville to sit quietly in the visitor section.

Wade: Harlan, they put the Senior Day ceremony on the clock at 11:38. I have been ringing this cowbell since breakfast. I brought a feed-store receipt and a handkerchief, and I'm only using one of them on purpose.

Harlan: You can use the handkerchief when Leatherwood throws one past your safety. Two thousand nine hundred twenty-three passing yards. Twenty-eight touchdown throws. Your own week was a 9-41 loss to Auburn at home. We didn't come here to sit quietly in the visitor section.

That's the tension in Starkville this week. One side is throwing a farewell, and the other side came to finish a regular season that already includes a 6-2 Southern Conference record and a 31-38 loss to Mercer. Jeff Lebby's club is 4-6 and 2-6 in the SEC after opening 4-0. Bobby Wilder's Golden Eagles are 9-2 and 6-2 in the Southern Conference, and on Nov 16 they were still No. 12 in the AFCA poll and No. 13 in the Stats Perform poll.

Wade: Here is the thing you don't understand about this Mississippi State team. It can still throw a party when the night is easy. The opener was 62-13 over UL Monroe. Fluff Bothwell has 649 rushing yards. Anthony Evans III has 65 catches for 800 yards. When the cowbell is loud and the seniors are on the field, this place still knows how to score.

Harlan: And Kamario Taylor has 17 touchdown throws this season. The last three games, AJ Swann has had the most throwing attempts, and the last five scores have been 0, 15, 3, 23 and 9. Leatherwood just set the single-season records for touchdowns and passing yards, even in a loss. M.J. Flowers has 688 rushing yards and seven scores. Tre' Holloway has 55 catches for 735 yards and eight scores. That's not a visitor looking for a souvenir.

Pike did his homework too, and he will tell you Tech's season has been more fireworks than heartache, with a 66-14 win over VMI and a 42-38 shootout at Wofford in the ledger, plus a 24-9 win at Chattanooga. The Mercer defeat stung, 31-38 at Tucker Stadium, after Tech had tied it three times. Leatherwood still went 31 of 49 for 294 yards in that one, and Qua Ashley ran for 100 yards and a score.

Harlan: I wrote a note about the Mercer game. It is two sentences long, and the second sentence is "we still rewrote the record book." We came to Starkville anyway.

Wade: Your defense gives up 21.9 points a game. My front is coming. Jaray Bledsoe has 4.5 sacks. Jardin Gilbert has 60 tackles.

Harlan: Your front had better bring a lunch. Greg Turner III has 73 tackles. Hudson Tucker has six sacks. Those two are not here for the senior photographs.

The two of them agree on exactly one point: this is a first meeting in the recent record, and that means nobody in the building is playing last year's script. Wade wants the seniors to leave Davis Wade with a win they can carry into the Nov 27 trip to Oxford. Harlan wants a road finish that looks like a 9-2 team, not like a walking visit.

What makes Senior Day dangerous is what it does to the home team's legs. The noise comes early, and the emotion comes with it. If Mississippi State starts fast, with Bothwell going downhill and Evans catching it in space, Tech will spend the second half trying to keep Leatherwood on schedule. If the Bulldogs start thinking about the next bus ride before the seniors have left the field, Leatherwood can hang around the way a 2,923-yard quarterback is built to hang around.

Wade: The seniors get honored before kickoff, the cowbell gets loud, and then the receipt gets filled in. That's the order of operations. Ring it if you mean it.

Harlan: And while you're ringing it, we'll be throwing it to Holloway. That's my order of operations.

Both of them want the same thing for the families in the stands, a hard game and a sendoff worth remembering. Wade wants that sendoff in maroon. Harlan wants a road Saturday Cookeville can take home.

Wade: Ring the cowbell for the people who already put in four years here.

Harlan: Cookeville did not send a 9-2 team to sit still.

My call: Mississippi State Bulldogs 38, Tennessee Tech Golden Eagles 17.
