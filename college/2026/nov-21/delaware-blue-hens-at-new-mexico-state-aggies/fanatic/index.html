Delaware vs New Mexico State: HomeGrown Saturday in Las Cruces

Delaware Blue Hens (5-5) at New Mexico State Aggies (3-7)

3:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Aggie Memorial Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

Las Cruces does its holiday shopping by the mouthful this weekend. HomeGrown, the twelfth annual New Mexico food show and gift market, fills the Farm and Ranch Heritage Museum on Saturday morning with salsas, pecans and chile, and by early afternoon the Delaware Blue Hens vs New Mexico State Aggies game kicks off at Aggie Memorial Stadium on Senior Day, with Esperanza Lujan-Tafoya and Hen Lofland ready to argue about everything from defense to dessert.

Esperanza has the morning planned down to the minute. She will be at the museum gate at nine, among the first hundred cars so she gets the free shopping bag, and she will fill it with jars from J.P.M.'s Salsa of Las Cruces and pecans from Worthington Pecan Farm before she ever thinks about parking at the stadium.

"You eat with us before you lose to us," she tells Hen. "That is valley hospitality."

Hen Lofland, who can recite the date Delaware ratified the Constitution faster than most people can say hello, accepts the snack and none of the premise. "We came to New Mexico for a road win, Esperanza. The salsa is a bonus."

The Blue Hens have earned some swagger. Delaware is 5-5 in its new life in the top level of college football, and Nick Minicucci has become a quarterback people circle on their calendar. He has thrown for 2,115 yards with 18 touchdowns and only four interceptions, and last week against FIU he completed 27 of his 30 throws in a 24-16 win. Sean Wilson has 54 catches for 717 yards. When Minicucci gets going, he gets going in a hurry: 414 yards at Western Kentucky, 408 at Kennesaw State, five touchdowns against Missouri State.

"Delaware was the first state," Hen says. "Minicucci intends to be first down the field."

Esperanza has a rebuttal, and his name is James Jones. The New Mexico State running back has 1,090 rushing yards on 179 carries, and he ran 25 times for 150 yards last week at Missouri State in an overtime loss that still makes her put her fork down. The Aggies are 3-7 and they have lost three in a row. But they beat Western Kentucky 34-13 in this stadium, and they won at Sam Houston, and Esperanza believes Jones can carry a team the way her grandmother carried a tray of tamales: steadily, and without dropping anything.

"Twenty-five carries," she says. "Thirty if he wants. Your defense will need a nap by the third quarter."

Delaware does have people who tackle. Blake Matthews leads the Blue Hens with five sacks, and Gavin Moul has 72 tackles at linebacker. New Mexico State answers with Gabe Peterson, who has five and a half sacks, and Tory Gethers, who has 75 tackles. The quarterback question on the Aggies' side is a little cloudier. Trey Hedden has thrown the most in each of the last two games, and he has 9 touchdowns against 10 interceptions this season, so Esperanza wants the ball in Jones's hands and Hedden throwing only when he has to.

There's also the travel. Delaware has been a tough out at home and a different team on the road, 1-4 away from Newark, with losses at Vanderbilt, Virginia, Middle Tennessee and Kennesaw State. The Kennesaw game was a 42-40 heartbreaker. The Middle Tennessee game was 33-30. Hen calls those games "close," which is the historian's word for "painful."

"You've never played in this valley," Esperanza says. "The light is different. The air is different. The chile is definitely different."

"I'll adjust," Hen says. "Delaware adjusts. We've been adjusting since 1787."

Senior Day gives the home crowd a reason to stay loud, and the conference has also marked Saturday for mental health awareness, which matters to Esperanza more than she lets on. She wants every player on that field, in crimson or in blue, to walk off knowing people care how they are doing, not just how they played.

Here's how it goes. Jones runs hard from the first snap, and the Aggies hold the ball long enough to frustrate Minicucci on the sideline. Delaware strikes back with two quick drives, Wilson making a pair of big catches. New Mexico State keeps it close behind Peterson and Gethers, and the crowd gets very loud in the fourth quarter with a chance to win it. Minicucci makes one more good throw at the end, and Delaware escapes by three.

Esperanza hands Hen a jar of salsa anyway. "For the flight," she says.

"I'll save it for the rematch," Hen says.

My call: Delaware Blue Hens 27, New Mexico State Aggies 24.
