Arkansas at Texas: Austin After a One-Point Escape in Baton Rouge

Texas just survived LSU by a point, 22-21, on the road, and now Arkansas comes to DKR-Texas Memorial Stadium for a 3:30 p.m. ET game on ESPN. The live card on this site lists Texas at No. 6, and that is the ranking this story will use. The Longhorns' own site has been billing the team as No. 8/8/5 this week (including a Butkus Award finalist note on linebacker Biles). ESPN's game header showed Texas at No. 5. When the school, the network and the card disagree, we stay with the card and tell you the rest.

Arkansas is 4-6 after a 34-6 win over South Carolina. Texas is 8-2. Arch Manning has 2,198 passing yards, 19 touchdowns and 10 interceptions; Raleek Brown leads the Longhorns' rush. KJ Jackson has 2,041 passing yards for the Razorbacks, and Chris Marshall has 761 receiving yards. Steve Sarkisian held a Nov. 19 media availability, posted as video by Texas, but the school did not publish a full Saturday designation list in the pages we could source.

This is not last week's one-score escape in Baton Rouge, and it is not a ranked-on-ranked date on the card. It is a November afternoon in Austin, a 4-6 visitor that just dropped 34 on South Carolina, and a Texas team whose official site cannot even agree with the card on its own ranking. If the Longhorns play like a group that just won in Tiger Stadium on the last snap, the afternoon should tilt orange. If Arkansas' passing game finds the same rhythm it had against South Carolina, DKR will have to earn the rest of the day.

For nine nights, local artists have been lighting up Waller Creek in downtown Austin for Creek Show, and Saturday is the last one. A few hours earlier and a short walk up the creek, the Arkansas Razorbacks vs Texas Longhorns game fills Darrell K Royal-Texas Memorial Stadium, and Caro Whitfield and Bubba Fayette-Dorsey are already arguing about which team gets to glow.

"Creek Show moved downstream this year," Caro says, sounding like a woman who reads the annual report. "First time on the new southern stretch, the Confluence, 4th Street to Lady Bird Lake. Ten installations. Tickets this year. Austin keeps growing, Bubba. So does this program."

Bubba wipes his glasses and lets out a short hog call, just enough to make the people near him jump. "Fayetteville has lights too," he says. "We just put ours on the field. And our boys are coming in hot."

He is not wrong about that. Arkansas is 4-6, but the Razorbacks just smashed South Carolina 34-6 behind KJ Jackson, who threw for 188 yards and two touchdowns. Chris Marshall caught one of them, and Marshall is the guy Bubba talks about at the dinner table: 52 catches, 761 yards, a receiver who makes contested balls look easy. Jackson has thrown for 2,041 yards this year, and he has thrown the most for Arkansas in every game this season.

"KJ is a grown man," Bubba says. "He took a beating at Utah and Texas A&M, and he got back up. Tennessee beat us by seven. Auburn beat us by three. We are not afraid of your big stadium."

Caro lets him finish, then gets to the part she likes. Texas is 8-2. The Longhorns beat Ohio State 24-23 in September, won at Tennessee 20-17 a week later, and last Saturday went into Baton Rouge and stole one, 22-21. Arch Manning has thrown for 2,198 yards and 19 touchdowns. Ryan Wingo leads the receivers with 591 yards. Raleek Brown has 572 rushing yards and six scores.

"And then there's Colin Simmons," Caro says, and she says the name the way some people say "blue-chip stock." Simmons has ten and a half sacks off the end. Jelani McDonald has 78 tackles in the secondary. Texas allows 17.8 points a game.

"Simmons is going to be in your backfield so often your center should charge him rent," she says.

"Our center charges everybody rent," Bubba says. "It's an Ozark thing."

Bubba's honest worry is the road. Arkansas is 1-3 away from home this season, and the trips to Utah and College Station were the kind of days a fan tries to forget. But he also has history he can hold up. In 2021, the Razorbacks beat Texas 40-21 in Fayetteville, and Bubba has the ticket stub framed in his classroom. Texas won the two meetings since, 20-10 in 2024 and 52-37 last year.

"That 52-37 game was a fireworks show," Caro says. "This one will be a light show. Different art form."

The Texas worry, quietly, is turnovers. Manning threw three interceptions at LSU and still won, and the Longhorns lost to Oklahoma and Florida by three points each this season. They have been close-game artists more than blowout specialists against good opponents. Caro does not love that. She would prefer a clean, profitable afternoon, a word she uses about football more than she should.

Here's how it unfolds. Texas opens with Brown running hard and Manning finding Wingo on a crossing route, and the stadium settles in for a long, happy day. Simmons gets a sack in the first quarter that sends the student section into a frenzy. Arkansas answers with Jackson hitting Marshall down the sideline, and Bubba lets loose a full hog call that turns several heads in burnt orange. The Hogs get a field goal or two more, but the Texas defense keeps them out of the end zone after that, and McDonald comes up with a big stop on fourth down. Texas wins comfortably, but Arkansas never quits, and Bubba drives home proud of that.

Down on Waller Creek, the lights come on for one last night. Caro goes to see them. Bubba goes too, because even a hog caller appreciates good art.

"Next year in Fayetteville," he says.

"Bring the lights," Caro says.

My call: Texas Longhorns 32, Arkansas Razorbacks 13.
