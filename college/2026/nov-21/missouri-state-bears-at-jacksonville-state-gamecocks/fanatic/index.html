Missouri State at Jacksonville State: One Win From a Title Game on Senior Day

Jacksonville State can punch its ticket to the Conference USA championship game on Saturday, and the school's Senior Day story did not hide it. A win in either of the Gamecocks' final two games would put them in the league title game, and AmFirst Stadium gets Missouri State at 1 p.m. in the home closer. Hosting that title game for a third straight year would take more help in the standings, the notes said, but the first job is simpler: win this one, honor the seniors, and keep the Southerners Reunion crowd in the building for another week.

Jax State is 7-3 and 5-1 in the league. Missouri State is 4-6 and 3-3, with wins in two of its last three conference games, and the Bears came south hoping to knock a title-game team off its own field. The matchup that has followed this game all week is quarterback Caden Creel's dual-threat work, 2,151 passing yards and 817 rushing yards, against a Missouri State team that has Isaiah Myers at 1,030 receiving yards. Those figures are from the season stats in the game files we use.

Senior Day in Jacksonville, Ala., is supposed to feel like a thank-you. This one also feels like a gate. The Gamecocks can walk through it with a win. The Bears can slam it shut and take a 4-6 season into November with a trophy-race scalp. The band alumni came home. The football still has to decide whether they get to come back next week.

Jacksonville State has a name for Saturday's game against Missouri State: Southerners Reunion. Alumni of the Marching Southerners, Jax State's marching band, come home to AmFirst Stadium, and Jax State insider Burl Ashcraft-Neeley is planning to feed as many of them as his smoker can handle.

"Old band folks are hungry folks," Burl says, tying on his red apron. "I've got four briskets going and a pan of beans the size of a tuba. Reunion means feeding people."

It's also a first. Jax State's own schedule release calls this the first meeting between these programs, and it's Senior Day in Jacksonville. Missouri State insider Clementine Bilyeu is driving down from the Ozarks with a canoe paddle in the back seat, because she brings it everywhere.

"New water," Clementine says. "I've never paddled this creek before. You read the current, you watch for rocks and you don't panic."

Here's the current she's reading. Jacksonville State is 7-3. The Gamecocks lost 34-24 at Western Kentucky last Saturday, which stung, but they'd won seven of eight before that. Caden Creel does almost everything for them. He's thrown for 2,151 yards with 17 touchdowns and run for 817 more. Deondre Johnson has 592 receiving yards on just 28 catches. On defense, safety Caleb Nix leads the team with 65 tackles, and Tae Floyd has three and a half sacks. The Gamecocks allow 20.7 points a game.

"Creel's like good bark on a brisket," Burl says. "It takes time to build, and once it's there, it's the best part of the whole thing."

Burl's team has a habit of making things close, though. Jax State beat Georgia Southern 31-27, Kennesaw State 27-26 and FIU 27-24. It lost at Ohio 29-27 in overtime. When the Gamecocks win big, it's usually at home, like the 47-14 Homecoming win over Sam Houston on Nov. 7 and the 49-7 win over Eastern Kentucky.

"We cook low and slow most weeks," Burl says. "Some weeks we just turn up the heat."

Clementine's Bears are 4-6, and they've had some rough water. They lost 50-0 at Texas A&M and 61-38 at Delaware. But they've won two of their last three, including last Saturday's 27-21 overtime win over New Mexico State. They also beat Sam Houston 24-3 and Kennesaw State 24-21.

Henry Belin IV has taken most of the snaps since mid-October. He's thrown for 1,489 yards with nine touchdowns and six interceptions. His top target is the reason Clementine drives so far. Isaiah Myers has 51 catches for 1,030 yards and eight touchdowns.

"Isaiah Myers is a spring-fed creek," Clementine says. "It doesn't matter how dry the summer gets. He keeps running."

Missouri State's defense starts with Jared Lloyd. The linebacker has 117 tackles, more than anyone on either roster. Tim Brantley Jr. has four sacks. The Bears allow 29.1 a game, and Burl keeps pointing at that figure with his tongs.

"Twenty-nine a game," he says. "You can't give that up to Creel. He'll carve you like a brisket on the cutting board."

The two programs do share some opponents, which helps. Both beat Sam Houston, Jax State by 33 and Missouri State by 21. Both played Western Kentucky on the road and lost, Jax State by ten and Missouri State by 21. Both beat Kennesaw State, each by a field goal or less. Missouri State lost a close one at FIU, 23-20. Jax State beat FIU at home, 27-24.

"Same rivers," Clementine says. "Different boats."

Here's how I see it. Jacksonville State is the better team, and it's playing at home on Senior Day with a reunion crowd in the stands. Creel should find room to run, and Missouri State will struggle to stop him on early downs. But Myers is the kind of receiver who can keep his team in it for a while, and Lloyd will be in on a lot of tackles. I expect Jax State to pull away in the second half, after Clementine's Bears make it uncomfortable early.

Burl will hand out plates long after the final whistle. Clementine will take one, because she's polite and because it's brisket.

"It's good bark," she'll say.

"Told you," Burl will say. "Come back next year and I'll make it better."

My call: Jacksonville State Gamecocks 31, Missouri State Bears 19.
