GameDay Report

Western Kentucky at Liberty: The People Who Run the Stadium Get Their Day

Liberty is 9-1 and 6-0 in Conference USA, it has already locked up the league's regular-season high ground, and Saturday is still being treated like a thank-you note. The school's game-day story billed the afternoon as Senior Day at Williams Stadium, with Employee Appreciation built in so the people who keep the place running get their turn in the building. Western Kentucky's official preview said the Flames would honor 20 seniors before kickoff. The Hilltoppers are 4-6 and 3-3, coming to Lynchburg at 1 p.m. on ESPN+ to see whether a 9-1 home team will let them hang around.

WKU's notes also said Liberty leads the series 5-0. That is a blunt piece of history, and it fits a blunt present: the Flames have been the class of this league, and the Hilltoppers have to steal a road win to keep a disappointing year from leaking away. Liberty's broadcast window is ESPN+, with the home radio crew on 88.3 FM, and the school has been selling the day as a celebration first. That only works if the football matches the ceremony.

A 9-1 team at home, on Senior Day, against a 4-6 visitor is supposed to feel settled by halftime. Western Kentucky did not ride six hours for a ceremony. The employees get honored. The seniors get introduced. Then somebody has to go play a Conference USA game that still counts for the people who came to work and the people who came to say goodbye.

Liberty is calling Saturday Employee Appreciation Day, which means the people who keep Williams Stadium running, from the ticket windows to the concession stands, get their turn to be thanked. Liberty insider Gideon Fairweather wants to add one more group to the list. "The bus drivers," he says. "Nobody ever thanks the bus drivers."

Gideon rates every team bus that pulls into Lynchburg, and he's been waiting for this one. Western Kentucky has made a long trip this fall. The Hilltoppers have played at Nevada, Georgia, Indiana, New Mexico State, Sam Houston and Middle Tennessee.

"That bus has earned a nine," Gideon says. "The team inside it, I'll pray for. I pray for both teams every Saturday. This week, I'll pray a little longer for the visitors."

Western Kentucky insider Tops Whitsitt hears that and drapes his red towel over his shoulder. Tops cuts hair in Bowling Green, and he's been listening to customers argue about this season since August.

"Pray all you want, Gideon," Tops says. "We just beat Jacksonville State 34-24. We beat Sam Houston on the road and gave up three points. We're sweeping up."

That's the Western Kentucky story in a nutshell. The Hilltoppers are 4-6 and they've had a rough fall, but there have been good afternoons mixed in with the bad ones. They lost 70-20 at Georgia and 38-0 at Indiana in September. They also beat Missouri State 34-13 and won 16-3 at Sam Houston in October. Last week, they beat Jacksonville State by ten.

Brock Glenn has done a little of everything. He's thrown for 1,121 yards and eight touchdowns and run for 604 yards and eight more. Rodney Tisdale Jr. has played quarterback too, going 14 of 16 against Jacksonville State. Jonathan Stafford Jr. leads the receivers with 56 catches. Jaylen Lewis has 70 tackles.

"Two quarterbacks, one haircut," Tops says. "Fresh cut on both sides, nice and even."

Liberty doesn't need two of anything. The Flames are 9-1, and their only loss came on opening day at James Madison, 20-13. They've won nine straight since then, and they've allowed 14.5 points a game. That's a defense that held Sam Houston to three, Kennesaw State to six and FIU to ten.

Deshawn Purdie runs the offense, and he's barely made a mistake. He's thrown for 1,769 yards with 12 touchdowns and one interception. Kam Davis has 792 rushing yards and nine touchdowns, and Makai Jackson has 752 receiving yards on 40 catches. Brenton Williams has six sacks from the secondary, and Derrell Farrar leads the team with 56 tackles.

"Purdie has thrown one interception all season," Gideon says. "One. I've had more flat tires on the way to church."

Last Saturday wasn't easy. Middle Tennessee came to Lynchburg and stayed close, and Liberty won 28-23 on a day Purdie threw for only 64 yards. Kam Davis carried the load with 126 rushing yards. Gideon calls that kind of win a test of faith.

"You win the hard ones and you say thank you," he says. "Then you go home and watch the tape."

The history is all Liberty. The Flames are 5-0 against Western Kentucky, going back to a 23-14 win in 1996. The three most recent meetings went 30-24, 42-29 and 38-21, and Liberty won all three. Tops has an answer for that, too.

"Five haircuts in a row, and Liberty's never once tipped the barber," he says. "We'll get the tip this time."

Senior Day is part of Saturday, too, and Liberty's seniors walk out with a chance at a tenth win. After this one, the Flames finish at Missouri State and then hope to play for the Conference USA title on Dec. 4.

Here's how I see it. Western Kentucky's defense allows 32.4 a game, and the Hilltoppers have given up 49, 70 and 51 in their worst afternoons. Liberty doesn't need many points to win with its defense, but Davis and Purdie should find plenty. Western Kentucky's two-quarterback approach may keep things interesting for a quarter. After that, Liberty's defense takes over.

Gideon will stand for the halftime show when the marching band plays toward the west side. Tops will sweep the floor of his shop on Monday and tell everyone about the road trip. One of them will be very happy.

"Good game, Tops," Gideon will say. "I'll pray for your bus on the way home."

My call: Liberty Flames 36, Western Kentucky Hilltoppers 16.
