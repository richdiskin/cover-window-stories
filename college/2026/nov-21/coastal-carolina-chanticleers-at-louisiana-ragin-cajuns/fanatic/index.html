Coastal Carolina vs Louisiana: Southern Screen Weekend, Senior Day Finale

GameDay Report

Coastal Carolina at Louisiana: A 12-Man Senior Class, and a Third Road Win to Chase

Lafayette will spend the hour before kickoff on a senior walk, not a movie premiere. Louisiana's official preview said a 12-man senior class will be recognized beginning at 1:45 p.m., 15 minutes before a 2 p.m. kickoff at Our Lady of Lourdes Stadium, with the Coastal Carolina game on ESPN+.

The list the school published is a full one: Tito Chikere, Kailep Edwards, Maurion Eleam, George Jackson, Collin Jacob, Jaydon Johnson, Logan Klotz, Princeton Malbrue, Kaden Moreau, Jake St. Andre, Tony Sterner and Fitzgerald West Jr. The ceremony is the send-off. The football problem is a 4-6 Coastal team that just won its home finale and, per the Chanticleers' own preview, is chasing a third straight Sun Belt road win.

Coastal is 4-6 and 3-3 in the league after a 28-21 victory over Arkansas State, a clean afternoon from Deuce Bailey (21-of-24, 208 yards, one score). The road wins already in the book are real: 27-21 at Marshall on Oct. 10 and 31-20 at Georgia State on Oct. 31. Louisiana is 7-3 and 4-2 in the West after a 42-21 win at UL Monroe on Nov. 12, when Steven Blanco and JJ Garner each ran for more than 100 yards and the Cajuns scored 28 unanswered points.

Recent meetings have not settled much. In the 2016-2025 games we can source, the series sits 2-2, including Louisiana's 34-24 win in 2024 and Coastal's 30-27 win in 2020. The Cajuns have the better record and the home finale. Coastal has the sneakier road form, and Bailey just completed 21 of 24.

A home crowd that gets its seniors on the field at 1:45, then a 4-6 visitor that has already stolen two league games away from home: that is the afternoon in Lafayette. If the Cajuns play like the group that ran away from Monroe, the send-off will feel like a send-off. If Coastal's passing stays this sharp, the seniors will have to wait a little longer to exhale.

Lafayette has movie people in town all weekend, with the Southern Screen Festival filling downtown with screenings, panels and late-night music from Thursday through Sunday. On Saturday afternoon the Coastal Carolina Chanticleers vs Louisiana Ragin' Cajuns game takes over Our Lady of Lourdes Stadium for Senior Day, and Mimi Fontenot has already decided the home team gets the happy ending while Goldie Seabrook keeps insisting on a twist.

"Southern Screen says come dance a two-step and eat well," Mimi says. "That is not a festival slogan. That is how we play football. We run, we hit, we dance, we eat. In that order."

Goldie, who writes poems about blocking schemes and means every verse she has ever made, just smiles. She came from the coast, where the turf is teal and the wind off the water teaches you to keep your feet.

"Your festival has films with plot twists," Goldie says. "So do we. Coastal Carolina is the twist."

The Cajuns have made this a very good fall. Louisiana is 7-3 and has not lost at home: Lamar, UAB, Arkansas State, Troy, South Alabama, five for five. The heart of it is Lunch Winfield, the quarterback who runs like the ball owes him money. He has 604 rushing yards and 10 rushing touchdowns, plus 1,664 throwing yards. Last week at UL Monroe he barely had to throw at all, because Steven Blanco carried 15 times for 113 yards in a 42-21 win.

"Lunch is the main course," Mimi says. "Blanco is the second plate. And Jake St. Andre has 83 tackles, which means your quarterback is going to meet him a lot."

That quarterback is Deuce Bailey, and Goldie lights up when she talks about him. Bailey has thrown for 2,386 yards and 20 touchdowns, and last week against Arkansas State he completed 21 of 24 in a 28-21 win. Colton Hinton is the deep target, with 452 yards. Marcellius Pulliam has 104 tackles at linebacker and Ibrahim Diawara has six sacks off the end.

"Diawara is the wave you don't see coming," Goldie says. "Lunch likes to run around. Diawara likes to catch people who run around."

"Diawara has never met Lunch," Mimi says. "Lunch has a way of making strangers fall down."

The honest part for Goldie is the record. Coastal is 4-6, and the losses have hurt in different ways. West Virginia, Georgia Southern and App State all won by a touchdown or less. Old Dominion beat them 21-8, and Bailey threw three interceptions in that one. But Coastal has also won on the road at Marshall and at Georgia State, and Goldie quotes those like scripture.

The history between these two is a real back-and-forth. Coastal won 30-28 in 2018 and 30-27 in 2020, the year both programs were roaring. Louisiana won 48-7 in 2019 and 34-24 in 2024. Two apiece since 2016.

"Two apiece," Goldie says. "Saturday is the tiebreaker. Every good festival has a closing-night surprise."

"Every good festival has a closing-night party," Mimi says. "It's going to be in our end zone."

Senior Day in Lafayette is its own kind of movie. The Cajuns close the home schedule, the families walk out with the seniors, and Cajun Field gets loud and a little teary at the same time. Mimi says she will not cry. Mimi cried at a trailer once.

Here's how it plays out. Louisiana runs early, Winfield keeping it outside the pocket and Blanco pounding inside, and the crowd counts every first down. Coastal stays close in the first half because Bailey is accurate and Hinton gets behind the secondary once for a big gain. Diawara gets one sack that brings the teal section to its feet. But Louisiana controls the second half the old-fashioned way, chewing clock and making Coastal's defense stay on the field until legs get heavy. St. Andre makes a fourth-down stop late, and that's the closing credits.

"See," Mimi says, "no twist."

"The twist is that I'm staying for the film after," Goldie says, and she means it.

My call: Louisiana Ragin' Cajuns 30, Coastal Carolina Chanticleers 18.
