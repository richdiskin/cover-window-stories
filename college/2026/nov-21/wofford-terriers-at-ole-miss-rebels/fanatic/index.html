Wofford at Ole Miss: A 62-Week Ranking Streak Meets a Farewell Class

Oxford will honor a senior class that coach Pete Golding says is 40-11 and can become the winningest group in Ole Miss history with three more victories, and it will do it under a ranking streak the school is proud of. Ole Miss is No. 25 this week, which the program's Friday "What To Watch For" notes said extends a run of 62 consecutive weeks in the AP Poll, the second-longest streak in school history behind only 1957-63.

The Rebels are 6-4 and host 5-6 Wofford at 11 a.m. Central at Vaught-Hemingway Stadium, just the second meeting in the series after a 2016 game in Oxford. Golding spent practice week talking about the people, not the mismatch. He called center Brycen Sanders "a warrior" who "loves this place" and "loves the game" and will give everything he has "regardless if he's 100 percent or not." Tight end Caleb Odom, who has 34 catches this season and ranks ninth in program history among tight ends with 53 career receptions, said the goal now is finishing with nine wins. "We built a brotherhood over this year and that's something that can't be broken," Odom said. He also said the Egg Bowl is out there, "though the focus right now is on Wofford."

That visitor is closing its own season. Wofford announced this month that head coach Shawn Watson will conclude his coaching career after 2026, and the school named Mike Houston as his successor. The Terriers' own preview called Saturday the final game of the year, coming after three straight losses. Ole Miss quarterback Trinidad Chambliss is among the national top 20 in passing touchdowns (19) and passing yards (2,486), and pass rusher Suntarine Perkins has 7.0 tackles for loss and 3.5 sacks this season, adding to career marks the school lists among the program's best.

Golding has been telling his team to treat the day like an SEC game anyway. Odom's version was simpler: "We just need to go out there and have fun. Just play free and just play fast." Oxford has a farewell to give. The Rebels still have to earn it.

Wofford vs Ole Miss is Senior Day at Vaught-Hemingway Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 21, with 17 student-athletes honored before a noon kickoff. Ole Miss insider Langston Fairbanks says the Grove is throwing a farewell with a footnote attached, while Wofford visitor Calhoun Vines says Gerald Modest Jr. and Shawn Watson did not ride up from Spartanburg to pose for anybody's photographs.

Langston: Calhoun, they will honor 17 seniors before kickoff. Pete Golding already did the arithmetic. That class is 40 and 11, and he said if they win three more they will be the winningest class in the history of the program. I brought a pocket square and a clean sentence. I intend to use both.

Calhoun: You can use the pocket square when Modest runs past your second level. One thousand fifty-four yards, Langston. Nine scores. Two hundred twenty carries. That young man is 11th in the FCS, and this is Watson's last game as a head coach.

So Oxford has two goodbyes going at once. The Rebels are throwing a going-away party for a class, and the Terriers brought a going-away party of their own for a coach. It is also the last home Saturday before Mississippi State arrives on Nov 27, which means the Grove gets one more afternoon of hugs, Golding gets one more shot at that class record, and Wofford gets cast as the houseguest who showed up with a running back instead of a casserole.

Langston: Let me walk you through this Ole Miss team, Calhoun, and please take notes. Trinidad Chambliss is 11-1 as a starter in this stadium. He has 2,486 passing yards and 19 touchdown throws, and the Davey O'Brien Foundation put him on a list of 13 semifinalists. When your quarterback is 11-1 at home, that is not a short story. That is a hard ending.

Calhoun: And last week?

Langston: Last week was Oklahoma, 17-24. Chambliss ran for 84 yards. Joshua Dye ran 11 times for 58 yards and a score off the bench. Tah'j Butler had 15 tackles, a career high, the most by a Rebel defender this season. Caleb Odom led the team with 56 receiving yards and scored. Plenty of yards, not enough finish.

Calhoun showed up with a binder, and the binder says Wofford's November has been thinner on joy than on yards. The Terriers opened with wins over The Citadel and Gardner-Webb, then hit a hard stretch, then found wins at Furman, at Elon and over VMI on homecoming. They have lost their last three, the latest a 17-20 home defeat to East Tennessee State in which Modest still found the end zone and Wyatt Ward, who leads the defense with 73 tackles, had a season-high 16.

Calhoun: I can live with a last chapter that has Modest in it. Watson said he will retire at the conclusion of this season. The school named Mike Houston as the next head coach on Nov 16. Saturday is still Watson's goodbye, and we are going to play it like a goodbye worth taking home.

Langston: Your defense gives up 21.9 points a game, and our big fellas have been waiting all week to hear that. Blake Purchase has five sacks. Butler has 62 tackles on the year, and he just had 15 in a row of Oklahoma ballcarriers.

Calhoun: Then I hope your big fellas ate a good breakfast. Modest has carried it 220 times, and he does not get tired, he gets interested. Isaiah Scott has 366 receiving yards. Wofford did not come to Oxford to be scenery.

Here is where they shake hands: this series is short. Ole Miss lists it as the second all-time meeting, the first a 38-13 Rebel win in Oxford in 2016. Calhoun counters with one modest request, a clean afternoon and a running game that still looks like itself in the fourth quarter.

Senior Day is a sneaky afternoon in Oxford. The cheers hit early, the tears hit right behind them, and somebody always forgets to tie a shoe. If Ole Miss comes out humming, with Chambliss throwing and Dye and Kewan Lacy going downhill, Wofford's whole second half turns into a long, polite jog. If the Rebels drift toward Nov 27 before the last senior has jogged off, Modest is the kind of 1,054-yard back who keeps knocking on the door until somebody answers.

Langston: Flowers at the start, noise in the Grove, and sharp footnotes by halftime. Write it on a napkin, Calhoun.

Calhoun: You recite the class record. We'll keep handing it to Modest until you ask us to stop, and you'll be asking politely.

Strip away the trash talk and they want the same afternoon for every family in the stands: tough football and a goodbye people will retell at the next family reunion. Langston wants it in red. Calhoun wants a road finish Watson can carry out of the stadium with his head up.

Langston: These 17 seniors have earned one last noon in Oxford, and I want the Grove shaking.

Calhoun: Watson gets one more Saturday. That is the only goodbye I came to see.

My call: Ole Miss Rebels 49, Wofford Terriers 7.
