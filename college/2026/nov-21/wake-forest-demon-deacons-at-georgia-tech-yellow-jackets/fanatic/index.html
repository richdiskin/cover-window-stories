Wake Forest at Georgia Tech: One Last Saturday on The Flats

Georgia Tech is calling it one last Saturday on The Flats, and the school spent the week inviting students to pack Bobby Dodd Stadium for Senior Day against Wake Forest. The live card on this site lists the Demon Deacons at No. 13; ESPN has them at No. 10 in the AP and coaches polls and No. 11 in the College Football Playoff rankings, and Georgia Tech's official preview billed them as a top-10 visitor. The football account said 44 seniors are being celebrated. Upper-level tickets were listed at $10. The annual Michael Isenhour Toy Drive is part of the day, and the Yellow Jackets' Friday notes even pointed fans toward a concert at Callaway Plaza after the game. That is a lot of ceremony for a 3-7 team hosting a 9-1 league heavyweight at noon on ESPN.

Georgia Tech is 3-7 overall but 4-0 at home against ranked ACC opponents under coach Brent Key, including a 28-25 win over No. 23 Duke earlier this season. That is the pride the home side is hanging onto. Wake Forest is 9-1 and 6-1 in the ACC, the kind of visitor that usually ends someone else's home schedule in a hurry, and this is the last home Saturday of the year against a 9-1 league heavyweight.

Key has been answering questions all week about how a 3-7 roster, banged up and short-handed, is supposed to throw a farewell party and a football game at the same time. The Jackets have a chance to send those 44 seniors out with a noise the rest of the ACC would have to respect. Wake Forest has a chance to take another step toward the league's championship game. Atlanta will be loud for the people walking off The Flats. It will be louder if they make the Demon Deacons work for the afternoon.

Wendell Quist is bringing a toy to Bobby Dodd Stadium on Saturday, new and unwrapped, because Wake Forest vs Georgia Tech is the Yellow Jackets' annual Michael Isenhour Toy Drive game and the home finale on The Flats. Wake Forest insider Eloise Brightman-Rowe is bringing a toy too, and a 9-1 team with Gio Lopez and Carlos Hernandez that would very much like to leave Atlanta with an ACC Championship Game ticket in its pocket.

Some background for anybody who hasn't been on North Avenue in November. The drive was started in 2001 by Michael Isenhour, a Georgia Tech basketball player who wanted to help families after the September 11 attacks. He died in 2002, and the student-athletes renamed the drive for him. Fans bring new, unwrapped toys, and in past years Tech student-athletes have stood at the gates with the donation bins. It's also Senior Day and First Responders Appreciation Day, so the Jackets have a full house of reasons to play hard.

Wendell: Eloise, I've done the physics on this. A toy in a bin has potential energy. A kid opening it at the holidays is kinetic. That's the best conversion rate on campus, and I go to Georgia Tech.

Eloise: Wendell, that's the most generous thing an engineer has ever said to me. I'm still going to roll the Quad when we win.

Wake has given her plenty to roll. The Demon Deacons have won seven in a row and just took apart SMU 49-7 in Dallas. They score 43.9 points a game and give up 15.0. Lopez has thrown 19 touchdowns and one interception all season, which is the kind of number that makes a debate coach weep with joy.

Eloise: One interception, Wendell. One. Gio treats the football like a first-edition library book. And Carlos Hernandez has 1,039 receiving yards. Your secondary is going to need a study group.

Wendell: Our secondary has Tae Harris, and Tae Harris has 63 tackles. Graph paper doesn't lie, Eloise. Neither does a safety who arrives on time.

Georgia Tech's season has been a grind, and Wendell isn't pretending otherwise. The Jackets are 3-7 and spent late October and early November getting pushed around, losing 49-27 to Boston College, 34-11 at Pittsburgh and 44-17 to Louisville. But last Saturday something clicked at Clemson. Georgia Tech won 20-6 in Death Valley, Justice Haynes ran for 116 yards and a score, and the defense held Clemson to six points.

Wendell: That's not luck, that's mechanics. We controlled the ball, we tackled in space and we made a road crowd go quiet. Physics says a body at rest stays at rest. A body coming off a win at Clemson stays moving.

Eloise: Physics also says a 9-1 team is heavier than a 3-7 team.

The history gives Wendell a little more to stand on. Georgia Tech has won the last five meetings in the series, according to the Jackets, including 30-29 at Wake last year. Tech leads the all-time series 24-8, and this is Wake's first trip to Bobby Dodd since 2017.

Eloise: I've read the brief, Wendell. History is not a defensive coordinator. History doesn't get Gio on the ground.

Wendell: No, but Taje McCoy might. Three sacks. And Alberto Mendoza has thrown for 2,081 yards for us. He has completed 190 of 279 throws, and he keeps getting up.

Here is where both of them get serious. Wake has plenty at stake Saturday. The Jackets know they can spoil the Deacons' title-game hopes, and nothing motivates a home team in November like the chance to be the reason somebody else's season gets complicated.

Eloise: Our guys know that, which is why I'm not taking this lightly. Lopez and Hernandez are going to come out throwing early, and Ty Clark III is going to run behind them when your defense backs up.

Wendell: And we're going to run Haynes into the teeth of it and make the clock our best friend. Nobody beats Wake in a track meet. You beat Wake by making them wait.

That's the whole puzzle. If Georgia Tech can keep Lopez on the sideline with long Haynes drives, the Flats stay loud deep into the afternoon. If Wake scores early, the toys in the bins will be the happiest part of the day for the home crowd.

Eloise: Bring a big toy, Wendell. You'll want something to hug on the drive home.

Wendell: I'm bringing a toy truck. Engineered for all terrain, like my patience.

The bins at the gates will fill up for the Atlanta kids either way. On the field, Wake is too good to stop and Georgia Tech is too proud to fold.

My call: Wake Forest Demon Deacons 36, Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets 18.
