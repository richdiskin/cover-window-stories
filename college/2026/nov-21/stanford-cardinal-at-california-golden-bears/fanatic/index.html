Stanford at Cal: The Campanile Glows Blue and Gold All Week

Stanford Cardinal (5-5) at California Golden Bears (3-7)

7:30 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · California Memorial Stadium · ACC Network

XFacebook

Quentin Oyelaran-Fitch has spent Big Game week making signs. He has one for the Tree Chopping Rally on the Savio Steps on Wednesday, one for Thursday night's rally, which starts at Bowles Hall and winds through campus and the city, and one for Friday's Bonfire Rally at the Greek Theatre. The last one, which he plans to carry into Databricks Field on Saturday, reads "SATHER GATE IS LIT. SO IS OUR RESOLVE."

Iggy Yamamoto-Ross, who flew up from Palo Alto with a laptop bag and an elevator pitch, read it twice.

Iggy: Is it a sign or a mission statement?

Quentin: It's a demand. The Campanile, Sather Gate, Doe Library and Wheeler Hall have been lit blue and gold every night this week until two in the morning. The campus is asking something of us.

Iggy: Have you considered turning that into an app?

Quentin: No.

This is the 129th Big Game, and it kicks off at 4:30 Pacific in Berkeley. The week started Monday with the Cable Car Rally in San Francisco at Ghirardelli Square and Union Square, and Wednesday brings the Big Sail at the St. Francis Yacht Club before the Tree Chopping. The Axe is the trophy, the Bay is the backdrop, and whatever the records say, the two teams have split the last ten meetings, five apiece.

Stanford comes in at 5-5. That's a big step forward on paper, and Iggy treats each win like a product launch. The Cardinal beat Hawai'i, Georgia Tech, Elon, NC State and, last week, Virginia Tech 40-22 on the road. Micah Ford ran 27 times for 149 yards and three touchdowns in that one and has 900 yards and 11 scores for the season. Davis Warren has thrown for 2,406 yards with 14 touchdowns and only three interceptions. Caden High leads the receivers with 509 yards. Jay Green leads the team with 65 tackles, and Gabe Kaminski has three sacks.

Iggy: We're pivoting to the run game.

Quentin: You've been pivoting all season.

Iggy: And the pivot is working. Ford is our killer feature.

Quentin: The Stanford defense gave up 57 at Wake Forest and 58 at Notre Dame.

Iggy: Legacy bugs. We've patched them.

Cal is 3-7 and has dropped four straight, to Wake Forest, SMU, NC State and Virginia. The offense has had a rough month. But the Bears beat Syracuse on the road, beat Virginia Tech at home, and Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele threw for 413 yards against Wagner back in September. Chase Hendricks has 65 catches for 597 yards, Adam Mohammed leads the backs with 404 rushing yards, and AJ Tuitele has 63 tackles. JD McKinley has three sacks.

Quentin: The record is a construct.

Iggy: It's a 3-7 construct.

Quentin: In the Big Game, everybody starts at zero. That's the whole philosophy of the week.

Iggy: I'd love to see the philosophy's metrics.

The last two Big Games went one apiece. Cal won 24-21 in Berkeley in 2024, and Stanford won 31-10 at home last year. Before that, Cal won 27-20 in 2022 and 27-15 in 2023. So nobody in either crowd can claim the rivalry belongs to them.

Quentin: The 2024 game is my thesis.

Iggy: The 2025 game is my rebuttal.

Quentin: A rebuttal isn't a conclusion.

What decides it? Stanford wants to run Ford early and often, and Cal's defense has to keep him from turning short runs into long ones. Cal needs Sagapolutele to find Hendricks on third down, and the offense needs one or two long drives that end in touchdowns instead of field goals. In a rivalry game in front of a home crowd, with the whole campus glowing for a week, one big play can tip it.

Here's how it goes: Ford scores twice in the first half, and Stanford leads at the break. Cal answers in the third with a long drive capped by a Mohammed run, Hendricks makes a big catch on fourth down in the fourth quarter, and the Bears kick the go-ahead field goal late. Stanford's last drive stalls near midfield.

Quentin raises his sign one more time. Iggy opens his laptop and types the word "iterate," and the Campanile stays blue and gold until two in the morning anyway.

My call: California Golden Bears 28, Stanford Cardinal 27.
