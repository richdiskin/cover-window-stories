GameDay Report

Georgia Southern at Troy: Senior Day at The Vet After a Loss on the Road

Troy will honor 21 seniors beginning at 2:35 p.m. at Veterans Memorial Stadium, 25 minutes before a 3 p.m. kickoff against Georgia Southern on ESPN+. The official preview called it the final home game of the season, and it also called it a game for the Trojans' postseason life: Troy is 4-6 and 2-4 in the Sun Belt, and the school said a win keeps the bowl hunt alive. Georgia Southern is 6-4 and 5-1, and Troy's preview said an Eagles win would move them closer to a first Sun Belt East title.

Goose Crowder is the home quarterback, and Saturday is the final home start of his career, per the school. Last week at South Alabama he threw and ran for scores, both on fourth down, in a 14-27 loss. He has 1,393 passing yards, 10 touchdowns and five interceptions; he also ran 11 times for 58 yards and a score in that loss. Georgia Southern comes in on a different kind of heater. Max Johnson has 2,398 passing yards, 17 touchdowns and only three interceptions, and last week he went 16-of-22 for 203 yards and two scores in a 34-17 win at Georgia State. The Eagles' own preview noted a sour history at The Vet: they have lost three straight there after winning their first three, and Troy has won seven of the 10 meetings in this stadium. The last meeting, in 2024 at Paulson Stadium, was a 28-20 Troy win that Georgia Southern said essentially knocked the Eagles out of the East race.

A Senior Day for 21 Trojans, a visitor with the league's most productive passing game in the official preview's telling, and a home team that still needs a win to keep a bowl in view: that is the afternoon at The Vet. Crowder gets one more start in front of this crowd. Johnson has thrown 17 touchdowns and three interceptions. Somebody's seniors, or somebody's title math, is going to feel this one.

Band kids from all over the Southeast get the South End Zone at The Vet on Saturday, seats right beside the Sound of the South. It's Troy's annual High School Band Day, so those bands practice with the Trojans' marching band before the game and then join them on the field for one giant halftime show.

"Three hundred and fifty members in the Sound of the South," Marlo Pickens says, a bag of boiled peanuts in one hand. "Then add every high school band that shows up. Royce, you're going to hear us in Statesboro."

Royce Ogeechee, Bulloch County farm kid and keeper of the Georgia Southern scrapbook, smiles politely. "I'm sure it'll be beautiful, Marlo. We've got a band too. And we've got a quarterback who doesn't throw it to the other team."

So the Georgia Southern Eagles vs Troy Trojans game comes with a lot of horns. It's also Senior Day and Season Ticket Holder Appreciation Day, and Troy is marking the 20th anniversary of its first Sun Belt championship team, from 2006. That's a full afternoon in the Wiregrass.

Royce has good reason to be smiling. Georgia Southern has won four straight and sits first in the Sun Belt East at 5-1 in conference games. The Eagles beat App State 19-3, crushed Marshall 49-13 and won 34-17 at Georgia State last week. Max Johnson has thrown for 2,398 yards with 17 touchdowns and only three interceptions, and he threw for 323 yards against App State. Alex Taylor leads the receivers with 621 yards. On defense, Anthony Bynum has five sacks and Brandon Tyson has 63 tackles.

"My granddad raised me on stories about the dynasty years," Royce says. "This team is starting to give me stories of my own."

Marlo cracks another peanut and doesn't argue right away. Troy is 4-6 and has lost four of its last five. The Trojans lost 33-6 at Louisiana, 21-14 to UL Monroe, 41-17 to Louisiana Tech and 27-14 at South Alabama last week. The one bright spot in that stretch was a 24-21 overtime win at James Madison.

"It's been a long month," Marlo admits. "But I've snapped in worse weather than this."

Troy's biggest weapon might be Donnie Smith, the defensive back who has eight sacks. Eight! From a defensive back. Keno Jones leads the team with 77 tackles. Goose Crowder has thrown for 1,393 yards and 10 touchdowns, and he ran one in himself at South Alabama. Gavin Griffin leads the runners with 503 yards.

"Donnie Smith comes off the corner like he's late for supper," Marlo says. "Johnson better know where he is on every snap."

Then Marlo plays his best card. Troy has won five of the seven meetings since 2016, including 28-20 in 2024 and a 49-28 blowout in 2019.

"Those are nice memories," Royce says. "We have newer ones."

"Not against us, you don't."

Here's how it goes. The high school bands fill the South End Zone, and The Vet is as loud as it's been all year. Georgia Southern scores on its first drive, with Johnson hitting Taylor on a slant. Troy answers when Griffin breaks a long run and Crowder sneaks it in. Smith gets home for a sack in the second quarter that stalls an Eagle drive, and the halftime show with all those bands nearly rattles the press box. The Eagles take the lead back in the third quarter with a field goal and a short touchdown. Troy closes to within four late, but Johnson converts a big third down and Georgia Southern runs out the clock.

Royce tips his cap toward the bands. "Beautiful Eagle Creek," he says, for no reason except that he always says it.

Marlo shrugs and finishes the bag. "That's peanuts," he says, and heads home to study film all night.

My call: Georgia Southern Eagles 25, Troy Trojans 21.
