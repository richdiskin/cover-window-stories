GameDay Report

Virginia Tech at Miami: College GameDay Is Back on the Miami Campus

ESPN's College GameDay is back on the Miami campus Saturday, the school's official preview said, with the main stage on the north end of the patio as a prelude to Virginia Tech's visit to Hard Rock Stadium, on ESPN, at 7 Eastern. Miami said this is the show's 10th visit for a Hurricanes game, the third trip to campus, and the second straight season the show has come for a Miami game (last year it was Florida). The Hurricanes, the school noted, are 8-1 in the previous nine games that featured College GameDay.

The live card on this site lists Miami at No. 7, and Miami's own preview used the same number. Virginia Tech's official preview billed the Hurricanes at No. 5. This story stays with the card and flags the Hokies' number. Miami is 9-1 after a 36-25 win against Duke, a night Darian Mensah threw for 382 yards. He has 3,076 passing yards, 29 touchdowns and only two interceptions. Javian Mallory ran for two scores against Duke and has 580 rushing yards. Virginia Tech is 6-4 after a 22-40 loss to Stanford. Ethan Grunkemeyer has 2,701 passing yards; Jeffrey Overton Jr. has 869 rushing yards.

The AccuWeather note on ESPN's game page had a 64 percent chance of rain in Miami Gardens, which is enough to make a wet night possible. The show will have signed off by then. The football will not.

A campus prefaced by College GameDay, a 9-1 home team with 29 touchdown passes, and a 6-4 visitor coming off a 40-22 loss: that is Saturday night in Miami Gardens. If Mensah repeats a 30-of-41 night, the Hokies will have to score early and often. If Overton finds the same 107 yards he had against Stanford, the night might last longer than the morning show.

Hard Rock Stadium turns Saturday night into Miami's Salute to Service Game, and of all the teams that could have drawn that date, it's Virginia Tech, the school with a Corps of Cadets marching through the middle of campus. Ruthanne Dellinger, who once led the Corps band as drum major and still stands at attention for the anthem, calls it a homecoming of a different kind for the Hokies, while Javi Castillo, the old Hurricanes defensive back who spent the morning wandering the Miami Book Fair Street Fair downtown, calls it the night the Canes write the next chapter of a 9-1 season.

Javi: Book fair had a poster this year. The Freedom Tower built entirely out of books. I bought two.

Ruthanne: Did you read anything?

Javi: I read the room. The room says Miami.

Ruthanne: The room is your living room.

The Virginia Tech vs Miami prediction starts with what both sides know: the Hurricanes have owned this matchup lately. Miami has won seven of the nine meetings since 2016, including 34-17 a year ago. But Ruthanne remembers the two that got away, a 37-16 Tech win in 2016 and a 42-35 shootout in 2019.

Ruthanne: You don't forget the good ones. You keep them folded like a flag.

Javi: Keep it folded. We're not reopening it tonight.

Miami's season has been close to perfect. The Canes are 9-1, 7-0 in the ACC and ranked No. 7, and the only loss came at Notre Dame, 31-24. Darian Mensah has thrown for 3,076 yards with 29 touchdowns and just two interceptions, which is the kind of number you read twice to make sure it's real. Malachi Toney has 1,027 receiving yards and 13 touchdowns, and Javian Mallory has nine rushing scores. Cooper Barkate caught eight balls for 134 yards against Duke last week.

Javi: Mensah throws it like he's mailing a letter. Addressed, stamped, delivered.

Ruthanne: Our defense intercepts mail.

Javi: Not his. He's thrown two all year.

The defense has teeth too. Marquise Lightfoot has seven sacks, and Mohamed Toure leads the team with 67 tackles. Miami has held four opponents to 13 points or fewer, including Clemson on the road, 41-13.

Virginia Tech comes in 6-4, and Ruthanne won't pretend the last two weeks didn't happen. The Hokies lost 51-28 at SMU, then 40-22 at home to Stanford. There's still real talent in maroon. Ethan Grunkemeyer has thrown for 2,701 yards and 20 touchdowns, Jeffrey Overton Jr. has 869 rushing yards, and Que'Sean Brown has 66 catches for 793 yards. Aycen Stevens has 8.5 sacks, more than anyone on either roster.

Ruthanne: Stevens is going to be in your backfield all night. Somebody tell Mensah to keep his head on a swivel.

Javi: Then Mensah gets the ball out quicker. He went 30 of 34 at Wake Forest. The man doesn't hold it.

Ruthanne jumps out of her seat when the Hokie defense makes a stop. She's counting on doing a lot of jumping.

Ruthanne: Every third-down stop, I'm on my feet. You'll hear me in the upper deck.

Javi: Bring earplugs for your neighbors, mi amiga, and bring a fan, because the Canes are bringing the heat and the beat.

Ruthanne: That rhymed.

Javi: Everything rhymes in Miami. It's in the water.

The game turns on whether Virginia Tech can slow down Mensah and Toney long enough to let Overton and Grunkemeyer keep the ball. Tech scored 33 on Pittsburgh and 34 on Georgia Tech this year, so the Hokies aren't going to be shut out. But Miami beat Pittsburgh 52-23, and Stanford, the team that just beat Tech by 18, lost to Miami 45-6 in September. That's the kind of math a drum major doesn't love.

Ruthanne: I don't love it. But we march anyway.

Javi: Respect. Salute to service, salute to the Hokies, and salute to the scoreboard operator, because he's going to be busy.

Miami makes it a long night for the visitors, the Hokies keep scrapping, and Ruthanne still stands straight as a rifle when the last whistle blows.

My call: Miami Hurricanes 44, Virginia Tech Hokies 18.
