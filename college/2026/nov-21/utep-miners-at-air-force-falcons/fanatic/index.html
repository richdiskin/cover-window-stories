UTEP Miners vs Air Force Falcons: Wings of Blue Over Senior Night

UTEP Miners (1-9) at Air Force Falcons (8-2)

8:00 PM ET Fri Nov 20 · Falcon Stadium · CW

XFacebook

Three hours before kickoff on Friday, the Air Force Drum and Bugle Corps will march into Falcon Alley, and Lt. Col. (Ret.) Marissa Okonkwo-Hale will be standing at the front of the crowd in her aviator shades like she's inspecting the formation. UTEP Miners vs Air Force Falcons is Senior Night at Falcon Stadium, kickoff 6:00 PM in Colorado Springs and 8:00 PM ET, and the Academy is rolling out the full show: a Wings of Blue parachute jump about twenty minutes before the game, a military flyover, and live falcons with their cadet falconers at halftime.

Neto Galindo drove up from El Paso with a thermos of green chile stew and a jacket that he admits is built for a West Texas November, not a Colorado one.

"You people put a flyover AND skydivers on Senior Night?" he says. "Back home we get Paydirt Pete. I respect the budget, Colonel."

"Call sign for you is 'Thermos,'" Marissa says. "Welcome to the Academy."

The scoreboard part is harder for Neto. UTEP is 1-9, and the Miners have scored 14.2 points a game. They opened the year at Oklahoma and lost 51-0, and the only win came against Texas Southern, 51-10, back in September. The last two weeks have been close, though. The Miners lost 17-14 to Hawai'i and then 27-15 to Wyoming, and in that Wyoming game Raymond Moore III ran for 164 yards on 20 carries.

"Moore's the real thing," Neto says. "He runs like he's late for a shift. That's my highest grade. Pure ore."

Moore leads UTEP with 686 rushing yards and has taken over most of the throwing lately, too. EJ Colson Jr. has 687 throwing yards and 7 touchdowns for the year. Jaden Smith leads the receivers with 314 yards, and Jayden Wilson leads the defense with 65 tackles. Sterling Miles has 4 sacks off the end.

Marissa listens politely, then gets to her briefing. Air Force is 8-2. The Falcons beat Navy 14-9 in October, beat Army 24-21 at West Point this month, and then held San José State to a field goal in a 24-3 win last week. Evan Medders, a quarterback who mostly keeps the ball, has 695 rushing yards and 8 touchdowns, and he ran for 146 yards on 13 carries against San José State.

"Medders is call sign 'Afterburner,'" Marissa says. "Fletcher is 'Radar.'"

Blake Fletcher leads Air Force with 92 tackles at linebacker. Liam Szarka leads the Falcons with 296 throwing yards, which tells you everything about how much this team likes to throw. Simon Kibbee, a tight end, leads in receiving with 166 yards. Max Mustell has 3 sacks from the secondary.

"Two hundred ninety-six yards in the air for a season?" Neto says. "We've had receivers do that by accident."

"We don't need the air," Marissa says. "We have the Wings of Blue for that."

There is one more thing Neto has working for him: the Falcons have stumbled before. They lost 38-32 at home to North Dakota State in September and fell 28-14 at Wyoming in October. Neto saw that Wyoming score and has been quoting it all week to anyone in earshot.

"Wyoming beat you by fourteen," he says. "Wyoming beat us by twelve. That makes us practically even, by my math."

"Your math needs a debrief," Marissa says.

Here's how it plays out. The Wings of Blue land on target, the flyover rattles the press box, and the seniors get a huge roar at kickoff. Moore breaks a long run on UTEP's first drive and Neto stands up and swings his imaginary pickaxe. Then the Air Force option starts its grind. Medders keeps it, pitches it, keeps it again, and the Falcons put together long drives that eat the clock and the Miners' energy. By halftime, when the cadet falconers bring out the birds, Air Force has a comfortable lead. Fletcher and the defense keep Moore boxed in during the second half, and UTEP grabs a late touchdown against the backups.

Marissa slides her aviator shades down and says "Mission accomplished." Neto zips his jacket, says "Back to the mine," and rolls up his sleeves for the long drive south.

My call: Air Force Falcons 36, UTEP Miners 13.
