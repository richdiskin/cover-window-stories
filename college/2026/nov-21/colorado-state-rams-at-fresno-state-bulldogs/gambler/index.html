Colorado State is 3-7 after a 7-73 loss at Washington State last Saturday, the same afternoon already written up from the other sideline. Hauss Hejny has 1,590 passing yards, 16 touchdowns and three interceptions. Durell Robinson has 474 rushing yards and nine scores. A 7-73 box does not get rewritten as a close game. It was 73-7, and the Rams have to play again Saturday night. Colorado State's official Setting the Stage note, published Friday, also called Fresno a 5-5 team trying to become bowl-eligible this week and quoted coach Jim Mora: "Without a doubt, it's a mental challenge and it's also a schematic challenge as well. We just have to continue to focus on doing the right things the right way with more consistency." That is the home-road contrast in the school's own words: a 5-5 host with a two-point loss last week, and a 3-7 visitor that has to find consistency after 73-7.

A 5-5 home team coming off a two-point loss, a 3-7 visitor coming off a 73-7 defeat, and a night game in Fresno: that is the matchup. If Donelson finds the end zone the way eight scores suggest, the Rams will have to keep pace after a week that already got away from them. If Colorado State's ground game looks like nine rushing touchdowns on the year, Fresno will have to tackle better than a 37-35 road loss allowed.
