UCLA vs Michigan: Unbeaten Bruins Walk Into the Big House Finale

UCLA Bruins (10-0) at Michigan Wolverines (5-5)

3:30 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Michigan Stadium · CBS

XFacebook

"Do you know how many songs are written out by number up there?" Gus Hartwell asks, pointing at the second floor of the Kerrytown market like it's a lecture hall. "More than 200. Seventeen bells. Anyone can play them. Saturday morning, 10:30 to 11, and I will be at the keys, and I will be playing 'The Victors' until somebody stops me."

"Nobody is going to stop you," Kenzie Rhee says. "That's the problem."

So the UCLA Bruins vs Michigan Wolverines game starts in Ann Arbor with a bell concert, a home finale and a Michigan insider who has turned a free public chime into his personal warmup act. Kenzie, the UCLA insider who storyboards every drive like she's pitching a movie, came east with a script she likes a lot.

"Picture it," she says, framing the Big House with her fingers. "Ten wins. Zero losses. First place in the Big Ten. We go into the biggest stadium in the country on its last home Saturday and we leave with eleven. Roll credits."

Gus clears his throat, which is what he does before he gets technical. "Your movie has had some slow scenes. You beat Oregon 17-15. You beat Wisconsin 17-7. Last week you beat Illinois 22-14. That's not a blockbuster. That's an indie film with good reviews."

"Good reviews win awards, Gus."

This is the strange part of the matchup. UCLA is unbeaten, and the Bruins have won with defense as much as anything, allowing 14.1 points a game. They gave up three at Maryland and none to Nevada. Linebacker Sammy Omosigho has 5.5 sacks and Jalen Woods has 75 tackles. On offense, Wayne Knight is the workhorse, with 845 rushing yards and 13 touchdowns. Ty Dieffenbach has thrown the most passes in each of UCLA's last five games, and last week he found Aidan Mizell for 176 yards against Illinois. Semaj Morgan leads the receivers with 525 yards.

Michigan, under Kyle Whittingham, is 5-5 and fighting for a bowl game. The Wolverines have not won at home since beating Penn State 21-18 on Oct. 17, and after this, the road goes to Ohio State. Whittingham said it plainly after the loss at Oregon: "They better be motivated to make a bowl game."

"We will be motivated," Gus says. "I have done the math, Kenzie. Our defense allows 17.7 points a game. John Henry Daley has six sacks. Troy Bowles has 79 tackles. If we keep you in the teens, we have a chance, and that is not a feeling. That is arithmetic."

"And your offense?"

Gus takes a long pause, the kind a proud man takes when the data is not on his side. Michigan scores 18.3 points a game. The Wolverines completed just six passes at Oregon. Tommy Carr has thrown the most passes in each of the last four games, and Bryce Underwood has six interceptions on the year. Savion Hiter gave the offense a pulse last week with 107 rushing yards.

"Our offense," Gus says, "is a work in progress. A noble one."

Kenzie softens, a little. "I've seen your home games. You beat Western Michigan by one. You beat Oklahoma by seven. You lost to Iowa by one and to Indiana in overtime. Nobody blows you out here."

"Correct," Gus says. "The Big House is where good teams come to sweat."

Here's how it goes. Gus finishes "The Victors" on the chime at 10:59 and gets polite applause from three tourists and a dog. By kickoff, the Big House is loud for its last home game of the year, and Michigan's defense makes the first quarter miserable for everyone. Hiter breaks a long run to set up a field goal. UCLA answers with Knight, again and again, and Dieffenbach hits one deep shot to Mizell for the only touchdown of the first half. Michigan hangs in, scoring on a short field after a Daley sack and fumble. The Wolverines take a late lead, and the students start singing "Mr. Brightside." Then Knight goes to work one more time, and the Bruins kick the winning field goal with under two minutes left.

Kenzie claps her hands together like a clapboard. "And... scene! That's a wrap!"

Gus closes his notebook. "Victors... valiant... later," he says, and heads for the library.

My call: UCLA Bruins 21, Michigan Wolverines 19.
