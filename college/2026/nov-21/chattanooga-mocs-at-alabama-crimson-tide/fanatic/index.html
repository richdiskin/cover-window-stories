Chattanooga vs Alabama: Mocs Finish the Regular Season in Tuscaloosa

Chattanooga Mocs (7-4) at Alabama Crimson Tide (8-2)

2:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Bryant-Denny Stadium · SECN+

XFacebook

Chattanooga vs Alabama is the Mocs' regular-season finale at Bryant-Denny Stadium this Saturday, a two o'clock trip that Chattanooga's own preview calls the fifth straight time this series has ended the year in Tuscaloosa. Alabama insider Mary-Kathryn Strickland says that makes Saturday a crimson home date with Auburn already written on the wall for Nov 28, while Chattanooga visitor Ellis Stringer says Kevin Lalin, Jestin Gilmore and 14 Alabama-born Mocs did not come back to the state to pose for a postcard.

MK: Ellis, this is Kalen DeBoer's first look at your club, and it is our house. Roll Tide is a greeting. It is also a warning.

Ellis: You can save the warning for when Lalin runs into the second level. One thousand forty-six yards. Eight scores. Two hundred eight carries. He just became the 16th 1,000-yard rusher in the program, first since 2022. Gilmore is from Tuscaloosa. He told our people he was pumped to play ball in his hometown.

That's the tension in Tuscaloosa this week. One side is 8-2 and No. 3 in the College Football Playoff, with Keelon Russell throwing for 2,480 yards and 21 scores, plus five rushing scores, and a Davey O'Brien Award semifinalist. The other side is 7-4 and 6-2 in the Southern Conference, allowing 12.7 points a game, the No. 1 scoring defense in the FCS on Chattanooga's own preview, and finishing the regular season on the road in a 100,077-seat stadium.

MK: Here is the thing you don't understand about this Alabama team. It can still go a long way in a hurry. Fifty-six to 23 at Mississippi State. Forty-nine to 18 over South Carolina. Forty-eight to 10 over East Carolina. Russell is in his second season here, 187 of 278, and Daniel Hill has 436 rushing yards. When the band is loud and the Tide is in rhythm, this place still knows how to score.

Ellis: And last week you scored 21 at Vanderbilt. Your largest winning margin this season is 38 points. We have not beaten an SEC team since 1958, I know the date, 14-6 at Tennessee. We came to run the clock and to let Gilmore play in front of the people who watched him grow up.

Stringer did his homework too, and he will tell you Chattanooga's season has been built on defense and Lalin. The Mocs beat Wofford 41-7, smothered Samford 19-3, and won 17-10 at Furman last Saturday, a game in which punter Heath Jehu, a Birmingham native, earned Southern Conference special teams honors. They also dropped an overtime game 7-14 to Mercer, and Matthew Clemmer's 3-of-7 day at Furman told you how this team wants to spend a Saturday.

Ellis: I can live with a last regular-season chapter that has Lalin in it and Gilmore going home. Rusty Wright is 49-38 in his eighth season. He is 0-1 against the Tide. We are going to play it like a goodbye worth taking back over the mountain.

MK: Your defense is proud, and it should be. Zion Rutledge has 103 tackles. Orlando Daniels has seven sacks. My front is still coming. Caleb Woodson has 74 tackles. Yhonzae Pierre has 5.5 sacks. The last time you were here, in 2023, it ended 66-10, and it was Nick Saban's last regular-season home game. We remember.

Ellis: We remember too. That is why we came back.

The two of them agree on exactly one point: this series is 0-14, and that does not tell you how Saturday will feel at 2 p.m. MK wants the home sideline clean and ready for Auburn on Nov 28. Ellis wants a road finish that looks like a 7-4 team with the No. 1 scoring defense in its classification, not like a walking visit.

What makes this date dangerous is the calendar. The noise comes early, and the next opponent is already in the building in spirit. If Alabama starts fast, with Russell throwing and Hill going downhill, the Mocs will spend the second half trying to keep the clock honest. If the Tide starts thinking about Nov 28 before the first quarter is over, Lalin can hang around the way a 1,046-yard back is built to hang around.

MK: The band gets loud, the Tide gets going, and then the afternoon gets decided. That's the order of operations. Roll Tide, and I mean it.

Ellis: And while you're looking at Nov 28, we'll be handing it to Lalin. That's my order of operations.

Both of them want a hard game and a finish worth remembering.

MK: We get Auburn next in this stadium. Saturday is still ours.

Ellis: Lalin and Gilmore came to play in front of the people who raised them. That is the whole trip.

My call: Alabama Crimson Tide 45, Chattanooga Mocs 7.
