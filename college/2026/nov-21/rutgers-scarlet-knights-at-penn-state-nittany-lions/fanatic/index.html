Rutgers at Penn State: After a 31-0 Loss, Rutgers Goes to Beaver Stadium

Rutgers goes to Beaver Stadium on Saturday at 3:30 p.m. on FS1, a 2-8 team coming off a 31-0 loss to Nebraska, and the loudest news around the program this month was not a depth chart. On Nov. 10, athletics director Keli Zinn published a statement that opened with this: the 2026 season "has fallen well below our expectations." She said the results are not acceptable. She also said, after consulting university leadership, that head coach Greg Schiano will be back in 2027. "Let me be very clear: this decision is not reflective of a lowering of standards or an indication that everything is fine. It is not."

That is the official word from the person who makes the hire. Zinn said she and Schiano have a plan, and that plans alone do not produce wins. The Scarlet Knights still have to play Penn State, which is 6-4 after a 42-14 win over Minnesota. Rutgers' own Game 11 preview noted a few places the math still looks tidy: fourth nationally in fewest penalty yards per game (35.30), 12th in yards per completion (14.23). Those are real numbers. They do not erase 0-31.

Dylan Lonergan has 1,066 passing yards, 10 touchdowns and six interceptions in the facts we can source. Antwan Raymond has 810 rushing yards. KJ Duff has 774 receiving yards and nine scores and was named a Biletnikoff Award semifinalist in a Nov. 16 school release. Penn State's Rocco Becht has 2,163 passing yards, 18 touchdowns and five interceptions; Carson Hansen has 115-plus carries in the facts pack.

A 2-8 visitor whose athletic director just told the fan base the season is not good enough, and a 6-4 home team that just scored 42: that is Saturday in State College. Schiano is coming back in 2027. The Scarlet Knights still have to walk out of the tunnel this week.

Luann Kessler has a plan for Thursday night, and it starts at 5 p.m. sharp in MLK Plaza. "Santa arrives at five," she says, counting it off on her fingers like a volleyball rotation. "Light show at seven. And at 7:30, Santa and one lucky kid light the evergreen at the Allen Street Gates. Then Saturday is Senior Day. Vinny, this is the best week of the year in State College."

Vinny Scolari, who drove in from Exit 9 with a sub on the passenger seat, looks at the schedule like it owes him money. "Light Up Night. Beautiful. Very festive. You know what lights up in New Brunswick? My phone, when people text me the scores."

So the Rutgers Scarlet Knights vs Penn State Nittany Lions game arrives at the end of a holiday week in Happy Valley, with the downtown tree glowing two days before the seniors take their last walk at Beaver Stadium. Luann, a former Penn State volleyball setter who has never met a linebacker she didn't love, is ready to celebrate. Vinny, the Jersey guy who grades every trench battle like a boardwalk arm-wrestle, is ready to argue.

"We're bowl eligible," Luann says. "Six and four, three wins in a row, and we just beat Minnesota 42-14. Carson Hansen ran for 126 yards. Rocco Becht threw three touchdown passes on nine completions. Nine! That's efficiency, Vinny."

"I saw it," Vinny says. "I didn't enjoy it."

Penn State's season had a rough middle. The Nittany Lions started 3-0, then lost four straight, to Wisconsin, Northwestern, USC and Michigan. Since then Matt Campbell's team has won three in a row: Purdue, a 28-25 thriller at Washington and the Minnesota rout that clinched a bowl trip. Becht has thrown for 2,163 yards with 18 touchdowns and five interceptions. Chase Sowell leads the receivers with 635 yards. On defense, Tony Rojas leads the team in both sacks, with five, and tackles, with 71.

"Tony Rojas," Luann says, with a hand over her heart. "That's a Linebacker U linebacker."

Vinny, to his credit, does not pretend. Rutgers is 2-8. The Scarlet Knights lost to UMass in the opener. They have lost four straight since winning at Maryland 37-30, and last week Nebraska shut them out, 31-0, in Piscataway. AJ Surace has thrown the most passes in each of the last four games, and last week he went 4 for 12 for 24 yards.

"Fuhgeddaboudit," Vinny says, and then he can't. "Okay, I'm still thinking about it."

But Vinny has his guys, and he lists them with the pride of a man ordering for the whole table. KJ Duff has 774 receiving yards and nine touchdown catches. Antwan Raymond has 810 rushing yards. Dylan Lonergan, who started the year at quarterback, has 10 touchdown passes.

"And here's the best part," Vinny says, sliding his chair closer. "Last year? Forty to thirty-six. We were right there."

Luann smiles the way only a Central Pennsylvania volleyball setter can, sweetly and with menace. "Penn State has won every one of these since 2016, Vinny. Nine in a row. Your best year was losing by four."

"Losing by four is a moral victory in Jersey," Vinny says. "We frame those."

Here's how it goes. The seniors run out of the tunnel to a full Beaver Stadium, and Penn State scores on its first two drives, one on a Hansen run and one on a Becht throw to Sowell. Rutgers answers when Duff gets behind the secondary for a long touchdown, and Vinny stands up so fast his sub hits the floor. Rojas makes two big stops in the second quarter, and the Lions lead comfortably at halftime. In the second half, Penn State keeps the ground game rolling, and the backups get their snaps late. Raymond grinds out a fourth-quarter touchdown for Rutgers to make the final look respectable.

Luann heads to the Creamery and orders a Peachy Paterno, eating it slowly and triumphantly in the cold.

Vinny orders a sub big enough for the whole studio anyway. "For the ride home," he says. "It's a long ride."

My call: Penn State Nittany Lions 37, Rutgers Scarlet Knights 16.
