Georgia State vs Marshall: Bowl Hopes Collide on Senior Day in Huntington

Georgia State Panthers (5-5) at Marshall Thundering Herd (5-5)

4:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Joan C. Edwards Stadium · ESPN+

XFacebook

GameDay Report

Georgia State at Marshall: Two 5-5 Teams, and a Bowl Bid Sitting on the Result

Marshall's official preview put the whole season on one sentence: the winner of Saturday's game at Joan C. Edwards Stadium becomes bowl eligible. The Herd is 5-5 and 2-4 in the Sun Belt after a 16-20 loss to App State. Georgia State is 5-5 after a 17-34 loss to Georgia Southern. Kickoff is 4:02 p.m., per the school's Know Before You Go, on ESPN+. Before the game, Marshall will honor 23 seniors in their final home appearance, including running back Jo'Shon Barbie, who ran 17 times for 108 yards last week. Offensive lineman Jalen Slappy is making his 40th career start, the school said.

The gameday note asked the crowd to wear white and billed a Fan Appreciation Celebration and a Salute to Service, with free popcorn for everyone in the building. That is the official welcome in Huntington, and it is a much warmer welcome than Georgia State got last week in Atlanta, where the Panthers lost 17-34 to Georgia Southern. The football problem is simpler. Carlos Del Rio-Wilson has 1,213 passing yards, six touchdowns and five interceptions for Marshall. Cameran Brown has 1,356 passing yards, 14 scores and five interceptions for Georgia State, and Lanear McCrary Jr. has 612 rushing yards.

Two 5-5 teams, 23 seniors walking out, and a bowl bid sitting on the result: that is 4 o'clock in Huntington. If Marshall plays like a group that just had a 108-yard afternoon from a senior back, the white shirts will have something to take home. If Georgia State's passing game looks like 14 touchdowns, the Fan Appreciation crowd will have to become a fourth-quarter crowd in a hurry.

Books are a dollar apiece on Friday night at the Gallaher Village Public Library in Huntington, or five dollars for a whole bag. Any bag. The library's semi-annual Night Market runs from 6 to 8 p.m. on Norway Avenue, and Mercy Holderby is bringing the biggest kelly green tote she owns.

"Any bag, Malik," she says. "They said any bag. I have a bag that could hold a linebacker."

Malik Overstreet, Georgia State insider and walking map of every MARTA stop in Atlanta, looks genuinely impressed. "In Atlanta, a used bookstore is a whole trip across town. Here it's a fundraiser with vendors and a parking warning. I respect it."

The parking warning is real. The library says the event gets crowded, and folks should walk, carpool or park at Cabell 911. Mercy, a nurse who claims she was born wearing kelly green, has already arranged a carpool. Saturday, she'll make the same kind of plan for Senior Day, when the Georgia State Panthers vs Marshall Thundering Herd game closes out the Herd's home schedule at Joan C. Edwards Stadium.

"This one matters," Mercy says. "We're 5-5. They're 5-5. Whoever wins goes bowling. Whoever loses has a long winter."

"Correct," Malik says. "And it's going to be us bowling."

Malik has some ammunition. Georgia State scores 32.7 points a game, and the Panthers can put up points in a hurry. They beat Old Dominion 42-10 and won 39-23 at App State two weeks ago. Cameran Brown threw six touchdown passes against Old Dominion, and Ayden Pereira has led the team in throws for the last four games. Lanear McCrary Jr. leads the runners with 612 yards, Rodshaun Dorsey has nine touchdown catches and linebacker Xavier Esqillion has ten sacks.

"Ten sacks," Malik says. "Esqillion gets to the quarterback faster than I get through Five Points at rush hour."

Mercy isn't rattled. She grew up hearing about the program's rebirth, and she knows every Marshall comeback story by heart. "We've beaten y'all three out of four times, Malik. We're 2-0 against you in Huntington, 28-23 in 2022 and 35-20 in 2024. And last year we won 30-18 in your town."

"Last year was last year."

"Last year was a very nice bus ride home."

Marshall's season has been all about close games at home. The Herd beat Middle Tennessee 28-26, Gardner-Webb 36-35 and South Alabama 20-13 at Joan C. Edwards Stadium. They lost to Coastal Carolina by six and to App State by four last week. Every home game has been tight. Jo'Shon Barbie ran for 108 yards against App State and leads the team with 443 on the season. Carlos Del Rio-Wilson has thrown for 1,213 yards, and Xayvion Turner-Bradshaw leads the receivers with 397. Caleb Clark-Glover leads the defense with 71 tackles.

"Our games come down to the last drive," Mercy says. "I'm a nurse. I'm trained for this."

Malik points out the bad news gently. The Herd allow 30.7 points a game, and they gave up 49 at Georgia Southern and 45 at James Madison.

"That was on the road," Mercy says. "This is home. On Senior Day. In white."

She's right about the white. Marshall has asked fans to wear it for Saturday's game, which is also Salute to Service.

Here's how it goes. The seniors are honored before kickoff, and the stadium is a sea of white. Georgia State strikes first with a long McCrary run. Marshall answers when Barbie pounds it in from close range, and the "We Are... Marshall" chant shakes the place. The teams trade touchdowns into the fourth quarter, with Dorsey catching two for the Panthers. Marshall goes up by three late, but Georgia State answers with a quick drive, and Esqillion's sack on Marshall's final possession seals a four-point win.

Malik dances like he just found a parking spot downtown.

Mercy holds up her giant tote bag. "I'm going back to the library," she says. "I need something sad to read."

My call: Georgia State Panthers 28, Marshall Thundering Herd 24.
