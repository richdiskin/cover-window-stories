Akron Zips vs Massachusetts Minutemen: Senior Night in Amherst

Akron Zips (1-9) at Massachusetts Minutemen (5-5)

7:00 PM ET Wed Nov 18 · Warren McGuirk Alumni Stadium · CBSSN

XFacebook

The first time UMass ever won a football game as an FBS program, the visitors were from Akron. That was Nov. 10, 2012, a 22-14 night with four Minutemen interceptions, and Abner Kettleworth has carried it around for fourteen years like a smooth stone in his coat pocket, ready for the night Akron Zips vs Massachusetts Minutemen came back to McGuirk Alumni Stadium.

That night is Wednesday at 7:00 PM ET. It's also Senior Night and Fan Appreciation night in Amherst, and Hollister "Zip" Grubbs has driven in from Ohio with a 1-9 team and the unshakable belief that rubber always comes back to its shape.

"Fourteen years," Hollister says. "Abner, that's a long time to hold a grudge against a tire company's hometown."

"It's not a grudge," Abner says, very slowly, the way people in the Pioneer Valley say everything. "It's a keepsake. Grudges are heavy. Keepsakes are light. Isaiah Rodgers took one back for six that night. I like to think about it when the leaves fall."

"Well, think about last year too," Hollister says. "Forty-four to ten. In Akron."

Abner looks out over the hills for a moment. "I think about that one when the leaves are already on the ground."

This is the seventh meeting, and Akron leads the series 4-2. But this season doesn't look anything like last season. UMass is 5-5, with a defense that ranks third in the MAC in yards allowed and an offense that has had to find answers at quarterback. William Watson III leads the MAC in completion rate, hitting 115 of 167, but the UMass notes say he missed the Miami (OH) and Buffalo games and most of the Toledo game with lower body injuries. Abner doesn't pretend to know who takes the first snap Wednesday.

"Whoever it is," he says, "he has T.Y. Harding. Harding has scored on a catch, a run and a punt return this year. Only two players in the whole country can say that. The other one plays in Boca Raton and has never had a proper Amherst apple."

Akron's year has been a long one. The Zips beat Robert Morris 45-10 in September and haven't won since, and they're 0-6 in conference play. Hollister knows it. He just refuses to let it shrink him.

"Jordan Gant has 791 rushing yards," he says. "Donelius Johnson has 89 tackles. And Cibastian Broughton threw 2 touchdowns to Marcel Williams against Western Michigan last week. We've found something. It's small. A blimp starts small too, before you fill it up."

"A blimp starts as a large bag," Abner says.

"A large bag full of hope."

There's one thing about Akron Abner can't shake, and it has nothing to do with the record. Akron games light up the scoreboard. Every one of the Zips' ten games has seen at least 48 points combined. They give up plenty, and lately they've scored some too.

"So we'll need points," Abner says. "Derek Morris has made four field goals from fifty yards or more this season. Only seven kickers in the country can say that. We don't need touchdowns every trip. We need patience and a strong left leg."

"Is it his left leg?"

"I have no idea. It's a figure of speech in the Valley."

UMass also does the little things. The Minutemen lead the MAC with a plus-0.40 turnover margin, they've thrown only four interceptions all year, and Edward Phillipson is booming punts 45.6 yards a kick, best in the conference. Abner calls it "colonial football." Hollister calls it boring and then admits it works.

Here's how Wednesday goes. The seniors walk out with their families, Abner rings his little colonial bell, and the Minutemen start fast with Harding getting loose on an early touch. Akron answers with Gant and a Broughton strike to Williams, because these Zips can make a game of it for a half. Then the UMass defense settles in, Johnson piles up tackles on the other side trying to keep Akron close, and Morris drops a long one through the uprights in the third quarter to make the crowd roar. Kimmons grinds out the fourth quarter on the ground, and the Minutemen send their seniors off with a win.

Hollister bounces on his heels at the final whistle anyway. "Tread's still good," he says. Abner walks him to the parking lot, hands him a bag of Amherst apples for the drive, and plants a small tree in his own honor, just in case.

My call: Massachusetts Minutemen 31, Akron Zips 19.
