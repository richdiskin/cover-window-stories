New Mexico Lobos vs Wyoming Cowboys: Light Up Laradise Night

New Mexico Lobos (8-2) at Wyoming Cowboys (7-3)

9:00 PM ET Fri Nov 20 · War Memorial Stadium · FS1

XFacebook

Wyoming is handing every fan an LED wristband on Friday night, giving away 1,000 glow-in-the-dark "Script Laradise" T-shirts, and handing out Cowboy Joe Club thundersticks to the first 1,000 fans, which means New Mexico Lobos vs Wyoming Cowboys at 7 p.m. in Laramie is going to look like a Balloon Fiesta dawn patrol that landed in a snowbank. That's how Paloma Begaye-Ortiz describes it, anyway, and she has flown in the real thing.

Colter Sprague, standing next to her in a Carhartt jacket with a wind gauge clipped to the zipper, has one word for the light show. "Pretty."

"Pretty? It's 'Light Up Laradise,' Colter. Your whole stadium is going to glow."

"Yep."

That's about as chatty as Colter gets, and Paloma has learned to fill in the rest. Wyoming has been on a tear. The Cowboys are 7-3, 6-1 in the Mountain West, sitting first in the conference and riding five straight wins. They are perfect at home, beating Northern Colorado, Hawai'i, Northern Illinois and Air Force at War Memorial. The Air Force game was a 28-14 statement in October, and Colter still smiles about it. That's two smiles in one month, which Paloma says is a personal record.

"Tell me about your quarterback," Paloma says.

Colter checks the wind gauge. "Depends."

It does. Tyler Hughes has run the offense most of the year and leads Wyoming in both throwing yards with 1,238 and rushing with 676. Then last week at UTEP, Landon Sims led the throws and went 19 of 25 for 289 yards in a 27-15 win. Kyle Frendt, the tight end, caught 4 balls for 113 yards in that game and leads the team in receiving. Ethan Stuhlsatz leads the defense with 79 tackles, and Esaia Bogar has 5.5 sacks.

"Two good quarterbacks," Colter says. Then, after a full ten seconds: "Good problem."

Paloma has her own stack of reasons. New Mexico is 8-2 and the defense has been brutal, giving up 11.9 points a game. The Lobos held UTEP to 7, Nevada to 3, and San José State and North Dakota State to 16 each. Scottre Humphrey has 736 rushing yards and 14 touchdowns, a back who runs like the wind's at his tail on every carry. Cade Keith leads the receivers with 384 yards, Darren Agu has 7.5 sacks, and Mercury Swaim leads the defense with 61 tackles.

"Our defense is green chile," Paloma says. "It sneaks up on you. One minute you're fine. Next minute you're crying and reaching for a sopapilla."

Then there's last week, which Paloma would rather not discuss. New Mexico lost 21-17 at home to UNLV, and Toa Fa'avae went 7 of 14 for 40 yards. She calls it "too much heat," sighs once, and moves on.

"And you've had our number lately?" she asks.

"Some," Colter says.

He's being modest. Wyoming won six of the last nine meetings, including a wild 49-45 shootout in 2024. New Mexico won back-to-back meetings in 2020 and 2021, but the Cowboys have taken the last three, and Colter knows it.

Here's how Friday goes. The wristbands light up on cue, the thundersticks never stop, and Wyoming feeds off the crowd early. Hughes or Sims, whoever starts, gets a touchdown drive going in the first quarter and Colter actually says "Yep" out loud. But New Mexico's defense settles in and starts taking the Cowboys' running lanes away. Humphrey finds space in the second quarter, scores twice before the night is through, and Agu gets home for a big sack on a third down. Wyoming kicks a field goal and adds a late touchdown, but the Lobos hold on in the cold and take the win home to Albuquerque.

Paloma pumps her fist and yells, "Red or green? BOTH!" Colter looks at his glowing wristband for a long moment, says "Nope," and walks to the truck.

My call: New Mexico Lobos 27, Wyoming Cowboys 17.
