North Texas vs Tulane: Doubloons, a Drum Kit and a Bowl Bid Uptown

North Texas Mean Green (7-3) at Tulane Green Wave (5-5)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Yulman Stadium · ESPNU

XFacebook

The first 2,000 fans through the gates at Yulman Stadium on Saturday will get a doubloon, a commemorative coin from the Louisiana America 250 Commission, and Tulane insider Twan Ducros-Labat already knows where his is going. "In my trumpet case, next to the mouthpiece," he says. "For luck. We need it."

That's an honest Uptown admission. Tulane is 5-5. The Green Wave beat Rice 20-17 last Saturday, two weeks after Tulsa came into Yulman on Homecoming and won 60-10. One more win makes this team bowl eligible, and the chance comes on Senior Day, when Will Hall walks his seniors and their families onto the field before kickoff. The first 2,500 fans get a Tulane scarf, too, which is a lot of optimism for an 11 a.m. kickoff in New Orleans.

North Texas insider Grady Mayhew-Ochoa is bringing his drumsticks. Denton has one of the most famous college jazz programs in the country, and Grady has been waiting all season to play a New Orleans room.

"Twan, I've got the whole set list written," he says. "We open with Tayven Jackson. We close with Jahiem White. And somewhere in the middle, your defense gets lost in the solo. Mean Green, baby."

Grady has the material. North Texas is 7-3 and scores 41.8 points a game. Jackson has thrown for 3,005 yards with 23 touchdowns and six interceptions. White has run for 1,218 yards and 16 touchdowns. Justin Stevenson has 58 catches for 937 yards. In five straight games this fall, the Mean Green put up 45 at Tulsa, 50 on Charlotte, 49 at Navy, 48 on Florida Atlantic and 52 on Rice.

Then came last Saturday in San Antonio. UTSA won 35-16, and Jackson finished 10 of 20 for 125 yards with an interception. Grady doesn't hide from it.

"Everybody misses a beat," he says. "You don't quit the gig. You count it off again."

Twan isn't buying the drummer's calm. He rates every opponent by its gumbo, and he's been tasting this one all week.

"North Texas gumbo smells great from the street," he says. "Big roux, lots of spice. Then you get it to the table and the bottom of the bowl gives up 31.3 points a game. That's a nice-looking pot with no okra in it. I give it a six."

He has a point. North Texas has allowed 35 or more six times, including 49 at Texas State and 41 to Florida Atlantic. Tulane's problem is finding enough offense to take advantage. The Green Wave score 21.0 a game, and Trace Johnson has thrown for 1,008 yards with three touchdowns and seven interceptions since taking over the job. Johnnie Daniels leads the team with 542 rushing yards, and Destyn Hill has 41 catches for 489 yards.

"We don't win pretty this year," Twan says. "We win at Charlotte in overtime. We win by three at Rice. We beat Memphis 31-17 on a Friday night under the lights. This team plays ugly jazz, Grady. It still swings."

The Tulane defense has carried most of that. Dickson Agu has 96 tackles, and Ed Smith IV has four sacks. The Wave held Duke, South Alabama, Southern Miss, Army and Rice to 24 or fewer.

Then there's the history, and Twan brings it up before Grady can stop him. Tulane has won the last three meetings, 35-28 in 2023, 45-37 in 2024, and 34-21 last December, when the Green Wave went on to win the American Conference and reach the College Football Playoff.

"Three for three, baby," Twan says. "You came to our house in December and left without a trophy. Now you're back for an encore?"

"That was a different band," Grady says. "Different Tulane, too. We've got the best offense on the field Saturday by a lot."

North Texas has its own defensive names. Zakye Barker has 83 tackles, and Davion Rhodes has six and a half sacks. Johnson has been intercepted seven times, and Barker would happily make it eight.

Here's how I see the set playing out. Tulane will feed on Senior Day emotion early, and the doubloons will be flashing in the student section. Hall's team will make North Texas work for the first quarter. But the Mean Green have scored at least 44 seven times this season, and Tulane hasn't scored more than 38 all year. At some point, the Green Wave need to match touchdowns, and they don't have the horns to do it.

Twan will play the alma mater on the trumpet after the game anyway. Grady will tap along on the rail. Both of them will agree on one thing: whoever wrote this set list put North Texas at the end.

"Roll Wave!" Twan says, because he always does.

"Mean Green, baby," Grady says back.

My call: North Texas Mean Green 35, Tulane Green Wave 26.
