Arizona at Kansas State: A Home Finale, and a Ranking the Card Does Not Share

Kansas State called this its home finale at Bill Snyder Family Stadium, with FS1 carrying the 7 o'clock Eastern kickoff, and Arizona's official preview billed the visitor as No. 19. The live card on this site lists Arizona at No. 23. ESPN's game header also showed No. 19. This story stays with the card and flags the school's number.

Both teams are walking in off a one-score kind of week, except only one of them was that close. Arizona, 7-3, lost 39-40 to Utah last Saturday, a night Noah Fifita threw for 356 yards and three scores. He has 3,150 passing yards, 24 touchdowns and six interceptions. Kansas State, 4-6, lost 10-20 at TCU. Dillon Duff went 22-of-33 for 244 yards in that one; Avery Johnson still leads the season passing numbers (1,301 yards), and Rodney Fields Jr. has 607 rushing yards.

K-State coach Collin Klein, in the school's Monday press conference, said the Wildcats "came out flat in the first half" at TCU and were not able to get a difference-maker play on special teams. That is the official recap of last week, not a prediction for Saturday. Arizona just took Utah, the last unbeaten team in that building, to the last snap and still went home with a loss. Manhattan gets a 7-3 visitor and a 4-6 home team trying to finish the year in front of its own crowd.

A home finale, a ranking the school and the card cannot agree on, and two teams coming off losses: that is Saturday night in the Little Apple. If Fifita throws like 356 yards again, Snyder Stadium will have to answer late. If K-State starts faster than it did in Fort Worth, the farewell night will feel like a football game and not a memorial.

Before the seniors take their last walk at Bill Snyder Family Stadium on Saturday, the youngest Wildcat fans in Manhattan get the field first, sort of: K-State is holding its first-ever Youth Combine Day, with kids in eighth grade and younger running pregame skills challenges inside the Shamrock Indoor Practice Facility. Hattie Brandvold says that's the most K-State thing she's ever heard, a farm-town school handing a stopwatch to a ten-year-old the same night it says goodbye to its seniors, and Inez Paloverde, who flew in from Tucson with a thermos of prickly-pear lemonade, says the Arizona Wildcats vs Kansas State Wildcats game will be decided by the grown-ups.

Inez: First thing. The sun sets before kickoff. I can't rank your sunset if I'm in a parking lot.

Hattie: You'll see it from the parking lot. Flint Hills sky. Best in the conference.

Inez: I'll be the judge. I have a spreadsheet.

Hattie: Of course you do.

This is the third straight year these two have met and the first time it counts as a Big 12 game. K-State won 31-7 in Manhattan two years ago, and Arizona answered 23-17 in Tucson last September. So it's one apiece, and Hattie points out that the home team won both.

Inez: That's a coincidence.

Hattie: That's a pattern. I studied soil. I know a pattern.

Arizona arrives 7-3 and ranked No. 23, but it arrives sore. A week ago, Utah beat the Wildcats 40-39 in Tucson, and it stung because Noah Fifita did his part, throwing for 356 yards and three touchdowns. Fifita has 3,150 yards and 24 touchdowns this season with only six interceptions, and his go-to target, Isaiah Mizell, has 868 yards and nine scores. Kedrick Reescano has eight rushing touchdowns, and on defense Chase Kennedy has 6.5 sacks.

Inez: Fifita doesn't panic. He threw for 417 at Texas Tech. He'll handle a cold night.

Hattie: Cold nights here are different. The wind comes across the prairie like it has somewhere to be.

Kansas State's season has been harder. The Wildcats are 4-6 and 1-6 in the league, and they've lost four straight. But a lot of the losses were close: 31-26 at Cincinnati, 27-24 in overtime at Colorado, 20-10 at TCU last week. The two that weren't close, 35-16 to Houston and 66-28 to Oklahoma State, both came at home, and Hattie doesn't want to talk about the second one.

Hattie: We don't discuss the Oklahoma State game. It's like a hailstorm in June. You replant and move on.

Inez: Sixty-six.

Hattie: We said we don't discuss it.

The K-State quarterback picture has rotated. Avery Johnson threw the most times early in the year and still leads the team with 1,301 throwing yards. Since mid-October, Jacob Knuth, Blake Barnett and Dillon Duff have each led the Wildcats in attempts at least once, and Duff went 22 of 33 for 244 yards at TCU. Whoever has the ball will look for Jaron Tibbs, who caught eight balls for 115 yards and a touchdown in that game, and hand it to Rodney Fields Jr., who has 607 rushing yards. Jayden Bryant has 4.5 sacks and will spend the night chasing Fifita, while safety Logan Bartley leads the team with 63 tackles.

Hattie calls a touchdown drive a bumper harvest. She's hoping for a few.

Hattie: Fields runs it, Tibbs catches it, we grind the clock. That's farming. Slow and steady, and you get paid in November.

Inez: Fifita plays piccolo. Fast, high and you can hear it over everything.

Hattie: You're comparing your quarterback to your old band instrument.

Inez: I'm comparing him to the best instrument.

What decides it is simple enough. Arizona's offense is better, and it should score. But Kansas State is at home for its seniors, it has a receiver who can make plays, and it has been in most of its games until late. The kids from the combine will be in the stands yelling for every snap, and that's a lot of noise from small people.

Inez: I'll give you this much. Your crowd is going to be loud.

Hattie: And your quarterback is going to be good. We'll see which one wins.

Fifita makes enough throws, Mizell gets loose once or twice, and the purple crowd keeps it close until the last few minutes.

My call: Arizona Wildcats 30, Kansas State Wildcats 25.
