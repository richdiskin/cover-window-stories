GameDay Report

South Florida at Florida Atlantic: A Red Out in Boca, Two Teams at 6-4

Florida Atlantic asked the crowd to wear red, and the school's "Know Before You Go" framed Saturday as a Coca-Cola Senior Day at Flagler Credit Union Stadium, 2:30 p.m. on ESPN+, honoring senior players and the support staff who keep the place running. Both teams are 6-4. That is a rivalry game dressed as a farewell, and it is the last time this senior class will hear its own building at full volume.

USF coach Brian Hartline, in week-of comments, said the Bulls have to get after FAU quarterback Caden Veltkamp. The Bulls have been starting Luke Kromenhoek in recent weeks after Michael Van Buren Jr.'s injury, with Van Buren's return still uncertain in the previews we used. FAU receiver Easton Messer was listed as questionable after leaving the Tulsa game, per midweek reports. Those are dated items, not a final Saturday card, but they sketch a 6-4 versus 6-4 game that can tilt on one quarterback and one receiver.

South Florida has the better recent defensive reputation in this league. Florida Atlantic has the home finale, the red shirts, and a Senior Day list that includes the people who work the building, not just the ones who play in it. Somebody is going to leave Boca with a seventh win and a cleaner path to December. Somebody is going to leave with a 6-5 record and a long ride home. The Owls asked the crowd to show up in red. The Bulls asked themselves to get a stop when it counts.

Boca Raton puts falling snow on Mizner Park Saturday night, and before that the South Florida Bulls vs Florida Atlantic Owls game turns Flagler Credit Union Stadium red for the last home date of the year. Kaia Brightwater wants the Owls to go out of Paradise unbeaten; Reina Valdés-Kowalski drove across the state to ruin the whole holiday mood.

"Red Out, Senior Day, Owls Giving Back Day," Kaia says, counting on her fingers like she is reading a surf report. "Then we walk downtown, the mayor lights a 40-foot tree at 7:30, and fifty thousand lights come on in the palm trees. That is the schedule. You are not on it."

"I am the first item on it," Reina says. "Two-thirty kickoff. I will be done by sunset and still make the snow."

Here is what makes this one fun. Both teams walk in at 6-4, both already bowl-eligible, and they do football in completely different languages. Florida Atlantic, under Zach Kittley, plays like the ocean is chasing it. Caden Veltkamp has 3,086 throwing yards and 29 touchdowns with just 6 interceptions. Easton Messer has 86 catches for 1,033 yards and 13 scores, and he is the guy Kaia names before she names her own family. The Owls score 38.3 points a game. They hung 60 on UTSA and 66 on Texas Southern, and they survived Rice 51-49 in a game Kaia still describes with her hands.

"Five home games, five wins," she says. "Navy, FIU, Texas Southern, Rice, UTSA. Nobody leaves Paradise happy."

Reina lets her finish, because Reina has a defense and Kaia does not. South Florida gives up 18.8 points a game. Florida Atlantic gives up 35.3. That difference is the whole argument from the Tampa side, and Reina makes it in one breath.

"You gave up 38 at Tulsa last week and your quarterback had to throw 58 times. Fifty-eight, Kaia. That is not an offense. That is a cry for help."

"That was a road game in the cold," Kaia says, which is a very Boca thing to say about Oklahoma in November.

The Bulls changed shape this fall. Michael Van Buren Jr. started the year as the man with the ball, and he still leads the team with 1,273 throwing yards. But Luke Kromenhoek has thrown the most in each of the last three games, and against Memphis he did a little of everything: 271 yards and three touchdowns through the air, then 30 carries for 146 yards and another score on the ground. Mudia Reuben caught two of those touchdowns. South Florida still lost 52-41, and Reina has not forgiven the Memphis secondary or the weather or whoever scheduled it.

"Kromenhoek runs like he is late for a boat show," she says. "And D.J. Crowther has 689 yards and seven touchdowns, and he is going to find out how many of your tacklers came to the game in sandals."

Kaia answers with the one name that keeps her up at night. C.J. Hicks has five sacks for the Bulls, and Veltkamp has to hold the ball long enough for Messer to break free. If Hicks gets home early, Paradise gets very quiet. If Veltkamp gets time, FAU can score from anywhere on the field, and Kaia will be on her feet for every snap until the fourth quarter ends.

The history does not help her much. Florida Atlantic crushed South Florida 56-14 in 2023, a game Kaia keeps a photo of. Then the Bulls won 44-21 in 2024 and 48-13 last October. This is only the eighth meeting ever, and the Owls are still chasing their third win in the series.

"Third win comes Saturday," Kaia says. "In red. On Senior Day. In front of the seniors who lost to you last year. That is a story."

"That is a wish," Reina says. "I came for the tree."

Here is how it goes. Veltkamp and Messer get their points, because they always do, and the student section turns the red up to full volume after an early touchdown. But the Bulls keep handing the ball to Crowther and Kromenhoek and keep the Owls offense standing on the sideline in the sun. Hicks gets one big sack in the third quarter. FAU answers late and Paradise roars, but South Florida runs out the last few minutes on the ground, and Reina gets to Mizner Park just in time to watch the snow fall on Kaia.

"It is fake snow," Kaia says.

"It is a real win," Reina says.

My call: South Florida Bulls 33, Florida Atlantic Owls 29.
