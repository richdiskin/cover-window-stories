Ohio State vs Nebraska: Sold-Out Senior Day in Lincoln

Ohio State Buckeyes (7-3) at Nebraska Cornhuskers (6-4)

12:00 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Memorial Stadium (Lincoln, NE) · Fox

GameDay Report

Ohio State at Nebraska: Rhule Puts Jeremiah Smith Next to Julio Jones

Lincoln is wrapping three farewells into one noon kickoff: Military Appreciation Day, a College Football Hall of Fame honor for Ndamukong Suh, and Senior Day for a group Matt Rhule said includes six players who spent their entire careers at Nebraska. "The best thing we can do is honor, whether it's the military salute, to honor our fans and our crowd and our families, to honor our seniors, to honor them is to play really good football versus a great team," Rhule said Monday.

The great team is Ohio State, 7-3 and 5-2 in the Big Ten. The live card on this site lists the Buckeyes at No. 20; ESPN has them at No. 14 in the College Football Playoff rankings, No. 16 in the AP poll, and No. 17 in the coaches poll. Rhule did not waste any scouting-speak on Jeremiah Smith. "Jeremiah Smith is undoubtedly the best player in college football," he said. "When I was in the NFL I had a chance to watch, compete against Julio Jones and this is what Jeremiah reminds me of, that level of player." He also pointed to Bo Jackson's running game and to a Matt Patricia defense he called "really, really multiple." Nebraska is 6-4 and 3-4, coming off a 31-0 win at Rutgers, and Rhule said the Huskers are second in the Big Ten in rushing offense at 187 yards a game and 6-0 when they run for 150. He also said Anthony Colandrea leads the league in conference-game offense and that Nebraska has not turned the ball over in the last two games.

Ohio State is coming to a sold-out Memorial Stadium with quarterback Julian Sayin still out, as he has been since an elbow injury against Indiana. Luke Fahey has been the Buckeyes' passer in that stretch. Ryan Day said Thursday he was "anxious" to get Brock Boyd back after four missed games and that he thought Boyd would be "available," even if "how much he'll play, I'm not really sure." Left guard Luke Montgomery, out last week with an illness, was off the report and expected back.

The catch for Nebraska is on the other side of the ball. The Friday Big Ten availability report, as relayed by Eleven Warriors, ruled out linebacker Owen Chambliss, defensive tackle Jahsear Whittington and cornerback Andrew Marshall, with defensive end Anthony Jones Jr. doubtful. Chambliss is tied for the team lead with 55 tackles. Whittington leads the Huskers with 5.5 sacks. A farewell day in Lincoln usually asks the home crowd to raise the noise. This one also asks a thinned-out defense to measure itself against the player Rhule just compared to Julio Jones.

Ohio State vs Nebraska is the last home game of the year at Memorial Stadium, Senior Day in Lincoln at 11 a.m. Central, and the Huskers announced it sold out more than a week ahead of time. Nebraska insider Darla Rasmussen says a full house and a team that just found itself at Rutgers make this the best chance Lincoln has had at the Buckeyes in years, while Ohio State insider Buck Holloway says Jeremiah Smith and Luke Fahey didn't come to Nebraska to be anybody's farewell gift.

Darla: Buck, I have one rule this week. You don't get to talk about our history until you've stood in that stadium when it's full. Every seat sold, every seat red, and the seniors running out of the tunnel for the last time.

Buck: Darla, I respect a sellout. We have plenty in Columbus. But I've stood in a full stadium before, and I've watched the home team walk out of it quietly. That's a Columbus problem for you, not for me.

The quiet part is the history, and Buck has it memorized. Ohio State won all seven meetings in this stretch going back to 2016, and some of them weren't close: 62-3 in 2016, 56-14 in 2017, 48-7 in 2019. The most recent one, in 2024, was a 21-17 game that Nebraska fans still replay in their heads.

Darla: Twenty-one to seventeen. In your building. We were right there, Buck. Right there.

Buck: Right there is a nice place to visit.

This year, though, Nebraska has a better case than usual. The Huskers are 6-4 and bowl eligible after a 31-0 win at Rutgers last Saturday. Anthony Colandrea threw two touchdowns and went 15 of 22, Jamal Rule ran 22 times for 103 yards, and the defense didn't let Rutgers score at all. That ended a four-game skid in which the Huskers lost to Indiana by three, Oregon by three, Washington by seven and Illinois by 11.

Darla: Three of those four were one-score games. We aren't a bad team, Buck. We're a close team that needed somebody to finish. Colandrea has 18 touchdown throws. Our defense gives up 14.9 points a game. Go Big Red.

Buck: Your defense is legit, and I'll say that on the record. But your offense has scored 17 or fewer in four of its last six. We give up 18.4 a game ourselves.

Ohio State's season has turned, too. The Buckeyes started 6-1, then lost at USC and to Oregon at home, and Julian Sayin hasn't been the one throwing it the last three weeks. Fahey has, and last Saturday he looked comfortable, going 21 of 32 for 201 yards and three touchdowns in a 28-19 win over Northwestern.

Buck: Fahey's growing up in front of us. And when your quarterback has Jeremiah Smith, you don't have to be perfect. You have to be pointed in the right direction. Eleven catches, 117 yards last week. Fifteen touchdown catches on the year. That young man is the best receiver I've ever watched in scarlet, and I've watched a lot of them.

Darla: We've got Kwazi Gilmer and his 603 yards, and Jahsear Whittington with five and a half sacks. Whittington's going to say hello to Fahey early and often.

Buck: Kenyatta Jackson Jr. has five sacks for us. Earl Little Jr. is everywhere. And Bo Jackson ran for 114 last week. We can hand it to him all afternoon if we have to.

The Senior Day ceremony is where Darla goes quiet for a second. She has watched these seniors through the hard years, and she wants them to walk off the field with something to point to, a home win over a name brand before the trip to Iowa City on Black Friday.

Darla: This isn't about revenge. It's about a group of kids who stuck around when it was hard. Give them one Saturday like this.

Buck: I hear you, Darla, and I mean it when I say congratulations on the bowl. But Ohio State doesn't hand out Saturdays. You have to take them.

There's real tension in that. Nebraska's crowd will be loud from the first snap, and the Huskers' defense can keep it close. Ohio State has more ways to score and the best player on the field in Smith. If the Buckeyes protect Fahey and keep it simple, the Huskers' farewell gets complicated in the fourth quarter.

Darla: Fill every seat, then make it loud. That's the plan.

Buck: The plan in Columbus is Jeremiah Smith on third down.

The seniors get their roar, and Memorial Stadium gives them everything it has. Smith gives Ohio State just a little bit more.

My call: Ohio State Buckeyes 27, Nebraska Cornhuskers 21.
