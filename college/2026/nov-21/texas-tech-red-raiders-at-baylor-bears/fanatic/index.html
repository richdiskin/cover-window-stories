GameDay Report

Texas Tech at Baylor: Senior Day in Waco Meets a 9-1 Visitor

Baylor's official preview put a double bill on Saturday at McLane Stadium: Senior Day, and a Salute to Service, with No. 12 Texas Tech in town at 2:30 p.m. CT on FOX. One ranking hitch, because Baylor and the card do not use the same number. Baylor's preview called the visitor No. 11. Texas Tech's own site, on Nov. 17, said the Red Raiders "stay put at No. 12 in CFP rankings." The live card on this site is No. 12, and that is the ranking this story will use.

Texas Tech is 9-1 after a 52-27 win at Oklahoma State. J'Koby Williams has already run for 1,078 yards and eight scores. Will Hammond has 1,566 passing yards. Baylor is 6-4 and 4-3 in the Big 12 after a 7-28 loss at BYU, a game the official recap said left the Bears with 238 yards, including 159 passing and 79 rushing. DJ Lagway has 2,059 passing yards, 16 touchdowns and five interceptions.

The recent series, from Baylor's notes, has not been kind to the visitors in Lubbock: the Bears won 59-35 there in 2024 and 45-17 in 2022. This one is in Waco, on Senior Day, against a 9-1 team that just put 52 on Oklahoma State. If Baylor plays like the club that scored 104 combined points in those two trips to Lubbock, the farewell class will have a memory worth keeping. If Tech's ground game looks like 1,078 yards of evidence, the Salute to Service crowd will be asked to stay loud deep into the afternoon.

Baylor and Texas Tech have played 83 times, and the Bears lead the whole thing by two games, 42-40-1. On Saturday the Texas Tech Red Raiders vs Baylor Bears game closes the home schedule at McLane Stadium with Senior Day, and Maggie Whitlock and Bodie Ratliff are treating it like the closing argument in a case nobody will ever finish.

Maggie has her house rules. She walks the stands at McLane like it's a showroom she just finished: the river out past the end zone, the gold everywhere, the seniors about to walk out for the last time.

"This is a remodel," she tells Bodie. "We had a coaching change this fall. Joe Klanderman stepped in as interim head coach, and he went and won at UCF and beat Iowa State in back-to-back weeks. That's how you renovate on the fly. You keep the bones and fix the kitchen."

Bodie, who rides bareback in the summer and charts Air Raid concepts the rest of the year, tips his hat. "Y'all lost 28-7 at BYU last week, Maggie. That kitchen's still got some sawdust in it."

Texas Tech is the team with the shine this fall. The Red Raiders are 9-1 and they won their first seven games. Their only loss came at Cincinnati in late October. Since then, Thomas Castellanos has thrown the most in each game, and the results have been loud: 362 yards and five touchdowns in a 53-48 win over West Virginia, then 263 yards and four touchdowns in a 52-27 win at Oklahoma State. J'Koby Williams has 1,078 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and he ran for 124 in Stillwater. Kenny Johnson leads the receivers with 707 yards.

"Castellanos throws it like he's roping a calf," Bodie says. "Quick, mean and right on target."

Maggie answers with the room she is proudest of, the defense. Baylor gives up 18.1 points a game. The Bears held TCU to 17 and Colorado to 13 and smothered UCF 41-9. Travion Barnes has 73 tackles at linebacker, and Garrick Ponder has four sacks off the end. And on offense, DJ Lagway has thrown 16 touchdowns with only five interceptions, while Gavin Freeman has caught 75 balls for 889 yards.

"Freeman is the crown molding," Maggie says. "Nobody notices him until you realize the whole room looks better because he's there."

The other storyline in this one has a Waco address. Joey McGuire, Tech's fifth-year head coach, used to be an assistant at Baylor. He knows the building. He knows the crowd. Bodie claims that's a home-field advantage for the Red Raiders. Maggie claims that's a man who should know better than to come back.

The history gives both of them material. Baylor has won five of the last seven meetings. Baylor also holds a 20-12 lead in games played in Waco. But Tech won the last time it came to town, 39-14 in 2023, and Bodie still keeps the box score folded in his truck's glove box. Baylor answered the next year with a 59-35 blowout in Lubbock that Maggie calls "a full gut job."

"Fifty-nine," she says. "On your homecoming. In your town."

"That was two years ago," Bodie says. "We've had a renovation too."

There is also a Salute to Service element on Saturday, with the Bears honoring active-duty military, veterans and first responders along with the seniors. Both of them stand for that part. Then Maggie sits down and gets back to work on Bodie.

Here's how it goes. Texas Tech gets the first punch, Castellanos finding Johnson on a quick slant that turns into a long gain and a touchdown, and the visiting section in the corner gets loud. Baylor settles in. Lagway lives on short throws to Freeman, the Bears grind out a couple of long drives, and the defense makes Castellanos throw it away twice in the second quarter. The game goes back and forth all afternoon. Holmes gets a big sack late, forcing a Baylor field goal instead of a touchdown, and that's the difference. Tech escapes, barely.

Maggie stands and applauds her seniors as they leave the field.

"Two points," Bodie says.

"Two points in a house this good is still a good house," Maggie says. "We'll fix the trim next year."

My call: Texas Tech Red Raiders 28, Baylor Bears 26.
