Iowa vs Illinois: A Former Hawkeye Faces His Old Team

Iowa Hawkeyes (8-2) at Illinois Fighting Illini (6-4)

2:30 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Gies Memorial Stadium · BTN

GameDay Report

Iowa at Illinois: A Third Quarterback, Maybe, Against No. 15

Champaign may be handing the ball to a third starting quarterback on Saturday, and that is the whole nervous energy around Gies Memorial Stadium. No. 15 Iowa, 8-2 and stingy, comes to town at 2:30 p.m. on the Big Ten Network, and Illinois coach Bret Bielema spent the week preparing Carson Boyd to play if Katin Houser cannot go. ESPN and local reports said Houser had surgery for a broken nose and is in the concussion protocol, listed as questionable, while backup Michael Clayton II is out with a hand injury. Bielema praised Boyd's accuracy and his ability to throw on the move. That is a hard way to host a top-15 defense.

Iowa is 8-2 and allows 11.7 points a game with three shutouts, a fact the Hawkeyes' own season file and every preview this week has repeated. Illinois is 6-4 and still playing for a finish that would make a Senior Day at home feel like a beginning instead of a shrug. The history between Bielema and Iowa is well known in both places, and it does not need another retelling today. The new part is the quarterback. If Houser is cleared, Illinois has its usual problem: how to score enough. If he is not, Boyd is the first answer the Illini have left.

A 2:30 kickoff in Champaign, a ranked visitor, and a home crowd that will spend the first half watching to see who takes the first snap: that is the scene. Iowa came to take care of business. Illinois came to find out if it still has a quarterback who can make the Hawkeyes play from behind.

Bret Bielema walked on at Iowa, earned a scholarship and ended up a co-captain up front on the Hawkeyes' defense. On Saturday, he'll stand on the Illinois sideline at Gies Memorial Stadium for Senior Day and try to beat the program that made him. Illinois insider Margery Vandermolen has done the math on that with her slide rule, and she likes the answer.

"A coach who knows the opponent from the inside," she says. "That's not a disadvantage. That's a calculation. Oskee-wow-wow!"

Iowa insider Orrin Kalvelage has a simpler response. "He knows us," Orrin says. "He also knows what we do to people in November."

That's a fair warning. Iowa is 8-2 and ranked No. 15, and the Hawkeyes have won three straight, 30-0 over Wisconsin, 24-10 at Northwestern and 28-7 over Purdue. The defense allows 11.7 points a game, and it has three shutouts this season. Linebacker Cam Buffington has 90 tackles, and Joseph Anderson has four sacks.

"Our defense doesn't give up points," Orrin says. "It gives up hope."

Orrin's best-loved part of any game, though, isn't the defense. It's the punt. He keeps a notebook of every Iowa punt that's downed inside the 10, and he'll tell you about each one in detail. He's also the first one on his feet at the end of the first quarter at home, when Kinnick turns to wave at the children's hospital. He'll miss that on Saturday, and he says so.

"You can't take the Wave on the road," he says. "But you can take the punter."

Iowa's offense is steady rather than flashy. Hank Brown has thrown for 1,504 yards with ten touchdowns and four interceptions. Kamari Moulton leads the backfield with 541 rushing yards, and Tony Diaz has 549 receiving yards. The Hawkeyes beat Michigan 20-19 on the road and Iowa State 16-13 at home. Their two losses were to Ohio State and at Minnesota, 13-10.

Margery's Illini are 6-4, and they're coming off a hard week. They had won four straight, including a 21-10 win over Oregon and a 24-13 win over Nebraska. Then they went to the Rose Bowl on Friday night, Nov. 13, and lost 22-14 to UCLA. Katin Houser threw three interceptions that night.

"One bad night in Pasadena," Margery says. "Statistically insignificant. Katin Houser has 18 touchdown passes. Collin Dixon has 13 touchdown catches. Those numbers have a very small margin of error."

She's right about Dixon. The receiver has 72 catches for 946 yards and 13 touchdowns, and he's the biggest threat on the field Saturday. Houser has thrown for 2,131 yards. Aidan Laughery leads Illinois with 664 rushing yards, and defensive back Matthew Bailey has 74 tackles.

The trouble is the history. Iowa has won seven of the last eight meetings. Illinois' only win in that stretch came in 2022, 9-6, and the most recent meeting, in 2023, went to Iowa 15-13. Iowa games against Illinois tend to look like a slide-rule problem, low numbers carefully worked out.

"Nine to six," Margery says. "Beautiful game. Elegant. I wouldn't change a thing."

"You wouldn't," Orrin says. "You won it."

So the question for Saturday is whether Dixon can find enough space against an Iowa defense that hasn't given up more than 31 points all season, and only did that once. Iowa's offense won't run away from anyone. It doesn't have to. If the Hawkeyes punt well, play defense and don't turn the ball over, they win a lot of low-scoring games.

I think Illinois makes it close. Houser and Dixon will get their touchdowns. Bielema will have his team ready for an opponent he knows better than anyone in Champaign. And the Senior Day crowd will be loud, with the Illini needing a bounce back after Pasadena. But Iowa's defense has been the best group in this game all season, and the Hawkeyes are good at winning the kind of game they want to play.

Margery will slide her rule back and forth after the final whistle and come up with a reason it was closer than it looked. Orrin will open his notebook and add another punt.

"Oskee-wow-wow," Margery will say, a little quieter.

"Downed at the 6," Orrin will say. "Beautiful."

My call: Iowa Hawkeyes 25, Illinois Fighting Illini 19.
