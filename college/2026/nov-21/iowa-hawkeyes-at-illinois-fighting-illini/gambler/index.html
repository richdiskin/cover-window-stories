Iowa at Illinois: A Third Quarterback, Maybe, Against No. 15

Market check, Iowa at Illinois: DraftKings IOWA -8.5, total 40.5.

Champaign may be handing the ball to a third starting quarterback on Saturday, and that is the whole nervous energy around Gies Memorial Stadium. No. 15 Iowa, 8-2 and stingy, comes to town at 2:30 p.m. on the Big Ten Network, and Illinois coach Bret Bielema spent the week preparing Carson Boyd to play if Katin Houser cannot go. ESPN and local reports said Houser had surgery for a broken nose and is in the concussion protocol, listed as questionable, while backup Michael Clayton II is out with a hand injury. Bielema praised Boyd's accuracy and his ability to throw on the move. That is a hard way to host a top-15 defense.

Iowa is 8-2 and allows 11.7 points a game with three shutouts, a fact the Hawkeyes' own season file and every preview this week has repeated. Illinois is 6-4 and still playing for a finish that would make a Senior Day at home feel like a beginning instead of a shrug. The history between Bielema and Iowa is well known in both places, and it does not need another retelling today. The new part is the quarterback. If Houser is cleared, Illinois has its usual problem: how to score enough. If he is not, Boyd is the first answer the Illini have left.

A 2:30 kickoff in Champaign, a ranked visitor, and a home crowd that will spend the first half watching to see who takes the first snap: that is the scene. Iowa came to take care of business. Illinois came to find out if it still has a quarterback who can make the Hawkeyes play from behind.

Iowa allows 11.7 points a game and has three shutouts, so the obvious play is a low score. I'm going the other way on the total in Champaign. The Iowa vs Illinois spread is Illinois Fighting Illini +8.5, the total is 40.5, and my pick is Illinois Fighting Illini +8.5 and Over 40.5.

DraftKings opened Iowa −5.5 and 43.5. As of 9:20 PM ET Friday, the side has moved three points toward Iowa and the total has come down three. Both moves give me a better number than the opener.

Start with the eight and a half points. Illinois is 6-4 and had won four straight before a 22-14 loss at UCLA on a Friday night. The Illini beat Oregon 21-10 and Nebraska 24-13 at home. Katin Houser has 2,131 passing yards and 18 touchdowns, and Collin Dixon has 72 catches for 946 yards and 13 touchdowns. Margery Vandermolen ran it through her slide rule: "One bad night in Pasadena. Three interceptions. It won't repeat."

Iowa is the better team. It's 8-2, ranked No. 15, and coming off wins of 30-0, 24-10 and 28-7. But the Hawkeyes' offense is modest. Hank Brown has 1,504 passing yards, ten touchdowns and four interceptions, and Iowa scored 16 against Iowa State, 20 at Michigan and 10 at Minnesota. Orrin Kalvelage says that's how Iowa likes it: "We win with defense and punting." That style keeps games close, and eight and a half points is a lot to give against a home team that scores 27.0 a game.

Now the Over. My final is Iowa Hawkeyes 25, Illinois Fighting Illini 19, and those 44 combined points clear 40.5 by three and a half. Illinois gets two touchdowns from Houser and Dixon, and Iowa's offense has scored 24 or more in each of its last three. The number has come down far enough to make it comfortable.

Iowa wins by six, and Illinois covers the 8.5.
