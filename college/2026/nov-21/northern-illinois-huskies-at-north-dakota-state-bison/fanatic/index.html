GameDay Report

Northern Illinois at North Dakota State: Harvest Bowl in the Dome, With an 0-10 Visitor

Fargo calls this one the Harvest Bowl, and North Dakota State has been calling it that since 1974. The Bison's official preview set kickoff for 3:10 p.m. at the Fargodome, on The CW, against Northern Illinois, with 22 seniors to be introduced before the game. NDSU listed itself 46-2-2 in the Harvest Bowl and said it has won 15 straight of them, including 48-16 over Northern Iowa last season.

The Bison are 8-2 after a 21-13 win at Hawai'i. The official preview also said NDSU is tied with Alabama, Missouri State and Texas Tech for the FBS lead with five defensive touchdowns this season. That is the school's number, and it is the kind of number a home crowd in the dome will want to see again. Northern Illinois is 0-10 after a 7-28 loss to Nevada. The Huskies have not won a game, and they have to open this one on the road, in a building that has been a Harvest Bowl problem for visitors for a long time.

NDSU's last game was a fourth-quarter rally in Honolulu. NIU's last game was a 21-point loss at home to Nevada. The facts we can source do not invent a hidden 0-10 plot twist. They do give you a 8-2 home team, a senior class of 22, and a visitor looking for its first win of the year.

A Harvest Bowl that dates to 1974, a 15-game streak in this setting, and an 0-10 visitor: that is Saturday in the Fargodome. Twenty-two names will be called. Then Northern Illinois has to see whether this dome feels any different than the first 10 Saturdays.

Yellow is the color in the Fargodome on Saturday. North Dakota State is asking every fan to wear it for the Harvest Bowl, the annual celebration that closes the Bison home schedule, and this year it lands on Senior Day at the end of the program's first season in the Mountain West.

"Harvest Bowl," Inga Halvorsen says, beaming like she just finished bringing in the wheat. "We thank the farmers, we honor the seniors, we wear yellow. It is the most North Dakota afternoon there is. You'll love it, Nola."

Nola Brandvik, who grew up in DeKalb and studied ag science, nods slowly, the way a person nods at a barn sale when the price is too good to be true. "I respect a harvest celebration. Back home, we'd call this time of year 'bringing it in.' This year, we haven't brought much in."

That's an understatement, and Nola knows it. The Northern Illinois Huskies vs North Dakota State Bison game puts a winless team in a dome full of yellow against a team that has made itself at home in its new league right away. North Dakota State is 8-2 and 5-0 in the Fargodome. Northern Illinois is 0-10.

"Oh, yah," Inga says, gently. "That's a tough one."

North Dakota State's first FBS season has gone the way people in Fargo expected it to, which is to say it has gone well. The Bison opened with a 33-7 win over Jacksonville State, beat Air Force 38-32 on the road and shut out both Fordham and Wyoming. They lost close games at UNLV, 13-7, and at New Mexico, 21-16, and have won two straight since, including 21-13 at Hawai'i last week. The defense allows 12.0 points a game. Kelton McCaslin has five and a half sacks and Donovan Woolen has 71 tackles.

"Twelve points a game," Inga says. "That is the kind of number an economist puts on a refrigerator."

Nathan Hayes runs the offense, with 1,807 passing yards and 14 touchdowns. Jackson Williams leads the receivers with 622 yards, and Myles Mitchell leads the runners with 419.

Nola has her own list, and she reads it with the calm of someone who has already made peace with the weather. Taron Dickens has thrown for 2,070 yards with 10 touchdowns. Telly Johnson Jr. leads the runners with 660 yards. DeAree Rogers has 80 catches for 783 yards. Linebacker Jack Murray has 78 tackles.

"And we've been close," Nola says. "Illinois State by four. UNLV by seven. San José State by 11."

"You did," Inga says kindly. "You also gave up 56 to Hawai'i."

"We don't talk about Hawai'i."

"We just beat Hawai'i," Inga says. "So we talk about it a lot."

The Huskies' defense has been the hard part, allowing 35.9 points a game, and last week Nevada beat them 28-7 at home. Johnson ran for only 31 yards in that game. Still, Northern Illinois has scored 17 or more in six of its ten games, so there is some life in that offense.

"Every field has a few good rows," Nola says. "I'm watching Rogers."

Here's how it goes. The seniors walk out to a dome full of yellow, and the Bison take the opening drive straight down the field, with Hayes finding Williams for the first touchdown. McCaslin gets a sack on the next Huskie series, and the Bison score twice more before halftime. Northern Illinois finally gets on the board with a Dickens throw to Rogers in the third quarter, and the small Huskie section stands up and cheers like it's the Fourth of July. North Dakota State adds two more scores, then lets the younger players finish it. A late Northern Illinois field goal makes the final a little friendlier.

Inga claps her mittens together. "Oh, we won! Hotdish for everybody anyway. It's a harvest."

Nola takes out her phone, pulls up a photo of an Illinois cornfield and stares at it for thirty seconds without saying a word.

My call: North Dakota State Bison 35, Northern Illinois Huskies 12.
