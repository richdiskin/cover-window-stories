Hawai'i at Nevada: Lifts Are Spinning, Last Home Game at Mackay

Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors (3-7) at Nevada Wolf Pack (4-6)

10:30 PM ET Sat Nov 21 · Mackay Stadium · FS1

XFacebook

Ridge Corbell has been on the mountain since Mt. Rose opened on November 6, the first ski area on the West Coast to start spinning lifts this winter, and he plans to take a couple of early runs Saturday before heading down the hill to Mackay Stadium. Kainoa Akana-Baird, who flew in from Honolulu in board shorts and a borrowed parka, has agreed to come along on the condition that nobody makes him stand on a snowboard.

Ridge: Dude, it's only a little powder.

Kainoa: I surf water. Water is warm. This is frozen water. That's a different sport.

Ridge: It's the same sport, just colder.

Kainoa: That's what everybody says right before they fall down.

Mt. Rose has the highest base elevation in Tahoe, 8,260 feet, and Ridge brings it up every chance he gets. Mackay, on the other hand, sits on North Virginia Street in Reno, and Saturday it hosts Nevada's last home game of the season, a 7:30 Pacific kickoff against Hawai'i.

Kainoa: Seven-thirty here is five-thirty back home. My family will be watching before dinner.

Ridge: That's a good time slot.

Kainoa: It's the only time slot. I do this math every week.

Nevada is 4-6 and coming off its best road game of the year, a 28-7 win at Northern Illinois. Herschel Turner ran for 153 yards and two touchdowns in that one, and he leads the team with 673 yards for the season. The quarterback job has bounced between Carter Jones, AJ Bianco and Luke Duncan, but the defense has been steady. EJ Smith has 99 tackles, and Dylan LaBarbera has 5.5 sacks. The Wolf Pack beat Western Kentucky 49-14 and San José State 28-13 at home, and they lost a tough one to Air Force, 36-33 in overtime.

Ridge: Turner is fresh corduroy. First chair, first run, nobody's touched it yet.

Kainoa: I don't know what that means.

Ridge: It means he's sick.

Hawai'i is 3-7, but the Rainbow Warriors have been better than that in spots. They won 56-21 at Northern Illinois, beat New Mexico State, and won 17-14 at UTEP earlier this month. Micah Alejado has thrown for 2,720 yards and ten touchdowns, DeVon Rice has seven rushing touchdowns, and Kainoa Carvalho leads the receivers with 484 yards. Jamih Otis has 71 tackles. Last week they lost 21-13 at home to North Dakota State.

Kainoa: Carvalho is a good name. I'd know.

Ridge: Any relation?

Kainoa: Every Kainoa is a little related. That's the rule.

Hawai'i has had the better of this series lately, winning six of the last nine meetings, including the last three: 31-16 in 2022, 27-14 in 2023 and 34-13 in 2024.

Kainoa: Three straight. Shoots.

Ridge: That's a powder day that went sideways for us. Three of them.

Kainoa: We'll catch the next wave.

Ridge: Not at Mackay you won't.

What decides it? Nevada wants to run Turner over and over, keep Alejado on the sideline, and win the game in the cold. Hawai'i needs Alejado to be careful with the ball and find Carvalho early, and the Rainbow Warriors' defense has to keep Turner from breaking big runs.

Here's how it goes: Turner runs hard on the opening drive and scores, and Nevada leads at halftime. Hawai'i gets a Rice touchdown in the third quarter to keep it close, but the Wolf Pack defense gets a stop late, and Turner finishes it off with a long run in the fourth.

Ridge: Sick!

Kainoa: Island time. We'll get you next year.

Kainoa zips the borrowed parka all the way up for the walk out of Mackay, and Ridge promises him that next time they'll do the math the other way and play in Honolulu.

My call: Nevada Wolf Pack 25, Hawai'i Rainbow Warriors 18.
