Eastern Michigan Eagles vs Western Michigan Broncos: Salute at Waldo

Eastern Michigan Eagles (4-6) at Western Michigan Broncos (7-3)

6:00 PM ET Tue Nov 17 · Waldo Stadium · ESPN2

XFacebook

Eastern Michigan Eagles vs Western Michigan Broncos prediction for Tuesday, Nov. 17: Waldo Stadium hosts its annual Military Appreciation Game, and the rivalry from the other side of the state gets a 6:00 PM ET kickoff in Kalamazoo. Corwin Dahlquist has a 7-3 Broncos team and a pocketful of thank-you notes, while Imogen Tremblay rolls in from Ypsilanti with a 4-6 Eagles team, thirteen days of rest and a chip on her shoulder roughly the size of Ann Arbor.

"Before anybody says a word about football," Corwin says, "three men on our staff served this country. Our analytics director spent more than three decades in the Army. Our head athletic trainer was deployed to Baghdad. Tuesday is theirs first."

"Agreed, and I'll clap the loudest," Imogen says. "Then I'm going to ruin your night."

Here's the funny part about this one. The Michigan MAC Trophy, the three-way prize between Western, Central and Eastern, is already staying in Kalamazoo. Central beat Eastern at Ford Field on Nov. 4, so the Broncos keep the hardware no matter what happens Tuesday.

"So we're playing for pride," Imogen says. "Pride is the only thing Eastern has ever needed. We run on it. It's cheaper than gas."

"You're playing for a bowl game," Corwin says gently. "You need to win out."

He's right, and she knows it. Eastern Michigan's season has a hinge in the middle. The Eagles opened MAC play 3-0, with a 38-14 win at UMass and a 25-23 escape at Akron, then dropped three straight to Toledo, Ohio and Central Michigan. Noah Kim, who took the most throws in every game through mid-October, hasn't played since the Toledo loss, according to Western Michigan's game notes. Brogan McCaughey has taken over, and it has been bumpy: 197 yards at Ohio in a four-point loss, then 59 yards on 12 completions against Central.

"The kid is learning in public," Imogen says. "That's the bravest thing you can do. I learned to parallel park in public."

Eastern still has a few names that make Corwin squint. Amareon Blue is listed at 5-foot-6 and leads the team with 447 rushing yards. Harold Mack has five touchdown grabs. Tight end Joshua Long has a team-high 44 catches, and Quincy Byas leads the Eagles in sacks.

"Byas is going to say hello to your quarterback," Imogen says.

"Broc Lowry has 12 touchdown throws and 3 interceptions," Corwin says. "He went 15 for 15 at Akron. He doesn't panic. He folds his laundry."

That's the Western Michigan personality in one sentence. The Broncos are 7-3, 5-1 in the conference and first in the MAC, and they win by pressing on people until they give up. Jalen Buckley has 832 yards and 10 touchdowns and needs 168 more to reach 1,000 for the third time in his career, which would put him in a club with three other Bronco backs in program history. Cole Cabana has broken an 82-yard touchdown run in each of the last two games, after running for 44 yards on 22 carries in his whole career before that.

"Eighty-two, then eighty-two again," Corwin says. "Same distance. I measured it twice. The second time I cried a little."

"You held the ball for 42 minutes against Akron," Imogen says. "Forty-two! My little brother can't hold anything for 42 minutes. He drops his phone in the soup."

The history is not on Eastern's side lately. Western Michigan leads the all-time series 37-22-2 and has won the last three, giving up 21 points or fewer in each. But Imogen has a date circled in her own heart: Oct. 22, 2022, the last time the Eagles beat the Broncos, and they did it right here in Kalamazoo.

"Waldo has been kind to green before," she says. "It can do it again."

Here's how Tuesday goes. Eastern comes out hot after almost two weeks of stewing, and Byas gets home once early to quiet the crowd for a series. Blue finds a crease or two. But Western Michigan does what it always does. Buckley carries and carries, Lowry hits Aveion Chenault on the one throw that matters, and the Broncos' defense, which allows 18.7 points a game, keeps McCaughey in third-and-long all night. Eastern gets a late score to make Imogen jump out of her seat, and the Broncos close it out with the clock.

Corwin stands for the final salute to the veterans and pours imaginary syrup over an imaginary full stack. Imogen opens a spreadsheet, finds the cell for Nov. 24 against Kent State, and colors it green in advance.

My call: Western Michigan Broncos 31, Eastern Michigan Eagles 15.
