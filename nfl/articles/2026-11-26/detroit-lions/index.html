Detroit Lions vs Chicago Bears Week 12: Three Seconds Short, 35-39

Final: Chicago Bears (9-2) 39, Detroit Lions (8-3) 35, Thu., Nov. 26, Ford Field, Detroit. Next: at Atlanta Falcons, Sun., Dec. 6, 1:00 PM ET, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta

XFacebook

The Chicago Bears beat the Detroit Lions 39-35 at Ford Field, and the winning catch came with three seconds left on a Thanksgiving that began so well. America's Thanksgiving Parade marched down Woodward Avenue for the 100th time Thursday morning, with the Detroit Youth Choir opening it and Dan Gilbert as grand marshal. By the afternoon the party was at Ford Field, and the Bears crashed it.

Jahmyr Gibbs was the best player on the field. Twenty-one carries, 205 yards, three touchdowns. He scored from the 1 twice, then took a first-down handoff from our 30 and went 70 yards for 35-24 with 8:20 left. Amon-Ra St. Brown caught seven balls for 146 yards, and Jared Goff found Sam LaPorta for an 11-yard touchdown with six seconds left in the half to make it 19-10. We piled up 549 yards. That should be plenty.

It wasn't, and the hidden points hurt. Jake Bates pushed the extra point after the Gibbs run wide left. Rome Odunze got behind us for 41 yards a minute later, Caleb Williams ran in the two, and 35-24 was 35-32. On our next drive Goff threw for Jameson Williams at the Chicago 21 and Dillon Thieneman took it away.

Then came the decision I'll be chewing on through leftovers. With 2:22 left and a three-point lead, we went for it on fourth down at the Chicago 33 instead of trying the kick, and Goff's throw to Gibbs gained a yard. Chicago got the ball at its own 32 and needed 13 plays. A face mask on D.J. Reed gave them 15 free yards, and Odunze caught a 6-yarder in the end zone with three seconds to go. Our lateral play at the gun ended in an illegal forward flag.

Bright spots, because there were some. Malcolm Rodriguez recovered the Colston Loveland fumble at our 16 on Chicago's first drive and sacked Williams for 10 in the fourth. Roger McCreary broke up three throws. Brian Branch made two tackles for loss, and Jack Fox boomed a 61-yard punt.

This was our first home loss of the season, and it drops us to 8-3, a game behind Chicago. One hundred parades on Woodward and this city has seen worse Thursdays, so nobody in Detroit is folding. The Atlanta Falcons are next at Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Sunday, Dec. 6, at 1:00 PM ET. Grit, Detroit. Go get it back.
