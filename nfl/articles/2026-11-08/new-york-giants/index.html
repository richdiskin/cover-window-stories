New York Giants vs Philadelphia Eagles Week 9: Built Can by Can 19-11

Final: New York Giants (5-2-1) 19, Philadelphia Eagles (2-7) 11, Sun., Nov. 8, Lincoln Financial Field, Philadelphia. Next: Washington Commanders, Thu., Nov. 12, 8:15 PM ET, MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford

XFacebook

New York Giants 19, Philadelphia Eagles 11, and yes, that happened in South Philadelphia. Down at Brookfield Place this weekend, architecture teams were stacking thousands of cans into giant sculptures for Canstruction, every can headed to City Harvest. On Sunday the Giants built a win the same way, one kick, one stop, one ugly yard at a time, and snapped a run of 13 straight Eagles home wins in this series that went back to October 2013.

Dominic Zvada was the whole construction crew early. He hit from 53 and 49 in the first quarter for a 6-0 lead, then added 38 and 49 in the fourth. Four tries, four makes. Jordan Stout punted six times for a 52.3 average and dropped five inside the 20.

Philadelphia got its only touchdown late in the first quarter when Saquon Barkley, yes, that Saquon Barkley, bulled in from 3 yards. After a Giants penalty on the extra point, Joe Milton ran in a two-point try for an 8-6 Eagles lead. Handing Philly free points never sits right with me.

Then the quarterback merry-go-round kept spinning. J.J. McCarthy hurt his hip in the third quarter with us down 8-6, and Jameis Winston came in and went to work on a 14-play, 76-yard drive that ate 7:56. He hit Darnell Mooney for 27, found Malachi Fields for 14 down to the 3, and two plays later flipped a 5-yard touchdown to Isaiah Likely. That made Winston 6 of 8 for 71 yards. McCarthy came back and finished 12 of 19 for 127, and the kicker handled the rest.

The defense was the real story. Arvell Reese had three of our seven sacks, 12 tackles and a strip of Milton that Micah McFadden fell on at midfield. McFadden, DJ Reader, Dawuane Smoot and Darius Alexander got a sack each. Philadelphia went 5 of 18 on third down and gained 220 yards all day. The last Eagles drive ended with Alexander dropping Milton on fourth-and-2.

The rough patch: the offense was rough. Devin Singletary ran 21 times for 31 yards, we gave up six sacks, and nine penalties for 86 yards kept handing Philadelphia free real estate. We were 6 of 17 on third down. Nobody is framing this one for the den.

But 5-2-1 is 5-2-1, and we are the only team in the NFC East with a winning record. Washington comes to MetLife on Thursday night. Bring cans.
