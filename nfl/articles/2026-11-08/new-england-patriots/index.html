New England Patriots vs Green Bay Packers Week 9: Gronk Glow Fades 45-27

Final: Green Bay Packers (6-3) 45, New England Patriots (5-4) 27, Sun., Nov. 8, Gillette Stadium, Foxborough. Next: at Detroit Lions, Sun., Nov. 15, 9:30 AM ET, FC Bayern Munich Stadium, Munich

XFacebook

Green Bay beat us 45-27 at home. On Saturday, Rob Gronkowski went into the Patriots Hall of Fame on Patriot Place Plaza, the man with 79 touchdown catches and three rings in this uniform. A day later at Gillette Stadium, our defense gave up more points than any Patriots team ever has in that building.

I judge this franchise by receipts I watched get printed, and Sunday printed a bad one. Drake Maye threw into traffic on our second snap, Xavier McKinney intercepted it and returned it to our 14, and Jordan Love scrambled in two plays later. It was 17-0 early in the second quarter, and the crowd that showed up loud for the 2016 team's 10th anniversary had gone quiet.

Maye threw three interceptions in the first half, two of them to McKinney, and two of those turned straight into Green Bay touchdowns. He also threw three touchdowns. Rhamondre Stevenson took a 30-yarder in, and Efton Chism III caught an 8-yarder that made it 24-14 at the half. That was the last time it felt like a game.

The Packers opened the third quarter with a 70-yard touchdown drive. Then Isaiah McDuffie stripped Stevenson, and Love hit Christian Watson from 39 on the next play. 42-14. Love finished 23 of 29 for 372 yards. Watson had 171 receiving yards and Matthew Golden had 164, and our secondary chased both of them all afternoon. Mack Hollins caught a 22-yard score and Corey Kiner ran one in from 4 late, but the two-point try failed and 45-27 was the final.

Some of it was worth keeping. DeMario Douglas caught five for 102. Kiner returned nine kickoffs for 224 yards and averaged 6.9 a carry. Christian Gonzalez made 10 tackles, Elijah Ponder had 1.5 sacks and Christian Barmore got one.

The tally: four turnovers, 21 points allowed off them, and 478 yards given up. ESPN says it was the most points the Patriots have allowed at Gillette Stadium, and the first time they gave up 45 in a regular-season game since 1993. The injuries hurt more. Stevenson left with a knee injury, and Carlton Davis III, Ben Brown and Will Campbell all went down with knee injuries too.

We're 5-4 and 3-1 at home. Next is Detroit, in Munich.
