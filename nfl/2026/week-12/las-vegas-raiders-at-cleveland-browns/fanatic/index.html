Las Vegas Raiders at Cleveland Browns

Las Vegas Raiders (4-7) at Cleveland Browns (6-4)

8:20 PM ET Sun Nov 29 · Huntington Bank Field, Cleveland, OH · NBC

XFacebook

Saturday afternoon, Public Square fills up for the WinterLand Kickoff, and Gordie Novak will be there from three o'clock on with a thermos and his granddaughter on his shoulders. At five-thirty, Destination Cleveland debuts Illuminate CLE, a new holiday light show that runs every half hour until eleven. Santa takes photos from six to eight. The skating rink will be packed. Gordie has lived in Parma his whole life and he'll tell you there's nothing like downtown Cleveland the weekend after Thanksgiving.

Luz Carrillo flew in from Henderson that morning. She landed to a cold, gray sky and a text from Gordie that just said "Welcome to real football weather."

Luz: It was warm when I left.

Gordie: You'll be fine. Layers.

Luz: I don't own layers. I live in the desert.

The forecast for Sunday night says rain is likely, with temperatures in the forties and a breeze off the lake, and Gordie couldn't be happier. His Browns are undefeated at home this year, and he credits the weather, the crowd and the general unpleasantness of the place.

Gordie: Teams come here and they don't want to be here. That's the whole secret.

Luz's Raiders are in a rough patch. They opened the season with three straight wins, and she was telling everybody in Henderson that this was finally the year. Then they won once in the next eight games. The last three losses came against San Francisco, Seattle and Denver, and Luz has stopped checking the standings.

Luz: Brock Bowers is the best tight end in football. Maxx Crosby is still Maxx Crosby. Nakobe Dean has a hundred and nineteen tackles.

Gordie: And?

Luz: And we have a young quarterback, and I'm being patient.

Fernando Mendoza started last week in Denver, and Luz likes what she's seen even when the scoreboard doesn't. She thinks Ashton Jeanty is going to break one eventually, and she's been saying that since September.

Gordie's team does things differently. The Browns don't blow anybody out. They win close games, a lot of them, and they win them with defense. Carson Schwesinger has more than a hundred tackles and Gordie talks about him like a nephew. Harold Fannin Jr. has been Deshaun Watson's most reliable target. Quinshon Judkins grinds out yards when everybody in the stadium knows he's getting the ball.

Gordie: We beat Houston by three. We beat Pittsburgh by three. We beat Tennessee by three. We beat Baltimore by three.

Luz: Is that a strategy?

Gordie: It's a lifestyle.

The Browns are coming off a bye, rested and at home in prime time, and Gordie is treating it like a playoff game. Luz is treating it like a trip to see friends, with football attached.

What makes it interesting is that the Raiders can score. They've put up points against good teams even in losses, and the Browns haven't always kept people out of the end zone. Bowers can turn a short catch into a long gain. If the rain holds off, it could turn into the kind of game Gordie's not used to, one where his defense has to make a stop late.

Gordie: If it comes down to the last drive, I'll take my chances in this building.

Luz: That's what everybody says right before something weird happens.

They'll watch from the upper deck, Luz in a borrowed coat that goes down to her knees, Gordie in the same brown stocking cap he's worn since the team came back. Win or lose, they're going back to Public Square after to catch the last light show at eleven.

Luz: You sure it's still running?

Gordie: Every half hour until eleven. I checked.

My call: Browns 24, Raiders 21.
