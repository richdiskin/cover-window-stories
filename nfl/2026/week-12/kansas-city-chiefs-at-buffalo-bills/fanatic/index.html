Kansas City Chiefs at Buffalo Bills

Kansas City Chiefs (8-2) at Buffalo Bills (9-1)

8:20 PM ET Thu Nov 26 · Highmark Stadium, Orchard Park, NY · NBC

XFacebook

Marty Kowalczyk will run eight kilometers through downtown Buffalo on Thursday morning and then sit in a cold seat in Orchard Park for three more hours that night, and he calls that a balanced holiday. The YMCA Turkey Trot turns 131 this year. It's the oldest consecutively run footrace in the world, and for the first time in a generation it has a new course, a single loop that starts and ends at the Buffalo Convention Center and still sends the runners down Delaware Avenue for the back half.

Della Ruiz came in from Overland Park on Tuesday and was informed, at the baggage claim, that she would be running too.

Della: I flew here for football. Nobody said anything about cardio.

Marty: Fourteen thousand people do it every year. Little kids. Grandmothers in turkey hats. You'll be fine. Just don't wear the red jacket, people will think you're lost.

Della: I'm wearing the red jacket to the game, Marty. I'm wearing it to the race. I might wear it to dinner.

This is the game both of them circled in August. The Bills have nine wins and one loss. The Chiefs have eight and two. It's Thanksgiving night, the whole country is full of turkey and watching, and somebody is going to walk out of Highmark Stadium holding the inside track to January.

Marty's whole argument is Josh Allen and the run game nobody in Kansas City wants to talk about. James Cook III has 1,060 rushing yards and 10 touchdowns, and Marty has started calling him "the Fourth of July" because every carry looks like a fireworks show. Greg Rousseau has 10 sacks. The Bills have won six straight since a home loss to New England, and Marty still won't say the word "Patriots" indoors.

Della: Patrick Mahomes has twenty-two touchdown throws and four interceptions. Four. You know how many your guy has thrown?

Marty: We don't count those in Western New York. It's bad luck.

Della's not just talking about Mahomes, though. The Kansas City defense has given up fewer points than Buffalo's has, and Nick Bolton is everywhere. George Karlaftis has eight sacks. Kenneth Walker III gave the Chiefs a ground game Della kept describing as "rude," and she is still sore that a groin injury keeps him out of this one.

Della: We play defense now. Mahomes doesn't have to win it by himself. He just has to win it at the end.

Marty: He's done that to us enough times. You don't have to remind me.

That's the history neither of them wants to say out loud. Marty has been to too many January nights where Mahomes had the ball last. He knows the feeling in his stomach when the clock gets inside two minutes and the red jerseys start celebrating. He's trying very hard to convince himself this year is different, and the record says it might be.

The forecast says the sky might open up Thursday night, and Marty is quietly thrilled. Rain in late November in Orchard Park means a slippery ball, a lot of handoffs and a crowd that refuses to sit down. Della packed a poncho. Marty told her ponchos are for tourists.

Della: I am a tourist.

Marty: Not tonight. Tonight you're a Bills fan who's wearing the wrong jacket.

They'll eat turkey sandwiches in the parking lot at five, argue about Travis Kelce until six, and by kickoff neither of them will be able to feel their legs from the morning run. Marty thinks Allen makes one more throw at the end. Della thinks Mahomes gets the ball with time left, which is the scariest thing in football.

Neither one will be shocked if it comes down to the final snap. That's what these two teams do to each other.

My call: Bills 25, Chiefs 23.
