Denver Broncos at Pittsburgh Steelers

Denver Broncos (8-2) at Pittsburgh Steelers (6-4)

3:00 PM ET Fri Nov 27 · Acrisure Stadium, Pittsburgh, PA · Prime Video

XFacebook

Downtown Pittsburgh is still glowing from last Saturday. Light Up Night turned 65 this year, with Zambelli fireworks above the rivers, bridges lit up and the city's 113th Christmas tree switched on in front of the City-County Building. Dom Petrakis was on Grant Street for all of it, holding his granddaughter on his shoulders and a Terrible Towel in his back pocket, because you never know.

Lorna Haskell landed from Denver on Wednesday and wanted to know why everyone in the airport was already talking about Friday.

Dom: Because it's Black Friday, it's three o'clock, and it's the Steelers at home. Nobody's shopping. The malls are going to be empty and the stadium is going to be shaking.

Lorna: Your team lost its last two.

Dom: We don't lose at home. Four games at Acrisure, four wins. Ask Atlanta. Ask Cincinnati. Ask Indianapolis. Ask Cleveland.

That's the whole Pittsburgh argument, and it's a good one. The Steelers have been a different team in their own building. The trouble is the road. A blowout in Cincinnati and a gut punch in Philadelphia have Dom's neighbors in Carrick calling the radio shows again, and that's never a good sign in this town.

Lorna's Broncos come in on a heater. Denver has won five in a row and beat Las Vegas going away last Sunday. The Broncos lost the opener in Kansas City and a game in San Francisco early on, and since then they've been the team nobody wants to see on the schedule.

Lorna: Alex Singleton has a hundred and eighteen tackles. A hundred and eighteen. He's in on every play like he's paying rent there.

Dom: T.J. Watt doesn't need a hundred tackles. He needs one good one on your quarterback.

Watt has seven and a half sacks, and Dom has a rule: if Watt gets home on the first series, the whole stadium comes alive. Lorna's answer is Bo Nix, who has twelve touchdown throws and only five interceptions and doesn't panic when the pocket caves. Jaylen Waddle gives him somebody fast to look for, and J.K. Dobbins keeps the chains moving when the wind kicks up.

The wind is going to howl. The forecast for Friday afternoon says low forties with gusts past twenty, and Dom has never been happier about a weather report.

Dom: That's Pittsburgh football. Nobody's throwing the ball sixty yards in that. You hand it to Jaylen Warren and you make them tackle him. Seven hundred and forty-seven yards. He runs angry.

Lorna: Our defense eats running backs.

Dom: Your defense never ate in Pittsburgh in November.

Aaron Rodgers is the part that makes this weird for both of them. Dom still can't get used to the old man in black and gold, and every time Rodgers finds DK Metcalf on a back-shoulder throw, Dom yells like he's been a fan for twenty years. Lorna thinks Rodgers has two great quarters left in him per game, tops. Dom thinks that's all he needs.

Both teams had five days to get ready after their Sunday games, and both coaching staffs will know exactly what's coming. This is the kind of game that turns on one turnover, one long punt, one third down where somebody gets a hand on the ball.

Dom has a plan for after the game, win or lose. He's walking his granddaughter back downtown to see the tree again, because a lit tree on a cold night fixes most things. Lorna has decided to come along. She says she wants to see if the city still looks that pretty after a home loss.

Dom: You'll find out the other way, kid.

Lorna: We'll see who's buying the hot chocolate.

My call: Broncos 23, Steelers 21.
