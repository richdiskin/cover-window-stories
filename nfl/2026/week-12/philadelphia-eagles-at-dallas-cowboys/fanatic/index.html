Philadelphia Eagles at Dallas Cowboys

Philadelphia Eagles (3-7) at Dallas Cowboys (5-6)

4:30 PM ET Thu Nov 26 · AT&T Stadium, Arlington, TX · Fox

XFacebook

Rhonda Castillo has watched every Cowboys Thanksgiving halftime show since Reba McEntire opened the whole tradition at Texas Stadium in 1997, and she has opinions about all thirty of them. This year's headliner for the Salvation Army Red Kettle Kickoff is Miranda Lambert, raised in East Texas about an hour from Dallas, and Rhonda has decided that a hometown girl on the halftime stage is a sign.

Vinnie Scarpa flew down from South Philadelphia on Tuesday night, mostly to argue with her in person. He brought a soft pretzel in his jacket pocket for luck. It's been in there since the airport.

Vinnie: You think a country singer decides a football game?

Rhonda: I think a Texas girl at halftime in Arlington on Thanksgiving doesn't happen by accident. Also, we beat you by seventeen in your own building last month.

That's the part Vinnie doesn't love. The Cowboys went into Philadelphia in October and ran the Eagles off their own field, and the Eagles then lost two more before they finally found a win. Seven losses in a row, start to finish. Vinnie stopped answering texts from his cousins around Halloween.

Then last Sunday happened. The Eagles beat Pittsburgh by three, Joe Milton III had the ball in his hands, and Vinnie walked out of the Linc like a man who'd been let out of jail.

Vinnie: One win. That's all I needed. Milton's got the strongest arm I've seen in green in years. He throws a deep ball that comes down with snow on it.

Rhonda: He'll be throwing it to Caleb Downs.

She's not wrong to feel good. Dak Prescott has 25 touchdown throws and only six interceptions. CeeDee Lamb has 1,108 receiving yards and nine scores, and Javonte Williams has run for 10 touchdowns. This Dallas team can score from anywhere, and it showed it Sunday by dropping fifty-one on Tennessee.

Vinnie: You also gave up forty-six to San Francisco the week before. Your defense has a revolving door.

Rhonda: Quinnen Williams has eight sacks. Tell Milton to bring a helmet with extra padding.

That's the honest truth about these Cowboys. They are thrilling and maddening in the same afternoon, a team that has scored a ton and given up nearly as much. Rhonda has a rule about Thanksgiving games: never leave the couch until the clock is all zeros, because this team has made her cry in the fourth quarter more than once this year.

Vinnie, for his part, knows the Eagles don't have the firepower to trade touchdowns all day. What they have is Nolan Smith Jr., who has six sacks, and a defense that wants to make this ugly, even with Zack Baun on injured reserve. DeVonta Smith and Saquon Barkley have had quieter years than anybody in Philadelphia expected, and Vinnie would like a word with the football gods about that.

Vinnie: Just keep it close into the fourth. Make Dak earn it. That's our whole plan.

Rhonda: Your whole plan is hoping.

Vinnie: Hoping got us one win. I'll ride it.

Here's what both of them agree on. The red kettles come out at halftime, Miranda Lambert sings, and everybody watching gets asked to give something to people who need it this winter. Rhonda has already put a twenty in the kettle outside her grocery store in Grand Prairie. Vinnie says he'll match it if the Eagles keep it within one score.

Rhonda: That's not how charity works, Vincent.

Vinnie: It's how Philadelphia works.

The turkey will be dry, the arguments will be loud, and the Cowboys will probably need a late stop to put the Eagles away. Rhonda will be on her feet for it. Vinnie will be holding a cold pretzel and praying.

My call: Cowboys 27, Eagles 22.
