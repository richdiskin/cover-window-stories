Seattle Seahawks at Las Vegas Raiders

Seattle Seahawks (5-4) at Las Vegas Raiders (4-5)

4:05 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Allegiant Stadium, Las Vegas, NV · CBS

XFacebook

Klint Kubiak helped Seattle win Super Bowl LX in February, and on Sunday he coaches the Las Vegas Raiders against the team he left. Kara Holm flew down for Seattle Seahawks vs Las Vegas Raiders in Week 10 to watch it happen, and Vic Moreno spent her first night in town complaining about the Grand Prix construction choking the Strip.

Crews spent the week bolting up gantries along the circuit, and Koval Lane shut down completely early Saturday. The Strip is half football town and half racetrack in the making.

Kara: "You live in Las Vegas and you're surprised by traffic?"

Vic: "I'm not surprised. I'm offended. The race isn't until next week."

He has a point. The Grand Prix runs Nov. 19 to 21, and the city is already bending itself around it. Bus stops closed on Koval, pedestrian bridges shut overnight, lanes pinched on Las Vegas Boulevard. Vic has followed this franchise through three relocations and kept a grudge from every one of them. A few orange barrels aren't going to break him.

Vic grades every game by who's across the field. Kansas City is a war. Denver is a war. Seattle is a different flavor, an old AFC West grudge from the years the Seahawks lived in the division.

Kara: "We left your division a long time ago."

Vic: "You left. The grudge stayed."

Kara is the rare fan who refuses to enjoy being champion. Seattle beat New England 29-13 in Super Bowl LX, and she spent the whole offseason telling anybody who would listen that champions get hunted. At 5-4, she's been proven right more than she'd like.

Kara: "Every team we play treats it like their own Super Bowl. Even you guys."

Vic: "Especially us. Your offensive coordinator is our head coach now."

That's the Kubiak thing, and for Vic it's the whole weekend. Klint Kubiak ran Seattle's offense in 2025, won a ring, and took the Raiders job. Vic is sure Kubiak walked out the door with every Seahawks secret. Kara is sure he left the good stuff in Seattle.

Kara: "He knows our old offense. Mike Macdonald's defense is a different conversation."

She's got numbers on her side. Seattle has allowed 162 points in nine games, and its run defense gives up 3.6 yards a carry. That's bad news for a Raiders team that runs for 3.1. Ashton Jeanty has spent a lot of this season running into a crowd.

Vic: "Jeanty is one crease from going the distance."

Kara: "Then Devon Witherspoon will meet him at the crease."

Vic's real hope is Brock Bowers and Maxx Crosby. Bowers is the one guy on the Raiders' offense every defense circles on Monday, and Crosby is the guy every right tackle in the AFC West sees in his sleep. The Raiders lost 38-17 at San Francisco last week, and Vic needs Crosby to make Seattle's quarterback miserable.

That's the other twist. Sam Darnold, the guy with the Super Bowl ring, didn't start last week's 17-3 win against Arizona, and Mike Macdonald said Monday he's out another week. Drew Lock gets the start again, oblique and all. Kara isn't complaining. She watched her team grind that one out at home, and she wants the same thing with a roof overhead.

Kara: "Seventeen points and a defense that gave up three. I'll sign for that every week."

Vic: "Sign for it somewhere else."

The Seahawks are 1-2 on the road, and that's the only number Vic keeps repeating. The Raiders are 1-3 at home, and that's the one Kara keeps repeating back. Allegiant Stadium will be loud with both colors in it, and half the crowd will be sitting in Strip traffic afterward. Vic says Crosby won't let the Raiders quit. Kara says champions get hunted, and sometimes the hunted team just wins by seven.

My call: Seahawks 26, Raiders 19.
