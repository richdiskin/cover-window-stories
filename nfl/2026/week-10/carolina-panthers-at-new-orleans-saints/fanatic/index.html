Carolina Panthers at New Orleans Saints

Carolina Panthers (3-5) at New Orleans Saints (3-5)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Caesars Superdome, New Orleans, LA · Fox

XFacebook

Giant puppets made of light are climbing the old buildings of downtown New Orleans all weekend, and Andre Thibault has decided LUNA Fête is the perfect warmup for Carolina Panthers vs New Orleans Saints in Week 10. Mitch Kovac came down from Charlotte to watch Bryce Young take on Tyler Shough at the Caesars Superdome, and he says the only thing getting lit up Sunday is the Saints' secondary.

Andre has been going every night since Friday. The festival stretches from Lafayette Square to the Piazza d'Italia, runs 5 to 10 p.m. through Sunday, it's the 12th edition, and this year's theme is The Puppetry Parade. He already has his spot picked out in front of Gallier Hall, and he's already told Mitch the Sunday plan: final whistle, quick walk, best seat on the square.

Mitch: "You're planning a victory lap before kickoff."

Andre: "I'm planning a walk. If it turns into a victory lap, take it up with your defense."

Mitch Kovac reads a depth chart the way an accountant reads a ledger. Who starts, how he got here, what it costs to replace him. Ask him the greatest Panther ever and he won't blink: Julius Peppers, drafted and grown in Charlotte, now in Canton. Sam Mills is the heart. Peppers is the template. Mitch will argue that with anybody in a teal jersey.

Andre is patient in a different way. He judges this franchise by what it grows at home, and his answer to every panic is the same: development is a timeline. It took New Orleans four decades to build the Super Bowl XLIV team, and he reminds people of that every time somebody wants to blow up the building in October.

Mitch: "So how's the timeline looking at 3-5?"

Andre: "Same as yours. We're both 3-5, Mitch. The difference is we just won a game."

He's not wrong. The Saints finally won in their own building last Sunday, 23-22 against Cleveland, and Andre swears the Dome sounded like January. Carolina is headed the other way. Three straight losses: 43-13 at home to Tampa Bay, 27-19 at Green Bay, and 26-21 to Denver last week.

Mitch: "Three losses. I can name the reason for each one. That's the difference between a problem and a mess."

Andre: "Name them."

Mitch: "Not in public."

What keeps Mitch up at night isn't the festival. New Orleans runs for just 3.8 yards a carry, which sounds like good news until you remember Carolina gives up 4.7. Alvin Kamara in a dome is still Alvin Kamara, and Mitch has seen that movie before.

Andre has his own worry. Derrick Brown plays inside for Carolina like the ground owes him rent, and Jaelan Phillips comes off the outside angry. Shough is still on his timeline, and Andre knows a young quarterback who gets hit early starts hearing footsteps late.

Andre: "Cameron Jordan and Chase Young are going to live in Bryce's backfield."

Mitch: "Bryce has been chased by better."

Andre: "Name one."

Mitch: "Not in public."

The rivalry doesn't need a festival to get loud. This is the NFC South, and these two have been trading punches since the Panthers came into the league in 1995. Carolina has two NFC titles, 2003 and 2015, and no ring. Andre has XLIV, and every Panthers fan within ten rows has heard about it.

Mitch: "One ring."

Andre: "One more than you. And we grew ours. Drafted it, coached it, built it."

Mitch: "We built Peppers."

Andre: "Call me when he comes out of retirement."

The puppets will be waiting Sunday night either way. Andre swears he's walking to Lafayette Square win or lose. Mitch says he's coming along just to watch Andre's face if Bryce Young and Tetairoa McMillan steal one late, with Chuba Hubbard grinding out the last few minutes. Two 3-5 teams, two fan bases sick of losing, and a downtown dressed for a party. Mitch thinks the party is his, by one point.

My call: Panthers 25, Saints 24.
