The Fanatic · NFL

Pittsburgh Steelers at Cincinnati Bengals

Pittsburgh Steelers (6-2) at Cincinnati Bengals (6-2)

8:20 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Paycor Stadium, Cincinnati, OH · NBC

XFacebook

A 24-foot hippo dressed as a nutcracker was the first thing Ed Kowalski saw in Cincinnati. Laura Kessel made sure of it, dragging him to the Cincinnati Zoo's new Fiona nutcracker on Saturday before Pittsburgh Steelers vs Cincinnati Bengals in Week 10, when Aaron Rodgers and Joe Burrow settle who runs the AFC North on Sunday night.

The zoo put Fiona up at its entrance last week, and Laura wanted a Steelers fan standing next to something bigger than his attitude.

Laura knows every detail. Twenty-nine buttons for Fiona's birth weight. Six slices of watermelon, because Fiona came six weeks early and loves the stuff. Her little brother Fritz painted on a toenail.

Ed: "You people built a 24-foot hippo for the holidays."

Laura: "We built a hippo. You built a whole personality out of one deflected ball in 1972."

Ed: "December 23, 1972. Fourth down, 22 seconds left, down 7-6 to Oakland. Say it with respect."

Ed Kowalski judges everything by stability. Chuck Noll held one head coaching job in his life, from 1969 to 1991, and won four Super Bowls in it. To Ed, that's the only blueprint that matters. So when Pittsburgh brought in Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers, he didn't call it a splash. He called it a reunion. The two of them won Super Bowl XLV together in Green Bay, and they won it against the Steelers, which Ed tries very hard not to think about.

Laura: "Your coach and your quarterback beat your team in a Super Bowl."

Ed: "And now they work for us. That's called continuity."

Laura starts every conversation with the quarterback, and in Cincinnati that means Burrow. With him, she says, the ceiling is always February. The floor is everybody else on the roster. Her personal hero is Ken Riley, the cornerback with 65 career interceptions who wasn't elected to the Hall of Fame until 2023, decades after he retired.

Both teams are 6-2. Pittsburgh is 4-0 at home and 2-2 on the road. Cincinnati is 4-1 away and 2-1 at Paycor Stadium. The Bengals just beat Atlanta 34-17. The Steelers had last week off after a 27-6 win against Cleveland, so they arrive with two weeks to get ready.

Laura: "Two weeks off. Rodgers needed the nap."

Ed: "Rodgers needed nothing. T.J. Watt needed two weeks to study your tackles."

The fight Ed actually cares about is on the ground. The Steelers run for 5.2 yards a carry, and the Bengals give up 5.2. Jaylen Warren has been the guy, and Ed wants him carrying it 25 times. The Bengals run for 4.0, and Pittsburgh gives up just 3.8, so Burrow and Ja'Marr Chase are going to have to do it through the air against a defense with 26 sacks.

Laura: "Start with the quarterback. Mine throws to Ja'Marr Chase."

Ed: "Mine throws to DK Metcalf, and mine has a ring."

Laura: "One ring with a different team, coached by a guy who also beat you."

Ed didn't love that one.

Cincinnati can rush the quarterback too, with 23 sacks of its own, and Laura has been telling Ed all weekend that Rodgers doesn't scramble like he used to. Ed has been telling her that Rodgers doesn't have to.

The zoo's Festival of Lights doesn't open until Nov. 19, so on Sunday night the brightest lights in town will be at Paycor. Laura thinks Burrow wins it late. Ed won't concede anything, but he admits the Bengals might get one more field goal than his guys. Neither of them is leaving early.

My call: Bengals 25, Steelers 22.
