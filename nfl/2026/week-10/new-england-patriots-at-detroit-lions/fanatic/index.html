New England Patriots at Detroit Lions

New England Patriots (5-4) at Detroit Lions (6-2)

9:30 AM ET Sun Nov 15 · FC Bayern Munich Stadium, Munich, Germany · Fox

XFacebook

Two American fan bases have taken the middle of Munich for New England Patriots vs Detroit Lions in Week 10, and they've set up camp a few blocks apart. Detroit's Fan Fest fills Odeonsplatz on Friday and Saturday, with Kevin Saunderson spinning Detroit techno on Friday evening and Big Sean on stage Saturday, while the Patriots Haus a short walk away has all six Super Bowl rings on display, plus a Tom Brady glove and a Drew Bledsoe shoe.

Martin Sorensen went to the Lions show first. Glen Tolliver went straight to the rings.

Glen keeps the Patriots' receipts, and this weekend the receipts flew across in their own luggage. He stood in front of those rings for twenty minutes, then texted Martin a photo of it with no words at all.

Martin: Real mature, Glen. You know what I've got? A Detroit rapper headlining the biggest square in the middle of Munich.

Glen: I've got six of those little circles. You can count them from the door.

Martin runs on one idea, the same one he's carried since this Lions run started: a window doesn't stay open forever, and Detroit's is open right now. The Lions are 6-2, they've won four straight, and they've held four opponents in a row to 21 points or fewer for the first time in a decade. They won 19-9 in Miami on Sunday without needing Jared Goff to do much, which Martin considers a sign of maturity and Glen considers a warning light.

Glen: Your quarterback got sacked three times by the Dolphins. The Dolphins, Martin.

Martin: And we still held them out of the end zone. That's the whole point.

Then Martin brings up Sunday in Foxborough, because he's a fair man but not a kind one. Green Bay hung forty-five on New England, the most points anybody has ever put up on the Patriots at Gillette Stadium, and Drake Maye threw three interceptions before halftime. He still finished with three touchdown throws, and Glen will tell you that nobody else's quarterback climbs out of a hole like that. Martin will tell you nobody else's quarterback digs it.

The injury list is where Glen gets quiet. Mike Vrabel said Monday he doesn't expect left guard Ben Brown to play, and New England already had Mike Onwenu and Alijah Vera-Tucker on injured reserve. Rhamondre Stevenson left Sunday with a knee and was headed for an MRI. A.J. Brown has missed two straight games with a knee. That leaves a patched-up front five trying to hold off Aidan Hutchinson, who leads the league in pressures and hasn't had a sack in two weeks, which Martin considers a debt about to be collected.

Glen has his own answer. Detroit lost Jack Campbell to injured reserve with a neck injury, and the Lions' interior is short of bodies. Milton Williams lives in that space.

Glen: Your middle is hollow, and our best lineman lives in the middle.

Martin: Your quarterback's going to be throwing from his own end zone.

The numbers keep them honest. Detroit runs for 4.7 yards a carry against a Patriots defense that allows 4.0. New England runs for 4.4 against a Lions defense that allows 4.2. The Lions are 4-0 in their real home building, but this one is in Munich at 3:30 local time, which is 9:30 back in Michigan and no kind of home for anyone. Detroit has scored 222 and allowed 189. New England has scored 211 and allowed 207 and sits at 5-4, needing this one more than it wants to admit.

On Saturday Martin will be in Odeonsplatz screaming every word Big Sean throws at him. Glen will be across town at Marienplatz, waiting his turn to shake Troy Brown's hand.

Martin: Six rings is history. I'm here for this year.

Glen: This year's going to need some history, pal.

Sunday morning the two of them ride out to the stadium together on the same train, Honolulu blue next to navy, not speaking. Whoever leaves happy gets to choose dinner, and the other one has to find his own way back to the hotel.

My call: Patriots 26, Lions 25.
