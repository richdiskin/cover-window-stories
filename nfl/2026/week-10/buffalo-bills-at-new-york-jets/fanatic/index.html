Buffalo Bills at New York Jets

Buffalo Bills (7-1) at New York Jets (2-7)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, NJ · CBS

XFacebook

Mike Lasker bought a football-shaped lamp at the Saturday flea in MetLife Stadium's Lot J, and Ellen Marsh hasn't stopped laughing at him since. That was the opening act for Buffalo Bills vs New York Jets in Week 10, when Josh Allen brings a 7-1 Bills team into the same stadium as Geno Smith and a 2-7 Jets team Mike still refuses to quit on.

The New Meadowlands flea sets up in Lot J every Saturday from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., free to get in and free to park, and Mike walked it end to end with Ellen. She bought a scarf. He bought the lamp and swore it was vintage.

Ellen: "That lamp still has the dollar-store sticker on it."

Mike: "Everybody says that about my lamps. Everybody is usually wrong."

Mike's whole personality grew out of one January afternoon in Miami. Everybody said the AFL champion had no chance in Super Bowl III. Joe Namath said otherwise, and the Jets beat Baltimore. Mike was raised on the idea that when the whole world agrees on something, you go check the work.

Ellen has the opposite problem. She's a Bills fan who stopped clapping for division titles years ago. Buffalo has a shelf full of them, and she'll remind you the last time this franchise finished a season as champion was 1965, in the AFL. Everything since then is a question she asks out loud every winter. Her saying is short: wake me in January.

Mike: "You're 7-1 and you're bored."

Ellen: "I'm not bored. I'm waiting. There's a difference."

Here's what Ellen can't wave off. The Bills are 4-0 on the road, and they just went into Minnesota on Monday night and won 23-6. Allen did Allen things, James Cook kept the chains moving, and the defense never let the Vikings breathe.

Mike: "Monday night in Minnesota, Sunday afternoon in Jersey. That's six days. Your guys are going to be dragging by halftime."

Ellen: "Our guys are going to be tired of scoring."

Mike has his own mess. The Jets are 2-7, and last week was rough, a 35-12 loss at Kansas City. They're 1-3 at home. Aaron Glenn's season hasn't gone the way the green half of MetLife hoped in August.

Ellen: "Two and seven, Mike."

Mike: "Two and seven is when the Jets are most dangerous. Nobody's watching. Nobody's scared. That's when you get them."

There are real arguments buried in the noise. Garrett Wilson can take a game and run off with it. Breece Hall can turn a nothing carry into a stadium on its feet. Demario Davis and Minkah Fitzpatrick have been around long enough to know where Allen likes to go when he scrambles. And this is the AFC East, where nobody is a stranger.

Ellen: "You just described a highlight package."

Mike: "I described Sunday."

The history lives in both of them. The Bills and Jets started together in 1960, original AFL teams. Ellen's two titles came in 1964 and 1965. Mike's came in Super Bowl III. They've been fighting about which one weighs more since the day they met.

Ellen: "Two championships."

Mike: "One Super Bowl."

Ellen: "Ours were real championships."

Mike: "Ours had Namath. Yours had a trophy nobody outside Buffalo remembers."

Ellen: "Everybody in Buffalo remembers. That's plenty."

Sunday, Mike is setting up in Lot J again, minus the lamp. Ellen is getting there early so she can watch him explain to strangers why this is the week. She's heard the speech. She's also watched Buffalo let teams hang around long enough to make a fourth quarter tense. Mike says the Jets make it uncomfortable for a while. Ellen says she'll be wide awake for the part where Allen ends it.

My call: Bills 28, Jets 21.
