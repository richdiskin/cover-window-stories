The Fanatic · NFL

Houston Texans at Cleveland Browns

Houston Texans (3-5) at Cleveland Browns (5-4)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Huntington Bank Field, Cleveland, OH · Fox

XFacebook

Downtown Cleveland gets a two-night stand this weekend: the Cavaliers host the Dallas Mavericks at Rocket Arena at 7:30 Saturday, and less than a day later the Houston Texans vs Cleveland Browns in Week 10 fills the lakefront. Frank Haller will be at both, and Nate Coleman, in from Houston with a Texans hoodie he refuses to zip, has agreed to the Cavs game only because Frank promised to explain Deshaun Watson on the walk there.

Frank never explains anything on a walk. He lectures. He carries the Browns' old championship scrolls around in his head, and he'll remind anyone within earshot that Cleveland was the gold standard of pro football before there was a Super Bowl to win.

Nate: Just tell me the quarterback, Frank.

Frank: Watson.

Nate: Our Watson?

Frank: He hasn't been yours for a long time.

That's the story of the week in both towns. Jeremy Fowler reported Monday that Watson will start against Houston, his old team, after the Browns went with Shedeur Sanders last Sunday in New Orleans. Watson started four seasons in Houston. This will be the first time he faces C.J. Stroud, the quarterback who replaced him there, and Nate has been thinking about that sentence since Monday morning.

Nate judges every team by how fast it gets to the quarterback, and he's never been more interested in a quarterback's footwork in his life. He's watched Watson come back from two Achilles tears and win the job back from Sanders in the summer. He's watched him get Cleveland to five wins. He's also watched the numbers: seven touchdown throws, three interceptions.

Nate: Pressure tells the truth, Frank. Ours is about to have a conversation with your guy.

Frank: Pressure has to get there first. This place eats visitors.

He's not wrong about that part. The Browns are 3-0 at home this season and 2-4 everywhere else. The Texans are the strangest team in the league: 0-4 at home and 3-1 on the road, which means Nate has finally found a stadium he likes, and it's not his own.

Frank: You're 3-5, Nate. You're road warriors with a losing record.

Nate: You're 5-4 and you've lost two straight. One of them in overtime on a two-point try.

That one still hurts. Sanders threw for nearly three hundred yards and two touchdowns in New Orleans, Harold Fannin scored late, and the Browns went for two to win it and didn't get it. Frank watched from his couch and didn't speak for an hour. His wife timed it.

Then they get to the part neither of them likes. Houston runs for only 3.7 yards a carry, which is no way to live, but Cleveland's defense gives up 5.0, which might rescue the Texans' ground game for one afternoon. The Browns run for 3.9 against a Houston defense that allows 3.9. Nobody is getting fat on the ground in this one.

Nate: Stroud's having a bounce-back year. Yours is a comeback story.

Frank: Comeback stories are the only kind we tell here. Read a history book.

The Browns have scored 161 points and allowed 196. The Texans have scored 209 and allowed 192. Frank has done that subtraction and does not want to discuss it.

On Saturday night they'll sit in the upper level at Rocket Arena, and Frank will spend timeouts arguing that Todd Monken's offense looks different with Watson's legs back in it. Nate will spend them reminding Frank that Houston already knows every one of Watson's habits by heart.

Sunday, Frank will wear the same brown sweater he wore the day the Browns came back to Cleveland, and Nate will finally zip the hoodie. It's November on the lake. Even road warriors get cold.

My call: Texans 23, Browns 18.
