Kansas City Chiefs at Atlanta Falcons

Kansas City Chiefs (6-2) at Atlanta Falcons (3-6)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta, GA · CBS

XFacebook

Kansas City Chiefs vs Atlanta Falcons in Week 10 shares a weekend with a church festival that got rained out of October and refused to stay gone. The St. Elias Mid-East Festival, a nearly fifty-year Atlanta tradition, was rescheduled to Saturday and Sunday on Ponce de Leon Avenue after its fall dates were called off for weather, and Russ Amundsen has decided Patrick Mahomes and the Chiefs are walking into exactly that kind of town: one that doesn't cancel, it reschedules.

Chet Morrell, who drove in from Kansas City with a notebook full of dynasty audits, would like it noted that his team has never needed a rain date for anything.

Russ measures the Falcons in NFC South math, and he showed up at St. Elias on Saturday afternoon with the division standings on his phone and a plate of food from the church kitchen. The festival's food is cooked by members of the St. Elias community from family recipes, and Russ has been going long enough that the volunteers know his order.

Chet: You brought me to a church festival to talk about a 3-6 team.

Russ: I brought you to a place that's been doing this for almost half a century. You could learn something about patience.

Chet audits every Chiefs season against the old Hank Stram teams and the Andy Reid run, and this year's ledger has a strange shape. Kansas City started 5-0, lost in Seattle and Denver, then blew out the Jets at home last Sunday. The Chiefs are 6-2, 4-0 at home and 2-2 on the road, with 202 points scored and only 143 allowed. Chet's notebook has one word circled next to all that: road.

Then there's the elbow. Mahomes took a strip sack from Jets rookie David Bailey on Sunday, never missed an offensive snap, and then went for an MRI. The tests didn't show a major injury, and the team is watching the elbow for pain and swelling this week. Chet has read every update twice. He watched Mahomes miss the end of last season with a torn knee, and he watched Kansas City miss the playoffs for the first time in the Mahomes era.

Chet: He didn't come off the field, Russ. He's fine.

Russ: Then why are you rereading the same article?

Russ has his own problem, and it's a home record that makes no sense. Atlanta is 1-4 in its own building and 2-2 on the road. The Falcons beat San Francisco 29-20 in Week 7, then got shut out 16-0 by Tampa Bay and lost 34-17 to Cincinnati. On Monday they traded Jessie Bates to Jacksonville, which Russ took personally and Chet took as a sign.

Chet: Your team just sold its safety. That's not a team planning on January.

Russ: That's a team planning on Bijan.

And Bijan Robinson is the one thing Chet can't audit away. He leads the league with eight hundred eighty rushing yards, and last week he went for exactly a hundred on nineteen carries against the Bengals. Atlanta runs for 4.9 yards a carry. The catch is that Kansas City allows only 3.7, the meanest number on Chet's whole sheet. The Chiefs run for 5.3 the other way against a Falcons defense that gives up 4.7.

Russ: Michael Penix just has to hand it to Bijan and get out of the way.

Chet: Thirty times? Against our front? Bring a chair.

Russ wants a slow, grinding afternoon where Robinson keeps Mahomes on the sideline and the building gets loud in the fourth quarter. Chet wants Mahomes to throw one early to prove the elbow is just an elbow. Both of them are quietly afraid of the same thing, which is a close game decided by whoever blinks last.

On Sunday morning the festival opens at 11:30, and Russ will stop for one more plate before he drives downtown. Chet says he'll come along. He's bringing the notebook, and Russ says that's fine as long as the first entry reads: Atlanta doesn't cancel, it reschedules.

My call: Chiefs 24, Falcons 21.
