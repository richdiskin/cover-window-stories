The Fanatic · NFL

Miami Dolphins at Indianapolis Colts

Miami Dolphins (1-7) at Indianapolis Colts (2-7)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 15 · Lucas Oil Stadium, Indianapolis, IN · CBS

XFacebook

Hoosiers turns forty this fall, and on Sunday afternoon the Payne and Mencias Palladium is screening it with a question-and-answer session at three o'clock, right in the middle of Miami Dolphins vs Indianapolis Colts in Week 10. Lou Ferraro has the movie ticket in one pocket and his Colts season ticket in the other, and he has spent the whole week pretending that's a hard choice.

It isn't. It never was. But Lou likes the drama of it, and he likes telling Carla Ruiz, who flew up from Miami and has never seen the movie, that the greatest long-shot story ever filmed is pure Indiana.

Carla: It's a basketball movie, Lou.

Lou: It's an Indiana movie. Small school, nobody gives them a chance, they win anyway. Sound familiar?

Carla: Your team has lost five straight.

Lou: So did Hickory, probably, at some point. Doesn't matter. Watch the ending.

Lou guards a Colts story that runs from Baltimore to Indianapolis, two Super Bowls and all, and he tells it at the speed of a man who knows he has a captive audience. Carla talks Miami like someone who has sat in the room where the decisions get made, and she reminds Lou during every history lesson that her team owns the only perfect season in the league.

Here's the uncomfortable truth they share: these are two of the worst teams in the league, and both of them know it. Miami is 1-7, winless at home, with its only win on the road against the Jets. The Dolphins lost 19-9 to Detroit last week. Indianapolis is 2-7 after starting 2-2, and Lou has spent five straight Sundays explaining to his neighbors that it's a process.

Carla: You've allowed 249 points. That's not a process, that's a leak.

Lou: You've scored 126. A leak needs water, Carla.

That one gets a laugh out of both of them, because it's true on both sides.

Lou's real problem is his quarterback. Daniel Jones has thrown an interception in eight of the Colts' nine games, including four straight, and he has ten of them against only six touchdown throws. Lou defends Jones the way you defend a cousin at a wedding: loudly and without much evidence. Carla would bring up Malik Willis, but Miami put him on injured reserve Tuesday, so Kyle McCord gets his first NFL start, and Carla is calling that a fresh look the Colts haven't seen.

The ground game is where Carla gets excited. Miami runs for 4.5 yards a carry, and Indianapolis gives up 5.3, the softest number in the whole matchup. The Colts run for 4.2 against a Dolphins defense that allows 4.2, which Lou calls even and Carla calls boring.

Carla: We run it down your throat for three hours and you're home in time to see the end of your movie.

Lou: The Colts beat you three straight times, including by twenty-five last year. Look it up.

She already had. Indianapolis has won the last three meetings, and Lou has those scores in his head the way other men remember birthdays.

Still, neither of them is pretending this is a heavyweight fight. The Colts have lost Alec Pierce to injured reserve. The Dolphins have scored only 126 points in eight games. Somebody is going to win this game ugly, and the other fan is going to fly or drive home very quiet.

Lou will be in his seat at one o'clock, and if the Colts are ahead by enough at halftime, he'll tell Carla he's thinking about the three o'clock screening. He won't go. He'll text a friend in Carmel to ask how the Q and A went and pretend that was the plan all along.

Carla says she'll watch Hoosiers on the flight home. Lou says that's the first smart thing anybody from Miami has done all season, and Carla lets it go, because for once he might be right.

My call: Colts 24, Dolphins 20.
