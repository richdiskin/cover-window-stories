New Orleans Saints at Chicago Bears

New Orleans Saints (3-6) at Chicago Bears (7-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 22 · Soldier Field, Chicago, IL · Fox

XFacebook

One million lights come on along North Michigan Avenue on Saturday night, block by block, and Walt Brenner intends to be standing at Oak Street when the first tree goes. Andre Thibault, up from New Orleans for New Orleans Saints vs Chicago Bears in Week 11, has been told the 35th Magnificent Mile Lights Festival is the warmup act, and that Sunday at Soldier Field is the main event.

Andre comes from a city that knows parades. He has opinions about parades. He has stood on St. Charles Avenue since before he could walk, and he arrived in Chicago ready to grade this one on floats, bands and whether anybody throws anything.

Walt: Nobody throws anything. It's a lights parade. Mickey and Minnie lead it.

Andre: Then what do you catch?

Walt: A cold.

The parade steps off at 5:30 from Oak Street, nearly 40 floats heading south toward the river, with fireworks above the Chicago River after the last float passes. Walt has done it with his grandkids every year they've let him. He also uses it the way some fans use a pregame meal. He calls it loading up on good luck before the Bears need it.

They might not need much. Chicago is 7-2, rested off a bye and riding four straight wins, the last one a 27-24 win against Tampa Bay on a Sunday night. Caleb Williams has eight touchdown throws and five interceptions, and Walt will defend every one of those five like they were somebody else's fault. D'Andre Swift has 10 rushing touchdowns. Luther Burden III has 644 receiving yards, and Walt says he runs routes like a man late for the L.

Andre has his own list, and it's longer than his record would suggest. Tyler Shough has 2,173 yards and 15 touchdown throws. Chris Olave has 59 catches for 764 yards. Carl Granderson has 8.5 sacks and has spent the season living in other teams' backfields.

Walt: You're 3-6.

Andre: We're 2-1 on the road. We play better when nobody's feeding us.

That part is true, and it's the part Andre hangs on to hardest. The Saints won at Baltimore and at the Giants. They lost by a point in Detroit on opening day. It's the home games that have been a mess, and Sunday isn't a home game. His Saints lost 20-17 to Carolina last week, and he drove to the airport still muttering about it.

Then there's the forecast, and this is where Walt gets a look on his face. AccuWeather has gusts up to 31 miles an hour at Soldier Field around kickoff with the temperature near 49. Andre has played in a dome for as long as he's been alive. Walt has lived on the lakefront for most of his.

Walt: You ever thrown a football into a 31-mile-an-hour wind?

Andre: I've never thrown a football into any wind. We have a roof. We have air conditioning. We have civilization.

Walt: You have a roof because you're scared of the weather.

Andre: We have a roof because it rains sideways eight months a year.

They both have a point, which neither will admit. Wind like that does strange things to kicks, to deep shots, to a quarterback's patience. Walt thinks it helps a team built on the ground, and Chicago's defense has allowed only 184 points in nine games. Andre thinks the wind is the great equalizer and that Shough's quick throws can cut right through it.

Saturday night they'll stand together on Michigan Avenue, Walt in his old navy parka and Andre in three borrowed layers, watching the trees light up one after another. Sunday afternoon they'll be on opposite sides of a cold, loud building by the lake, and one of them will be very glad he came.

My call: Bears 28, Saints 20.
