Cincinnati Bengals at Washington Commanders

Cincinnati Bengals (7-2) at Washington Commanders (3-5-1)

8:15 PM ET Mon Nov 23 · Northwest Stadium, Landover, MD · ESPN

XFacebook

Claire Benton had two tickets for Monday night. One was for Stevie Wonder at Capital One Arena, Songs in the Key of Life in its entirety for the album's 50th anniversary, doors at seven, show at eight. The other was for the Commanders in Landover, kickoff at 8:15.

She can't be in both places. She has known this for weeks, and she has handled it the way any reasonable person would, by complaining about it to everyone she meets.

Laura Kessel: You picked football.

Claire: I picked football. Don't make me say it again.

Laura drove in from Cincinnati for Cincinnati Bengals vs Washington Commanders in Week 11, and she has been trying very hard not to be smug about it. It isn't going well. The Bengals are 7-2 and have won five straight, the last one a 38-18 win against Pittsburgh on Sunday night, and Laura has been smiling at strangers on the street.

Joe Burrow has 2,795 yards and 27 touchdown throws against six interceptions. Ja'Marr Chase has 64 catches for 878 yards and seven touchdowns. Chase Brown has 441 rushing yards. Boye Mafe has 6.5 sacks, and Barrett Carter has 74 tackles. Cincinnati has scored 278 points in nine games, and Laura can tell you where she was for most of them.

Laura: Twenty-seven touchdowns, Claire.

Claire: I heard you the first time. And the second.

Washington is 3-5-1 and has had a strange season. The Commanders lost three of their first four, played to a 27-27 tie with the Giants in October, and have since beaten the Eagles at home and won 24-21 in New York on Thursday night. They've had eleven days since, and Claire has used every one of them to convince herself this is a turning point.

Jayden Daniels has 1,499 yards and ten touchdown throws against two interceptions, plus 280 yards on the ground, and Claire says watching him is the closest thing football has to a great live album. Stefon Diggs has six touchdown catches. Odafe Oweh has five sacks, and Sonny Styles has 80 tackles.

Claire: We beat Seattle 33-31.

Laura: In September.

Claire: It still counts.

The album came out in 1976, went Diamond and won Album of the Year, and Claire has played it in her car on the way to every home game this season. "Sir Duke" for the drive in. "As" for the drive home, if things went well. She doesn't want to say what she plays if they didn't.

Laura: So you'll miss the show for a team that's 3-5-1.

Claire: I'll miss the show for my team. That's different.

Laura respects that, even if she won't say so. Cincinnati fans know a thing or two about loyalty to a team that hasn't always earned it.

They'll sit together in the lower bowl, and Claire will have the Stevie Wonder T-shirt she bought for the concert she isn't going to on beneath her coat, a small act of loyalty to both of the things she loves. Laura has promised to keep her voice down. Nobody who knows Laura believes her.

Somewhere downtown, around the third quarter, Stevie will play "I Wish," and Claire will feel it in her bones without hearing a note. She won't regret her choice. Probably. Laura will be too busy watching Chase to notice either way.

My call: Bengals 27, Commanders 23.
