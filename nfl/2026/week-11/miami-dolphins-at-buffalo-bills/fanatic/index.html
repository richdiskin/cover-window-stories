Miami Dolphins at Buffalo Bills

Miami Dolphins (1-8) at Buffalo Bills (8-1)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 22 · Highmark Stadium, Orchard Park, NY · CBS

XFacebook

Ellen Marsh has a new seat, a new concourse to get lost in and a new parking routine, and on Sunday she finally gets to show all of it to the one Dolphins fan she'd happily drive through a snow squall to annoy. Carla Ruiz flies up from Miami for Miami Dolphins vs Buffalo Bills in Week 11, her first look at the new $2.1 billion Highmark Stadium in Orchard Park, and Ellen has promised her the full tour, slowly.

The building officially opened on June 23. Sixty thousand seats, 1.6 million square feet, the biggest construction job Western New York has ever seen, and not a roof in sight.

Carla: You spent two billion dollars and forgot the top.

Ellen: We didn't forget it. We declined it. Roofs are for people who are afraid of November.

Carla is from a city that considers anything below seventy a coat day, so she has packed like an arctic explorer. Ellen has packed a seat cushion, a thermos of cocoa and a list of grievances that she reads like scripture.

The list starts with the only home loss of the season, 29-26 to New England on October 4. Ellen does not discuss it. She refers to it as the incident. Everything else in the new place has gone right. The first regular-season game in the building was a Thursday night in September, a 41-31 win against Detroit, and Ellen was there two hours before the gates opened.

Buffalo is 8-1. The Bills are 5-0 on the road, which Ellen enjoys almost as much as winning at home, because it means somebody else's fans had to go home quiet. They won 31-12 at the Jets on Sunday.

Carla's season has been the opposite of all that. Miami is 1-8, with the only win coming at the Jets in October. Malik Willis is on injured reserve, and Kyle McCord started last week's 24-22 loss in Indianapolis. Carla liked parts of it. She liked the two points. She did not like the part where it was still a loss.

Carla: We were a field goal from 2-8. You know how close that is to respectable?

Ellen: That's not a sentence anyone has ever said out loud.

Carla's real hope is the ground game. Ollie Gordon II has 539 yards and four rushing touchdowns, and he runs like a guy who's personally offended by tacklers. Jordyn Brooks has 84 tackles and spends most Sundays chasing somebody else's mistakes. Chop Robinson has five sacks, and Carla wants every one of those numbers doubled by about four o'clock.

Ellen's answer is a list of names that sounds like a winter forecast. Josh Allen has 13 touchdown throws. James Cook III has 971 rushing yards and nine touchdowns, which Ellen will remind you is more yards than some entire backfields. Greg Rousseau has eight sacks. Dalton Kincaid has 487 receiving yards, though he's doubtful Sunday with thumb and ankle injuries, and Ellen reads that part faster. She recites all of it in the voice of a woman reading a will.

Carla: You know you have more yards from one running back than some teams have from their quarterback?

Ellen: I know. I wrote it on my mittens.

There's a thing Western New Yorkers do with visitors that Carla has heard about and dreads: they explain the stadium funding before they explain the football. Ellen does it in the parking lot. Thirty years, she says. The team stays in Western New York for the next 30 years. She says it twice, slower the second time, like it's a promise she personally negotiated.

Carla's going to hate the cold, love the wings and complain about both on the flight home. Ellen's going to make her stand for every Bills first down and call it hospitality. If the wind kicks up in the third quarter, Carla will finally understand why nobody up here wanted a roof.

My call: Bills 30, Dolphins 17.
