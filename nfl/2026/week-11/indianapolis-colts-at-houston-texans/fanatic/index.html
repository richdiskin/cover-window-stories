Indianapolis Colts at Houston Texans

Indianapolis Colts (3-7) at Houston Texans (3-6)

8:15 PM ET Thu Nov 19 · Reliant Stadium, Houston, TX · Prime Video

XFacebook

Indianapolis Colts vs Houston Texans on Thursday night in Week 11 lands in a building that threw the Texans a 25th birthday party and still hasn't handed them a win. Nate Coleman was at Reliant Stadium on October 4 when the old Liberty White jerseys came back out, Andre Johnson and Arian Foster waved at halftime and Dallas walked off with a 34-30 win, and Lou Ferraro has texted him that date every morning since.

Lou drove down from Indianapolis on Wednesday with a cooler of sandwiches and a printed copy of the Week 3 box score. It says Colts 19, Texans 17. He had it laminated.

Nate: You laminated a September game.

Lou: I laminate everything that goes right for us. It's a short stack.

He isn't wrong about the stack. Indianapolis is 3-7. The Colts lost four straight before Sunday, including a 42-7 day in Jacksonville that Lou refers to only as the weather event. Then they beat Miami 24-22 at Lucas Oil, and Lou walked out of that building like somebody had handed him the deed.

Nate's problem is a strange one. His team is 3-6, and all three wins came somewhere else: Tennessee, London, and a 44-22 beating of the Chargers in Los Angeles. At home it's four games and four losses, to Buffalo, Cincinnati, Dallas and the Giants. The Texans have been better tourists than hosts all year, and in a 25th season that was sold as a homecoming, that hurts.

Nate: We wore the Battle Red socks for Dallas. They invited back Billy Miller, the guy who scored the first touchdown this franchise ever had. And we lost.

Lou: Maybe stop inviting people.

What Nate does have is C.J. Stroud, and he'll talk about him until you physically leave. Stroud has 19 touchdown throws and only three interceptions. Nico Collins is the target Nate trusts on third down, and Will Anderson Jr. has 10.5 sacks, a figure Nate keeps on a sticky note on the dashboard right next to his toll tag.

Lou answers with one name. Jonathan Taylor has 739 rushing yards and eight touchdowns, and Lou's entire plan for Thursday is to hand Taylor the ball until the crowd gets quiet enough to hear the air conditioning. He doesn't enjoy talking about Daniel Jones and his 12 interceptions, so he simply doesn't.

Both teams are on four days of rest. Houston lost 27-24 in Cleveland on Sunday, the Colts squeezed past Miami by two, and nobody's legs will be fresh by the fourth quarter.

Lou: You know what I like about a Thursday game? Nobody has time to fix anything. You are what you are.

Nate: We're 0-4 at home. That's what we are.

He says it, then hears it, and hates every word.

Nate does have a theory, the kind only a man who has sat in the same section since 2002 is allowed to have. His Texans have looked loosest on the road, where nobody expected anything and Stroud could just throw. At home, with the anniversary banners and the legends and the H-Town chant, they've pressed. Thursday is just a Thursday. No ceremony, no commemorative ticket, no halftime tribute. Only the Colts.

Nate: No more birthday parties. Just football.

Lou: That's the saddest thing I've ever heard a man say about his own stadium.

Lou will sit in the visitors' corner in a horseshoe cap and hold up the laminated box score after every Taylor first down. Nate will be in his usual seat with his Battle Red socks pulled up to his knees, waiting on luck that hasn't shown up yet. For once he thinks it's his turn.

My call: Texans 26, Colts 20.
