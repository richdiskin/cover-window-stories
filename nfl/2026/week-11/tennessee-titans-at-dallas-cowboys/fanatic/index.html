Tennessee Titans at Dallas Cowboys

Tennessee Titans (1-8) at Dallas Cowboys (4-6)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 22 · AT&T Stadium, Arlington, TX · Fox

XFacebook

DeMarcus Ware will be shaking hands at AT&T Stadium on Saturday, and Cole Garrison has already decided that's the closest thing to a fourth-down stop the Cowboys will see all weekend. Pete Varga drove down from Nashville for Tennessee Titans vs Dallas Cowboys in Week 11 and got dragged straight to Rally Day, the open house the Cowboys throw in Arlington the day before every home game.

It runs 10 to 5 with free parking, stadium tours and free health screenings for anyone who registers. Cole signed them both up.

Pete: You drove me to a football stadium to get my blood pressure checked.

Cole: I drove you here so somebody could take it before Sunday. After Sunday, it's going to be a medical event.

Pete laughed, because he's 1-8 and laughing is about all a Titans fan has left in November. Tennessee hasn't won a home game all season. Its only win came in Indianapolis in October, 26-20, and Pete has the radio call saved on his phone the way other people save wedding videos. Last Sunday was a 40-23 loss to Jacksonville at home, and he left early, which he has never done in his life.

What keeps him coming back is the kid. Cam Ward has 1,714 yards and eight touchdown throws in a season where the protection has been a rumor. Tony Pollard has 628 yards and six touchdowns. Jacob Martin has 7.5 sacks, and Anthony Hill Jr. has 82 tackles, most of them earned the hard way.

Cole doesn't argue. He can't, not after the week he's had.

Dallas lost 46-40 to San Francisco on Sunday. Forty-six. At home. Cole was there, and he says it felt like watching your house get robbed while you held the door. The Cowboys are 4-6 and 1-4 at AT&T Stadium, and the only home win came in September against Washington.

Cole: We score. Nobody's ever said we don't score.

That much is true. Dak Prescott has 2,724 yards and 21 touchdown throws against six interceptions. CeeDee Lamb has 77 catches for 976 yards and eight touchdowns, and Cole talks about him like he's describing a sunset. Javonte Williams has nine rushing touchdowns. Dallas has put up 286 points in ten games.

It's the other 282 that keep Cole up at night.

Pete: You've given up 282 points.

Cole: I know what we've given up. Don't read me my own mail.

So the tour, for Cole, had a purpose. He narrated it like a museum guide for better defenses, then went quiet near the field like a man at a memorial. Ware was the headliner on Saturday for a reason. Cole has a Ware jersey he still wears to Thanksgiving, and he reminds anyone who'll listen that the Cowboys have their own holiday game coming in a few days and would like to arrive at it in a better mood.

Pete's hopes are simple. He wants Ward to have time. He wants Pollard to get 20 carries. He wants Dallas to keep giving up points the way it has, because the Titans have scored only 167 all year and need all the help they can get.

Cole's hopes are simpler. Stop somebody. Anybody. Quinnen Williams has six sacks and safety Caleb Downs has 66 tackles, and Cole says those two are the reason he still bothers to stay through the fourth quarter.

They'll go to the game together on Sunday, Pete in navy and light blue, Cole in a Ware jersey that hasn't fit right in years. Somewhere in the second half, Pete will start to think the Titans might hang around. Cole will start to think about the holiday.

My call: Cowboys 28, Titans 23.
