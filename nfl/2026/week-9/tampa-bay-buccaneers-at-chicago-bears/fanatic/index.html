Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Chicago Bears

Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-5) at Chicago Bears (6-2)

8:20 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Soldier Field, Chicago, IL · NBC

ShareXFacebook

Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Chicago Bears is Baker Mayfield and Todd Bowles against Caleb Williams and Ben Johnson, Sunday night at 8:20 Eastern from Soldier Field. Ringling Bros. is in Rosemont all weekend calling itself "The Greatest Show On Earth," and Walt Brenner would like a word with whoever wrote that sign.

Walt: The greatest show in this town was running before most of this league had a logo, and it doesn't need elephants.

Sal Ferris got in Sunday morning and Walt took him straight to the Chicago History Museum on North Clark, where the "Aquí en Chicago" exhibit was wrapping up with a closing program from noon to four. Walt figured a man from Tampa could stand to learn something about a city with real history.

Sal: You brought me to a museum on the day of a night game.

Walt: I brought you to the right building. We were here first. Red Grange. Bronko Nagurski. Eight championships between 1921 and 1963 before anybody had heard of a Super Bowl, and one more after it.

Sal: And you're going to tell me about 1985.

Walt: I'm going to tell you 1985 is one chapter. People who think it's the whole book never read the front of it.

Sal doesn't do nostalgia. Sal does accountability. When something goes wrong in Tampa, he wants a name, a decision and a bill, and this season he's got a strange one to file.

Sal: We're 3-5. We've scored 178 and allowed 151. We've outscored people and we're still two games in the hole. Who owns this? Bowles owns it. That's the job. You don't get to blame bounces when you've scored more than you've allowed.

Walt: That's a lot of words for 3-5.

Sal: The 178 and 151 are real. That's what makes me mad.

Here's what's real. Tampa Bay just shut out Atlanta 16-0, and the Buccaneers are 2-1 on the road and 1-4 at home. Chicago is 6-2, 3-1 at Soldier Field, with 214 points scored and 160 allowed, and the Bears are coming back on six days after going to Seattle on Monday night and winning 34-17.

Walt: Thirty-four in Seattle. On a Monday. Against the champs.

Sal: Your quarterback had a night. I'll give you that and nothing else.

Caleb Williams has Rome Odunze outside, Colston Loveland working the middle, and D'Andre Swift out of the backfield. Ben Johnson has given Williams a playbook with a little of everything in it, and Walt has stopped pretending he doesn't love it.

Sal's guys aren't impressed. Mayfield plays like every snap is a personal insult. Emeka Egbuka gets open fast, and Bucky Irving runs angry between the tackles. On defense, Vita Vea takes up two blockers and half the oxygen, and Antoine Winfield Jr. hits like he's being graded on it.

Sal: Lee Roy Selmon. Greatest Buccaneer. Our own Hall of Famer. Our defense still plays in his shadow and it should.

Walt: Nagurski played both ways.

Sal: Nagurski never had to deal with a Tampa August.

They walked out of the museum at four, and Walt pointed the car toward the lake. Sal asked him who owned it if the Bears lost at home on national television to a 3-5 team. Walt said nobody, because it wasn't going to happen, and that if it did, he'd blame the circus for stealing the crowd.

Sal: That's an excuse.

Walt: That's history. We were here first. We get to name the villain.

Sal said Tampa has two Super Bowls, XXXVII and LV, and nobody in Chicago has to remind him how many the Bears have since 1963. Walt said one, and that he'd like a second, and that Sunday night is a fine place to start building toward it.

My call: Bears 24, Buccaneers 20.
