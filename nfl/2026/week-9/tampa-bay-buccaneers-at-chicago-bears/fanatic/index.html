Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Chicago Bears

Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-5) at Chicago Bears (6-2)

8:20 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Soldier Field, Chicago, IL · NBC

ShareXFacebook

Walt Brenner took his grandkids to the circus on Friday night. Ringling Bros. is at Allstate Arena all weekend, and Walt figured it was a good way to spend an evening before the biggest Bears game of the season so far. The kids loved the acrobats. Walt spent the whole time talking about Caleb Williams.

Sal Ferris, his old Army buddy from Tampa, flew in Saturday morning to see the game. When Walt told him about the circus, Sal said that was a perfect warmup for watching the Bears play football.

Walt: You want to say that again in front of my wife?

Sal: I'd rather say it in front of the scoreboard.

Walt and Sal have been arguing about football since they met at basic training. Walt is Chicago through and through. He was a teenager when the Monsters of the Midway won the Super Bowl, and he can still do the whole Super Bowl Shuffle if you give him enough room. He'll tell you about Walter Payton and Mike Singletary and Dick Butkus, and he'll tell you that Chicago football is about defense, period.

Sal grew up on the creamsicle Bucs, the orange uniforms and the losing seasons. He stuck with them through the bad years and got rewarded twice. First came the Super Bowl with Jon Gruden and that defense. Then came Tom Brady and the second ring. Sal says he's earned the right to talk.

Sal: Two rings. How many you got?

Walt: One. But ours came with a song.

This year the Bears are 6-2, and Walt is having the time of his life. They just beat Seattle on Monday night, and Walt says Caleb Williams finally looks like the guy Chicago has been waiting for since the Sid Luckman days. Walt says Luther Burden III has hands like glue, and he can't stop talking about the way Burden goes up for the ball.

Sal's Bucs are 3-5, and he knows they need a win. But he says Baker Mayfield is the toughest quarterback in the league, and Bucky Irving can run through anybody. Sal saw Irving break a tackle earlier this season that he says he still dreams about.

Walt: You dream about running backs?

Sal: I dream about winning in Chicago.

On Sunday afternoon, before the game, they're going to the Chicago History Museum for the closing of "Aquí en Chicago." Walt's wife says it's the best exhibit she's seen in years, and Walt is going because she told him he was going. Sal is going because he thinks it'll be warmer inside than anywhere else in the city.

Then they'll head to Soldier Field. Walt has his old orange and navy Payton jersey and the same knit hat he's worn to every big game for twenty years. Sal has his Mayfield jersey and three layers of thermal shirts underneath, because he hasn't been north in November since basic training and he remembers how it felt.

Walt: You're going to freeze.

Sal: I've been frozen before. I'm from Tampa, not the moon.

Walt has waited his whole life to feel this good about a Bears quarterback, and he's not letting a guy from Tampa ruin it. Sal says he didn't come to ruin it. He came to watch Irving run through it.

Walt's grandkids have already picked their side. They want the Bears, the acrobats and another night at the circus, in that order. Sal says he'll settle for the circus.

My call: Bears 24, Buccaneers 20.
