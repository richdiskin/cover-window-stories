Los Angeles Rams at Washington Commanders

Los Angeles Rams (4-4) at Washington Commanders (2-4-1)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Northwest Stadium, Landover, MD · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Los Angeles Rams vs Washington Commanders, Week 9, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET on Fox from Northwest Stadium in Landover, Maryland. Matthew Stafford against Jayden Daniels. Earlier that morning in the District, the National Veterans Parade runs its 5K at 8 a.m., and Claire Benton has told everyone in her group chat that she's running it, then driving straight to the stadium.

Claire: "Structure first. Run at eight, on the road by nine, parked by ten. You can't run a Sunday without a plan."

Diane: "You're doing a 5K before a football game?"

Claire: "I'm doing a 5K so I've earned the yelling."

Claire is the Washington fan who still talks about Joe Gibbs like he could walk back in the door any minute. Three Super Bowl titles, XVII, XXII and XXVI, with three different quarterbacks. That's her argument for everything: build the structure, plug in the guy, win. She'll mention Sammy Baugh and the 1937 and 1942 championships before you've finished saying hello.

Diane Kerr flew in from Los Angeles and wants to talk about trees. Coaching trees. Her motto is "Follow the tree," and she can draw the whole Sean McVay family on a napkin, every assistant who left town to run somebody else's team. She has the history too: Super Bowl LVI at SoFi, Super Bowl XXXIV with the Greatest Show on Turf, titles in 1945 and 1951, Deacon Jones and Merlin Olsen wrecking people up front.

Diane: "Kliff Kingsbury's on our staff now. You know where he was last year?"

Claire: "Don't."

Diane: "Calling plays for your quarterback."

Claire: "Then he knows exactly how good Jayden is. Enjoy the nightmares."

The seasons haven't gone the way either of them wanted. The Rams are 4-4. Washington is 2-4-1 and hasn't won a road game. At home, though, the Commanders are 2-0-1, and Claire wants that painted on the parking lot.

Claire: "In this building we're fine. It's the airport that's killing us."

Diane: "We're 2-1 on the road. We like airports."

Claire: "You're flying more than 2,300 miles to play at ten in the morning your time."

Diane: "Stafford spent twelve years in Detroit. He's played more one o'clock games than you've had hot dogs."

Then it gets to the part they both secretly love: the coaches. Diane trusts McVay to have a script for the opening drive that leaves Washington's defense chasing ghosts. Claire trusts Dan Quinn to settle his guys down once they've seen that script one time. Daniels is the wild card for both of them. When he gets out of the pocket and takes off, the whole stadium comes off its seat, and Diane admits, quietly, that she holds her breath too.

It's that kind of matchup: the Rams have more weapons, Washington has the crowd and the quarterback who can make something out of nothing. Neither defense is giving away much on the ground, and neither of these two is giving an inch in the stands.

Here's how it goes. The Rams are the better team, and Stafford makes two throws in the second half that nobody on the Washington sideline can do a thing about. Daniels keeps it close all the way to the end, and Claire leaves saying the structure held. Diane leaves saying the tree is still growing.

My call: Rams 26, Commanders 21.
