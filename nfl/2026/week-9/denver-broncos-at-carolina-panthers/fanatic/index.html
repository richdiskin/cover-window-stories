Denver Broncos at Carolina Panthers

Denver Broncos (6-2) at Carolina Panthers (3-4)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Bank of America Stadium, Charlotte, NC · CBS

ShareXFacebook

Denver Broncos vs Carolina Panthers, Week 9, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET at Bank of America Stadium. Bo Nix brings a 6-2 Denver team to Charlotte. Bryce Young and the 3-4 Panthers have had 10 days to get ready. Mitch Kovac has had ten days to get angry.

Mitch: "Super Bowl 50. Don't."

Ruth: "I didn't say a word."

Mitch: "You're wearing the Von Miller jersey. You said it with your shirt."

Ruth Calder came in from Denver with an Elway story for every situation. Her question about any quarterback is "Where's he in the arc?" Elway lost three Super Bowls before he won two, XXXII and XXXIII, and Ruth figures every Broncos quarterback since has been somewhere on that same road. Then came 24-10 in Super Bowl 50, February 2016. She keeps Terrell Davis, Champ Bailey and Floyd Little in the conversation the way some people keep family photos in their wallet.

Ruth: "Nix is early in the arc. Second-year guys learn, third-year guys win. This is his third year. Do the math."

Mitch: "Bryce is in his fourth. Do that math."

Mitch doesn't care about arcs. He cares about the depth chart. "Show me the depth chart" is how he answers every question, including what's for lunch. He grew up on Sam Mills, Julius Peppers and Luke Kuechly, and he still leaves the room when someone brings up Super Bowl XXXVIII. He studies the two-deep like it's a tax return and he's getting audited.

Ruth spent Saturday up in Huntersville at the Carolina Renaissance Festival, because she says anybody who survived the Elway years can handle a day around people in armor. Mitch skipped it. He spent Saturday walking past the Carolinas Veterans Day Festival at Romare Bearden Park and then sat home watching tape. The Ally Tipoff is uptown at the Spectrum Center on Sunday afternoon, South Carolina against North Carolina and NC State against Kentucky in women's college basketball, so Charlotte gets a full day of yelling no matter what happens at the stadium.

Then they get into the actual teams, which is where it gets loud.

Mitch: "We've scored 178. You've scored 172 in one more game. Whose offense is the scary one?"

Ruth: "The one that's 6-2."

Mitch: "We won the division last year."

Ruth: "So did we. And we've been perfect at home."

Mitch: "This isn't your home."

Ruth: "Fine. We're 2-2 away. I'll take Sean Payton's game plan against anybody's on the road. The man has a play for every situation, and three backups for every play."

Mitch: "Dave Canales has Ejiro Evero running our defense, and Evero coached in your building. He knows where you keep the spare keys."

That last one lands, and Ruth knows it. Both of them also know the ugly truth: these defenses give up points. Denver has allowed 165. Carolina has allowed 193. Neither of them expects a shutout, and both of them are already nervous about the fourth quarter.

Here's how it goes. Sunday stays tight into the final minutes. Young keeps Carolina right there, the stadium stays loud the whole way, and Mitch spends most of the second half on his feet screaming about tackling angles. But Nix makes the late throws a third-year quarterback is supposed to make, and Ruth gets to say the word "arc" about forty more times on the drive home.

My call: Broncos 24, Panthers 22.
