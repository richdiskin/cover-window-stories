Cincinnati Bengals at Atlanta Falcons

Cincinnati Bengals (5-2) at Atlanta Falcons (3-5)

9:30 AM ET Sun Nov 8 · Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid, Spain · NFL Net

ShareXFacebook

Cincinnati Bengals vs Atlanta Falcons in Week 9 goes to Madrid, and the first thing both fan bases will see in town is a ring from every Super Bowl ever played, lined up in a glass case inside the NFL House at the Casa de las Alhajas on Plaza de San Martín. Laura Kessel and Russ Amundsen walked in together on Friday, found the Lombardi Trophy, counted every ring from the first one forward, and came up empty for both of their teams.

Laura: Not one. We came four thousand miles to look at other people's jewelry.

Russ: Speak for yourself. I've looked at other people's jewelry my whole life. I'm a Falcons fan.

The building makes it worse. The Casa de las Alhajas was a nineteenth-century pawn-and-savings bank, a place where people in Madrid brought their valuables in and hoped to get them back someday. Russ stood beneath that ceiling and thought about February in Houston and a lead he still won't say out loud. Laura thought about three Super Bowl trips and three long flights home. They agreed not to talk about it, which lasted roughly eleven seconds.

Then it was down to Plaza de España, where the league set up flag football for kids and a Bengals Menina, one of those big painted Meninas statues Madrid puts around the city, dressed in tiger stripes. Laura took a picture with it. Russ took a picture of Laura taking a picture with it and sent it to his brother in Marietta with no caption.

Laura reads quarterbacks the way some people read weather, and she's been reading Joe Burrow since LSU. She'll tell you about his footwork on third down before she'll tell you the score. Cincinnati is 5-2, 3-1 on the road, with 206 points scored, and last Sunday at home against Tennessee, Burrow threw five touchdowns and then ran in the winner himself in overtime. She watched the whole fourth quarter standing up.

Laura: When Joe's feet are quiet, the ball comes out on time and Ja'Marr Chase is already turning upfield. That's the whole scouting report. Everything else is paperwork.

Russ: Paperwork is my department.

He means it. Russ does division math for fun. Atlanta is 3-5, and he can walk you through every way 3-5 in the NFC South turns into a January home game, with a pencil, on the back of a napkin. The trouble is last week. The Falcons went to Tampa Bay in the rain and didn't score a point. Sixteen to nothing, and the Buccaneers won it without completing a single throw. Russ called it a rough afternoon. Laura called it a shutout, because it was.

Russ: Michael Penix Jr. has a bigger arm than anyone on this continent.

Laura: He threw four of them to the other team in the rain.

Russ: The rain's gone. Bijan Robinson is the best back in the building. We scored 171 in eight games. I'm not here to apologize. I'm here to get to four wins.

The ground game is where Russ sees daylight. Atlanta runs for 4.8 yards a carry, and Cincinnati allows 5.0. The Bengals run for only 3.8 against an Atlanta defense that gives up 4.6. That's Bijan's whole argument, and it's a good one: hand it to him until the Bengals prove they can stop it.

Laura's answer is the same answer she always has. Burrow doesn't need a run game to beat you. He needs one clean pocket a quarter and Chase on a slant.

Sunday morning, before the 3:30 local kickoff, the Falcons fans are marching the Dirty Birds Fan Walk from the Paseo de la Castellana up to the Bernabéu, and Russ plans to be in the front row. Laura has already told him she'll be waiting at the gate.

The Bernabéu has seen bigger nights than this one. It hasn't seen two fan bases this hungry for a ring they could actually bring home and keep. Whoever wins walks back past the Casa de las Alhajas on Monday knowing it's still a long way to that glass case.

Russ thinks Bijan keeps it close all afternoon. Laura thinks Burrow gets the last word, because he usually does.

My call: Bengals 25, Falcons 23.
