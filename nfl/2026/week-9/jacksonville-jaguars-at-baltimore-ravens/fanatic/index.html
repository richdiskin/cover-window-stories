The Fanatic · NFL

Jacksonville Jaguars at Baltimore Ravens

Jacksonville Jaguars (5-2) at Baltimore Ravens (4-4)

8:15 PM ET Thu Nov 5 · M&T Bank Stadium, Baltimore, MD · Prime Video

ShareXFacebook

Owen Pratt was pressed against the glass at MICA's Pinkard Gallery on Mount Royal Avenue a full hour before "Globe Posters for the People" opened on Thursday. The show celebrates the old Baltimore print shop whose fluorescent posters used to scream fight nights and soul revues off every telephone pole in this town, and Owen wanted one with Joe Fagnano's name on it, in letters big enough to read from the upper deck.

Ivy Corcoran, up from Duval in a teal toboggan, read the first poster in the window out loud. "One Night Only." She said that sounded about right for whoever plays quarterback in Baltimore this week.

Owen: Laugh all you want. This town won a Super Bowl with Trent Dilfer.

Ivy: This town won a Super Bowl with a defense that ate people, and a quarterback who handed off and got out of the way.

Owen: Exactly. That's the plan for Thursday. Hand off and get out of the way.

Owen grades every Ravens season with one question: who's hitting people? Ray Lewis and Ed Reed are in Canton, and to Owen they're the measuring stick for every defense this town puts on the field. Lamar Jackson hurt his thumb on the first snap in Buffalo and is headed to injured reserve. Tyler Huntley hurt his toe later that same afternoon. The official injury report lists both of them Out. Owen took that news the way his old man took the Mayflower trucks leaving for Indianapolis: in dead silence, then at full volume for about a week.

Ivy doesn't do feelings. She does standings, folded in her jacket and pulled out like a badge. Jacksonville is 5-2. The Jaguars have given up 111 points in seven games, which she has circled twice in red marker, and they sit on top of the AFC South. Baltimore is 4-4 and 1-2 at home.

Ivy: You want a poster? Print the standings. Big letters. Fluorescent.

Owen: You've been in first place for a month and you act like you invented it.

Ivy: I've been waiting since Tony Boselli was a rookie. I earned the attitude.

Jacksonville fans sat through the years with tarps on the upper-deck seats. Ivy watched every snap of them, so Ravens history doesn't scare her. She just asks what he's done lately.

What Baltimore has lately is Derrick Henry, and Owen will ride that horse until it drops. The Ravens run for 4.8 yards a carry. Ivy fires back that Jacksonville's front gives up only 3.4, and that the Jaguars run for 4.6 against a Baltimore defense that allows 3.8. Thursday turns into a shoving match up front, and neither one wants a track meet.

Owen: Kyle Hamilton is going to play like his hair is on fire. Roquan Smith too. A short week is a street fight, and we're a street-fight town.

Ivy: Your secondary is held together with tape. Hawkins is out. Wiggins is questionable.

Owen: So is your guy Walker. Rib. And Tuten. I read the report too.

Ivy gave something up to be here, and she wants credit for it. Back home, JJ Grey & Mofro are playing the Jax Amphitheater on Thursday night at the Greater Jacksonville Agricultural Fair, out at the fair's new home on Normandy Boulevard. She traded swamp rock and a fried-everything food row for a cold parking lot on Russell Street and a Ravens fan who wants to talk about Dilfer.

Owen: You'll catch the fair next week. My quarterback might not catch a break all month.

Ivy: First honest thing you've said since I landed.

The way Owen sees it, Henry gets his carries, Fagnano hits a couple of throws that shake the building, and Hamilton keeps it ugly enough that the fourth quarter is still a fight. The way Ivy sees it, Trevor Lawrence finds one more drive than Baltimore can answer and she flies home with the standings sheet still folded neatly in her pocket.

Owen buys a poster before kickoff anyway. It says BALTIMORE in screaming orange, and he swears it'll look even better in January.

My call: Jaguars 25, Ravens 21.
