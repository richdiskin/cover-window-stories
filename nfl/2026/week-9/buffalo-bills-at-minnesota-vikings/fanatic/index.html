Buffalo Bills at Minnesota Vikings

Buffalo Bills (6-1) at Minnesota Vikings (6-1)

8:15 PM ET Mon Nov 9 · U.S. Bank Stadium, Minneapolis, MN · ESPN, ABC

ShareXFacebook

Buffalo Bills at Minnesota Vikings is Josh Allen and Joe Brady against Kyler Murray and Kevin O'Connell, Monday night at 8:15 Eastern on ESPN and ABC from U.S. Bank Stadium. Two teams at 6-1, a national audience, and two fans who refuse to get excited about any of it until the calendar flips to January.

Ellen Marsh flew in Saturday and Erik Dahl took her straight to Huntington Bank Stadium for Gophers and UCLA, an 11 a.m. kickoff on Salute to Service day. Erik wanted her to see what a Minnesota crowd sounds like in the cold. Ellen wanted to know when the real game was.

Ellen: Wake me in January.

Erik: It's November.

Ellen: Then I'm early.

Right before kickoff the student section did the ROW chant, oars in the air, and Ellen admitted it was louder than she expected. Erik said wait until Monday.

Ellen doesn't clap for division titles anymore. Buffalo has piled up enough of them that she treats them like utility bills. The last time the Bills finished a season as champions was 1965, the second of two straight AFL titles after 1964, and she has spent every year since asking out loud what it's going to take.

Erik is the same animal in a different jersey. Minnesota won the 1969 NFL championship and hasn't won a Super Bowl since. He gives credit for every fall win and saves his trust for January.

Erik: Seven games is a rumor. A longer one than five, but a rumor.

Ellen: Ours is a rumor with 233 points in it.

That's the Bills right now. They're 6-1, 3-0 on the road, with 233 points scored and 181 allowed, and they just beat Baltimore 36-10. Minnesota is also 6-1, 3-0 at home, with 140 scored and 93 allowed. The one loss came at Detroit, 19-15, and Erik has been chewing on it all week.

Erik: Ninety-three points in seven games. That's Brian Flores. That's a defense that shows up every week.

Ellen: It's a rumor, Erik.

Erik: It's a very well-coached rumor.

Ellen's case is the obvious one, and she makes it like she's bored of making it. Allen is the whole show. James Cook takes the hits so Allen doesn't have to, DJ Moore wins outside, and Dalton Kincaid finds the soft spot in the middle every time a defense leaves one.

Erik's answer has more names in it. Murray can hurt you with his arm or his legs. Justin Jefferson is still the guy every defensive coordinator plans around. T.J. Hockenson works the middle, and Aaron Jones still runs like a man ten years younger than he has any right to.

Ellen: Ten years younger is a strong claim.

Erik: I'll take it back. He runs young. That's all I'm saying.

Both teams had eight days to get ready. Both quarterbacks can turn a broken play into a highlight. And both of these fans have watched a great fall turn into a cold January more times than they want to count.

Monday, before kickoff, the Blind Boys of Alabama are doing "Songs in the Key of Life" at the Dakota on Nicollet Mall, a Gospel Music Hall of Fame group singing on a football night. Erik said if anybody can get a Vikings fan to hope out loud, it's them. Ellen said she'd hope in January, and only after the coin toss.

Erik: Seven games, Ellen.

Ellen: Wake me in January, Erik.

My call: Bills 24, Vikings 21.
