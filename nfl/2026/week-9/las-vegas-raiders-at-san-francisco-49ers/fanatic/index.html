Las Vegas Raiders at San Francisco 49ers

Las Vegas Raiders (4-4) at San Francisco 49ers (6-1)

4:25 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, CA · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Vic Moreno has one question for every game on the schedule, and he asked it before he set his bag down in Santa Clara: who's across the field? This week it's San Francisco, at Levi's Stadium, 4:25 p.m. Eastern on Fox, Kirk Cousins and Klint Kubiak against Brock Purdy and Kyle Shanahan. Call it the Battle of the Bay, even with one side living in the desert now. Vic doesn't care where the Raiders sleep. He cares who they're playing, and this one counts double.

Nora Haskell found him Saturday after she'd spent the afternoon at CEFCU Stadium watching San Jose State and Northern Illinois. She was still in a teaching mood.

Nora: Count the rings, Vic. Five. The Catch started it. Montana to Dwight Clark, 58 seconds left, 1981 NFC title game. Bill Walsh went 10-4 in the playoffs.

Vic: Three in eight seasons. XI, XV, XVIII. Jim Otto, Art Shell and Ted Hendricks are in Canton. And nobody had to catch anything in the back of an end zone to get us started.

Nora: Our last ring was the 1994 season. I know. I count that one too. I count Super Bowl LIV against Kansas City, 31-20, every time somebody tells me an NFC title means something.

Vic: See, that's where we agree. Kansas City is the only name I put above yours.

That's the whole difference between them. Nora keeps score in rings and nothing else. Vic keeps score in grudges, and beating the right opponent in a lost season means more to him than beating a stranger in December. Sunday, both of them are getting what they want: a real opponent with a real record.

The Niners are 6-1, and nobody has won in their building yet, four tries and four losses. The 49ers have scored 197 points and allowed 139, and they sat home last weekend with their feet up. Las Vegas is 4-4, and the Raiders' split is the strangest in the building: 1-3 at home, 3-1 on the road. They went to New Jersey last Sunday and won 38-33.

Nora: You gave up 33 to the Jets.

Vic: And scored 38. Next question.

Nora: Your home record.

Vic: We play angry in other people's stadiums. Ask anybody who's hosted us.

The Raiders' case runs through two young guys who don't scare easy. Brock Bowers catches everything near him, and Ashton Jeanty runs like the ground personally offended him. Cousins has been around long enough to know when to get the ball to both. And then there's Maxx Crosby.

Vic: Crosby is the measuring stick for every defensive end in our division. This week he gets to measure somebody from another one.

Nora's answer is a roster built to make that measuring stick look short. Purdy distributes it. Christian McCaffrey gets it in space. George Kittle blocks like a lineman and runs like a receiver, and Mike Evans gives Purdy a big target outside. On defense, Nick Bosa comes off the outside and Fred Warner seems to be standing wherever the ball ends up.

Nora: Shanahan's had the week off to draw this up. That's a scary thing for a visiting team.

Vic: Shanahan's had a lot of weeks. You still counted five rings, not six.

Nora: That's a fair shot.

Vic: That's the only kind I take.

The 49ers have the record and the rest. The Raiders have the road form and a defensive end who makes quarterbacks hurry. Nora will be in her seat early, counting. Vic will be across the aisle, looking at the other sideline and reminding everyone nearby exactly who's across the field.

My call: 49ers 31, Raiders 20.
