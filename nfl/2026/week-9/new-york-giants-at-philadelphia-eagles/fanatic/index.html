The Fanatic · NFL

New York Giants at Philadelphia Eagles

New York Giants (4-2-1) at Philadelphia Eagles (2-6)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Lincoln Financial Field, Philadelphia, PA · Fox

XFacebook

Ray Donnelly ran the Rocky Steps Challenge at the Philadelphia Museum of Art on Saturday morning, and when he got to the top, he turned around, threw both arms in the air and yelled something about the Giants that can't be printed. A guy from New Jersey a few steps below him yelled something right back.

That guy was Leon Drobny, who came down the Turnpike on Friday to stay with Ray, his old college roommate, for the weekend. They have been fighting about this game since the first week of freshman year.

Leon: You know Rocky lost the first fight, right?

Ray: He went the distance. That's the point. Philly goes the distance.

Leon: Philly is 2-6.

Ray: Philly is a mood, not a record.

Ray is the kind of Eagles fan who takes every insult personally and every loss as a crime against the city. He was there the night the Philly Special won the Super Bowl against New England, and he tells that story every time somebody brings up a bad season, which is lately. He grew up worshipping Reggie White and Brian Dawkins, and he believes any defense that doesn't hit like Dawkins is just a group of guys standing around.

Leon is a Giants fan, which means he carries around a different kind of grudge. Four Super Bowl titles, two of them against the Patriots, one with a guy catching a ball against his helmet. Lawrence Taylor. Michael Strahan. Eli Manning, who Leon still defends like a family member.

Leon: You booed Santa Claus.

Ray: Santa had it coming. Ask anybody who was there.

This week is weird for both of them, because neither quarterback is the guy they expected. Philadelphia benched Tanner McKee on Wednesday and handed Joe Milton his first career start with Jalen Hurts out, and J.J. McCarthy is starting for New York in place of Jameis Winston. Ray calls it the backup bowl. Leon calls it a chance for McCarthy to prove something.

Ray has a bigger worry. DeVonta Smith is out with a hamstring injury, Saquon Barkley has no game designation, and Ray has been pacing his kitchen ever since. Barkley is the one guy on the team who makes Leon nervous, because Barkley used to be a Giant, and Leon has never forgiven him for leaving.

Leon: If Saquon plays, I'm booing him.

Ray: If Saquon plays, you're going to be booing him from the ground.

The Giants are 4-2-1 and coming off a bye, so they're rested and Leon is feeling cocky. New York runs for 4.2 yards a carry, and Philadelphia's defense gives up 4.4. Leon thinks the Giants just run it right down the middle all day.

Ray thinks the same about his own team. The Eagles only run for 3.8 a carry, but the Giants give up 5.0, and Ray says that means somebody in green is going to find a crease. He doesn't care who.

Ray: You come into my building with a backup quarterback and expect the crowd to be polite?

Leon: I expect the crowd to be loud for ten minutes and then start booing its own team.

Ray didn't argue that. It's happened before.

Sunday morning, before they head to the stadium, Ray is taking Leon for a cheesesteak in South Philly. Leon has been warned that if he orders it wrong, Ray will leave him on the sidewalk. Leon has been practicing the order in the mirror.

Ray says the Linc is going to shake for a kid making his first start. Leon says he's heard the Linc shake at its own guys plenty. One of them rides home quiet, and Leon is already rehearsing the grin he'll wear up the Turnpike.

My call: Giants 22, Eagles 21.
