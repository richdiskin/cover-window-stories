Houston Texans at Los Angeles Chargers

Houston Texans (2-5) at Los Angeles Chargers (0-7)

4:05 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · SoFi Stadium, Inglewood, CA · CBS

ShareXFacebook

Houston Texans vs Los Angeles Chargers, Week 9, Sunday at 4:05 p.m. ET on CBS at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood. C.J. Stroud against Justin Herbert. A 2-5 team against an 0-7 team. Marty Szabo has the tape cued up on a laptop in the parking lot, and he's not taking questions about the record.

Marty: "Roll the tape. Not the scoreboard. The tape."

Nate: "The tape says oh and seven, Marty."

Marty: "The tape says we played the Rams in our own stadium and it counted as a road game. That's not football. That's paperwork."

Marty is a Chargers lifer who goes back further than most people in Los Angeles realize this team does. He'll tell you about the 1963 AFL championship and Lance Alworth before you've parked. Ron Mix. Dan Fouts. The AFC title in the 1994 season. He keeps a running list of every bad bounce since the San Diego days, and it's long enough to need a table of contents.

Nate Coleman flew in from Houston with one rule: pressure tells the truth. Put a defender in a quarterback's face and Nate wants to know what happens next. He's been a Texans fan since the very first season in 2002, and his Hall of Fame list is short: Andre Johnson. He'll talk about Andre for an hour if you let him, and he won't let you leave.

Nate: "We're 0-4 at home and 2-1 on the road. I'm starting to think the problem is Houston."

Marty: "You want to switch? We've got road games at home. Best of both worlds."

The two of them have been friends since a Texans trip out here years ago, and the insults have only gotten sharper.

Nate: "We had the week off. DeMeco Ryans had two weeks to get the front ready. Stroud got a breather. Our guys are coming to hit people."

Marty: "Jim Harbaugh has been waiting all season for somebody to hit back. That man would coach a fistfight if they let him."

Nate: "You've scored 99 points in seven games."

Marty: "Quality points. Handcrafted."

Marty's real hope is the offense. Mike McDaniel is running it now, and Marty swears it looks better on film than it does on the scoreboard. Herbert still makes throws nobody else in the league tries. The problem is everything around those throws, and Marty knows it.

Marty: "Every week I watch the tape, and every week I see the play that should've changed it. One block. One catch. That's it."

Nate: "That's what every 0-7 guy says."

Marty: "And every 2-5 guy flies 1,375 miles to say it to my face."

The Clippers play the Pelicans at Intuit Dome that night, so Inglewood stays busy after the final gun. Marty plans to head across the way and watch somebody else's team for a change. Nate is already planning his victory lap through the parking lot.

Nate: "Pressure tells the truth. Sunday, the truth comes off the corner with bad intentions."

Marty: "Roll the tape on Monday. We'll see who's telling the truth."

Here's how Sunday shakes out. The stadium finally gets a home winner, sort of, except it's the visitors. Nate's front gets home enough times to make him unbearable for a week. Herbert keeps the Chargers in it until late, and Marty goes home to roll the tape, because he swears the tape looked better than the score.

My call: Texans 23, Chargers 19.
