Cleveland Browns at New Orleans Saints

Cleveland Browns (5-3) at New Orleans Saints (2-5)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Caesars Superdome, New Orleans, LA · CBS

XFacebook

Frank Haller showed up at Beignet Fest in City Park on Saturday wearing a brown and orange stocking cap in Louisiana. He had beignet dust on it within ten minutes. He said that was the most Cleveland thing that ever happened to him, and Andre Thibault agreed.

Andre: You came to New Orleans in a winter hat.

Frank: I came to New Orleans in a lucky hat. Different thing.

Andre has lived in the city his whole life and has worn a paper bag to at least one Saints game, which he admits only when pressed. He remembers the years when the team was a joke, and he remembers the night the Superdome reopened after the storm, when Steve Gleason blocked that punt and the whole building shook. That night, he says, is why New Orleans will never quit on this team.

Frank respects that, because Frank's team has broken his heart in more ways than he can count. He grew up on stories about Jim Brown and Otto Graham, who won championships before Frank was born. He lived through the Drive and the Fumble, then watched the team leave town and come back as something unrecognizable. The Browns have never played in a Super Bowl. Frank never forgets it, and he never lets anybody else forget it either.

Frank: You've got a ring. Drew Brees. Sean Payton. The onside kick in Miami. I'd trade my car for that.

Andre: Your car isn't worth much.

Frank: Neither is your defense.

That one stung. New Orleans is 2-5, and the Saints haven't won a home game all season. Andre has stopped explaining it. He just shakes his head and points at the roof.

Cleveland, meanwhile, is 5-3 and unbeaten at home, and Frank is enjoying every minute of it, even if the Browns win games in the ugliest way possible. He calls them the Kardiac Kids, after the old Brian Sipe teams that kept winning in the last minute and nearly gave everybody in Ohio a heart attack.

Frank's real worry came on Wednesday. Deshaun Watson is inactive, Maliek Collins is out, and Quincy Williams has no game designation. Coach Todd Monken has named Shedeur Sanders the starter. Frank refreshed his phone so many times on the streetcar that a stranger asked if he was okay.

Andre: You're worried about one guy.

Frank: Andre, I'm a Browns fan. I'm worried about everything.

Andre has his own list. Alvin Kamara sat out Wednesday too, and Andre needs Kamara the way the city needs a working pump station. He believes Tyler Shough is the guy, and he thinks Chris Olave is going to make Cleveland's secondary look silly.

The way Frank sees it, the Browns run right at the Saints. Cleveland goes for 4.1 yards a carry, and New Orleans has been giving up 5.1. Andre fires back that the Browns defense gives up 5.0, so the Saints can run too, even though they only average 3.5.

Andre: Both of these teams can run, and neither one can stop it. That's a long afternoon for the defenses.

Frank: That's a long afternoon for your fans.

Sunday morning they're hitting the Oak Street Po-Boy Festival before the game, and Andre has already planned the route. Roast beef first, then shrimp, then something fried that Frank won't be able to pronounce. Frank says he'll eat anything as long as it's not served by somebody wearing a Saints jersey. Andre says that's going to make for a hungry day.

Frank: You're lucky Cleveland doesn't have food like this.

Andre: Cleveland has pierogi and lake effect snow. I've seen the brochure.

Frank isn't taking the hat off until the Browns lose, and he hasn't figured out what happens if they win. Andre told him that if Cleveland steals one in the dome, he's going to make him wear it straight through Mardi Gras.

My call: Saints 22, Browns 21.
