Green Bay Packers at New England Patriots

Green Bay Packers (5-3) at New England Patriots (5-3)

4:25 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Gillette Stadium, Foxborough, MA · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Glen Tolliver got to Patriot Place Plaza on Saturday early enough to stake out a spot right up front for Rob Gronkowski's Patriots Hall of Fame induction. He wore the old number 87 jersey, the one with the grass stain he refuses to wash out, and he spiked an imaginary football every time the crowd chanted Gronk's name.

Paul Lindgren, up from Green Bay for the weekend, stood next to him in a Packers parka and politely clapped for a tight end who used to run through his team's secondary like it owed him rent.

Glen: Nine seasons. Three rings with us. The most fun player this franchise ever had.

Paul: He was fun. I'll give you fun. Now ask me how many championships Green Bay has.

Glen: Please don't.

Paul: Thirteen.

Paul counts titles the way some guys count push-ups, out loud and with his chest out. He goes back to Curly Lambeau, then Vince Lombardi and Bart Starr on the frozen field, then Brett Favre and Aaron Rodgers. To Paul, the Packers aren't a franchise. They're a town that happens to own a football team, and every fan in it acts like a part owner because a lot of them are.

Glen has his own numbers, and they're more recent. Six Super Bowl titles in New England, all of them in the lifetime of anybody younger than forty. He was in the stands for the snow games, the comebacks and the parades through Boston. He doesn't care about leather helmets. He cares about what happened on his watch.

Glen: You've got history. We've got the receipts that still have ink on them.

Paul: Your receipts all have the same two names on them.

That's the shot every Patriots fan hears eventually, and Glen has an answer ready. This team is 5-3 and 3-0 at home with Drake Maye throwing it and Mike Vrabel on the sideline. Different names, same building, same attitude.

The Packers are 5-3 too, and Paul likes how fresh they are. Green Bay played last Thursday, so the Packers walk into Foxborough with ten full days of rest. Jordan Love has had time to heal up and get his reads right, and Paul thinks that matters more than anything said at a podium this week.

Glen's worry list is longer. A.J. Brown, TreVeyon Henderson, Hunter Henry and Christian Barmore all sat out Wednesday's practice, and Glen has refreshed the team site so often his thumb hurts. He keeps telling himself it's one practice.

Paul: Your best receiver, your back, your tight end and your big guy up front. That's not a practice report. That's a cry for help.

Glen: It's Wednesday. Ask me on Sunday.

Neither one expects a shootout. Green Bay runs for 3.1 yards a carry, and the Patriots aren't much better at 4.4, with both defenses giving up 4.0. Glen sees a slugfest decided by whoever wins the turnover battle. Paul sees Love hitting one deep shot to change everything.

After the induction, they wandered past the Bass Pro Shops to see Santa's arrival at 5 o'clock, because Glen's nephew made him promise. Paul stood in a crowd of kids and told every one of them that Santa is a Packers fan. Glen told every one of them that Santa lives in the North Pole, which is basically New England.

Paul: He comes down chimneys in Wisconsin first. Everybody knows that.

Glen: He'd freeze to death in Green Bay before he got to the second house.

Glen is still riding the Gronk weekend and says nothing can bring him down. Paul says that's fine. He'll be the guy in the parka in the third quarter, clapping politely again, only this time for his own team.

My call: Patriots 24, Packers 21.
