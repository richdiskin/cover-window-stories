Dallas Cowboys at Indianapolis Colts

Dallas Cowboys (3-5) at Indianapolis Colts (2-6)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Lucas Oil Stadium, Indianapolis, IN · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Dallas Cowboys vs Indianapolis Colts in Week 9 arrives the same weekend the Indianapolis Ballet stages its first-ever company Cinderella at Clowes Hall with the Indianapolis Symphony Orchestra in the pit, and Lou Ferraro has decided that's not a coincidence. A 2-6 team, a famous visitor rolling up in a shiny coach, and a clock everybody's watching. Cole Garrison, down from Dallas with a carry-on full of team gear, points out that in this version the Colts are the ones sweeping up.

Lou: Cinderella was nobody at the ball until she wasn't. That's all I'm saying.

Cole: Cinderella had a fairy godmother. You've got Daniel Jones.

That stings, because it's true. Jones threw two interceptions in the forty-two to seven beating at Jacksonville last week and got pulled late for Anthony Richardson, and on Monday Shane Steichen said Jones keeps the job against Dallas. Lou took that news the way he takes everything: with a history lesson.

Lou measures every Colts season against the whole family tree, not just the Indianapolis branch. He'll start with Johnny Unitas and the Baltimore years, walk through the Mayflower trucks leaving in the snow in the spring of nineteen eighty-four, and land on Peyton Manning and the Lombardi in Miami before you can stop him. Continuity is his religion. Teams that keep their quarterback, keep their coach and keep their nerve win in the end. Teams that panic in November stay 2-6.

Lou: Steichen didn't blink. I respect that. Panic is how you end up shopping for a quarterback every March.

Cole lets that one go, because Cole's whole life is the gap between the Dallas brand and the Dallas results. He can tell you exactly how many people in Mexico, Japan and Ohio own a Cowboys hoodie, and he can tell you how many of those people have seen Dallas reach a conference title game since the Aikman years. The first number is enormous. The second one isn't.

Last Sunday was the brand-versus-results problem in one afternoon. Dallas led Arizona by 14 in the fourth quarter and lost it, with Gardner Minshew bringing the Cardinals all the way back. Cole spent the flight to Indianapolis staring at the seatback in front of him. Dallas is 3-5 now, with 219 points scored and 213 allowed, which means the offense shows up every week and the defense keeps handing it back.

Cole: Dak Prescott is fine. CeeDee Lamb is fine. George Pickens is fine. We scored plenty. We just keep giving it away in the fourth quarter like it's a door prize.

Lou: In Indy we'd call that a Sunday.

Here is where Lou actually has something. Neither defense stops the run. Dallas gives up 5.2 yards a carry, and Indianapolis gives up 5.2 too. The Colts run for 4.2 and the Cowboys run for only 3.8, so whichever back gets loose first sets the tone. Lou wants the Colts to stop asking Jones to win it with his arm and just keep handing it off into a defense that hasn't proven it can tackle.

The Colts are 1-2 at Lucas Oil and have scored 161 points in eight games, which Lou admits is nowhere near the Manning years. He also notes that 161 points scored and 222 allowed still make for a team that's been in more fights than its record says. Cole notes that it's still 2-6.

The weekend around the game helps Lou's mood. Christmas Nights of Lights opens Friday at the State Fairgrounds, and Sunday is the last day of Heroes from the Heartland at the Indiana State Museum, the exhibit honoring Hoosier veterans, which Lou plans to walk through with his grandson before kickoff. Cole asked if they make the Cowboys' fans pay extra for parking. Lou said only when they lose.

For one Sunday afternoon, Lucas Oil gets to be the ballroom. Lou thinks Jones finally keeps the ball in his own hands for four quarters and the Colts' run game punishes a defense that blew a 14-point lead a week ago. Cole thinks the clock strikes twelve on Indy the way it always does.

Lou says this time the slipper fits.

My call: Colts 27, Cowboys 24.
