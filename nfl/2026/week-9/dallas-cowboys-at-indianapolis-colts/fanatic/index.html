The Fanatic · NFL

Dallas Cowboys at Indianapolis Colts

Dallas Cowboys (3-5) at Indianapolis Colts (2-6)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 8 · Lucas Oil Stadium, Indianapolis, IN · Fox

ShareXFacebook

Lou Ferraro bought two tickets to Cinderella at Clowes Hall for Friday night, the Indianapolis Ballet's company premiere with the symphony playing live, and he wants everyone to know it wasn't his idea. His wife picked the show. Lou chose the seats, because Lou chooses everything.

Cole Garrison drove up from Dallas on Thursday and immediately asked where the nearest decent barbecue was. Lou handed him a pork tenderloin sandwich the size of a hubcap and told him to stop complaining.

Cole: You're going to the ballet two days before a football game.

Lou: I'm going to watch a girl in rags win when nobody gives her a chance. It's research.

That's the Colts fan's whole mindset this season. Indianapolis is 2-6. Lou has accepted it the way he accepts snow in March, with a lot of grumbling and no plans to leave. But his team just lost by five touchdowns in Jacksonville, and he figures the bottom has already fallen out. There's nowhere left to go but up.

Cole lives on the other end of the emotional scale. Dallas is America's Team, and Cole believes every Cowboys game is a national event. He brings up the five Super Bowl trophies, Roger Staubach, Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith and Michael Irvin, and he walks like a guy who personally won all of them.

Lou: Five trophies, and the most recent one is older than half the players on your roster.

Cole: At least we have trophies with names people remember.

Lou: Peyton Manning. Reggie Wayne. Marvin Harrison. Dwight Freeney. Want me to keep going?

Cole: No.

Lou has his own Super Bowl memory, the rainy night in Miami with Manning, and he plays it in his head every time a Dallas fan starts bragging. Indianapolis knows how to win the big one. It just hasn't done it in a while.

Cole's real problem is Arizona. Last Sunday the Cowboys had a two-touchdown lead in the fourth quarter and gave the whole thing away. Cole hasn't fully recovered. He keeps saying Dak Prescott didn't lose that game. Lou keeps asking who did.

Cole: CeeDee Lamb is the best receiver on the field Sunday. Write it down.

Lou: Jonathan Taylor is the best player on the field Sunday. I already wrote it down and framed it.

The ground game is where Lou thinks his team takes control. The Colts run for 4.2 yards a carry against a Dallas defense that gives up 5.2. Lou sees Taylor carrying it thirty times and Daniel Jones keeping the chains moving with short throws. Cole says Dallas only runs for 3.8 a carry but doesn't need to, because Prescott throwing to Lamb is the best plan in football.

Lou: Your plan was the best plan in football right up until the fourth quarter last week.

Cole stood up, walked around the block and came back.

Saturday they hit the Indiana State Museum, where the Heroes from the Heartland exhibit closes Sunday. Cole wanted to see it because his grandfather served. Lou took him, bought the tickets and didn't say one word about football for an entire hour. That's how Hoosiers treat a guest. Then they drove past the State Fairgrounds, where the Christmas Nights of Lights opened Friday, and Lou went right back to trash talking.

Lou: You see those lights? That's what our stadium looks like when Taylor breaks a long one.

Cole: That's what your stadium looks like from the parking lot when people leave early.

Cole swears this is the week Dallas finally closes one out. Lou says he's heard that speech every fall since he was ten. Win or lose, Cole is eating another tenderloin sandwich on the way out of town, and he's never going to admit he likes it.

My call: Colts 27, Cowboys 24.
