Indianapolis Colts at Jacksonville Jaguars

Indianapolis Colts (2-5) at Jacksonville Jaguars (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · EverBank Stadium, Jacksonville, FL · CBS

Indianapolis Colts vs Jacksonville Jaguars is an AFC South game in Week 8, Daniel Jones and Jonathan Taylor against Trevor Lawrence and a rested Jaguars team coming off its bye, 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS at EverBank Stadium. Downtown Jacksonville warms up Saturday with Treats at the Plaza at Riverfront Plaza on the St. Johns, and Lou Ferraro arrives from Indianapolis, where the Irvington Halloween Festival filled the old east-side neighborhood this month, ready to tell Ivy Corcoran that history outweighs her splits.

Ivy Corcoran trusts records and splits and almost nothing else, and she opened with both.

Ivy: Jacksonville is 4-2, 3-1 at home and 1-1 on the road, with 154 points scored and just 104 allowed in six games. Indianapolis is 2-5, 1-2 at home and 1-3 away, with 154 scored and 180 allowed. That's the conversation, Lou.

Lou: Same points scored. I'll take it as a compliment.

Ivy: We did it in one fewer game.

Lou insists the Colts are one franchise from Baltimore to Indianapolis, and he will argue it at any tailgate in Duval County. The Colts make their second straight road trip here, but his history travels with him: the NFL championship in the fifty-eight season, the one people still call the greatest game ever played, with Raymond Berry catching everything.

Lou: Three NFL championships before the merger. Super Bowl V and Super Bowl XLI. Art Donovan, Marvin Harrison, Dwight Freeney, Eric Dickerson, Marshall Faulk. I don't start counting when the moving trucks crossed into Indiana.

Ivy: Tony Boselli. One Hall of Famer, and he's the one this franchise points to first. We're younger. We're still writing our list.

Lou: In pencil.

Ivy: And you keep yours in an attic.

Lou: That one landed.

Lawrence started the Jaguars' last game, at home against Houston, before the bye. Jones started the Colts' last game, at Minnesota.

Shane Steichen runs Indianapolis, with Lou Anarumo calling a 4-3 defense and Jim Bob Cooter running the offense. Liam Coen runs Jacksonville, with Anthony Campanile calling a 4-3 and Grant Udinski on offense. The toughest group in this game is Jacksonville's run defense, allowing only 3.3 yards a carry, and Indianapolis runs for 4.2. Going the other way, Jacksonville runs for 4.3 against an Indianapolis defense allowing 5.1, which is the split Ivy circled first.

Ivy: Taylor against 3.3. That's the matchup, Lou. Your best player against our best group.

Lou: Jonathan Taylor finds cracks in walls. It's what he does.

In the pocket, the Colts' quarterbacks have been sacked about once every fourteen dropbacks, and Jacksonville's rush gets home about once every twenty-three. Jacksonville's blockers allow a sack about once every nineteen dropbacks, and the Colts' rush arrives about once every seventeen.

Ivy: Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen will find Jones. And Travis Hunter is on my depth chart. Sit with that.

Lou: DeForest Buckner and Laiatu Latu will visit Lawrence. Sauce Gardner will shadow whoever you throw to.

Ivy: Duval will be loud. I don't do Duval magic, though. I do splits.

Wednesday's official practice report was short on drama: no game statuses yet and no quarterback on it. Gardner and Tyler Warren were limited for Indianapolis, and Jakobi Meyers was limited for Jacksonville.

Lou: Would you count the Baltimore years if you were me?

Ivy: I'd count anything that helped my argument. Honest answer.

Lou: Finally, a numbers person who admits it.

The treats at Riverfront Plaza run out Saturday evening. Ivy figures Jacksonville saved the rest for the Colts, and Lou figures his team will bring its own.

My call: Jaguars 28, Colts 20.
