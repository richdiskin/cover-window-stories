Indianapolis Colts at Jacksonville Jaguars

Indianapolis Colts (2-5) at Jacksonville Jaguars (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · EverBank Stadium, Jacksonville, FL · CBS

Sunday at EverBank Stadium in Jacksonville, 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS, the first Sunday back on standard time. It's an AFC South game. Indianapolis comes in at 2-5 on its second straight road trip. Jacksonville is 4-2 and coming off a bye. Lou Ferraro insists the Colts are one franchise from Baltimore to Indianapolis, and he'll fight you on it. Ivy Corcoran trusts records and splits and almost nothing else.

Lou: It's one franchise, Ivy. Baltimore Colts, Indianapolis Colts. Same team. The greatest game ever played, the NFL championship in the fifty-eight season, Raymond Berry catching everything. That's our history.

Ivy: The record is the argument, Lou. Your team is 2-5. My team is 4-2. That's the whole conversation.

Lou: Three NFL championships before the merger, Ivy. Super Bowl V and Super Bowl XLI. Art Donovan. Marvin Harrison. Dwight Freeney. Eric Dickerson. Marshall Faulk. I don't start counting when the team got to Indianapolis.

Ivy: Tony Boselli. One Hall of Famer, and he's the one this franchise points to first. We're younger, Lou. We're still writing our list.

Lou: You're writing it in pencil.

Ivy: And you're writing yours in an attic.

Lou: That's fair. That one hurt.

The season so far: Jacksonville is 4-2, 3-1 at home and 1-1 on the road, with 154 points scored and just 104 allowed in six games. Indianapolis is 2-5, 1-2 at home and 1-3 on the road, with 154 scored and 180 allowed. Trevor Lawrence started the Jaguars' last game, at home against Houston before the bye. Daniel Jones started the Colts' last game, at Minnesota.

Ivy: Same points scored, Lou, and we've played one fewer game. The record is the argument.

Lou: Same points scored. I'll take it as a compliment.

The matchup: Shane Steichen runs Indianapolis, with Lou Anarumo calling a defense that sets up in a 4-3 base and Jim Bob Cooter calling the offense. Liam Coen runs Jacksonville, with Anthony Campanile calling a 4-3 and Grant Udinski calling the offense. Jacksonville's run defense is the toughest number here, allowing only 3.3 yards a carry, and the Colts run for 4.2. Jacksonville runs for 4.3 against an Indianapolis defense allowing 5.1.

In the pocket, the Colts' quarterbacks have been sacked about once every fourteen dropbacks, and Jacksonville's rush gets home about once every twenty-three. Jacksonville's blockers allow a sack about once every nineteen dropbacks, and the Colts' rush gets there about once every seventeen.

Lou: Jonathan Taylor, Ivy. He'll find a crack in that wall.

Ivy: Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen will find Daniel Jones first. And Travis Hunter is on my depth chart. Think about that.

Lou: DeForest Buckner and Laiatu Latu will be visiting Trevor. And Sauce Gardner will be shadowing whoever you throw it to.

Ivy: Duval will be loud, Lou. But I don't do Duval magic. I do splits.

The Wednesday report is out, and it's short on drama: no game statuses yet, no quarterback on it. Sauce Gardner and Tyler Warren were limited for Indianapolis, and Jakobi Meyers was limited for Jacksonville.

Lou: Last thing. Ivy, would you ever count the Baltimore years if you were me?

Ivy: Lou, I'd count anything that helped my argument. That's the honest answer.

Lou: Finally, a numbers person who admits it.

Respect to both: a Jacksonville defense that has allowed just 104 points in six games, an Indianapolis team still carrying one of the oldest histories in the sport, and a division Sunday in Florida where the rested team gets the first word.

My call: Jaguars 28, Colts 20.
