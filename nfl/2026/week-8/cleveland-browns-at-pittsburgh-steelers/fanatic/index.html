Cleveland Browns at Pittsburgh Steelers

Cleveland Browns (5-2) at Pittsburgh Steelers (5-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Acrisure Stadium, Pittsburgh, PA · CBS

Sunday at Acrisure Stadium in Pittsburgh, 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS, the first Sunday back on standard time. Both teams are 5-2. Both are unbeaten at home. Frank Haller will remind you that Cleveland was the gold standard of professional football before the Super Bowl existed. Ed Kowalski measures every franchise by one thing: how long it keeps its people.

Frank: Look before nineteen sixty-five, Ed. Four AAFC championships in a row, then four NFL championships. Cleveland ran this league while your team was still waiting on its first title.

Ed: Noll had one job, Frank. Ever. Chuck Noll coached the Steelers for more than two decades, and it was the only head coaching job he ever had. Six Super Bowls: IX, X, XIII, XIV, XL and XLIII.

Frank: Six is a lot. I'll give you six. But I won't let anybody call Cleveland the Factory of Sadness. We had a dynasty. Look it up.

Ed: The Immaculate Reception, Frank. Fourth down, twenty-two seconds left, trailing by a point against the Raiders. And history changes forever.

Frank: You always bring that up.

Ed: Because it's the best thing that ever happened. And the Steel Curtain team in the bicentennial season started one and four, then posted five shutouts in its last nine games. Five shutouts, Frank.

Frank: Impressive. Still not eight championships.

Ed: Mel Blount. Terry Bradshaw. Alan Faneca. Dermontti Dawson. I can keep going until kickoff.

The season so far: Cleveland is 5-2, 3-0 at home and 2-2 on the road, with 133 points scored and 146 allowed. Pittsburgh is 5-2, 3-0 at home and 2-2 on the road, with 153 scored and 138 allowed. Deshaun Watson started the Browns' last game, at Tennessee. Aaron Rodgers started the Steelers' last game, at New Orleans. Cleveland makes its second straight road trip.

Ed: Your team has allowed more than it scored, Frank, and it's 5-2. That's not a dynasty. That's a lucky penny.

Frank: Wins are wins, Ed. Nobody asks how.

The matchup: Todd Monken runs Cleveland, with Mike Rutenberg calling a defense that sets up in a 4-3 base. Mike McCarthy runs Pittsburgh, with Patrick Graham calling a 3-4. Pittsburgh runs for 5.0 yards a carry against a Cleveland defense allowing 4.6. Cleveland runs for 4.2 against a Steelers defense allowing only 3.9.

The pocket is a mosh pit on both sides. Cleveland's quarterbacks have been sacked about once every ten dropbacks, and Pittsburgh's rush gets there about once every fourteen. Pittsburgh's quarterbacks go down about once every thirteen dropbacks, and Cleveland's rush gets home about once every thirteen.

Ed: T.J. Watt and Cameron Heyward, Frank. Your quarterback will hear footsteps in his sleep.

Frank: Jared Verse and Mason Graham will be visiting yours. And Denzel Ward will be waiting for anything thrown his way.

Ed: Patrick Queen in the middle. He likes this rivalry.

Frank: Everybody in Cleveland likes this rivalry, Ed. We just like it more when we win it.

The Wednesday report is out, and the editor allowed exactly one look at it. T.J. Watt, Cameron Heyward and Jalen Ramsey all sat out Pittsburgh's practice, Cleveland's Jared Verse sat out too, and nobody in this game has a game status yet.

Frank: Last thing, Ed. Mike McCarthy. How long does he get?

Ed: If he stays as long as Noll, Frank, I'll be very old and very happy.

Respect to both: two teams that have been perfect at home, two defenses that hunt quarterbacks, and an old rivalry in Pittsburgh where every yard is going to cost something.

My call: Steelers 22, Browns 19.
