Minnesota Vikings at Detroit Lions

Minnesota Vikings (6-0) at Detroit Lions (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Ford Field, Detroit, MI · Fox

Sunday at Ford Field in Detroit, 1 p.m. Eastern on Fox, the first Sunday back on standard time. Minnesota arrives at 6-0. Detroit is 4-2 and unbeaten at home. Erik Dahl is the man who refuses to get excited about any Vikings start before January. Martin Sorensen believes in exactly one thing: that a team's window doesn't stay open forever, and Detroit's is open right now.

Martin: Erik, you're 6-0. Smile. Just once. For me.

Erik: Five games is a rumor, Martin. Six games is a slightly louder rumor. I've seen this film before. We won the NFL championship in the nineteen sixty-nine season and haven't been back to the top since. Three NFC titles in the seventies. No Super Bowl trophies. I'll smile in January.

Martin: The window is a clock, Erik. You don't wait for January. You win now, because the clock doesn't care how you feel. Detroit won four NFL championships, the last one in nineteen fifty-seven, and I am done waiting for the fifth.

Erik: Four championships, none since the Eisenhower years. And you call me the gloomy one.

Martin: Back-to-back NFC North titles, Erik. Dan Campbell turned this place around. Nobody in this building is just happy to be here anymore.

Erik: Fran Tarkenton, Paul Krause, Warren Moon. And yes, Brett Favre is on our Hall of Fame list. Don't tell the folks in Green Bay.

Martin: Lem Barney. Dutch Clark. Jack Christiansen. Old-school Lions who'd have tackled your quarterback for fun.

The season so far: Minnesota is 6-0, 3-0 at home and 3-0 on the road, with 125 points scored and only 74 allowed in six games. Detroit is 4-2, 3-0 at home and 1-2 on the road, with 184 scored and 165 allowed. Kyler Murray started the Vikings' last game, at home against Indianapolis. Jared Goff started the Lions' last game, at home against Green Bay.

Martin: Three and oh at Ford Field, Erik. We don't lose here this year.

Erik: Three and oh on the road. We don't lose anywhere. Which, again, is a rumor.

The matchup: Kevin O'Connell runs Minnesota, with Brian Flores calling a defense that sets up in a 3-4 base. Dan Campbell's Lions play a 4-3 base for Kelvin Sheppard, with Drew Petzing calling the offense. On the ground, it's dead even. Minnesota runs for 4.3 yards a carry and Detroit for 4.4, and both defenses allow 4.2.

The pocket is where it gets wild. Minnesota's rush gets a sack about once every nine dropbacks, and Detroit's blockers give one up about once every twenty-one. But Minnesota's quarterbacks have been sacked about once every ten dropbacks, and Detroit's rush gets there about once every thirteen. Both quarterbacks should bring a helmet with extra padding.

Erik: Andrew Van Ginkel and Dallas Turner on the outside, Martin. Your quarterback will see purple in his sleep.

Martin: Aidan Hutchinson will see yours first. And when Goff has a second, he's finding Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta and Jahmyr Gibbs.

Erik: Justin Jefferson would like a word.

Martin: Justin Jefferson can have a word. We'll have the scoreboard.

Neither one gets to talk injuries. The Week 8 report isn't out yet, and we took their phones at the door.

Erik: Last thing, Martin. If we win Sunday, it's seven games. Still a rumor.

Martin: If you win Sunday, I'm going to need a long walk by the river. The clock is ticking, Erik.

Erik: It always is. That's why I don't celebrate in November.

Respect to both: an unbeaten Minnesota defense that hasn't blinked, a Detroit team that hasn't lost at home, and a division Sunday indoors in Detroit that should come down to the last possession.

My call: Lions 23, Vikings 22.
