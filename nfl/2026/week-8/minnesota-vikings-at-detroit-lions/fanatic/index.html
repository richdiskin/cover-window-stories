Minnesota Vikings at Detroit Lions

Minnesota Vikings (6-0) at Detroit Lions (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Ford Field, Detroit, MI · Fox

Minnesota Vikings vs Detroit Lions is the unbeaten Vikings, 6-0 with Kyler Murray, against Jared Goff's Lions, 4-2 and unbeaten at Ford Field, in Week 8 at 1 p.m. Eastern on Fox. Downtown Detroit spends the same weekend hosting Youmacon at Huntington Place, so Erik Dahl, who left a Minneapolis Halloween weekend headlined by Wobbleween at the Armory, will share downtown with anime fans in full costume and pretend he isn't enjoying it, while Martin Sorensen insists the only costume that matters Sunday is Honolulu blue.

Martin Sorensen met him outside the stadium.

Martin: Erik, you're 6-0. Smile once. The cosplayers are smiling, and they don't even have a team in this.

Erik: Six games is a slightly louder rumor than five. We won the NFL championship in the nineteen sixty-nine season, made three NFC title runs in the seventies and have never won a Super Bowl. I'll smile in January.

Erik refuses to get excited about any Vikings start before January, and he has history on his side. Martin runs on one idea: a team's window doesn't stay open forever, and Detroit's is open right now.

Martin: The window is a clock. You win now. Detroit won four NFL championships, the last in nineteen fifty-seven, and I'm done waiting for the fifth.

Erik: None since the Eisenhower years, and I'm the gloomy one.

Martin: Back-to-back NFC North titles. Dan Campbell turned this place around.

Erik offered Fran Tarkenton, Paul Krause, Warren Moon and, quietly, Brett Favre, who sits on the Vikings' Hall of Fame list too. Martin answered with Lem Barney, Dutch Clark and Jack Christiansen.

Minnesota has scored 125 points and allowed only 74, going 3-0 at home and 3-0 on the road. Detroit has scored 184 and allowed 165, 3-0 at home and 1-2 away. Murray started the Vikings' last game, at home against Indianapolis. Goff started the Lions' last game, at home against Green Bay.

Martin: Nobody wins here this year.

Erik: Nobody beats us anywhere. Also a rumor.

Kevin O'Connell runs Minnesota, with Brian Flores calling a defense that sets up in a 3-4 base. Campbell's Lions play a 4-3 for Kelvin Sheppard, with Drew Petzing calling the offense. The ground game is dead even: Minnesota runs for 4.3 yards a carry, Detroit for 4.4, and both defenses allow 4.2.

So this one gets decided in the pocket, where the numbers turn wild. Minnesota's rush gets a sack about once every nine dropbacks, and Detroit's blockers allow one about once every twenty-one. Minnesota's quarterbacks, though, have been sacked about once every ten dropbacks, and Detroit's rush gets there about once every thirteen.

Erik: Andrew Van Ginkel and Dallas Turner coming off the outside. Goff is going to see purple in his sleep.

Martin: Aidan Hutchinson sees Murray first. And when Goff gets a second, he has Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta and Jahmyr Gibbs.

Erik: Justin Jefferson would like a word.

Martin: He can have a word. We'll keep the scoreboard.

Then Martin read Wednesday's official practice report and lost some of his swagger. Hutchinson, Penei Sewell, St. Brown and Gibbs all sat out for Detroit. Aaron Jones sat out for Minnesota. Nobody has a game status yet, and Erik was asked not to gloat. He is a Vikings fan; he doesn't know how.

Martin: If all four of mine are out there Sunday, this building gets loud enough to rattle the convention center.

Erik: And if they aren't?

Martin: Then I take a long walk by the Detroit River and come back angry.

Erik: If we win, it's seven games. Still a rumor.

Martin: The clock is ticking, Erik.

Erik: It always is. That's why I don't celebrate in November.

Youmacon packs up Sunday evening a few blocks away. One of these two fan bases will be leaving downtown in a much better mood than the other.

My call: Lions 23, Vikings 22.
