Tennessee Titans at Cincinnati Bengals

Tennessee Titans (1-6) at Cincinnati Bengals (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Paycor Stadium, Cincinnati, OH · CBS

Sunday at Paycor Stadium, 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS, the first Sunday back on standard time. Tennessee comes in at 1-6. Cincinnati is 4-2. Pete Varga judges the Titans by growth, not wins, which is a handy philosophy at 1-6. Laura Kessel thinks every football question is a quarterback question, and she has the clipboard to prove it.

Laura: Pete, start with the quarterback. Always start with the quarterback. We have Joe Burrow. You have a young man named Cam Ward who started your last game against Cleveland. Discuss.

Pete: Growth, not grades, Laura. Cam Ward is the reason I wake up on Monday mornings with hope. You don't judge a rebuild by October.

Laura: I judge everything by October. October is when the quarterback shows you who he is.

Pete: Then let me show you who this franchise is. Two AFL championships, the first two ever played, back when we were the Houston Oilers. Earl Campbell. Ken Houston. Mike Munchak. George Blanda.

Laura: Three AFC titles for Cincinnati, Pete. And Anthony Muñoz, Forrest Gregg and Dick LeBeau. And Ken Riley, sixty-five career interceptions, finally voted into the Hall of Fame decades after he should have been.

Pete: Charlie Joiner.

Laura: Charlie Joiner is ours too, Pete. He's on both lists. We share him like a custody arrangement.

Pete: Then we get him on Sundays.

The season so far: Cincinnati is 4-2, 3-1 on the road, 1-1 at home, with 163 points scored and only 122 allowed in six games. Tennessee is 1-6, 0-4 at home, 1-2 on the road, with 108 scored and 156 allowed. Burrow started the Bengals' last game, at Baltimore. Ward started the Titans' last game, at home against Cleveland.

Pete: Our one win came on the road, Laura. These guys like a change of scenery.

Laura: Paycor is lovely this time of year. Bring a coat.

The matchup: Both defenses set up in a 4-3 base. Robert Saleh runs Tennessee, with Gus Bradley calling the defense and Brian Daboll calling the offense. Zac Taylor runs Cincinnati, with Al Golden on defense and Dan Pitcher on offense. Neither team runs the ball well. Cincinnati gets 3.8 yards a carry against a Tennessee defense allowing 4.4. Tennessee gets 4.0 against a Cincinnati defense allowing 4.9.

Laura: See? Everything is a quarterback question. When neither team can run, the guy throwing it decides the afternoon. Ja'Marr Chase is on my depth chart, and Joe knows where to find him.

Pete: Jeffery Simmons is on mine. And he knows where to find Joe.

That brings us to the pocket, which is where Pete gets nervous. Cincinnati's rush gets a sack about once every ten dropbacks. Tennessee's blockers give one up about once every twenty-three dropbacks, which is respectable. Tennessee's rush gets home about once every fourteen dropbacks, and Cincinnati's protection allows a sack about once every twenty-three.

Pete: Once every ten, Laura. That's a lot of knocks for a young quarterback.

Laura: Growth, not grades, Pete. Getting knocked down and getting back up is the growth part.

Pete: You've been waiting all week to use that on me.

Laura: Since Monday.

The Wednesday report is out, and the house rule still holds: stick to the sheet, no rumors. Nobody in this game has a game status yet, and both quarterbacks are clear. Ja'Marr Chase did not practice, which made Laura very quiet for about four seconds. Tyjae Spears sat out for Tennessee too.

Pete: Last thing. When Joe Burrow is your answer, what's the question?

Laura: Every question, Pete. That's the point. But even I'll admit 3.8 yards a carry means Joe has to carry the whole thing on his arm.

Respect to both: a young Tennessee team still finding itself on the road, a Cincinnati team whose defense has quietly done its job, and a November Sunday by the river where the quarterbacks get the last word.

My call: Bengals 27, Titans 18.
