Tennessee Titans at Cincinnati Bengals

Tennessee Titans (1-6) at Cincinnati Bengals (4-2)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Paycor Stadium, Cincinnati, OH · CBS

Tennessee Titans vs Cincinnati Bengals is Cam Ward against Joe Burrow in Week 8, 1 p.m. Eastern at Paycor Stadium on the first Sunday back on standard time. Cincinnati spends the weekend at the zoo's HallZOOween and at TQL Stadium, where FC Cincinnati plays the Philadelphia Union on Saturday, while Pete Varga's East Nashville neighbors in Five Points will still be sweeping up after All Hallows East, and Pete has come to tell Laura Kessel's Bengals that growth beats a better record.

Pete: Laura, I left a street party for this. Costumes, live music, the whole neighborhood out until ten. I'm here because I'm committed to growth.

Laura Kessel, who has believed for years that every football question is really a quarterback question, did not look up from her notes.

Laura: You're here because Burrow is here. Sit down. Tennessee is 1-6, 0-4 at home and 1-2 on the road. Cincinnati is 4-2, 3-1 away and 1-1 in this building, with 163 points scored and only 122 allowed.

Pete: And 108 scored, 156 allowed for us. I know the figures. Our one win came on the road, which is why I'm cheerful in your parking lot.

Burrow started the Bengals' last game, at Baltimore. Ward started the Titans' last game, at home against Cleveland. Pete judges the Titans by growth rather than wins, which is a handy philosophy at 1-6, and Ward is the whole reason he keeps the philosophy.

Pete: You don't judge a rebuild by October.

Laura: I judge everything by October. That's when a quarterback tells you who he is.

The family histories make a better argument than the standings do. Pete gets two AFL championships, the first two ever played, from the Houston Oiler years, plus Earl Campbell, Ken Houston, Mike Munchak and George Blanda. Laura answers with three AFC titles and Anthony Muñoz, Forrest Gregg and Dick LeBeau, then adds Ken Riley and his sixty-five career interceptions, voted into the Hall of Fame decades late.

Pete: Charlie Joiner.

Laura: Ours too. We split him like a custody arrangement.

Pete: Then we get him on Sundays.

Both defenses set up in a 4-3 base. Robert Saleh runs Tennessee, with Gus Bradley calling the defense and Brian Daboll the offense. Zac Taylor runs Cincinnati, with Al Golden on defense and Dan Pitcher on offense. Neither club runs it well: Cincinnati gets 3.8 yards a carry against a Tennessee defense allowing 4.4, and Tennessee gets 4.0 against a Cincinnati defense allowing 4.9.

Laura: When neither team can run, the man throwing it decides the afternoon. That's not a slogan, Pete, that's arithmetic.

Pete: Jeffery Simmons knows where to find your man throwing it.

That's the part that worries Pete. Cincinnati's rush gets a sack about once every ten dropbacks. Tennessee's blockers allow one about once every twenty-three, which is respectable, and Tennessee's rush gets home about once every fourteen against a Cincinnati front that allows one about once every twenty-three.

Pete: Once every ten is a lot of knocks for a young quarterback.

Laura: Getting knocked down and getting up again is the growth part. Your word, not mine.

Pete: You've been saving that since Monday.

Wednesday's practice report kept both quarterbacks clear, and nobody in this game has a game status yet. Ja'Marr Chase did not practice, which made Laura very quiet for about four seconds. Tyjae Spears sat out for Tennessee.

Laura: If Chase can't go, Joe has to carry 3.8 yards a carry on his arm. I'll admit that much.

Pete: Then I'll admit one thing back. I'd trade the whole Five Points party for one Ward touchdown drive at the end of this one.

Laura: Stick to the sheet, Pete. No rumors. House rule.

The zoo's pumpkins will be gone by Monday. Laura expects Tennessee's road optimism to be gone first.

My call: Bengals 27, Titans 18.
