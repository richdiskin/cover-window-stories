Baltimore Ravens at Buffalo Bills

Baltimore Ravens (4-3) at Buffalo Bills (5-1)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Highmark Stadium, Orchard Park, NY · CBS

Sunday at Highmark Stadium, 1 p.m. Eastern on CBS, the first Sunday back on standard time. Baltimore arrives at 4-3. Buffalo is 5-1 and coming off a bye. Owen Pratt judges every Ravens team by its defense first. Ellen Marsh judges every Bills team by January, and only January. Put them in the same parking lot and you get a tailgate with a debate club attached.

Owen: Ellen, I'll start where Baltimore always starts. Defense signs the checks. Super Bowl XXXV, the defense that won it, Ray Lewis in the middle and nobody scoring. We have two Lombardi trophies, XXXV and XLVII. Count them.

Ellen: Two is a fine number, Owen. Wake me in January. Buffalo won the AFL championship twice, back to back, and then we went to four straight Super Bowls with Thurman Thomas, Andre Reed and Bruce Smith. Four AFC titles in a row.

Owen: And how did those four Sundays in late January go?

Ellen: You know exactly how they went. That's why I don't throw parades for division titles. We've won five AFC East crowns in a row and I didn't buy one hat.

Owen: Ed Reed would have bought you the hat. Then intercepted it.

The season so far: Buffalo is 5-1, 3-0 on the road, 2-1 at home, with 197 points scored and 171 allowed in six games. Baltimore is 4-3, 3-1 on the road and 1-2 at home, with 169 scored and 167 allowed. Josh Allen started Buffalo's last game, in Week 6 at Las Vegas, before the bye. Tyler Huntley started Baltimore's last game, at home against Cincinnati, but Lamar Jackson was a full participant in Wednesday's practice and is expected back at quarterback.

Ellen: Two weeks of rest, Owen. Joe Brady had the whole staff in the building drawing up plays while your guys were still icing their knees from Cincinnati.

Owen: Rest is nice. Hitting people is nicer. Jesse Minter and Anthony Weaver have this defense set up in a 3-4 base, and Roquan Smith sits right in the middle of it.

Ellen: We're a 3-4 base too, Owen. Jim Leonhard runs ours. Same blueprint, different zip code.

The matchup: Both teams run the ball, and both defenses have been stingy against it. Buffalo runs for 5.4 yards a carry, and Baltimore allows 3.9. Baltimore runs for 4.8 a carry, and Buffalo also allows 3.9. Something has to give, and both of these insiders are certain it's the other guy's front.

Owen: Derrick Henry is on my depth chart, Ellen. You know what he does to defenses in the fourth quarter.

Ellen: I know what James Cook does to defenses in the first quarter. That's when the game gets decided in our house.

The pocket tells a different story. Baltimore's quarterbacks have been sacked about once every twelve dropbacks, and that's the stat that keeps Owen awake. Buffalo's rush has been quieter, getting home about once every twenty-five dropbacks. Buffalo's blockers allow a sack about once every fifteen dropbacks, and Baltimore's rush gets there about once every sixteen.

Owen: Once every twelve. I'll admit it. My Ravens are supposed to give those out, not collect them.

Ellen: Greg Rousseau and Bradley Chubb are happy to help you collect more.

The Wednesday report is out, and Owen read it twice. The good news for Baltimore: Lamar Jackson practiced fully. The less good news: Roquan Smith, Kyle Hamilton and Calais Campbell all sat out. Nobody in this game has a game status yet. Ellen pointed out that Buffalo's list is mostly limited practices. Owen pointed out that nobody asked.

Owen: Fine. Last thing, Ellen. When Buffalo finally wins one, will you celebrate, or ask about next year?

Ellen: I'll celebrate for exactly one night. Then I'll ask about next year. That's how finishers think.

Owen: That's how Baltimore thinks every year, Ellen. We just get to do the celebrating part.

Respect to both: a Bills team that has been a road warrior, a Ravens team that has been at its best away from home too, and a Sunday in Orchard Park that will feel like January a few weeks early.

My call: Bills 29, Ravens 24.
