Las Vegas Raiders at New York Jets

Las Vegas Raiders (3-4) at New York Jets (2-5)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, NJ · Fox

Sunday at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, 1 p.m. Eastern on Fox, the first Sunday back on standard time. Las Vegas comes in at 3-4 after a cross-country flight. The Jets are 2-5. Vic Moreno decides what every Raiders game means by checking who's on the other sideline. Mike Lasker trusts nothing the experts agree on, and he has Super Bowl III tattooed on his heart as proof.

Vic: Who's across the field, Mike? The Jets. No offense, but this isn't the Chiefs. Nobody in Las Vegas is circling this one.

Mike: Good. The experts are usually wrong, Vic. Everybody thought we had no chance in Super Bowl III. Joe Namath said we'd win, and we won. That's the whole philosophy.

Vic: Three Super Bowls in eight seasons, Mike. XI, XV and XVIII. Jim Otto, Art Shell, Gene Upshaw, Ted Hendricks, Willie Brown. We have enough Hall of Famers to field a defense.

Mike: Curtis Martin. Don Maynard. And yes, Brett Favre played for us. He's on our list too. Everybody claims Favre.

Vic: And we've packed the vans a few times. Oakland, Los Angeles, Oakland, Las Vegas. I don't use it as an excuse. A football team is a football team wherever the stadium is.

Mike: Our stadium is in New Jersey and our name says New York. We're the last people who'll lecture you about geography.

The season so far: Las Vegas is 3-4, 1-3 at home and 2-1 on the road, with 183 points scored and 197 allowed. The Jets are 2-5, 1-2 at home and 1-3 on the road, with 137 scored and 150 allowed. Kirk Cousins started the Raiders' last game, at home against the Rams. Geno Smith started the Jets' last game, at home against Miami.

Vic: Two and one on the road, Mike. My guys like a trip.

Mike: Your guys allow 197 points. They like a trip, and so do the other team's running backs.

The matchup: Klint Kubiak runs Las Vegas, with Rob Leonard calling a defense that sets up in a 3-4 base. Aaron Glenn runs the Jets, with Brian Duker on defense in a 4-3 base and Frank Reich calling the offense. The run game is a struggle on both sides. Las Vegas runs for 3.2 yards a carry against a Jets defense allowing 4.3. The Jets run for 3.9 against a Raiders defense allowing 5.2, which is the softest number in this matchup.

In the pocket, it's a tale of two quarterbacks. The Raiders protect well, allowing a sack about once every twenty-eight dropbacks. The Jets do not, allowing one about once every eleven. The Raiders' rush gets home about once every fourteen and a half dropbacks, and the Jets' rush about once every twelve.

Vic: Maxx Crosby is going to live in your backfield, Mike.

Mike: Demario Davis and Minkah Fitzpatrick are going to make Kirk Cousins think twice about throwing the ball to Brock Bowers.

Vic: Ashton Jeanty is on my depth chart. Think about that one.

Mike: Breece Hall and Garrett Wilson are on mine. I think about them constantly.

No injury talk, as always. The Week 8 report isn't out, and we confiscated their phones at the gate.

Mike: Last thing, Vic. Everybody says this game doesn't matter. That's exactly why it will.

Vic: Everybody says that about every Jets game, Mike.

Mike: And everybody is usually wrong. Super Bowl III. Look it up.

Respect to both: a Raiders team that plays its best football on the road, a Jets defense that has kept games close all year, and a Sunday in New Jersey that only a contrarian could love.

My call: Jets 24, Raiders 21.
