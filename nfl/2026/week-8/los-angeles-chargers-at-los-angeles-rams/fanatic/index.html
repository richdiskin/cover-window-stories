Los Angeles Chargers at Los Angeles Rams

Los Angeles Chargers (0-6) at Los Angeles Rams (3-4)

4:05 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · SoFi Stadium, Inglewood, CA · Fox

Sunday at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, 4:05 p.m. Eastern on Fox, the first Sunday back on standard time. The Chargers are 0-6 and coming off a bye. The Rams are 3-4. Both teams call SoFi home, so the only real road trip is from one locker room to the other. Marty Szabo watches Chargers film the way a detective watches security footage. Diane Kerr reads every Rams roster like a family tree.

Marty: Roll the tape, Diane. I've watched all six games. Twice. I'm not angry. I'm taking notes.

Diane: Follow the tree, Marty. Sean McVay built this staff, and you can trace every branch. Chris Shula on defense, Nathan Scheelhaase on offense, Kliff Kingsbury as assistant head coach. That's how you win two Super Bowls, XXXIV and LVI.

Marty: We won the AFL championship in the sixty-three season. Lance Alworth. Dan Fouts. Ron Mix. And Johnny Unitas is on our Hall of Fame list, which nobody remembers but me.

Diane: Deacon Jones, Marty. Merlin Olsen. Isaac Bruce. Kevin Greene. Eric Dickerson. Marshall Faulk.

Marty: Deacon Jones is on our list too, Diane. We share him. We share everything. We share a parking lot.

Diane: And now we share an Aaron Donald story. He retired, and he came back this year. He's on my depth chart again.

Marty: I saw. I rolled that tape too. I didn't enjoy it.

And Marty refuses to say the phrase same old Chargers. He'll talk about every missed block on film, but he won't say that.

The season so far: The Rams are 3-4, 1-3 at home and 2-1 on the road, with 170 points scored and 159 allowed. The Chargers are 0-6, 0-3 at home and 0-3 on the road, with 90 scored and 151 allowed. Justin Herbert started the Chargers' last game, at Kansas City in Week 6. Matthew Stafford started the Rams' last game, at Las Vegas.

Diane: Two weeks off, Marty. I'd guess Jim Harbaugh ran a practice that felt like training camp.

Marty: Jim Harbaugh runs every practice like training camp. And Mike McDaniel had two weeks to draw up something new.

The matchup: Both defenses set up in a 3-4 base. Jim Harbaugh runs the Chargers, with Chris O'Leary on defense and Mike McDaniel on offense. The Rams run for 5.0 yards a carry against a Chargers defense allowing 4.3. The Chargers run for 4.2 against a Rams defense allowing 4.2.

The pocket tells the story Marty keeps rewinding. The Chargers' quarterbacks have been sacked about once every ten dropbacks, while the Rams' rush gets home about once every twenty-three. The Rams' blockers allow a sack about once every twenty-six dropbacks, and the Chargers' rush gets there about once every seventeen.

Marty: Khalil Mack and Derwin James Jr., Diane. They'll be in Stafford's kitchen.

Diane: Aaron Donald and Kobie Turner will be in Herbert's living room. And Puka Nacua will be wherever your safeties aren't.

Marty: Ladd McConkey and Omarion Hampton. Somebody on our team will score. I'm almost sure.

Diane: Trent McDuffie would like to discuss that with Ladd.

No injury talk this week. The Week 8 report isn't out, and both insiders were told to keep the rumors on the cutting-room floor.

Marty: Last thing, Diane. If we win this one, I'm framing the tape.

Diane: If you win this one, Marty, I'm following the tree all the way back to see where we went wrong.

Respect to both: a Chargers team with two weeks to find its first win, a Rams team that has played its best football on the road, and a Sunday afternoon where the home team and the visitors use the same front door.

My call: Rams 26, Chargers 18.
