Arizona Cardinals at Dallas Cowboys

Arizona Cardinals (2-5) at Dallas Cowboys (3-4)

1:00 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · AT&T Stadium, Arlington, TX · Fox

Sunday at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, 1 p.m. Eastern on Fox, the first Sunday back on standard time. Arizona brings a 2-5 record. Dallas is 3-4. Neil Carver measures every Cardinals team against the club that won the NFC title in the oh-eight season, the franchise's only trip to a Super Bowl. Cole Garrison separates the Cowboys business from the Cowboys football team, and he'll tell you the business is winning.

Cole: Neil, welcome to Arlington. Look up. Take it all in. Try not to stare at the scoreboard.

Neil: Show me the comparison to the oh-eight team, Cole. That's all I ask of any Cardinals team. Does it look like the group that went to the Super Bowl? This one doesn't yet, and I'll say it before you do.

Cole: Valuation isn't a trophy, Neil. I'll say that before you do. We have five Super Bowls: VI, XII, XXVII, XXVIII and XXX. The last one was XXX, and I am painfully aware of how long ago that was.

Neil: We have two NFL championships from before the Super Bowl existed. I don't use them as proof of anything modern. But I do enjoy pointing out that Jim Thorpe is on our Hall of Fame list.

Cole: Troy Aikman, Tony Dorsett, Michael Irvin, Bob Lilly. I could fill this stadium with Hall of Famers. I just can't fill the trophy case lately.

Neil: Night Train Lane, Cole. Charley Trippi. We were playing football before you had a logo.

Cole: And Tom Landry, our first head coach, coached here for twenty-nine seasons and created the 4-3 defense everybody copied. Funny thing is, we play a 3-4 now.

That last part is technically true, and Neil enjoys it a little too much. Arizona sets up in a 4-3 base. Dallas, the franchise that gave football the 4-3, plays a 3-4.

The season so far: Dallas is 3-4, 1-2 at home and 2-2 on the road, with 189 points scored and 180 allowed. Arizona is 2-5, 0-3 at home and 2-2 on the road, with 142 scored and 208 allowed. Dak Prescott started the Cowboys' last game, at Philadelphia. Jacoby Brissett started Arizona's last game, at home against Denver.

Neil: Both of our wins came on the road, Cole. We like visiting.

Cole: You'll love it here. The parking alone is a day trip.

The matchup: Both run defenses have leaked. Each one allows 4.9 yards a carry. Dallas runs for 4.1 and Arizona runs for 3.6, so neither offense has made the most of it. In the pocket, Dallas has been a fortress, allowing a sack about once every forty dropbacks. Arizona's rush gets home about once every fourteen dropbacks, so something has to give. Arizona's blockers allow a sack about once every nineteen dropbacks, and the Dallas rush gets there about once every twenty-two.

Neil: Josh Sweat and Budda Baker are on my depth chart, Cole. They'll find a way into that fortress.

Cole: CeeDee Lamb is on mine. He finds his way into end zones. Quinnen Williams and Rashan Gary will find Brissett.

Neil: Trey McBride is the best thing on our roster. Watch him.

Cole: I'll be watching from the luxury suite, Neil. It's the business side.

As always, no injury talk. The Week 8 report isn't out yet, and neither of them is allowed to guess.

Neil: Last thing. Mike LaFleur, Nathaniel Hackett, Nick Rallis. Our staff is building something. What's yours building, Cole?

Cole: Brian Schottenheimer and Christian Parker are building a football team. Jerry Jones is building a brand. One of those is going very well.

Neil: Which one?

Cole: Ask me in January. If we're playing, it's both.

Respect to both: an Arizona team that plays its best football away from home, a Dallas team that can protect a quarterback with the best of them, and a Sunday afternoon beneath the roof in Arlington.

My call: Cowboys 28, Cardinals 25.
