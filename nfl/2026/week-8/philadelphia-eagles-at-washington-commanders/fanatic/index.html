Philadelphia Eagles at Washington Commanders

Philadelphia Eagles (2-5) at Washington Commanders (1-4-1)

8:20 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Northwest Stadium, Landover, MD · NBC

Philadelphia Eagles vs Washington Commanders on Sunday Night Football in Week 8 comes one day after the fifty-first Marine Corps Marathon shut down the Key and Memorial bridges, and Claire Benton, who thinks structure beats talent every time, spent Saturday admiring how Washington moves thousands of runners on schedule. Ray Donnelly, who explains every Eagles result by watching the guards, drove down I-95 from Philadelphia muttering that nobody ever won a marathon or a football game without somebody big clearing the road.

Claire: Mile markers, water stations, bridge closures timed to the minute. That's coaching, Ray. Joe Gibbs won three Super Bowls here, XVII, XXII and XXVI, with exactly that kind of plan.

Ray: Super Bowl LIX, forty to twenty-two against Kansas City. The guards won that game, and I'm the only one who wrote it down.

The trophy talk went both ways. Ray has Super Bowls LII and LIX and three NFL championships before the merger; Claire has two pre-merger titles and the Gibbs years. She offered Sammy Baugh, Cliff Battles, George Allen and Bobby Beathard. He offered Chuck Bednarik, Reggie White, Steve Van Buren, Pete Pihos and Bob Brown, and pointed out that every one of them was large.

Claire: Of course you'd go with the big men.

Ray: Of course I would. I won't take a quarterback opinion as a substitute for watching the guards.

Claire: And I won't pretend one quarterback saves a franchise without a structure around him.

Neither of them is enjoying the fall. Philadelphia is 2-5, 1-3 at home and 1-2 on the road, with 127 points scored and 175 allowed. Washington is 1-4-1, 1-1-1 at home and 0-3 away, with 132 scored and 172 allowed in six games. Tanner McKee started the Eagles' last game, at home against Dallas. Jayden Daniels started the Commanders' last game, at San Francisco in Week 6.

Claire: Thirteen days of rest. Dan Quinn had two weeks to rebuild the structure.

Ray: Six days for us. My guards are tired and yours are fresh. Somebody in this city just ran twenty-six miles, and he's probably less sore than my left guard.

Both defenses set up in a 3-4 base. Nick Sirianni runs Philadelphia, with Vic Fangio on defense and Sean Mannion calling the offense. Quinn has Daronte Jones on defense and David Blough on offense. Washington runs for 4.8 yards a carry against an Eagles defense allowing 4.3; Philadelphia runs for 3.9 against a Commanders defense allowing 4.2.

That 3.9 is what bothers Ray, and the pocket doesn't comfort him. The Eagles' rush has been the quietest in the building, a sack about once every thirty-one and a half dropbacks, and Philadelphia's quarterbacks go down about once every fourteen. Washington's blockers allow one about once every fifteen, and its rush gets home about once every twenty-one.

Ray: Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis are due. They've been due for weeks.

Claire: Daron Payne and Frankie Luvu are due too. Terry McLaurin is always due.

Ray: Saquon Barkley and DeVonta Smith. And Quinyon Mitchell will be glued to McLaurin.

Claire: Jacory Croskey-Merritt. Say it three times fast and he's already in the end zone.

The official practice sheet from Wednesday was lopsided. Philadelphia listed nobody. Washington had Daniels practicing fully and Stefon Diggs sitting out, with no game statuses yet. Claire wants Diggs back for the structure; Ray wants the structure tested without him.

Ray: If we win, the game ball goes to a guard.

Claire: If we win, it goes to the play sheet. Laminated, like a marathon course map.

Ray: The runners got the bridges Saturday. Sunday night, whoever wins up front gets the city.

My call: Commanders 23, Eagles 22.
