New England Patriots at Miami Dolphins

New England Patriots (4-3) at Miami Dolphins (1-5)

4:25 PM ET Sun Nov 1 · Hard Rock Stadium, Miami Gardens, FL · CBS

Sunday at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, 4:25 p.m. Eastern on CBS, the first Sunday back on standard time. It's an AFC East game. New England comes in at 4-3, unbeaten at home and struggling on the road. Miami is 1-5. Glen Tolliver thinks championships are receipts for work already done, and he cares only about the next one. Carla Ruiz looks at a broken roster the way a contractor looks at a house that needs new floors.

Glen: Rings are receipts, Carla. Six Super Bowls: XXXVI, XXXVIII, XXXIX, XLIX, LI and LIII. Those are receipts. They don't win next Sunday.

Carla: Add up the rebuild, Glen. That's my whole job. Miami won Super Bowls VII and VIII back to back, and I'm proud of that. But I don't throw good years after bad decisions. Sunk costs are sunk.

Glen: I respect that. I'm the guy who refuses to get nostalgic about the dynasty. We lost Super Bowl LX to Seattle, twenty-nine to thirteen. That's the receipt I keep in my wallet.

Carla: That's a painful wallet.

Glen: John Hannah. Mike Haynes. Ty Law. Randy Moss. Curtis Martin. Fine people to have on a receipt.

Carla: Five AFC titles for Miami, Glen. Our history is real. Our present is a construction site.

The season so far: New England is 4-3, 3-0 at home and 1-3 on the road, with 160 points scored and 143 allowed. Miami is 1-5, 0-2 at home and 1-3 on the road, with 98 scored and 157 allowed in six games. Drake Maye started the Patriots' last game, at Chicago. Malik Willis started the Dolphins' last game, at the Jets.

Carla: Our one win came on the road, Glen. Home cooking hasn't helped.

Glen: Our road record hasn't helped us either. Three and oh at home, one and three away. Process says fix the road.

The matchup: Mike Vrabel runs New England, with Zak Kuhr calling a defense that sets up in a 3-4 base and Josh McDaniels calling the offense. Jeff Hafley runs Miami, with Sean Duggan on defense in a 4-3 base and Bobby Slowik calling the offense. Miami runs for 4.7 yards a carry against a Patriots defense allowing 4.1, the best ground matchup on the field. New England runs for 4.4 against a Dolphins defense allowing 4.2.

In the pocket, Miami's quarterbacks have been sacked about once every eleven dropbacks, and New England's rush gets home about once every fourteen. Miami's rush has been the quietest in this game, getting a sack about once every twenty-nine and a half dropbacks. New England's blockers allow one about once every seventeen.

Glen: Christian Gonzalez and Milton Williams, Carla. They'll make Malik Willis earn every yard.

Carla: Ollie Gordon II and Malik Washington will make your defense earn every tackle. And Zach Sieler and Jordyn Brooks will be visiting Drake Maye.

Glen: A.J. Brown and Hunter Henry will be visiting your secondary.

Carla: We've got room for guests. We're rebuilding the guest house too.

No injury talk, as always. The Week 8 report isn't out, and we took their phones.

Glen: Last thing, Carla. When does the rebuild end?

Carla: When the books balance and the scoreboard agrees, Glen. Not one day sooner.

Respect to both: a New England team with a process and a perfect home record, a Miami team whose run game can make this a fight, and a division Sunday in South Florida where the visitors have ten days of rest.

My call: Patriots 26, Dolphins 19.
