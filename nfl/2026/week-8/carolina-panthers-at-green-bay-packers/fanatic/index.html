Carolina Panthers at Green Bay Packers

Carolina Panthers (3-3) at Green Bay Packers (4-3)

8:15 PM ET Thu Oct 29 · Lambeau Field, Green Bay, WI · Prime Video

Thursday night at Lambeau Field, 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Prime Video, the last kickoff before the clocks fall back. Carolina comes in at 3-3, Green Bay at 4-3, and both teams are on a short week. Paul Lindgren keeps the Packers' ledger of championships. Mitch Kovac reads the Panthers' depth chart like a bank statement. Somebody bring them hot cocoa, because neither one is leaving the porch.

Paul: Welcome to Lambeau, Mitch. Wipe your feet. Check the ledger: thirteen league championships. Eleven of them before the merger, and four Super Bowls, and yes, I count the two that overlap only once, so don't start.

Mitch: Thirteen. You say it like a phone number you memorized as a kid.

Paul: I did memorize it as a kid. Then I memorized the Super Bowls: I, II, XXXI and XLV. Roman numerals, Mitch. We write our history like the Senate.

Mitch: Show me the depth chart, Paul. Banners don't tackle. Carolina won the NFC South last season, and we did it the right way, from the inside out. Julius Peppers is the template: drafted here, developed here, a Hall of Famer. Homegrown defenders are the backbone of every good team this franchise has ever had.

Paul: Homegrown is lovely. So were Herb Adderley, Jerry Kramer, Willie Davis and LeRoy Butler. I can keep going until the brats get cold.

Mitch: You always do.

The season so far: Green Bay is 4-3, 2-1 at home, and has been walking a tightrope all year, 144 points scored and 145 allowed. Carolina is 3-3, 2-1 on the road, with 159 scored and 166 allowed in six games. Jordan Love started Green Bay's last game, at Detroit. Bryce Young started Carolina's, at home against Tampa Bay.

Paul: A tightrope is still a rope, Mitch, and we're still standing on it.

Mitch: By one point across seven games. That's not a ledger, Paul. That's a gas station receipt.

The matchup: Both defenses set up in a 3-4 base front. Ejiro Evero runs Carolina's, Jonathan Gannon runs Green Bay's. The ground game is where the argument lives. Green Bay is running for 3.2 yards a carry, and Carolina's defense has been allowing 4.9. Carolina runs for 4.5 a carry against a Green Bay defense that allows 4.1.

Mitch: Read that again, Paul. Your offense gets 3.2 a carry against a defense that gives up 4.9 to everybody else. Matt LaFleur and Adam Stenavich are going to be drawing plays on a napkin by halftime.

Paul: Napkins are for brats, Mitch. When the ground game stalls, we throw it. You've seen Lambeau in late October. The ball flies fine. Your fingers are the problem.

Then there is the pocket. Carolina's quarterbacks have been sacked about once every fourteen dropbacks, and Green Bay's rush gets home about once every eighteen. Green Bay's blockers give up a sack about once every twenty dropbacks, and Carolina's rush arrives at about that same pace.

Paul: Once every fourteen, Mitch. Our guys can count that high, and they will.

Mitch: Dan Morgan built this roster front first, Paul. Derrick Brown and Jaelan Phillips are on my depth chart for a reason. We'll count, too.

One thing neither of them gets to talk about is the injury report, because the Week 8 report isn't out yet. They both tried. I took their phones.

Paul: Fine. Last thing. Since your first season, how many Super Bowl trophies, Mitch?

Mitch: None yet. Two NFC titles, a division banner from last year, and a building full of players we drafted ourselves. Ask me again in February.

Paul: I'll write it in pencil. The ledger only takes ink for titles.

Mitch: Keep the pencil sharp, Paul. You'll need it Thursday night.

Respect to both: a Carolina team that has played its best football away from home, a Green Bay team that cannot stop playing one-score games, and one Thursday night in the most storied building in the sport.

My call: Packers 24, Panthers 22.
