Monday Morning Quarterback: NFL weekend review, Nov 1, 2026

Monday, Nov. 2, 2026.

ShareXFacebook

Part 1: Handicap Recap

Thirteen Week 8 games are final, from Thursday night in Green Bay through Sunday night in Washington, and the people holding tickets felt every snap of them: two overtime games, a pile of half-point finishes, an over/under that won or lost depending on which number you grabbed, and a Sunday night field goal that hit an upright and then counted for nothing.

Bears at Seahawks tonight is the last Week 8 game, and it gets graded in Tuesday's edition.

Eagles 10, Commanders 27

For one quarter the Eagles crowd was counting money. Andrew Mukuba picked off Jayden Daniels on Washington's first drive, but Jonathan Greenard was offside and the pick came off the board. Two Washington false starts later, Drew Stevens missed a 55-yarder wide right. Jake Elliott hit from 40 at 6:10. Then Mukuba punched the ball away from Chig Okonkwo, Zack Baun recovered at the Washington 26, Tanner McKee hit Saquon Barkley for 24 yards to the 2, and Barkley took a direct snap in from 3 yards at 4:27. Eagles 10, Commanders 0, and every Eagles ticket on the board was cashing.

That was the last point Philadelphia scored. Stevens hit from 56 with 28 seconds left in the first quarter. Daniels threw an 8-yard touchdown to Stefon Diggs at 4:56 of the second, and Stevens hit from 39 with 19 seconds left in the half. Commanders 13, Eagles 10 at halftime, 23 points on the board.

Late in the third, Dorance Armstrong strip-sacked McKee and Charles Omenihu fell on it. Daniels hit Treylon Burks for 53 yards to the Philadelphia 7, the drive stalled, and Stevens kicked a 25-yarder at 1:33. Commanders 16, Eagles 10. The Eagles at the closing +5.5 were losing by half a point, and the Over at the closing 40.5 needed 15 more points. With 10 seconds left in the quarter Trey Amos intercepted McKee, and a roughing the passer flag on K'Lavon Chaisson wiped it out. Eagles backers kept the ball and got a second life.

That drive is the one Eagles ticket holders will be replaying all week. It got to the Washington 13. A Philadelphia false start backed it up, Elliott's 36-yarder went through, and an illegal formation flag on Philadelphia took it off the board. From 41 yards, Elliott hit the left upright at 9:01 of the fourth. Three points there makes it 16-13, and the Eagles are covering the closing number with nine minutes to play.

Instead Stevens hit from 42 at 3:43. Commanders 19, Eagles 10, 29 points. Chaisson sacked McKee for an 11-yard loss, Rasul Douglas picked off McKee and returned it 22 yards to the Philadelphia 37, Rachaad White ran 33 yards to the 4, and White scored from a yard out at 1:52. Austin Ekeler ran in the two-point try. Commanders 27, Eagles 10, 37 points.

The Over at the closing 40.5 still had one breath left. Odafe Oweh sacked McKee with 31 seconds to go, and on the last snap of the night McKee hit Eli Stowers for 34 yards to the Washington 8 as time ran out. A touchdown on that play puts the game at 43 points and cashes the closing Over. It ended 8 yards short. Add Elliott's wiped-out field goal to the 37 and it is still 40, half a point under the closing number. The Over at the opening 46.5 never had a prayer after halftime.

Called it at both numbers: Follow The Money on the Commanders, covered by 18.5 points at the opening Commanders +1.5 and covered by 11.5 points at the closing Commanders −5.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the over/under; In The Trenches on the side or the over/under.

Chiefs 24, Broncos 28

The Broncos were the team on most of the cards, and for three quarters that looked like a long Sunday. Mahomes threw an 11-yard touchdown to Rashee Rice, then a 73-yarder to Emmett Johnson at 7:40 of the second quarter for a 14-6 lead. Denver got back in it when a Kansas City interference flag set up Nix's 1-yard throw to Jaylen Waddle at 1:15, and Harrison Butker's 45-yarder sent the Chiefs to the half up 17-13. Mahomes found Noah Gray from 4 yards out at 1:38 of the third, and it was 24-13.

Then the fourth quarter happened. At 11:23 Nix hit Adam Trautman for 22 yards and an unnecessary roughness flag tacked on 15 more. At 8:55 Nix threw a 2-yard touchdown to Nate Adkins and found Waddle for the two-point try. That made it 24-21, and it also put 45 points on the board, which cashed the Over at both the opening 43.5 and the closing 42 right there.

The side still needed work. Alex Singleton sacked Mahomes for 6 yards at 2:44, and Butker's 54-yarder sailed wide right. One snap later Nix hit Jonah Coleman for 51 yards to the Kansas City 5, and Coleman ran it in at 1:56. Broncos 28, Chiefs 24.

And then the longest 1:56 of the weekend. Johnson returned the kickoff 44 yards, Travis Kelce caught one for 32 to the Denver 18, and Walker got the Chiefs to the Denver 1 with 35 seconds left. A Kansas City touchdown there flips the side. Mahomes threw incomplete. Walker got stuffed at the 1 by Skinner with 9 seconds left. Kansas City called its last timeout at 0:04, then a flag for moving early backed the Chiefs up to the 6, and Malcolm Roach sacked Mahomes at the 12 as the clock ran out. Broncos tickets got to breathe again.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side.

Patriots 24, Dolphins 19

Miami's backers were holding the Dolphins plus a touchdown, and the Over crowd needed a shootout that took its time showing up. Miami led 13-3 at the half after a JuJu Brents fumble recovery set up a Zane Gonzalez field goal, Gonzalez added a 52-yarder, Andy Borregales missed from 40 for New England, and Malik Willis threw a 1-yard touchdown to Malik Washington with 8 seconds left in the half, a drive helped by an unnecessary roughness flag on New England's Christian Elliss.

New England ran off 21 straight. Maye's 43-yard touchdown to Kyle Williams at 9:23 of the third counted only after the Patriots won a challenge, Maye ran one in from 2 yards at 0:59, and after Kevin Byard intercepted Willis, Maye hit DeMario Douglas for 48 yards at 12:39 of the fourth. Patriots 24, Dolphins 13, and the Dolphins were 4 points on the wrong side of the number. At 11:54 Elliss picked off Willis, but an offside flag on New England wiped it out. Gonzalez missed from 43 at 6:45.

With 2:04 left Willis threw a 35-yard touchdown to K. Coleman. That made it 24-19 and the Dolphins were covering again. Then the two-point throw to Will Kacmarek fell incomplete, and the scoreboard froze at 43 points. Maye knelt three times. The Over at the opening 45.5 came up 2.5 short, but the closing number was 41.5, so the Over cashed at the close by a point and a half. Same game, same 43 points, two different tickets.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Vikings 15, Lions 19

This one came down to half a point, and which half you got depended on when you got your number. The game opened Lions −4.5 and closed Lions −3.5.

Detroit had a St. Brown touchdown taken off the board by replay early, and Jake Bates kicked field goals from 25 and 37 around Kyler Murray's 1-yard touchdown to Justin Jefferson, which was set up by a 23-yard interference flag on D.J. Reed. Vikings 7, Lions 6 at the half. Bates then clanked a 46-yarder off the left upright in the third quarter, and Jahmyr Gibbs went 30 yards for a touchdown at 4:40, but the two-point try failed and it was 12-7.

Murray hit T.J. Hockenson for an 8-yard touchdown at 8:44 of the fourth and Jordan Addison caught the two-point throw, 15-12 Vikings. Goff answered with a 7-yard touchdown to Jameson Williams at 4:35. Minnesota went three-and-out, Goff knelt it away, and Detroit won by 4.

Four points. Vikings +4.5 at the open cashed by half a point. Vikings +3.5 at the close lost by half a point. The Lions' failed two-point try in the third and Bates's doink off the upright are the only reasons it was that close.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Cardinals 33, Cowboys 30

The Cardinals side was never the problem. Arizona had the points at every number on the board, and Arizona won the game outright. The Under was the story, and it is a sad one.

Dallas went up 14-0 after Shemar James returned an interception of Jacoby Brissett 33 yards to the Arizona 5 and James Mitchell fell on a fumble in the end zone, then Dak Prescott threw a 9-yard touchdown to Ryan Flournoy. Tyler Allgeier ran 63 yards for a touchdown late in the third, and Prescott scrambled in from 5 early in the fourth for a 24-10 lead.

Then Minshew, behind center for Arizona since late in the third, went to work. Two Arizona flags for moving early inside the Dallas 5 did not stop him from throwing a 5-yard touchdown to Allgeier at 10:47. Brandon Aubrey's 35-yarder at 3:54 made it 27-17, and the scoreboard sat at 44 points. Roughing the passer on Rashan Gary set up Minshew's 14-yard touchdown to Trey McBride at 2:11. Aubrey hit a 41-yarder at 1:09 to make it 30-24. That put 54 points on the board, half a point under the closing 54.5, with 69 seconds left. Minshew needed four completions and three spikes to get to the Dallas 13, and with 6 seconds left he hit Michael Wilson for the tying touchdown. Sixty points. Chad Ryland missed the extra point wide left, which did not save a single Under ticket, and then made a 42-yarder in overtime to win it 33-30.

The opener was 47.5, so the Under was gone a lot earlier on those tickets. At the close it lasted until the final 6 seconds of regulation.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Raiders 38, Jets 33

Nobody needed this to be a 71-point game, unless they had the Over. Dylan Laube returned a kickoff 95 yards for a touchdown right after the Jets scored, Brock Bowers caught two touchdowns, and Kirk Cousins ran one in from a yard out. It was 23-14 Jets at the half, 37 points. Bowers's second touchdown at 9:59 of the third killed the Under at the opening 40.5, and a Braelon Allen 21-yard touchdown run 99 seconds later killed it at the closing 46.5.

The side was the sweat. The Jets were laying 1.5 at both numbers. Jalen Nailor's 2-yard touchdown at 11:33 of the fourth put the Raiders up 35-30, and Jason Sanders cut it to 35-33 at 4:31. Down 2, the Jets were in the worst spot on the board, where even a winning field goal loses Jets −1.5 by half a point. The Raiders then kicked a 53-yarder with 27 seconds left, 38-33. Now a Jets touchdown wins the game by 2 and cashes Jets −1.5. An unnecessary roughness flag on Jeremy Chinn gave New York 15 free yards. On the next snap Geno Smith went deep for Garrett Wilson, Maxx Crosby got to him, and Chinn picked it off in the end zone.

Called it at both numbers: The Formula and Next Man Up on the Over, went over by 30.5 points at the opening 40.5 and went over by 24.5 points at the closing 46.5; Next Man Up, Letdown Watch and In The Trenches on the Raiders, covered by 6.5 points at the opening Raiders +1.5 and covered by 6.5 points at the closing Raiders +1.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Titans 36, Bengals 43

Tennessee opened +6.5 and closed +13.5, and that seven-point gap turned out to be the whole story of the side. Amani Hooker intercepted Joe Burrow three times. Tony Pollard scored twice. Cincinnati got a safety from Demetrius Knight Jr. late in the third, and the teams traded the lead in the fourth: Cam Ward's 11-yard touchdown to Chimere Dike, Burrow's 25-yarder to Mike Gesicki, Ward's 15-yard touchdown to Wan'Dale Robinson plus a two-point catch by Gunnar Helm, and Burrow's 18-yarder to Tee Higgins with 1:17 left. It was 36-36 after regulation.

In overtime Burrow scrambled 9 yards for a touchdown at 7:00, and Tennessee's last fourth-down throw fell incomplete. Bengals by 7. Titans +13.5 at the close cashed by 6.5. Titans +6.5 at the open lost by half a point on an overtime scramble.

The Under was dead by 11:29 of the fourth quarter. In The Trenches, the lone Over voice, cashed by 29.5 at the open and 32.5 at the close.

Called it at both numbers: In The Trenches on the Over, went over by 29.5 points at the opening 49.5 and went over by 32.5 points at the closing 46.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Panthers 19, Packers 27

Thursday night set the tone for the whole week of half-point sweats.

Green Bay led 20-10 at the half on Jordan Love touchdowns to Tucker Kraft and Matthew Golden and a Josh Jacobs 1-yard run, and Love's 4-yard touchdown to Jacobs at 7:30 of the third made it 27-10. Trey Smack's first extra point hit the left upright, which mattered later. Zakee Wheatley intercepted Love at the Carolina 2 early in the fourth to keep Carolina alive.

Bryce Young hit Jalen Coker for 26 yards at 5:37, and the two-point try failed. That left it 27-19 with 46 points. At the close, the Under 47.5 stayed under by 1.5, and the Panthers +3.5 crowd was 4.5 short. At the open, the Panthers +7 crowd lost by 1, and the Under 45.5 lost by half a point. Carolina's last play was a catch-and-lateral that Green Bay's Cox recovered. One more missed kick, by either team, and the opening Under cashes.

Called it at both numbers: Letdown Watch on the Packers, covered by 1 point at the opening Packers −7 and covered by 4.5 points at the closing Packers −3.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under; In The Trenches on the side or the over/under.

Colts 7, Jaguars 42

The side was over by halftime. Trevor Lawrence threw touchdowns to Thomas and Nate Boerkircher, Bhayshul Tuten ran 39 yards for another, and Lane returned a Daniel Jones interception 92 yards for a touchdown with 10 seconds left in the half. Jaguars 28, Colts 0.

The over/under was the drama. The game opened at 49.5 and closed at 45.5. Josh Downs caught a 6-yard touchdown at 3:59 of the third, and on the last play of the quarter Lawrence hit Tuten for a 25-yard touchdown. That made it 42-7, 49 points. Nobody scored in the fourth. The Over cashed at the closing 45.5 by 3.5 and lost at the opening 49.5 by half a point. One field goal in those 15 minutes, and the Over cashes at both numbers.

Called it at both numbers: Letdown Watch and Follow The Money on the Jaguars, covered by 30.5 points at the opening Jaguars −4.5 and covered by 24.5 points at the closing Jaguars −10.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the over/under.

Ravens 10, Bills 36

Buffalo won by 26. Josh Allen ran one in from a yard, Tyler Bass kicked three field goals, and Allen added touchdowns to DJ Moore and Dalton Kincaid in the fourth. A Derrick Henry touchdown run in the third was wiped out by a holding call, Baltimore settled for a field goal, and the Ravens never scored again. Bills −2.5 at the open and −3 at the close both cashed easily. The teams finished with 46 points, under the opening 51.5 and the closing 55.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side.

Browns 6, Steelers 27

Pittsburgh scored the last 14 points and never let Cleveland into the end zone. Rico Dowdle ran for a touchdown, Chris Boswell kicked two field goals, Pat Freiermuth took a direct snap in from 1 yard with a second left in the half, and Jaylen Warren scored in the third. Andre Szmyt's two field goals were all the Browns got. The Over needed 40 at the open and 39 at the close, and the game stopped at 33.

Called it at both numbers: Letdown Watch on the Steelers, covered by 15 points at the opening Steelers −6 and covered by 17.5 points at the closing Steelers −3.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side or the over/under.

Falcons 0, Buccaneers 16

Atlanta got shut out. Alex Anzalone intercepted Penix twice in the first quarter, Zyon McCollum added one in the fourth, Chase McLaughlin kicked three field goals, and Bucky Irving ran 4 yards for the only touchdown. Tampa Bay won by 16, the Falcons lost at both numbers, and the Under cashed by 30.5 at the open and 27 at the close.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side.

Chargers 9, Rams 24

The Chargers led 9-3 on three Cameron Dicker field goals, and then the Rams took over. Kyren Williams scored with 19 seconds left in the half, Blake Corum ran 23 yards for a touchdown in the third, and after Trent McDuffie intercepted Justin Herbert, Matthew Stafford hit Puka Nacua for 18 yards. Rams by 15, covering −4.5 at the open and −6.5 at the close. The scoreboard stopped at 33 points, well under the opening 48.5 and the closing 45.5.

No pick: Follow The Money on the side.

Part 2: Looking forward

Bears at Seahawks.

Kickoff is 8:15 PM ET tonight on ESPN at Lumen Field in Seattle. The Bears come in 5-2, 2-1 on the road, and beat the Patriots 30-24 last time out. The Seahawks are 4-3, 3-1 at home, and beat the Chiefs 20-3 last time out.

This game opened Seahawks −4.5 with the over/under at 47.5. As of 6:35 PM ET Monday the Action Network consensus was still Seahawks −3.5 with the over/under at 46.5, and DraftKings on ESPN had Seahawks −3 and 46.5. The card was built on Seahawks −3 and 46.5.

Everybody with a pick is on the Under, so the whole panel will be rooting for punts, field goals and a running clock. The side splits three to one. Seattle by exactly 4 is the nightmare finish: it beats the current 3.5 and loses to the opening 4.5, and this week has already had plenty of those.

ESPN lists Seattle tackle Charles Cross and wide receiver Rashid Shaheed out, and linebacker Uchenna Nwosu doubtful. Chicago has running back Kyle Monangai out and tight end Cole Kmet on injured reserve. Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 58 catches for 867 yards and 8 touchdowns for Seattle. D'Andre Swift has 476 rushing yards and 7 touchdowns for Chicago, and Kyler Gordon has 4 sacks.
