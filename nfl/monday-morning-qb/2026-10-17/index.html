Monday Morning Quarterback: NFL weekend review, Oct 17, 2026

19, 2026. College Saturday (Oct. 17) and NFL Sunday (Oct. 18). Then Monday night and next weekend.

Monday, Oct.

Part 1: The misses

Every pick below is graded against the line printed on its card when the game kicked off, not the closing line. A push counts as neither a hit nor a miss.

Monday night's Commanders-49ers game has not been played. It leads off Part 3.

Bears 31, Falcons 23

The Formula's case was a ratings gap: Falcons +2.6, Bears −5.9, plus home field, for Atlanta by 10.3. Follow The Money's case was the line moving from Falcons +3.5 to +1.5. Chicago led 21-7 at the half, and the ratings gap never showed up on the field.

Atlanta's first drive of the second half died on fourth-and-3 at the Chicago 28 with an incompletion. Late in the third, Zachariah Branch fumbled a kickoff and Chicago recovered at the Atlanta 31. That became a Cairo Santos 50-yard field goal and a 31-7 lead.

Atlanta still had a way back at 31-23 with about three minutes left. A holding call erased a 9-yard completion on third-and-6. On third-and-16, Kyler Gordon strip-sacked Michael Penix Jr. and Chicago recovered at the Atlanta 28. That ended the cover.

Ravens 10, Browns 13

The Formula had the Ravens by 9.6 on the ratings. The Ravens scored 10 points in the first quarter and none after it.

The game turned in about 90 seconds early in the fourth quarter. Tyler Loop missed a 49-yard field goal wide left at 10-3. On Baltimore's next snap, Derrick Henry fumbled and Cleveland recovered at the Baltimore 35. Audric Estime scored from 2 yards out to tie it 10-10 with 8:33 left. From there Baltimore needed to win by four, and it never scored again.

The finish was a penalty story. With 2 seconds left, Andre Szmyt's 42-yard try was blocked, but the Ravens were flagged for offside on the play, so the block did not count. Szmyt made the next one from 37.

Titans 26, Colts 20

The averaged projection was a 44-point game, and the game went right up to the number. With 2:03 left it was 23-20, a 43-point game. Indianapolis then went backward: a sack on third-and-10, then an incompletion on fourth-and-17 from its own 31. That handed Tennessee the ball at the Colts' 31, and Joey Slye's 44-yard field goal with 43 seconds left made it 46.

No projection includes a blocked punt returned for a touchdown, and this game had one. J. Chestnut blocked it and Oluwafemi Oladejo returned it 14 yards for a 20-13 lead with 10:17 left.

Jets 19, Patriots 26

The card named this exact risk before kickoff: "A Patriots win by exactly 7 lands on the Jets' side, which is why the hook matters here." A win by exactly 7 is what happened.

Three plays did it. First, Andy Borregales missed the extra point after Hunter Henry's 7-yard touchdown with 14:10 left, so it was 23-13 instead of 24-13. Second, with 1:14 left New England had fourth-and-1 at the Jets' 4, took a delay-of-game penalty, and kicked a 27-yard field goal for 26-16. Third, on the Jets' last drive, K. Byard was called for pass interference, 17 yards, to the New England 18 with 46 seconds left. Jason Sanders kicked a 36-yarder with 32 seconds left.

That one kick made it a 7-point game and a 45-point game. It flipped the side and pushed the total over at the same time.

Saints 34, Giants 27

The card saw a coin flip: the ratings had the Giants by 0.1. It was 10-10 at the half, and then New Orleans scored 24 straight points. Tyler Shough threw a 31-yard touchdown to Alvin Kamara and ran one in from a yard out. Daniel Carlson added a 51-yard field goal. Jameis Winston threw two interceptions in that stretch, and the second set up Shough's 18-yard touchdown to Chris Olave for 34-10. J.J. McCarthy took the Giants' snaps from there.

The Under was still alive at 44 points with 11:31 left. Then the Giants scored 17 points in the final 11 minutes. Tyrone Tracy Jr.'s 5-yard touchdown run plus McCarthy's two-point pass to Isaiah Likely made it 52 with 7:00 left, and the Under was gone.

The side had one last chance. McCarthy drove the Giants from their own 29 to the Saints' 14 in 45 seconds. On fourth-and-1 with 12 seconds left, his pass for Likely fell incomplete. A touchdown and the extra point there would have tied the game, and New York +1.5 would have been safe.

Steelers 26, Buccaneers 19 (overtime)

The Formula had two nearly identical teams, Tampa Bay by 1.6 with home field. Follow The Money had the total falling from 40.5 to 38.5. At the end of regulation, both were still alive.

Tampa Bay led 16-3 going into the fourth quarter. Pittsburgh's touchdown drive early in the fourth got a 15-yard roughing-the-passer call on C. Kancey and ended in Darnell Washington's 5-yard touchdown. Later, on third-and-5, a 12-yard pass interference call on J. Parrish kept alive the drive that ended with Chris Boswell's 58-yard field goal to tie it 16-16.

On Tampa Bay's second snap after the kickoff, J. Dean intercepted Jalon Daniels and returned it 40 yards to the Tampa Bay 3. Pittsburgh settled for Boswell's 20-yarder. Chase McLaughlin's 53-yard field goal with 8 seconds left tied it 19-19. At the end of regulation, Tampa Bay +2.5 was covering and the total was 38, half a point under.

Overtime took both. Pittsburgh went 70 yards in 12 plays, and Aaron Rodgers hit Germie Bernard for a 6-yard touchdown with 3:15 left in the extra period. One throw flipped the side and the total.

Cardinals 18, Rams 16

Follow The Money had no pick on the side. The Formula's case was Rams +6.1, Cardinals −8.9, plus home field, for the Rams by 16.8.

Brooks for 21 yards and J. Wallace was flagged 15 more for unnecessary roughness, to the Rams' 29. That penalty put Arizona in field-goal range, and Ryland made the 43-yarder as time expired.
